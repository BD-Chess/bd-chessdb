01 · The Floor That Counted

Varda Ring, Service Deck Four, the Rask apartment · night one, 03:10

The pulse came up through the floor at ten past three, and Illuminara felt it before the monitor on Pia Rask's wrist did. She had been sitting cross-legged beside the bed since midnight with both palms flat on the deck plate, reading it the way a musician reads a page: every push in the metal had a place it came from and a place it meant to go, and tonight every push that mattered was headed for a nine-year-old's pillow.

Pia's breathing changed. Her father crossed the room before the first chime.

Odd RaskNow? Is it now?

IlluminaraNow. On her side, and don't hold her down. I have the floor.

She did, as far as anyone could. She caught the pulse where it entered the plate, took it through her wrists and shoulders and let it out into the hull along a path of her own choosing, so that the bed rode on a floor that only looked as though it shook. It was like carrying a full bath across a room. She could do it for one night.

The seizure lasted ninety seconds. It felt much longer to the two people counting.

Afterwards Pia lay with her eyes open and her hand against the wall, and tapped.

PiaSeven. Seven in one second. The drum is slower than that.

Odd RaskShe counts it. The clinic said not to let her. She does it anyway.

IlluminaraI'd like her to. Pia, what does the drum feel like?

PiaLike someone stepping. The seven isn't stepping. The seven is when two people hum and one of them is a bit wrong.

Illuminara looked up. Two hums, a bit apart. No single machine made a pulse like that; two notes that disagreed made it, and only where they met.

Not the hall alone, she thought. The hall and something that answers it.

Odd RaskWe pay for it, you know. Two years. Twenty-eight credits a month on every service deck, 'structural maintenance.' They told us it was for the noise.

IlluminaraWho told you?

Odd RaskThe man who sells the quiet.

Pia's fingers found Illuminara's sleeve and held it the way a child holds a railing. Illuminara's arms ached to the elbows and she did not move them. It was a small, unreasonable happiness, to be used as a railing.

02 · The Version That Frightens You

Varda Ring, the Meridian Hall, Sel Venn's office · day two, late afternoon

Sel Venn kept his office directly above the concert hall's ceiling, so that the whole room carried a faint, expensive hum. He was sixty and courteous and visibly short of sleep. He laid a ledger on the table before she asked, and she noted it: the gesture of someone who has had to defend this before.

Sel VennYou've been sleeping on a service deck. I would rather you saw my numbers than someone's account of them.

IlluminaraThen show me the version that frightens you.

He did. Forty-one Quiet-tier leases paid sixty-two parts in a hundred of the ring's air bill. Concert fees paid thirty-one, a part for every night the hall sold; the Quiet leases capped that at thirty-one nights a year. Ordinary rents paid the last seven. The service-deck surcharge paid for the hush plates in the Quiet tier and for the hall's debt, and it was not labelled as either.

Sel VennThe air contract renews in five months. If the Quiet tier leaves, nobody on this ring breathes on credit. That is Deck Four too, Illuminara.

IlluminaraAnd the vibration?

Sel VennGoes where the map says it can go.

He unrolled it: a survey older than his father's tenure. Relief ribs through Decks Three to Five were inked blue and labelled DAMPED PATH. Maintenance Cavity Nine was hatched red and marked SEALED, DO NOT COUPLE.

Sel VennThe hall pushes bass into the hull. The hull has to put it somewhere. The map says Four. I didn't draw the map.

IlluminaraDid you choose not to test it?

He looked at the posters for the Autumn Cycle and not at her.

Sel VennA test means the hall goes dark for a night. The Quiet tier reads dark as failing, and fourteen of those leases are month to month. I did the arithmetic on whose sleep, and I got it wrong the way people do when the sum is on one side of the page and the faces on the other. My own sensors flagged Deck Four eleven times. I read them as noise.

IlluminaraI'm not asking you to close the hall. I'm asking for one rehearsal night and the key to Cavity Nine. I won't take your building from you, Mr. Venn. I'll take your map to people who can check it.

He did not say yes. He laid a key on top of the ledger, and it was all the agreement he could afford.

03 · Deck Nine

Varda Ring, Deck Nine, Quiet tier, Marit Osei's apartment · day three, evening

Marit Osei was seventy-one, a retired acoustics technician, and her flat was so still that Illuminara could hear the tea cooling. She had not asked her to come. She had read about the seizures on the deck boards, like everyone, and she met Illuminara at the door with her arms folded.

Marit OseiI paid for this silence. My wife's migraines are real and so is my hearing aid. I won't apologise for it, and I won't give it away to prove I'm a good person.

IlluminaraNo one is asking you to. I'm asking whether you can read a survey.

Marit took the map and read it with a professional's contempt for draughtsmen. She stopped at the legend.

Marit OseiThere's no stage on this. No bass stack, no hall. The damped path assumes a ring that carries nothing low. Whoever hung the hush plates on this hull just inherited the map.

IlluminaraAnd Cavity Nine?

Marit OseiSealed because the old ring would have rung against the hub. The hub's been recoupled twice since. Nobody re-tested. It might ring clean now. It might not. I'd want a mallet, not a theory.

IlluminaraWould you test it with me?

Marit OseiOn one condition. My wife's quiet isn't cut to fund anyone's anything.

IlluminaraIt won't be. But if your quiet only exists because someone else's floor shakes, it isn't quiet, it's a debt. I'd like to find out whether it can be yours and theirs together.

Marit looked at her for a long moment, the way one looks at a tuner who has just played the right note.

Marit OseiBring the mallet. I'll bring ears.

04 · A Rib One Deck Down

Varda Ring, Maintenance Cavity Nine · night five, 02:00

Sixty families had taped phones to their walls, one in every bay on every deck, each set to feel the hull. Odd Rask stood at the cavity's old seal with a mallet borrowed from the dockyard. Marit watched a tablet, and Illuminara stood with one hand on the wall, because she had told them where the energy would go. Pia was asleep on Deck Two at her aunt's, out of the test's reach. Illuminara had insisted. The test would use the girl's number, not her bed.

IlluminaraJunction A. Upper rib. It will go up and sideways and fade.

Odd struck once. The seal rang like a bell wrapped in cloth.

Marit OseiNot A. It went to B and kept going. Your rib is wrong.

Illuminara read the plate again. Her reading had been clean and wrong: she had followed the load as the survey drew it, and the survey had put a rib one deck too high.

IlluminaraMy path was wrong. The rib is a deck lower than the map says. That tells us where. It doesn't tell us why Pia counts seven.

Marit OseiNo, it doesn't. I came to measure how loud. Loud isn't what the child counted. Seven is a difference between two notes. Find me both notes.

Marit OseiThree runs. Hall alone. Plates alone. Both.

Odd's crew went up to Deck Nine and braced the hush plates solid with steel bars. Overhead, the hall began its bass check, as scheduled. On the tablet, Deck Four drew one flat band.

Marit OseiThirty-six cycles. That's the hall, steady as a table. Nothing to count.

The bass stopped. Upstairs, one of Odd's crew struck a hush plate with a rubber mallet and let it ring through the frame. A second band appeared, lower and just as flat.

Marit OseiTwenty-nine. That's what the plates hum back when they're hit. Deck Nine thinks it bought a wall. It bought a bell.

The bars came off. The bass came back. Both bands drew together on Deck Four, and between them the line began to swell and shrink, swell and shrink, so fast that on the screen it looked like a solid block. Marit counted the swells against her watch, her lips moving.

Marit OseiSeven in one second. Thirty-six less twenty-nine. That's your girl's number. The hall pushes, the plates answer a bit wrong, and the rib lands both of them on Four.

Footsteps came up behind them through the cavity. Sel Venn stood in the hatch alone, with the key in his hand.

He crossed to the wall and laid his palm flat against it. Illuminara watched him feel it: what had been in the Rasks' bed for two years, seven times a second, coming up through his hand. He sat down on a crate as though his knees had made the decision.

Sel VennAny night in two years I could have heard this. I paid people so I wouldn't have to.

Odd RaskDon't say sorry to me. Say it to the numbers. I'll be reading them.

Venn nodded once. Illuminara did not ask Odd to say anything else, and did not look at Venn to see whether he wanted her to.

Marit OseiCrack the seal. A hand's width. Same again.

Odd levered the old seal open with the mallet and a bar. The swell on Deck Four thinned to a single band in two seconds. Marit ran her thumb down the other fifty-nine bays, looking for where it had gone.

Marit OseiNowhere. It goes down the cavity wall into the hub. Take away the second note's road and the first has nothing to beat against. No bay is worse off than an hour ago.

Odd chocked the seal at that width with a steel wedge, and Marit chalked the setting on the frame: OPEN, TESTED. It would stand that way until real dampers could be built.

IlluminaraThere is a way you keep the hall and the building, Mr. Venn. It starts with telling sixty families what you just told me.

Sel VennAnd if they vote me out?

IlluminaraThen they vote. It's theirs to decide.

05 · What the Ledger Allows

Varda Ring, the deck boards, then the Deck Six assembly hall · day six, noon to 20:00

Venn's notice went up on every deck board at noon, under his own signature. Eleven sensor flags, dated and unanswered, one for each time his system had told him about Deck Four. Behind them, the surcharge ledger with the two labels he had never written: hush plates, hall debt. He had not warned his board or the air contractor. Illuminara heard it from a woman in the laundry queue, who read it aloud to the queue in the voice of someone reading a summons.

By four o'clock nine Quiet-tier tenants had given notice and the contractor's agent had asked for a call. Venn took it in the corridor, and did not look round to see whether Illuminara was listening.

Odd read the notice twice at the hall door.

Odd RaskThat's the notice. It isn't an apology, and I'm not treating it as one.

Sel VennNo. It's so that tonight you can vote on something.

She put the design on the wall in four lines and then stopped talking, because it was not hers to sell. Tonight the old flange of Cavity Nine would be bolted open at the hand's width they had tested, so the hull's low note had somewhere to go until it no longer needed to. Dampers tuned to twenty-nine would go into the cavity after the Cycle, so that the plates' answer died there for good. The Autumn Cycle would go ahead as sold, and its gate would pay for them. Every deck, not just the Quiet tier, would get the same night floor, written into its lease.

Venn spoke first, for under a minute, with his own notice behind him on the wall.

Sel VennI sold you a silence made out of your children's floors. I told myself the sum needed it. I never checked. The rest of what I owe isn't mine to price.

Marit OseiAnd the Quiet tier pays what, exactly, for a floor it already has? Sixty-two parts of the air came from us. His fear is a good one.

IlluminaraI don't know the right number. His ledger does, and you can read it.

Marit took the chalk from the rail meant for the agenda and drew two columns.

Before. Quiet leases, 62. Gate, 31. Ordinary rents, 7. After, for twenty-four months. Quiet leases, 44. Gate, 40. An equal air share on every lease, 16. Gate counted in event nights over a rolling year, the way the Quiet cap had always counted them.

Marit OseiThe Quiet tier stops buying silence and pays an air share, sliding down to level over two years. The premium was only ever buying a cap on concert nights, thirty-one a year. With a floor in every lease and a cavity that takes the bass, nobody needs the cap. Forty nights a year, a part a night. The gate carries what we stop paying.

Sel VennThe gate carries it if the hall sells forty. It sold thirty-one last year.

Odd RaskThen say what happens short of forty.

Marit wrote a third line under the columns. Gate under forty event nights in any rolling twelve months: the reserve pays the gap. Under thirty-four: the reserve pays the air, and this room votes again. More event nights, or a flat share on every lease. Nobody's quiet is cut to cover it. Everybody's bill moves.

A man on Deck Five asked whether the leases would really change. Marit answered him before Illuminara could. She had already crossed out one word in the clause and written another: quiet would stop being a product and become the floor. That was the amendment, and it was hers.

The package passed by a show of hands, with two further amendments on the back of the agenda: a ledger open to any resident once a month, and a six-week closure of the hall after the Cycle's last night. Odd added a third, that a missed payment would hand the board seat and the cavity keys to the residents. Venn signed under all three. He lost sole signature on the accounts that night and appeared, on the whole, relieved. Twelve of the fourteen month-to-month tenants signed the covenant before the meeting broke up. Two went home to think. The ledger on the wall kept a line for them.

At midnight, Illuminara held the old flange true at the tested hand's width for the forty minutes it took Odd's crew to bolt it open. Her arms shook afterwards. Nobody asked her to do anything else.

06 · Floor Music

Varda Ring, Service Deck Four, the Rask apartment · night thirteen, 23:40

The Autumn Cycle had been loud: three nights of the big drum, the loudest Venn's hall had ever sold. Down on Deck Four the floor had only breathed, because the flange had stood bolted open at its tested width the whole time. Pia slept with her fingers loose against the wall.

Odd put the clinic's printout on the kitchen table and slid it across. Seven nights, three of them concert nights, no seizures.

Odd RaskI still won't shake his hand.

IlluminaraI wasn't going to ask you to.

Odd RaskHe was down in the cavity again this evening. With the auditor and a clipboard. He read me his figures aloud, like I was a committee.

IlluminaraWere they right?

Odd RaskYes. I checked.

He said it as a man names a small, unwelcome fact about the weather. The hall would stay shut for six weeks while the dampers went in, and Venn no longer held his own keys. Two of the fourteen month-to-month tenants had left. The reserve had taken their share, and the ledger said so in plain figures on the wall. The air contractor's agent had read that ledger and accepted the covenant and the reserve as security for the renewal, against a deposit of one quarter's air bill out of the gate's first forty nights. It was not signed. It was no longer in doubt, and Odd said those were two different things. None of it was clean, and none of it was an apology, and the bed on the other side of the wall was quiet.

From the next block the tail of the last concert was still coming through, three flutes and a slow drum. Illuminara listened for the first time since she had boarded, not as a load and not as a path, but as a piece of music, played somewhere by people who loved it.

She stayed for the tea. At the door she looked once through the bedroom doorway. Pia's fingers moved against the wall, tapping something that was not seven, slower than seven, with no number in it yet.

What the critic still says doesn't work

Illuminara traces a child's seven-count pulse to two systems that are harmless alone but harmful together, then helps residents replace purchased quiet with a tested repair, open accounts and a freely chosen covenant that preserves both sleep and air.

Tonight's shape

Six scenes across thirteen nights: a seizure night held at a bed plate, a ledger and a map, a deck of paid silence, a three-run test that turns a child's count into two notes, a landlord's costly notice and a meeting that votes on a repair, and a night when the floor only plays music.

01 · Tla, ki so štela

Vardin obroč, servisna paluba štiri, stanovanje Raskovih · prva noč, 03.10

Utrip se je skozi tla dvignil ob deset minut čez tretjo in Illuminara ga je začutila prej kakor merilnik na zapestju Pie Rask. Od polnoči je sedela prekrižanih nog ob postelji, z obema dlanema plosko na palubni plošči, in jo brala, kakor glasbenik bere stran: vsak pritisk v kovini je imel kraj, od koder je prišel, in kraj, kamor je hotel iti, to noč pa je bil vsak pomemben pritisk namenjen proti blazini devetletnice.

Pijino dihanje se je spremenilo. Njen oče je prečkal sobo pred prvim zvoncem.

Odd RaskZdaj? Je zdaj?

IlluminaraZdaj. Na bok in ne držite je pri miru. Jaz imam tla.

Imela jih je, kolikor jih je kdo lahko imel. Ujela je utrip tam, kjer je vstopil v ploščo, ga sprejela skozi zapestja in ramena ter spustila v trup po poti, ki jo je izbrala sama, tako da je postelja počivala na tleh, ki so bila le videti, kakor da se tresejo. Bilo je kakor nesti polno kad čez sobo. To je lahko počela eno noč.

Napad je trajal devetdeset sekund. Osebama, ki sta šteli, se je zdel veliko daljši.

Potem je Pia ležala z odprtimi očmi in roko ob steni ter trkala.

PiaSedem. Sedem v eni sekundi. Boben je počasnejši.

Odd RaskOna to šteje. Na kliniki so rekli, naj ji ne dovolimo. Vseeno počne.

IlluminaraRada bi, da šteje. Pia, kakšen je občutek bobna?

PiaKakor da nekdo stopa. Sedem ni stopanje. Sedem je, ko dva človeka brenčita in je eden malo zgrešen.

Illuminara je dvignila pogled. Dve brnenji, malo narazen. Noben posamezen stroj ni ustvarjal takšnega utripa; ustvarjala sta ga dva tona, ki se nista ujemala, in le tam, kjer sta se srečala.

Ne samo dvorana, je pomislila. Dvorana in nekaj, kar ji odgovarja.

Odd RaskZa to plačujemo, veste. Dve leti. Osemindvajset kreditov na mesec na vsaki servisni palubi, »vzdrževanje konstrukcije«. Rekli so nam, da je za hrup.

IlluminaraKdo vam je to rekel?

Odd RaskMož, ki prodaja tišino.

Pijini prsti so našli Illuminara-in rokav in ga držali, kakor otrok drži ograjo. Illuminara so roke bolele do komolcev in jih ni premaknila. Biti uporabljen kot ograja je bila majhna, nerazumna sreča.

02 · Različica, ki vas straši

Vardin obroč, dvorana Meridian, pisarna Sela Venna · drugi dan, pozno popoldne

Sel Venn je imel pisarno neposredno nad stropom koncertne dvorane, zato je vsa soba nosila rahlo, drago brnenje. Imel je šestdeset let, bil je vljuden in očitno neprespan. Preden ga je vprašala, je na mizo položil knjigo računov, in to je opazila: kretnjo nekoga, ki se je moral pred tem že braniti.

Sel VennSpali ste na servisni palubi. Raje bi, da vidite moje številke kot pa pripoved nekoga o njih.

IlluminaraPotem mi pokažite različico, ki vas straši.

Pokazal ji je. Enainštirideset najemov razreda Tišina je plačevalo dvainšestdeset stotin obročnega računa za zrak. Koncertne vstopnine so plačevale enaintrideset, po del za vsako prodano noč dvorane; najemi Tišine so to omejevali na enaintrideset noči na leto. Običajne najemnine so plačevale zadnjih sedem. Doplačilo servisnih palub je plačevalo dušilne plošče v razredu Tišina in dolg dvorane, označeno pa ni bilo kot nič od tega.

Sel VennPogodba za zrak se obnovi čez pet mesecev. Če razred Tišina odide, na tem obroču nihče ne diha na kredit. Tudi četrta paluba ne, Illuminara.

IlluminaraIn tresljaji?

Sel VennGredo tja, kamor jim zemljevid dovoljuje.

Razgrnil ga je: načrt, starejši od očetovega mandata. Razbremenilna rebra skozi palube tri do pet so bila narisana modro in označena DUŠENA POT. Vzdrževalna votlina devet je bila rdeče šrafirana in označena ZATESNJENO, NE SPAJAJ.

Sel VennDvorana potiska base v trup. Trup jih mora nekam odložiti. Zemljevid pravi na Štirico. Zemljevida nisem narisal jaz.

IlluminaraSte se odločili, da ga ne boste preizkusili?

Pogledal je plakate Jesenskega cikla, ne nje.

Sel VennPreizkus pomeni, da dvorana za eno noč ugasne. Razred Tišina temo bere kot propad, štirinajst teh najemov pa je iz meseca v mesec. Računal sem, kdo bo spal, in se zmotil tako, kot se ljudje zmotijo, kadar je vsota na eni strani lista, obrazi pa na drugi. Moji senzorji so četrto palubo označili enajstkrat. Prebral sem jih kot šum.

IlluminaraNe prosim vas, da zaprete dvorano. Prosim za eno noč vaje in ključ do votline devet. Ne bom vam vzela stavbe, gospod Venn. Vaš zemljevid bom odnesla ljudem, ki ga lahko preverijo.

Ni rekel da. Ključ je položil na knjigo računov in to je bilo vse soglasje, ki si ga je lahko privoščil.

03 · Deveta paluba

Vardin obroč, deveta paluba, razred Tišina, stanovanje Marit Osei · tretji dan, zvečer

Marit Osei je imela enainsedemdeset let, bila je upokojena akustična tehničarka, njeno stanovanje pa je bilo tako mirno, da je Illuminara slišala ohlajanje čaja. Ni je povabila. O napadih je kakor vsi drugi brala na palubnih oglasnih deskah in Illuminara pričakala pri vratih s prekrižanimi rokami.

Marit OseiTo tišino sem plačala. Migrene moje žene so resnične in prav tako moj slušni aparat. Zanjo se ne bom opravičevala in je ne bom oddala, da bi dokazala, da sem dober človek.

IlluminaraNihče vas ne prosi za to. Sprašujem, ali znate brati načrt.

Marit je vzela zemljevid in ga brala s poklicnim prezirom do risarjev. Ustavila se je pri legendi.

Marit OseiNa tem ni odra. Ni basovskega sklada, ni dvorane. Dušena pot predpostavlja obroč, ki ne nosi ničesar nizkega. Kdor je na ta trup obesil dušilne plošče, je preprosto podedoval zemljevid.

IlluminaraIn votlina devet?

Marit OseiZatesnjena, ker bi stari obroč zvenel ob pestu. Pesto so odtlej dvakrat ponovno spojili. Nihče ni znova preizkusil. Zdaj bi lahko zvenelo čisto. Lahko pa tudi ne. Hotela bi kladivo, ne teorije.

IlluminaraBi jo preizkusili z menoj?

Marit OseiPod enim pogojem. Tišina moje žene se ne bo rezala, da bi financirala karkoli za kogarkoli.

IlluminaraNe bo se. Toda če vaša tišina obstaja samo zato, ker se tresejo tla nekoga drugega, to ni tišina, temveč dolg. Rada bi ugotovila, ali je lahko hkrati vaša in njihova.

Marit jo je dolgo gledala, kakor človek gleda uglaševalca, ki je pravkar zaigral pravi ton.

Marit OseiPrinesite kladivo. Jaz prinesem ušesa.

04 · Rebro eno palubo nižje

Vardin obroč, vzdrževalna votlina devet · peta noč, 02.00

Šestdeset družin je na stene prilepilo telefone, po enega v vsakem predelu na vsaki palubi, vsak je bil nastavljen tako, da je čutil trup. Odd Rask je stal pri starem tesnilu votline s kladivom, izposojenim v dokih. Marit je opazovala tablico, Illuminara pa je stala z eno roko na steni, ker jim je povedala, kam bo šla energija. Pia je spala na drugi palubi pri teti, zunaj dosega preizkusa. Illuminara je vztrajala. Preizkus bo uporabil dekličino številko, ne njene postelje.

IlluminaraSpoj A. Zgornje rebro. Šlo bo navzgor in vstran ter ugasnilo.

Odd je enkrat udaril. Tesnilo je zazvenelo kakor zvon, ovit v blago.

Marit OseiNe A. Šlo je do B in nadaljevalo. Vaše rebro je napačno.
