THE BELL THAT TUNED THE CITYZVON, KI JE UGLASIL MESTO

A cathedral has begun to ring itself, forty thousand pilgrims are nine days away, and the tram line beneath it cannot be switched off. Illuminara reads what the tower is already doing, and the ringers and dispatchers have to carry it.

01 · The Minster Mutters

At ten past six on the ninth evening before the Festival of Saint Aldric, with the light going copper along the river Orne, the bells in the crossing tower began to talk to themselves. Nobody was in the ringing chamber. Bram Dacey, who had been tower captain for thirty-one years, stood on the belfry stair with one hand on the cold rail and counted. The sound was small: a clapper kissing a sound bow here, another there, the fourth bell and the fifth and the fourth again, about three taps in every four seconds. It was not music. It was what a bell does when something beneath it will not keep still.

In the works office under the roof, Dr. Ravi Taneja had the same rhythm on two screens, drawn as a line. He was the Minster's structural engineer, and he had not slept well since the Riverside extension opened eleven days ago and Line 7 began stopping at the Quay, a hundred and forty metres from the west door. He laid a pencil on the trace. "My limit at the lantern is eight milli-g. Before the line opened this tower never read above a half. At this evening's peak it read six point one. Every spike is a tram braking at the Quay." He did not look up. "At festival crowding I would have said hours of margin. At the rate the peaks are growing I would not promise the hours."

Canon Marit Solvang, master of the fabric, had her answer ready. Forty thousand pilgrims would arrive on the ninth day, the Festival had been kept through plague and a siege, and the Chapter would not cancel it. Hanneke Orr, chief dispatcher of Vessin Trams, leaned on the wall with her arms folded and had hers ready too. On festival day the bridges closed for the procession, and the Quay was the only way into the old town for anyone who could not walk four kilometres. She needed a tram every ninety seconds. "And on an ordinary morning I carry thirty thousand people to work on that line. Tell me which of them to strand."

Illuminara had come because the Canon wrote to her, and Wren Odira had come to keep the clock, because someone had to. She had been a court interpreter before she was anything else, and she began as an interpreter does, by asking what each party would have to believe for the other's sentence to be true. Then she asked Taneja something he had not expected. "When a peak comes, how long does the tower take to build, and how long to forget?"

"Twenty seconds to build. Forty to forget."

"So one tram is not the danger. One tram is a push of four seconds, and the tower forgives four seconds. The danger is pushes that arrive in step."

She asked to stand on the lantern gallery for the next tram. The force paths of a masonry tower were legible to her the way a pulse is legible to a doctor: a slow lean of load from the crossing piers to the lantern and back again. She could read what leaned. What it meant was her own interpretation, and she did not ask anyone to take it on trust. "It leans and returns once every one point three five seconds," she said into Wren's radio. "The lean runs along the line to the Quay, not along the nave. Dr. Taneja, check your axes before the next tram, and write your own number first."

He wrote a number on the margin of the trace and covered it with his hand. At 6:41 a tram braked at the Quay. The line on his screen climbed from three point four to four point two, the period read one point three five, and the dominant axis pointed at the river platform. He took his hand away. The paper said 1.35, in his writing, before the tram had finished stopping. He said nothing, which from Taneja was a form of respect.

02 · Metronomes on a Plank

Hanneke brought the braking logs to the works office at nine, because by then she wanted a verdict more than she wanted to be right. The new Orsolo trams blended electric braking into friction brakes below nine kilometres an hour, and in that handover the controller hunted: release, apply, release, a stutter of about three-quarters of a cycle a second that passengers felt as a shiver and the manufacturer certified as within tolerance. No two trams stuttered alike. Speed and load tuned each one a little differently.

Illuminara asked for the pulse hour, when the timetable brought three trams into the Quay at once. Hanneke pulled it up. The three traces began at point seven one, point seven four and point seven seven cycles a second. In twelve seconds all three were at point seven four.

"Their controllers do not talk to each other," Hanneke said.

"No. The ground does." Illuminara laid her finger on the clay under the Quay, drawn as a brown band on the engineer's section. "The tower's lean comes down through the piers into this, and out along the rails, and every tram feels it at the wheel. Every stutter tips a little toward it. They are metronomes on a plank, and the tower is the plank. We have been asking what the trams are doing to the tower. The tower is also pulling the trams into step."

Wren, who had written the three frequencies in her book, said, "So slowing them down?"

"Slowing shifts each stutter, and the pull of the tower is stronger than the shift," Hanneke said. She had already tried it. Sanding the rails cut slip but not the hunt. The fault was in firmware, and a firmware change to a braking controller needed eleven weeks of recertification. She could stagger arrivals on an ordinary day. On festival day the crowd made the dwell times, and dwell times made bunches.

Illuminara turned to the other window of the problem. "Captain Dacey. Which bells were talking this evening?"

"Four and five." Bram answered without checking. "Fourth swings short, one point three three seconds on a small arc. Fifth, one point three eight. Every ringer knows what a bell does on a small arc. It is just a swing."

"The tower's period is one point three five. Two of your bells keep the same time within three percent. The Minster is trying to grow itself a damper. Those two are the only parts of it free to move against the lean, and no one is keeping them going."

Taneja was already shaking his head, and Illuminara was glad of it. "A tuned mass wants a few percent of the modal mass. I put the lantern mode at three hundred and twenty tonnes. Your whole ring is eleven. If every bell did its job that is three and a half percent, and only two are in tune. Next, the crowd. Eight thousand people in the nave lengthen the period, and a damper tuned to an empty church is a damper tuned to the wrong building. Third, a band of ringers pulling ropes near that frequency is a pump. You propose to bolt a pump to a tower that is drowning."

"All of that is true," Illuminara said, "so I would not use the idea on my say-so. Write the number that kills it."

He wrote two. A rehearsal at four in the morning with the line closed; Hanneke to bunch six trams into the Quay; Bram's band to work the ring. If the lantern peak did not stay under three, or if the tower did not ring down from a bunch in under twenty-five seconds instead of forty, the Festival moved to the Meadow outside the walls and he closed the tower, in writing, over the Canon's signature. "A lower peak alone proves nothing. Any trick can shave a peak for one cycle. I want the tower to forget faster." Canon Marit read it twice and signed.

"And you?" the Canon asked. "What do you do?"

"I read the tower to a precision nobody else here can, and I tell the ringers which bells it wants. I can also hold the load on the top stage for a short while, a quarter of an hour, perhaps a little more. A festival is twelve hours. It is the ringers' day, and the dispatchers'."

03 · A Damper That Cannot Lose

On the seventh morning, at four, twelve ringers stood in the dark with their ropes, and Bram told them what Illuminara had asked: lash the clappers, set the fourth, fifth and sixth swinging on small silent arcs, and keep them going. She had reasoned that every clapper strike was motion wasted as sound, and that silent bells would give the tower everything they had. Hanneke put six trams into the Quay in a bunch.

The trace did not look like the plot of a rescue. Taneja had it on the big screen, a line that should have been a single hump, and it grew two. The peak read five point eight. With the ring still it had read six point two. Then the bunch ended and the line did not fall. It rang down over thirty-eight seconds, two seconds better than nothing.

"Split peak, no loss," Taneja said, quietly, because he had written the numbers and did not need to say them. "A neat textbook absorber with nowhere for the energy to go."

Illuminara had watched it with her attention along the piers and had seen it before the trace finished drawing itself. The tower pushed, the bells swung up, and then the bells handed the push back, a half-beat late, to a tower that had no way to refuse it. She said so at once. "I was wrong to quiet them. A silent bell can only trade energy with the tower. The mutter we heard that evening was the sound of it leaving: each clapper strike takes a little away and does not give it back. A damper that cannot lose anything is only a second building."

"You tied your own hands," Taneja said, and then, grudgingly, "You said it before the screen did."

It was Teo Vance, nineteen, the youngest in the band, who found the next thing. He was holding the fifth's rope and said, to nobody in particular, "I can feel it. When the tram comes, the sally lifts on its own. It's the tower. I could tell you the lean without the screen." The older ringers looked at him, and Bram, who had felt that lift for thirty-one years without ever giving it a number, said, "He's right. Every one of us can."

Illuminara knelt on the boards and drew it in chalk, because three people in the room needed to see it and one of them had to be able to say it back. A line for the tower. A line under it for the bell. "Energy comes in through the frame, here, as the lift in the sally. It goes into the bell, which is a weight on a rope. It leaves at the end of the swing, where the clapper hits the bronze, as sound and a little heat. Three places. In, across, out. Last night we closed the out."

Teo said, "So if the tower lifts the sally and I pull, I'm helping it."

"Yes. Then you are the pump Dr. Taneja warned about."

"So I don't pull on the lift." He worked it out with his hands on the rope while the others watched. "I let it come up. I give it a half-beat. Then I take the rope on the way back down, and the bell is late on purpose. And the clapper has all that swing to hit with."

"Yield on the lift," Illuminara said. "Pull on the return. Then the bell trails the lantern and fights it. If it works you will see two things on Dr. Taneja's screen, the bell line running behind the lantern line, and the tower forgetting in under twenty-five seconds." She turned to Taneja. "The ring-down is your number. Not mine."

But the band had been awake twenty-seven hours, and she would not ask a tired ring to do a second rehearsal on nerve. She moved it to the fourth night. Taneja read the numbers, agreed, and kept his veto over the Festival where it was.

04 · Twenty-Nine Hundred Coats

The Canon had asked the city for a favour, and the city came. On the fourth night, at half past three, twenty-nine hundred people filed into the nave in their coats, the choirs of nine parishes and as many neighbours as they had brought, and sat in the dark under the lantern. Illuminara had asked for them. Their mass was the rehearsal's real test, because the tower with a crowd in it was not the tower that had failed on the seventh morning.

She felt it at once. The period had lengthened from one point three five to one point three seven. "The fifth is in tune now, and the fourth is too short," she told Bram. "Put the sixth on with the fifth. Let the clappers rock and strike as they want."

Taneja's own estimate of the new frequency arrived twenty seconds later and said the same. Teo, with his hands on the rope, said it a breath before either, in the way a ringer says it: "She's lifting early." That was the third voice in agreement, and the only one of the three that had not needed a machine.

Bram called it down the ring in the old tower words: "Yield. Wait. Now take it." Twelve people who had spent their lives pulling on the beat learned, in the dark, to give way to a building and then lean in against it. Six trams came into the Quay in a bunch, and the sound that went through the nave was small, a clapper kissing a bow, the fifth and the sixth and the fifth again, a sound like a congregation clearing its throat. The twenty-nine hundred people sat very still. The lantern line rose to two point four and bent over.

Taneja did not look at the peak. He had the two traces laid one over the other and his pencil on the gap between them. "The bell runs behind the lantern by a quarter of a swing. Clear as a ruled line." The pencil moved to the tail. "And twenty-one seconds to forget. Under three, under twenty-five, with a crowd in the nave and the worst bunch Hanneke can build." He sat down and took his hand off the rail. "That is damping. I would sign that."

"Eight thousand will lengthen the period more," Illuminara said. "The ring does not cover every case. Bells seven and eight are in reserve for it. And a crowd does not fill a nave in one step." She looked at Taneja. "So we ask the nave to fill slowly, on the morning, while the trams are quiet, and we measure every step. The band must know the handover from each pair to the next before anyone is in danger."

"The morning of the Festival," Taneja said. "With the congregation in."

"With the congregation arriving."

The remaining problem was not a bell. Ilya Brandt, the union's organiser, read the dispatchers' plan to space the Quay arrivals and refused it flatly. The plan would hold laden trams at the previous stop, doors closed, pilgrims standing in them for four minutes, because a tower needed it. "My drivers will not hold a full tram for a building." Hanneke did not argue. She went away with the schedule and came back after an hour with a different one: hold only the empty return trams, at Orsolo Bridge, two minutes at a time, so that no bunch of three could form on the Quay side; laden trams never held.

Ilya read it and then read the second page, which Hanneke had headed Pre-Mortem and which she had written the way she wrote incident reports, in the voice of a woman who already knew how it would go wrong. "One failure I cannot schedule away. If anything stops a laden tram inside the hundred and forty metres before the platform, the trams behind must stop behind it, nose to tail, at the safe gap. Five trams fill the zone. When the front one moves, all five go for the platform together, in a convoy forty seconds long, and every one of them brakes into the Quay. A timetable ninety seconds apart does not survive a stop. That is the morning I would be afraid of." She did not say that it was a fear of hers. It was in the margin in her own hand. "I can hold empties. I cannot hold the people already in the stack."

Ilya signed. "That I can sign. An empty tram can stand. And the stack, God help us, we manage by hand."

The Canon had one condition of her own. "A festival with a tower that mutters. I shall have visitors asking why the cathedral is whispering."

"Tell them it is listening," Teo said. And then, because he was nineteen and in the Canon's presence, "Sorry."

"No," Canon Marit said. "That will do."

05 · The Hour After the Mass

On the morning of the Festival, from half past seven, the nave filled in steps. Hanneke held the trams to the plan. At each thousand people through the west door Taneja called the period and Illuminara called the pair: fifth and sixth until the nave held five thousand, sixth and seventh at seven thousand, then the longest period of all, one point four one, at nine thousand, when the whole congregation would be standing for the Creed. Bram's bands rotated through the ropes with the handover words, from each pair to the next, yield and wait and take. By the time the procession entered, every number the church was likely to make had a pair of bells and a captain's signature beside it. Taneja's last line on the sheet read: Ring-down twenty-three seconds at nine thousand. Taneja had not smiled, but he had crossed out the word Hours.

The Festival of Saint Aldric opened under a hard blue sky, and for six hours it went the way he had written it down. The procession crossed the old town; the bridges were closed; trams ran every ninety seconds and the empty ones waited at Orsolo Bridge. The bells in the crossing tower muttered through the sermon and the blessings. The lantern read two, three, a high of three point four.

Then the closing Mass ended, and eight thousand seven hundred people, who had all stood for it, began to leave the nave together.

Illuminara had been on the lantern gallery since the Mass began, because a falling crowd was the one thing the rehearsals had not made at this speed. It was not that the building would be in a new state; the bands had qualified every pair down the whole ladder. It was the speed at which the mass was leaving. As the nave emptied the period would shorten, faster than any ramp-down they had walked, and the ringers on the sixth and seventh would be late to hand back to the fifth and sixth. She felt it before Taneja's screen did. "One point four oh. Bram, bring the fifth in now. Yield on this lift, and do not wait for the exit to finish."

At the same moment, at 15:04, a laden tram stopped for a child who had run out of the crowd onto the track, sixty metres short of the platform, inside the zone. The child was unhurt. The paramedics took six minutes to say so, and the driver would not move until they did. Behind her, Hanneke's ninety-second trams came on and stacked up as her pre-mortem had said they would, nose to tail, five of them on one track, full of people who had been standing since dawn, each braking in turn inside the hundred and forty metres. "They are full," Hanneke said into the radio. "They cannot be held back, and I cannot reorder them. The order belongs to the track. What I own is the gap." She had two choices, and each had a price. She could keep the followers standing once the front driver was cleared, and let the stack grow while a sixth laden tram came up behind it, a longer wait and a longer convoy later. Or she could release the five at the shortest separation the signals would safely allow, forty seconds from first to last, and take the braking burst at the Quay now, while the lantern was still the stronger for the fifth bell being in. She chose the second, because the first only made the burst larger.

At 15:10 the front driver was cleared and the front tram moved. The convoy followed at the minimum safe interval and braked into the Quay in a tight line, and the stutter of five controllers fell into step on the clay. Taneja was calling the number into the radio, not looking at it: "Six point eight, at 15:12. Seven point one. Seven point four," at 15:13, and then the number he had written on the first night: if the lantern held above seven and a half for thirty seconds, he would call the evacuation of the nave and the square.

The ringers were halfway through the handover. The fifth and sixth were in tune with a period that was already gone, and the seventh was still ringing against a nave that was nearly empty. Illuminara put her hands on the cold stone, found the line where the lean was loading the lantern, and took it. She turned the load aside through the piers to the crossing arches, which could carry more than they had ever been asked to. The lantern line on the screen, which had been climbing, hung where it was.

"Fifteen minutes is the most she has," Wren said into the radio, as she had said numbers into other radios, in a level voice. "Two gone."

It was not strength she needed so much as precision, and she had spent nine days learning which stones would hold. It was not a thing she could do for an afternoon. The ringers had to take the tower from her in the time she bought them. In the ringing chamber Bram's third band, with Teo on the fifth, finished the handover, yielded on the lift, took the rope on the return, and kept it. Bell lines ran behind the lantern line on Taneja's screen like the ruled gap on the rehearsal night. The clappers struck. The cluster muttered louder than it had all day, five bells and six and five again, and on the gallery the stone began to give back the lean in smaller and smaller pieces.

At fifteen twenty-six, thirteen minutes in, she let go. Taneja held his breath for the forty seconds the tower should have taken to forget. It forgot in twenty-two. The line read three point one and stayed there, and the tower stood without her.

"The Quay is clear," Hanneke said into the radio. "Next bunch is two. Ilya's holding the empties at the bridge."

Nobody was evacuated. By dusk the trams had carried nineteen thousand pilgrims out of the old town without suspending service, and the lantern had not once crossed seven and a half. The Chapter kept the bells muttering for the evening service, and the pilgrims going down the Quay steps looked back up at the tower and heard it, a sound like a very large building clearing its throat before it sang.

There were costs. The ringers would train a year to keep a watch whenever trams braked in autumn, and Bram's band lost a month of its quiet practice. Hanneke's staggered timetable cost eleven percent of her festival capacity and would run until the new controller software arrived in spring. Taneja's report recommended a proper damper in the lantern, which the Chapter could not afford, and which would be cut from the budget twice before it was built.

At ten past six on the evening after the Festival, with the light going copper along the Orne, the bells began to talk to themselves again. This time, standing on the belfry stair with his hand on the cold rail, Bram Dacey knew what had asked them, and how to answer.

Stolnični zvonovi so se začeli oglašati sami od sebe. Čez devet dni bo prišlo štirideset tisoč romarjev, tramvajske proge pod stolnico pa ni mogoče ustaviti. Illuminara razbere, kaj stolp že počne, toda rešitev morajo v svojih rokah obdržati zvonarji in dispečerji.

01 · Stolnica mrmra

Deset minut čez šesto, devetega večera pred praznikom svetega Aldrica, ko je svetloba ob reki Orne dobivala bakren odtenek, so se zvonovi v stolpu nad križiščem ladij začeli pogovarjati sami s seboj. V zvonarski sobi ni bilo nikogar. Bram Dacey, ki je zvonarski skupini poveljeval enaintrideset let, je stal na stopnicah zvonika z dlanjo na mrzli ograji in štel. Zvok je bil droben: kembelj je tu in tam poljubil bronasti rob, četrti zvon, peti in spet četrti, približno trije udarci v štirih sekundah. To ni bila glasba. Tako zveni zvon, kadar nekaj pod njim noče mirovati.

V delovni pisarni pod streho je doktor Ravi Taneja gledal isti ritem, izrisan kot črto na dveh zaslonih. Bil je statik stolnice in ni dobro spal, odkar so pred enajstimi dnevi odprli podaljšek proge ob reki in je tramvaj številka sedem začel ustavljati pri Nabrežju, sto štirideset metrov od zahodnih vrat. Svinčnik je položil na zapis meritev. »Moja meja pri lanternetnem delu stolpa je osem milig. Pred odprtjem proge ni stolp nikdar presegel pol milig. Ob današnjem večernem vrhu je dosegel šest celih eno desetinko. Vsak sunek pomeni tramvaj, ki zavira pri Nabrežju.« Ni dvignil pogleda. »Pri praznični gneči bi prej rekel, da imamo še več ur rezerve. S hitrostjo, s katero naraščajo vrhovi, pa si teh ur ne upam več obljubiti.«

Kanoninja Marit Solvang, skrbnica stavbe, je imela odgovor pripravljen. Čez devet dni bo prišlo štirideset tisoč romarjev, praznik so obhajali med kugo in obleganjem in stolni kapitelj ga ne bo odpovedal. Hanneke Orr, glavna dispečerka Vessinskih tramvajev, se je prekrižanih rok naslanjala na steno in je bila prav tako pripravljena. Na praznični dan bodo zaradi procesije zaprti mostovi; Nabrežje je edina pot v staro mesto za vse, ki ne zmorejo prehoditi štirih kilometrov. Tramvaj mora pripeljati na vsakih devetdeset sekund. »Tudi ob navadnem jutru po tej progi peljem trideset tisoč ljudi na delo. Povejte mi, koga naj pustim na cedilu.«

Illuminara je prišla, ker ji je pisala kanoninja; Wren Odira pa je prišla meriti čas, saj ga je nekdo moral. Pred vsem drugim je bila nekoč sodna tolmačka. Zato je začela po tolmaško: vprašala je, v kaj bi morala vsaka stran verjeti, da bi bil stavek druge strani lahko resničen. Nato je Tanejo vprašala nekaj, česar ni pričakoval. »Ko pride sunek, koliko časa se stolp krepi in koliko časa potrebuje, da ga pozabi?«

»Dvajset sekund, da se okrepi. Štirideset, da pozabi.«

»Torej en sam tramvaj ni nevarnost. En tramvaj je štirisekundni sunek in stolp mu te štiri sekunde odpusti. Nevarni so sunki, ki prihajajo v istem taktu.«

Za prihod naslednjega tramvaja je prosila, da bi stala na galeriji pod vrhom stolpa. Poti sil skozi zidani stolp je brala tako jasno, kot zdravnik bere utrip: počasno prenašanje obremenitve od stebrov v križišču ladij do stolpnega vrha in nazaj. Videla je, kaj se nagiba. Pomen tega je bil njena razlaga in od nikogar ni zahtevala, naj ji verjame na besedo. »Nagib in vrnitev trajata eno celo petintrideset stotink sekunde,« je rekla po Wreninem radiu. »Nagib potuje v smeri proge do Nabrežja, ne vzdolž ladje. Doktor Taneja, pred naslednjim tramvajem preverite osi in najprej zapišite svojo številko.«

Številko je napisal ob rob merilnega lista in jo pokril z dlanjo. Ob 6.41 je pri Nabrežju zaviral tramvaj. Črta na zaslonu se je povzpela s tri cele štiri na štiri cele dve, perioda je kazala 1,35 sekunde, glavna os pa je kazala proti peronu ob reki. Umaknil je dlan. Na papirju je že prej, v njegovi pisavi, pisalo 1,35. Tramvaj se še ni povsem ustavil. Taneja ni rekel ničesar. Pri njem je bila to oblika spoštovanja.

02 · Metronomi na deski

Hanneke je ob devetih prinesla zapise o zaviranju, ker je tedaj bolj kot lastno zmago potrebovala sklep. Novi tramvaji Orsolo so pod devetimi kilometri na uro prehajali z električnega na torno zaviranje, pri prehodu pa je krmilnik začel loviti ravnovesje: popusti, pritisni, popusti; trzanje s približno tremi četrtinami cikla na sekundo, ki so ga potniki čutili kot drhtenje, proizvajalec pa ga je še vedno potrjeval kot dopustno odstopanje. Nobena dva tramvaja nista trzala enako. Hitrost in obremenitev sta vsakemu nekoliko prilagodili ritem.

Illuminara je prosila za uro največje obremenitve, ko so po voznem redu hkrati pripeljali k Nabrežju trije tramvaji. Hanneke je prikazala podatke. Trije zapisi so se začeli pri 0,71, 0,74 in 0,77 cikla na sekundo. V dvanajstih sekundah so bili vsi trije pri 0,74.

»Njihovi krmilniki se med seboj ne pogovarjajo,« je rekla Hanneke.

»Ne. Govorijo pa tla.« Illuminara je položila prst na glino pod Nabrežjem, narisano kot rjav pas na inženirjevem prerezu. »Nagib stolpa se prek stebrov prenese v to plast, od tod vzdolž tirnic, vsak tramvaj pa ga zazna na kolesih. Vsak trzljaj se mu malce prilagodi. Kot metronomi so na deski, stolp pa je ta deska. Ves čas smo spraševali, kaj tramvaji delajo stolpu. Toda tudi stolp vleče tramvaje v isti ritem.«

Wren, ki je vse tri frekvence zapisala v beležnico, je vprašala: »Bi jih torej upočasnili?«

»Upočasnitev spremeni ritem trzanja, toda vpliv stolpa je močnejši od te spremembe,« je rekla Hanneke. To je že poskusila. Posipanje tirnic s peskom je zmanjšalo drsenje, ne pa nihanja krmilnika. Napaka je bila v programski opremi, popravek zaviralnega krmilnika pa bi zahteval enajst tednov ponovnega certificiranja. Ob navadnem dnevu bi lahko razmaknila prihode. Na praznik pa gneča določa čas ustavljanja in ta povzroča kopičenje tramvajev.

Illuminara se je obrnila k drugi plati problema. »Kapitan Dacey. Kateri zvonovi so se nocoj pogovarjali?«

»Četrti in peti.« Bram je odgovoril brez preverjanja. »Četrti na kratkem zamahu potrebuje 1,33 sekunde. Peti 1,38. Vsak zvonar ve, kaj dela zvon pri majhnem zamahu. Pač niha.«

»Perioda stolpa je 1,35 sekunde. Dva vaša zvonova nihata skoraj v istem času, znotraj treh odstotkov. Stolnica si skuša sama ustvariti dušilec. Ta dva sta edina njena dela, ki se lahko prosto gibljeta proti nagibu, a nihče ne ohranja njunega gibanja.«

Taneja je že zmajeval z glavo in Illuminara je bila tega vesela. »Ubran masni dušilec potrebuje nekaj odstotkov modalne mase. Za način nihanja stolpnega vrha ocenjujem tristo dvajset ton. Vseh vaših zvonov je enajst ton. Če bi vsak opravil svoje delo, bi to pomenilo tri odstotke in pol, pa sta uglašena samo dva. Potem je tu množica. Osem tisoč ljudi v ladji podaljša periodo; dušilec, uglašen za prazno cerkev, je dušilec za napačno stavbo. In tretjič: skupina zvonarjev, ki vleče vrvi blizu te frekvence, je lahko črpalka. Predlagate, da črpalko pritrdimo na stolp, ki se utaplja.«

»Vse to drži,« je rekla Illuminara. »Zato nočem, da sprejmete zamisel samo na mojo besedo. Zapišite številko, pri kateri odpove.«

Zapisal je dve. Vaja bo ob štirih zjutraj, ko bo proga zaprta; Hanneke bo pri Nabrežju združila šest tramvajev, Bramova skupina pa bo nihala zvonove. Če največji pospešek stolpnega vrha ne ostane pod tremi milig ali če nihanje po skupini tramvajev ne izzveni v manj kot petindvajsetih sekundah namesto štiridesetih, bodo praznik prestavili na Travnik zunaj obzidja, on pa bo zaprl stolp. Zapisano, s kanonjinjinim podpisom. »Manjši vrh sam po sebi ničesar ne dokazuje. Vsak trik lahko za en cikel odbije vrh. Hočem, da stolp hitreje pozabi.« Kanoninja Marit je zapis dvakrat prebrala in podpisala.

»In vi?« jo je vprašala kanoninja. »Kaj boste storili vi?«

»Stolp lahko berem natančneje od kogarkoli tukaj in zvonarjem povem, katere zvonove potrebuje. Obremenitev na zgornji stopnji lahko za kratek čas tudi zadržim, morda četrt ure, mogoče malo dlje. Praznik traja dvanajst ur. To bo dan zvonarjev in dispečerjev.«

03 · Dušilec, ki ne more izgubljati

Sedmega jutra ob štirih je dvanajst zvonarjev stalo v temi ob svojih vrveh. Bram jim je povedal, kaj je prosila Illuminara: privežite kemblje, naj se četrti, peti in šesti zvon tiho zibajo v majhnih lokih, in jih ohranjajte v gibanju. Sklepala je, da se pri vsakem udarcu kemblja gibanje izgublja v zvoku; nemi zvonovi bi zato stolpu predali vso svojo energijo. Hanneke je v en sam val usmerila šest tramvajev proti Nabrežju.

Merilni zapis ni bil videti kot rešitev. Na Tanejevem velikem zaslonu bi moral nastati en sam vrh, a nastala sta dva. Največja vrednost je bila 5,8. Pri mirujočih zvonovih je bila 6,2. Potem je val tramvajev minil, črta pa ni padla. Nihanje je izzvenelo šele po osemintridesetih sekundah, samo dve sekundi hitreje kot brez ukrepa.

»Razcepljen vrh, brez izgube energije,« je tiho rekel Taneja. Številke je že zapisal in ni mu jih bilo treba poudarjati. »Lep učbeniški blažilnik, ki energije nima kam oddati.«

Illuminara je pozorno spremljala stebre in videla izid, še preden je črta na zaslonu končala svoj potek. Stolp se je nagnil, zvonovi so se dvignili in nato sunek s pol utripa zamude vrnili stolpu, ki ga ni mogel zavrniti. To je takoj povedala. »Motila sem se, ko sem jih utišala. Nem zvon si lahko s stolpom samo izmenjuje energijo. Tisto mrmranje, ki smo ga slišali zvečer, je bilo slišati, kako energija odhaja: vsak udarec kemblja je nekaj odnese in je ne vrne. Dušilec, ki ne more ničesar izgubiti, je samo druga stavba.«

»Sama sebi ste zvezali roke,« je rekel Taneja, nato pa z nejevoljnim priznanjem dodal: »To ste povedali, še preden je to pokazal zaslon.«

Naslednjo stvar je odkril devetnajstletni Teo Vance, najmlajši med zvonarji. Držal je vrv petega zvona in rekel, bolj samemu sebi: »Čutim ga. Ko pride tramvaj, se ročaj vrvi dvigne sam od sebe. Stolp ga premakne. Njegov nagib bi lahko povedal brez zaslona.« Starejši zvonarji so ga pogledali, Bram pa je, potem ko je enaintrideset let čutil prav ta dvig in mu nikoli pripisal številke, rekel: »Prav ima. Vsak od nas ga čuti.«

Illuminara je pokleknila na deske in narisala s kredo, ker so morali trije ljudje v prostoru to videti in vsaj eden je moral znati ponoviti. Črta za stolp. Pod njo druga za zvon. »Energija vstopi skozi ogrodje, tukaj, kot dvig ročaja na vrvi. Potuje v zvon, ki je utež na vrvi. Ob koncu zamaha odide, ko kembelj udari ob bron, kot zvok in malo toplote. Tri mesta: vstop, prenos, izhod. Sinoči smo izhod zaprli.«

Teo je rekel: »Torej, če mi stolp dvigne vrv in jaz potegnem, mu pomagam.«

»Da. Potem ste prav tista črpalka, na katero je opozoril doktor Taneja.«

»Torej ne potegnem, ko se dvigne.« Medtem ko so ga drugi gledali, je z rokami na vrvi razmišljal na glas. »Pustim, da gre gor. Počakam pol utripa. Potem primem vrv, ko se vrača navzdol, in zvon namenoma zaostaja. Kembelj pa lahko z vso to silo udari.«

»Popusti ob dvigu,« je rekla Illuminara. »Potegni ob vračanju. Potem zvon zaostaja za vrhom stolpa in deluje proti njemu. Če uspe, boste na zaslonu doktorja Taneje videli dvoje: črta zvona bo zaostajala za črto stolpa, nihanje stolpa pa bo ugasnilo v manj kot petindvajsetih sekundah.« Obrnila se je k Taneji. »Čas izzvenevanja je vaša številka. Ne moja.«

Toda skupina je bila budna že sedemindvajset ur, Illuminara pa od utrujenih zvonarjev ni hotela zahtevati še ene vaje na silo. Prestavila jo je na četrto noč. Taneja je prebral številke, se strinjal in obdržal svojo pravico, da praznik prepove.

04 · Dva tisoč devetsto plaščev

Kanoninja je prosila mesto za uslugo in mesto je prišlo. Četrto noč ob pol štirih se je v ladjo v plaščih zgrnilo dva tisoč devetsto ljudi, zbori devetih župnij in prav toliko sosedov, kolikor so jih pripeljali. Posedli so v temo pod vrhom stolpa. Illuminara jih je prosila, naj pridejo. Njihova masa je bila pravi preizkus vaje, kajti stolp s polno ladjo ni bil več stolp, pri katerem je sedmega jutra vaja spodletela.

Takoj je začutila razliko. Perioda se je podaljšala z 1,35 na 1,37 sekunde. »Peti je zdaj uglašen, četrti pa niha prehitro,« je povedala Bramu. »Skupaj s petim vključite šestega. Naj kemblji prosto nihajo in udarjajo, kolikor je treba.«

Dvajset sekund pozneje je Tanejeva lastna ocena nove frekvence pokazala isto. Teo pa je z rokama na vrvi to povedal že trenutek prej, kakor zna povedati zvonar: »Prezgodaj jo dviguje.« To je bil tretji glas, ki se je strinjal, in edini od treh, ki ni potreboval stroja.

Bram je staro zvonarsko povelje zaklical vzdolž vrvi: »Popusti. Počakaj. Zdaj primi.« Dvanajst ljudi, ki so vse življenje vlekli na udarec, se je v temi naučilo popustiti stavbi in šele nato potegniti proti njej. Šest tramvajev je v valu pripeljalo na Nabrežje. Skozi ladjo je šel tih glas: kembelj je poljubil bron, peti zvon, šesti, spet peti, kakor bi si občestvo nekoliko odkašljalo. Vseh dva tisoč devetsto ljudi je sedelo popolnoma pri miru. Črta vrha stolpa se je dvignila do 2,4 in se obrnila navzdol.

Taneja ni gledal vrha grafa. Zapis zvona je imel položen čez zapis vrha stolpa, svinčnik pa je položil na razmik med njima. »Zvon zaostaja za vrhom za četrt zamaha. Jasno kakor s črto narisano.« Svinčnik je premaknil na konec grafa. »Stolp pozabi v enaindvajsetih sekundah. Manj kot tri, manj kot petindvajset, z ljudmi v ladji in najhujšim valom tramvajev, ki ga Hanneke lahko pripravi.« Sedel je in spustil roko z ograje. »To je dušenje. Pod to se podpišem.«

»Osem tisoč ljudi bo periodo še podaljšalo,« je rekla Illuminara. »Vsi zvonovi ne pokrijejo vsakega primera. Sedmi in osmi sta v rezervi. In ladja se ne napolni v enem trenutku.« Pogledala je Tanejo. »Zato bomo na praznično jutro prosili, naj se ladja polni počasi, ko so tramvaji mirni. Izmerili bomo vsak korak. Zvonarji morajo poznati prehod z vsakega para na naslednjega, še preden je kdo v nevarnosti.«

»Na jutro praznika,« je rekel Taneja. »Ko bodo ljudje že v ladji.«

»Ko bodo ljudje prihajali.«

Preostala težava ni bila povezana z zvonovi. Ilya Brandt, sindikalni predstavnik, je prebral dispečerski načrt za razmikanje prihodov na Nabrežje in ga zavrnil brez omahovanja. Po načrtu bi polne tramvaje pri prejšnji postaji zadrževali štiri minute z zaprtimi vrati in romarji na nogah, samo zato, ker stolp tako zahteva. »Moji vozniki ne bodo zadrževali polnega tramvaja zaradi stavbe.« Hanneke ni ugovarjala. Odšla je z voznim redom in se čez uro vrnila z drugim: na mostu Orsolo se bodo po dve minuti zadrževali samo prazni tramvaji na povratni vožnji, da se na strani Nabrežja ne bi združili trije. Polnih ne bodo ustavljali.

Ilya ga je prebral, nato pa še drugo stran, ki jo je Hanneke naslovila Predhodna analiza neuspeha in jo napisala kakor poročila o nesrečah, z glasom ženske, ki že ve, kako se bo vse podrlo. »Enega dogodka iz voznega reda ne morem odstraniti. Če se poln tramvaj ustavi znotraj sto štiridesetih metrov pred peronom, se morajo naslednji ustaviti za njim, drug za drugim, na varni razdalji. Pet jih zapolni ta odsek. Ko prvi spelje, gre vseh pet proti peronu v koloni, ki traja štirideset sekund, in vsak zavira pri Nabrežju. Vozni red z devetdesetsekundnimi razmiki ne preživi izredne ustavitve. Tega jutra se bojim.« Ni rekla, da gre za njen strah. To je že pisalo z njeno pisavo na robu lista. »Prazne lahko zadržim. Ne morem pa zadržati ljudi, ki so že v koloni.«

Ilya se je podpisal. »To lahko podpišem. Prazen tramvaj lahko stoji. Kolono pa bomo, bog nam pomagaj, upravljali ročno.«

Kanoninja je imela še lasten pogoj. »Praznik s stolpom, ki mrmra. Obiskovalci me bodo spraševali, zakaj stolnica šepeta.«

»Povejte jim, da posluša,« je rekel Teo. Ker je imel devetnajst let in je govoril pred kanoninjo, je dodal: »Oprostite.«

»Ne,« je rekla kanoninja Marit. »To bo povsem prav.«

05 · Ura po maši

Na praznično jutro se je od pol osmih dalje ladja polnila postopoma. Hanneke je tramvaje usmerjala po dogovorjenem načrtu. Ob vsakem tisočem človeku, ki je prišel skozi zahodna vrata, je Taneja povedal periodo, Illuminara pa ustrezni par zvonov: peti in šesti do pet tisoč ljudi, šesti in sedmi pri sedmih tisoč, nato najdaljša perioda, 1,41 sekunde pri devetih tisoč, ko bo celotno občestvo stalo ob veroizpovedi. Bramove skupine so se menjavale pri vrveh in ponavljale besede za predajo z enega para na drugega: popusti, počakaj, primi. Ko je vstopila procesija, je imela vsaka vrednost, ki jo je cerkev utegnila doseči, ustrezen par zvonov in kapitanov podpis. Tanejeva zadnja vrstica je glasila: izzvenevanje triindvajset sekund pri devet tisoč ljudeh. Taneja se ni nasmehnil, vendar je prečrtal besedo ure.

Praznik svetega Aldrica se je začel pod trdim modrim nebom in šest ur je vse teklo tako, kot je zapisal. Procesija je šla skozi staro mesto; mostovi so bili zaprti; tramvaji so vozili vsakih devetdeset sekund, prazni pa so čakali pri mostu Orsolo. Zvonovi v stolpu so med pridigo in blagoslovi tiho mrmrali. Meritev vrha je kazala dve, tri, najvišja vrednost je bila 3,4.

Nato se je sklepna maša končala in osem tisoč sedemsto ljudi, ki so vsi stali med obredom, je začelo skupaj zapuščati ladjo.

Illuminara je bila od začetka maše na zgornji galeriji, ker je bil prav hiter odhod množice tisto, česar na vajah niso preizkusili. Stavba ne bi prišla v novo stanje; skupine zvonarjev so poznale ustrezen par za vsak korak. Težava je bila hitrost, s katero je masa odhajala. Ko se je ladja praznila, se je perioda krajšala hitreje kot pri kateremkoli vajenem postopnem praznjenju in zvonarji pri šestem in sedmem bi zamudili vrnitev k petemu in šestemu. Začutila je pred Tanejevim zaslonom. »Ena cela štiri nič. Bram, vključite petega že zdaj. Popusti ob tem dvigu, ne čakajte, da množica odide.«

Prav tedaj, ob 15.04, se je poln tramvaj ustavil zaradi otroka, ki je iz množice stekel na tire, šestdeset metrov pred peronom, znotraj nevarnega odseka. Otrok ni bil poškodovan. Reševalci so potrebovali šest minut, da so to potrdili, voznica pa do takrat ni hotela premakniti tramvaja. Za njo so se v devetdesetsekundnih razmikih približevali naslednji Hannekejini tramvaji in se ustavljali, kot je napovedala njena analiza neuspeha: pet jih je stalo drug za drugim na enem tiru, polnih ljudi, ki so že od zore stali na nogah, vsak je zavrl znotraj sto štiridesetih metrov. »Polni so,« je po radiu rekla Hanneke. »Ne morem jih premestiti in ne morem jim spremeniti vrstnega reda. O vrstnem redu odloča tir. Jaz lahko določam samo razmik.« Izbirala je med dvema možnostma, vsaka pa je imela svojo ceno. Ko bi bila prva voznica pripravljena, bi lahko še zadržala naslednje tramvaje in pustila, da se kolona podaljša, medtem ko bi se za njimi približeval šesti poln tramvaj: daljše čakanje in še daljša kolona pozneje. Ali pa bi pet tramvajev spustila pri najmanjšem varnem razmiku, ki ga dovoljujejo signali, štirideset sekund od prvega do zadnjega, in sprejela silovit val zaviranja pri Nabrežju zdaj, ko je bil stolp zaradi vključenega petega zvona še nekoliko odpornejši. Izbrala je drugo, ker bi prva možnost sunek samo povečala.

Ob 15.10 je bila prva voznica razrešena čakanja in njen tramvaj je speljal. Kolona mu je sledila v najmanjših varnih razmikih in v strnjenem valu zavirala pri Nabrežju. Trzanje petih krmilnikov je v glini ujelo isti ritem. Taneja je po radiu govoril številke, ne da bi pogledal zaslon: »Šest celih osem ob 15.12. Sedem celih ena. Sedem celih štiri,« ob 15.13, nato pa številko, ki jo je zapisal prvo noč: če bo vrh stolpa trideset sekund ostal nad 7,5, bo ukazal evakuacijo ladje in trga.

Zvonarji so bili sredi predaje. Peti in šesti sta bila uglašena na periodo, ki je ni bilo več, sedmi pa je še vedno zvonil proti skoraj prazni ladji. Illuminara je položila roke na mrzel kamen, našla črto, kjer je nagib obremenjeval vrh stolpa, in jo prevzela. Obremenitev je preusmerila skozi stebre v loke križišča, ki so lahko prenesli več, kot se je od njih kadarkoli zahtevalo. Črta stolpa na zaslonu, ki se je vzpenjala, je obstala.

»Največ petnajst minut ima,« je Wren rekla po radiu z enako mirnim glasom, s katerim je po drugih radijskih zvezah sporočala številke. »Dve sta že minili.«

Ni potrebovala toliko moči kot natančnosti in devet dni se je učila, kateri kamni bodo vzdržali. Tega ni mogla početi celo popoldne. Zvonarji so morali v času, ki jim ga je kupila, prevzeti stolp. V zvonarski sobi je Bramova tretja skupina, s Teom na petem zvonu, dokončala predajo, ob dvigu popustila, ob vračanju potegnila vrv in obdržala ritem. Črte zvonov so na Tanejevem zaslonu zaostajale za črto vrha kakor skrbno izmerjen razmik na nočni vaji. Kemblji so udarjali. Zvonovi so mrmrali glasneje kot ves dan, peti, šesti in spet peti, na galeriji pa je kamen začel vračati nagib v vse manjših in manjših delih.

Ob 15.26 je, po trinajstih minutah, spustila obremenitev. Taneja je zadržal dih za štirideset sekund, ki bi jih stolp moral potrebovati, da pozabi. Pozabil je v dvaindvajsetih. Črta je kazala 3,1 in tam ostala. Stolp je stal brez nje.

»Nabrežje je prosto,« je po radiu rekla Hanneke. »Naslednja skupina sta dva tramvaja. Ilya pri mostu zadržuje prazne.«

Nikogar niso evakuirali. Do mraka so tramvaji iz starega mesta prepeljali devetnajst tisoč romarjev, ne da bi prekinili obratovanje, in vrh stolpa niti enkrat ni presegel 7,5. Kapitelj je zvonovom dovolil, da so mrmrali tudi med večerno službo, romarji na stopnicah Nabrežja pa so se ozirali proti stolpu in slišali zvok, kakor bi si zelo velika stavba odkašljala, preden bi zapela.

Uspeh je imel svojo ceno. Zvonarji bodo eno leto vadili, da bodo lahko vsakič, ko tramvaji jeseni zavirajo, bdeli nad stolpom; Bramova skupina bo izgubila mesec mirnih vaj. Hannekejin razmaknjeni vozni red je zmanjšal zmogljivost prazničnega prometa za enajst odstotkov in bo veljal, dokler spomladi ne pride nova programska oprema za krmilnike. Taneja je v poročilu priporočil pravi dušilec na vrhu stolpa, ki si ga kapitelj ni mogel privoščiti in ki so ga iz proračuna izločili še dvakrat, preden so ga vendarle zgradili.

Deset minut čez šesto zvečer po prazniku, ko je svetloba ob reki Orne znova postajala bakrena, so se zvonovi začeli pogovarjati sami s seboj. Tokrat je Bram Dacey stal na stopnicah zvonika z roko na hladni ograji in vedel, kaj jih je nagovorilo in kako odgovoriti.
