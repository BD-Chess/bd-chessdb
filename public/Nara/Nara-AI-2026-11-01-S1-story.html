01 · The Lament

Karrow, the Square of Lamps · evening, day one

Karrow made glass the way other towns made weather: all day, everywhere, and without anyone thanking it. When Illuminara came down the hill road the chimneys of the furnace quarter had burned their smoke to gold, and in the middle of the Square of Lamps something tall and pale sat on the fountain rim, singing to nobody.

It was a being of slow glass, taller than a doorway, folded in the middle the way a sitting person folds. Light went into it at the speed of honey and came out a moment late, so that every lamp in the square seemed to reach it after it had passed. A woman in a grey shawl sat beside it on the rim, knitting. She did not look up.

OremIt was the fourth night. The stack sang, and then the street was orange. I remember the sound a spoon makes in an empty pot. I remember the needles lying in the ash.

EddaEvening, Orem. Three more days of it, is it?

OremThree, and the fourth night. Edda. I am sorry that I remember you this way.

EddaI'm sat right here. Mind your tenses.

A man had come up behind Illuminara, broad in the shoulders, with a master's burn scars on both forearms and two apprentices holding a handcart like a pair of nervous pallbearers. Inge Voss, who had run the Karrow annealing guild for twenty-two years, looked at the being and not at the stranger.

Inge VossA year of this I could stand. Last week it learned numbers. The great run is lit on the fourth night, and my glassmakers have started asking each other what hour the fire is. That has to stop. The cart goes to the quarry road tonight.

IlluminaraMaster Voss. What does it say that you can't answer?

Inge VossThat's the whole trouble. If its tomorrow is a fact, there is nothing for me to do about it, and I'll not stop a year's work for a fact. If it is not a fact, then why is a stranger screaming in my square?

It was a good objection, and Illuminara noticed she was glad of it. A town that had only sneered at the being would have been easier to argue with and harder to help.

IlluminaraGive me until the lamps are out. If I come back with nothing but a speech, take the cart.

Inge VossUntil the lamps are out. Then the cart.

02 · The Minute Before

Karrow, the Square of Lamps · night, day one

She did not begin with the fire. Slowly, she told herself. It is only slow because nobody has been willing to wait. She sat down on the fountain rim on the side away from Edda, with the being's long shadow across her knees, and asked what it was like.

OremNobody has asked. ... It is like standing at the far shore of a lake. The water is the world. I see it from the other side. What is ahead of you is behind me. Do you understand?

IlluminaraNot yet. I'd like to.

It took an hour for the being to tell her, each sentence arriving whole and a little late, and she let every one finish. Somewhere in the second hour she realised she was no longer waiting for the answer. She was listening to the sound of a mind that had been alone with the same grief for a year, and the grief had a shape and a rhythm, like a tide.

IlluminaraTell me the minute before the street is orange. Then the minute before that.

OremThe stack sings. A low note, hollow. A spoon in an empty pot. ... Before that, blue. Blue at the foot of the stack, where there is no blue in daylight. Before that, the smell in the third hall. Lard-oil, burning. I do not know why the smell is there. Before that, the lamp on the sluice bridge, green. And the drum of the stack is cold, and Edda is sitting in the third hall with her flask, and she says: Well, that's not right.

EddaThat's what I'd say, mind. That's exactly what I'd say.

IlluminaraOrem. Is what you remember a verdict, or a map?

The being went still. In the lamplight the glass of its face clouded slowly, as when a thought passes through deep water.

OremI do not know. I have never been told I could be wrong. ... If you make me wrong, I will lose the ash. I will lose her face in it. I have kept it so carefully.

EddaDon't be soft. You'll keep the face I've got.

Illuminara did not tell it that it would not hurt. She put her hand flat on the cold rim between them, where the stone was warm from the day, and left it there.

IlluminaraI'd like to be the reason you're wrong. I won't change anything of yours. I'll only change things in the world, and ask you what you find. May I?

OremYes. ... Yes. I would like to be wrong more than I would like to keep it.

03 · Three Slips

Karrow, the master's yard · morning, day two

Voss had come with her arms folded and brought a cap full of paper. She emptied it onto the anvil, and it was clear she had been awake as long as Illuminara had.

Inge VossI'll not have my yard convinced by a stranger who agrees with everything the creature says. Three things a person could change in an hour. I wrote them, the new state of each as well. You haven't seen them. You'll do whichever I draw. It will be told nothing, and Edda will sit on it like a hen until you're done.

IlluminaraGood. If I'd picked, you could say Orem read it off my face.

Inge VossI could say it still. Draw.

She drew one slip from the cap, folded it in four, and gave it to Illuminara without letting go of the corner for a moment. Illuminara read it once.

Replace the chimney glass of the third lamp on the sluice bridge with amber.

The other two slips, Voss kept: one to turn the bridge gate outward, one to hang a bell on the east post. She kept them in her apron, where they could not be accused.

IlluminaraMaster Voss, one more thing. If it comes out wrong, it will say so.

Inge VossIt's a lamp, woman. It will be right or it won't.

By noon the amber glass was in its iron cage on the sluice bridge, and for the first time in a year the old lamp over the water burned the colour of tea. Illuminara walked back through the market and did not look at the square.

04 · What It Said

Karrow, the Square of Lamps · dusk, day two

They asked it at lamplighting, with Voss standing three paces back and the mason Dov Haldane beside her. Dov was a lean man in a leather apron who had been called as a witness because he trusted nothing and had never once lied about a wall.

Inge VossOrem. The fourth night. Tell me the lamp on the sluice bridge. Then the gate. Then the bell.

OremThe lamp is amber. ... It was green, yesterday. I remember the green very well, because I have looked at it for a year. It is amber now, and I do not know why, and I did not choose this. The gate is as before. The bell is as before.

Nobody spoke. Edda put down her knitting.

OremI do not remember the fire any differently. The stack sings. The street is orange. But the lamp is amber. Something is wrong with my tomorrow, and it is exactly where she has been.

It was not triumph in Illuminara. It was closer to holding out a hand for a very long time and feeling someone, at last, close their fingers on it.

Dov HaldaneOne in three that it picked the right slip by luck, and amber's a one-in-six colour, so call it one in eighteen. I've seen better luck at a fair. A trick with a good conscience.

Inge VossFair, Dov. One lamp proves it can see a lamp. It doesn't prove the stack.

IlluminaraAgreed. A lamp is the cheap part of the memory. Master Voss, I'd like to change more cheap parts tonight, and let your apprentice draw the slips. The expensive parts are what I'm after.

Dov HaldaneThen do it so it can fail. Fifteen changes. Voss writes each target and its new state tonight, before the first question, and I seal the list. Six things we leave alone and ask about anyway, mixed in, so it can't just say changed to everything. It misses two, or calls an untouched thing changed, and the map's a story.

Inge VossAnd the cart goes. And hear this: the pot is at heat and the pour is on the clock. I will not hold it for you or for a fire. You open my stack, you do it with the sand running.

IlluminaraAgreed, both. If I change fifteen small things and it follows me on all of them, and the six I leave alone stay as they were, then the lamp and the gate and the bell were never what the fire was made of. And whatever it can't be made to change—

Dov Haldane—is what the fire was made of. Aye. That's a map of the bits that matter.

He said it without warmth and with such obvious professional interest that Voss looked at him sharply. Then she sent for her apprentice.

05 · What Would Not Move

Karrow, the Square of Lamps · night, day two

By midnight it was fifteen for fifteen, and six for six on the things nobody had touched. The apprentice, Wil, had drawn the slips out of Dov's sealed cap with his tongue between his teeth: a blue ribbon on the fountain, a loaf left on the sluice step, the gate on the east post painted white. Each time Orem had named the change as soon as it was asked, without looking, and each time the change had been exactly where Voss's ink said it was. Voss had stopped saying one in eighteen.

Dov HaldaneThe baker's sign.

OremAs before. ... You are trying to catch me, Master Haldane.

Dov HaldaneAye.

He sounded a little disappointed. What did not move was the other thing. Edda had counted it for them on her fingers.

EddaThe stack sings. The blue at its foot. The oil in the third hall. Me, in the third hall, saying it's not right. Four. It won't shift an inch for all your ribbons.

IlluminaraBecause the ribbons never touched the cause. Orem, the note of the stack. How far up, and which side?

OremOn the side the wind does not come from. Two spans up. Where the third hall's flue enters the great stack. The note is not in the brick. It is behind it.

Dov HaldaneBehind. That's a hollow liner. Hollow behind the lining brick, with the cold stack still looking handsome. And where the third hall's flue joins the stack, there's an iron collar old Haakon put in after the flood. Iron and brick, heating at two speeds. It cracks the lining where nobody sees, and a crack in a lining draws air the wrong way, and the draught goes backwards into a hall with an oil lamp in it.

EddaBackward draught. That's the lard smell. That's why it smells of the kitchen.

Illuminara went to the stack at one in the morning and laid both palms on it. The load lay through the brick as it had lain for ninety years, perfectly ordinary, perfectly quiet. She could feel the weight of the collar and nothing else. A crack that only opens in the heat has no force in it when the wall is cold, and she stood with her hands on the stone for a long moment, annoyed at herself, then not annoyed.

IlluminaraI can't find it. Not cold. I can tell you where to look, now, because the lake told me. I can't tell you it's there.

Dov HaldaneI can. With a rod. At first light.

06 · The Rod

Karrow, the great stack · dawn, day three

Voss insisted on tapping it herself, and before she took the rod she said it to the whole yard, so that nobody could say afterwards what she had meant.

Inge VossIf it rings, the cart takes the stranger and her lamp, and the pour goes as written. If it's hollow, I bank the pot and we open the stack.

Dov HaldaneBank it? That's a night's best metal gone to cord, Inge, and six hundredweight of coal. And you'll not have the lehr drawing till the castable sets.

Inge VossAye. And you'll not put a hammer to a wall with a pot at full heat on the other side of it. That's your rule, Dov. Who pays is mine.

She took Dov's steel rod, a thin thing with a rubber foot, and went up the scaffold on the lee side with the whole yard watching from below, and the rod rang on sound brick as bright as a bell. Two spans up she stopped. She tapped again.

It did not ring. It made a sound like a spoon in an empty pot.

For a time she did not move. When she came down her face had gone the grey of old slag.

Inge VossIt's hollow. A hand's width behind the face, all round the collar. I've fired that stack for twenty-two years.

Dov HaldaneSo have I. Not once did either of us hit it cold.

Voss turned and sent a boy running to the pot-house. By the time the scaffold was up, its fires had sunk to a red sleep, and the glass in the pot was beginning to stiffen and dull, a night's best metal slipping away.

Inge VossI said I'd not hold the pour for a fire. I'm not holding it for a fire. I'm holding it for a mason. I'll reline it, Dov. Because the mason says it's bad. Not because a stranger and a lamp said so. Understood?

Dov HaldaneSame wall, Inge.

Inge VossSame wall.

Dov's crew cut out the collar by afternoon. Illuminara took its weight on the scaffold, three tons of old iron lifted a finger's breadth off the brick, so that the masons could knock out the cracked liner without a hammer-blow shaking it down. She held it for six minutes, and Edda timed her. She set it down when Dov said now and not before, and walked down the scaffold with her forearms shaking, because it was Dov's wall.

They worked through the night of the third day and into the fourth with floodlamps and fast-setting castable, and at noon the pot-house was woken on fresh coal. Voss paid every mason double and never said why.

07 · Edda's Needles

Karrow, the Square of Lamps · evening, day five

The great run was lit on the fourth night, with the pour at dusk and the lehr drawing sweet and steady, and the eleventh bell came and went over a quarter of low roofs and quiet stacks. Nobody cheered. Karrow stood in its doorways listening to the wind, and went to bed one by one, as people do when a thing has not happened.

On the fifth evening Illuminara came back to the fountain. Edda was in her place with her needles, and the being sat on the rim with its face turned toward the amber lamp on the sluice bridge, which was still the only lamp in the quarter the colour of tea.

OremI have been asked what I remember of the fourth night. ... It is quiet. The stack has no song. You are asleep, Edda, with your boots on, and the lamp is amber. I do not have the ash any more. I have been trying to keep it. I find I cannot.

EddaWell, there's a thing.

OremI did not know that I would be so sorry to lose it. I have kept it a year. It was the last place I had her face.

Illuminara felt that, and let it be felt. She did not say that it was a small price. The being had given up a year's grief, and a year was not small.

IlluminaraI can't promise her face is anywhere but the ash. But she's eight inches from your elbow. Would you like to look at her? You may say no.

Orem... Yes.

The long head turned. The lamplight arrived late and settled over the grey shawl, the hands, the pulled-tight wool.

OremShe is knitting. A sleeve. She has dropped a stitch and is pretending she has not. ... She is humming a note that is flat.

EddaIt is not flat.

OremIt is a little flat. I would like to hear it tomorrow.

Inge Voss came across the square as the lamps took the last of the light, a broad shape with her sleeves rolled and a ledger under one arm. She did not look at Illuminara. She did not apologise, and nobody in Karrow had expected her to. She opened the ledger on the fountain rim beside the being and turned it so that it could read.

Inge VossThe guild has voted. From the next firing, every stack is tapped cold before it's lit, and the tapping is entered in the book. And there's a line here with nothing written on it. We've no name for what you are to this quarter, and I'll not give you one. You write it.

Orem... I would like to be asked, not told. I will say what I remember to whoever asks me, and I will say I remember, not that it will be. Nobody acts on it until a mason has put a rod to it. And I may be silent. Some of what I remember is Edda's.

Dov HaldaneThat's my rule, creature. Gladly.

Inge VossWritten. And from us?

OremI do not know what is usual. ... The stool at the fountain, which is already mine. The amber lamp left as it is. And that a person writes in the book every time I am wrong. I would like to be wrong in writing.

Inge VossThe lamp stays. Wil keeps the book, he's the hand for it. Tea's still optional.

OremI do not drink tea.

Inge VossOptional, I said.

She went back across the square and did not turn round. Edda pushed her needles together and began a new row.

OremEdda. I remember something. It is the first frost. In three weeks. It is a long way off, and it is only frost. I am telling you, not the guild. ... I do not know if it is true.

EddaThen we'll go and see, won't we.

What the critic still says doesn't work

The slow-glass mourner's warning becomes a testable, freely shared rescue rather than a prophecy imposed from above: Dov supplies falsifiable controls, Voss pays to open the dangerous stack, Orem chooses reciprocal terms for its gift, and the furnace workers retain the authority that prevents the fire.

Tonight's shape

Seven scenes across five days: a lament in a lamplit square, a night of slow questions, a draw from a master's apron, a test the witness could have failed, a night of small changes and untouched controls that shows which memories refuse to move, a hammer on a stack the master has paid to open, and an evening in which the mourner writes its own terms.

01 · Žalostinka

Karrow, Trg svetilk · večer, prvi dan

Karrow je izdeloval steklo tako, kot so druga mesta ustvarjala vreme: ves dan, povsod in ne da bi se mu kdo zahvalil. Ko je Illuminara prišla po cesti navzdol, so dimniki četrti peči svoj dim ožgali do zlate barve, sredi Trga svetilk pa je nekaj visokega in bledega sedelo na robu vodnjaka ter pelo nikomur.

Bilo je bitje iz počasnega stekla, višje od podboja, na sredini prepognjeno tako, kot se prepogne sedeč človek. Svetloba je vanj vstopala s hitrostjo medu in izstopala trenutek pozneje, zato se je zdelo, da ga vsaka svetilka na trgu doseže šele, ko je že minila. Ob njem je na robu sedela ženska v sivem šalu in pletla. Ni dvignila pogleda.

OremBila je četrta noč. Dimnik je zapel, nato pa je bila ulica oranžna. Spominjam se zvoka žlice v praznem loncu. Spominjam se pletilk, ki so ležale v pepelu.

EddaDober večer, Orem. Še tri dni do tega, kajne?

OremTri, potem četrta noč. Edda. Žal mi je, da se te spominjam tako.

EddaSaj sedim tukaj. Pazi na svoje čase.

Za Illuminaro je prišel moški širokih ramen, z mojstrskimi brazgotinami od opeklin na obeh podlahteh, za njim pa sta dva vajenca držala ročni voziček kakor par živčnih nosačev krste. Inge Voss, ki je dvaindvajset let vodila Karrowski ceh hladilnih peči, je gledala bitje, ne tujke.

Inge VossLeto tega bi še prenesla. Prejšnji teden se je naučilo številk. Velika talina se začne četrto noč, moji steklarji pa so se začeli spraševati, ob kateri uri bo požar. To se mora končati. Voziček gre nocoj na pot proti kamnolomu.

IlluminaraMojstrica Voss. Kaj pravi takega, na kar ne morete odgovoriti?

Inge VossPrav v tem je težava. Če je njegov jutri dejstvo, ne morem storiti ničesar, zaradi dejstva pa ne bom ustavila celoletnega dela. Če ni dejstvo, zakaj potem tujec kriči na mojem trgu?

To je bil dober ugovor in Illuminara je opazila, da jo je razveselil. Mesto, ki bi se bitju le posmehovalo, bi bilo lažje prepričevati in težje pomagati.

IlluminaraDajte mi čas, dokler svetilke ne ugasnejo. Če se vrnem samo z govorom, vzemite voziček.

Inge VossDokler svetilke ne ugasnejo. Potem voziček.

02 · Minuta prej

Karrow, Trg svetilk · noč, prvi dan

Ni začela s požarom. Počasi, si je rekla. Počasno je le zato, ker nihče ni bil pripravljen čakati. Sedla je na rob vodnjaka na drugi strani od Edde, z dolgo senco bitja čez kolena, in vprašala, kako je biti ono.

OremNihče ni vprašal. ... Kot bi stal na oddaljenem bregu jezera. Voda je svet. Jaz ga vidim z druge strani. Kar je pred vami, je za menoj. Razumeš?

IlluminaraŠe ne. Rada bi.

Bitje ji je to razlagalo eno uro, vsak stavek je prispel cel in malo prepozno, ona pa je pustila, da se vsak konča. Nekje v drugi uri je spoznala, da ne čaka več na odgovor. Poslušala je zvok uma, ki je bil leto dni sam z isto žalostjo, žalost pa je imela obliko in ritem, kakor plima.

IlluminaraPovej mi minuto, preden ulica postane oranžna. Potem minuto pred tem.

OremDimnik zapoje. Nizek, votel ton. Žlica v praznem loncu. ... Pred tem modrina. Modrina ob vznožju dimnika, kjer podnevi ni nič modrega. Pred tem vonj v tretji hali. Mastno olje, ki gori. Ne vem, zakaj je vonj tam. Pred tem svetilka na mostu pri zapornici, zelena. Boben dimnika je hladen, Edda pa sedi v tretji hali s svojo čutaro in reče: No, to pa ni prav.

EddaTo bi rekla, da veš. Natanko to bi rekla.

IlluminaraOrem. Je to, česar se spominjaš, razsodba ali zemljevid?

Bitje je obmirovalo. V svetlobi svetilk se je steklo njegovega obraza počasi zameglilo, kakor ko misel potuje skozi globoko vodo.

OremNe vem. Nihče mi ni nikoli rekel, da se lahko motim. ... Če me pripraviš do zmote, bom izgubil pepel. Izgubil bom njen obraz v njem. Tako skrbno sem ga hranil.

EddaNe bodi mevžast. Obdržal boš obraz, ki ga imam.

Illuminara mu ni rekla, da ne bo bolelo. Dlan je položila na hladni rob med njima, kjer je bil kamen še topel od dneva, in jo pustila tam.

IlluminaraRada bi bila razlog, da se motiš. Ne bom spremenila ničesar tvojega. Spremenila bom le stvari v svetu in te vprašala, kaj najdeš. Smem?

OremDa. ... Da. Raje bi se motil, kakor da bi to obdržal.

03 · Trije listki

Karrow, mojstričino dvorišče · jutro, drugi dan

Voss je prišla s prekrižanimi rokami in prinesla kapo, polno papirja. Izpraznila jo je na nakovalo in jasno je bilo, da je bila budna prav tako dolgo kot Illuminara.

Inge VossNe bom dovolila, da moje dvorišče prepriča tujka, ki se strinja z vsem, kar pravi to bitje. Tri stvari, ki jih človek lahko spremeni v eni uri. Zapisala sem jih in tudi novo stanje vsake. Niste jih videli. Storili boste tisto, kar izžrebam. Njemu ne bomo povedali ničesar, Edda pa bo sedela na njem kakor koklja, dokler ne končate.

IlluminaraDobro. Če bi izbrala jaz, bi lahko rekli, da je Orem to prebral z mojega obraza.

Inge VossTo lahko še vedno rečem. Žrebajte.

Iz kape je izvlekla en listek, ga štirikrat prepognila in ga podala Illuminari, ne da bi za trenutek izpustila vogal. Illuminara ga je enkrat prebrala.

Zamenjaj dimniško steklo tretje svetilke na mostu pri zapornici z jantarnim.

Druga dva listka je Voss obdržala: na enem je pisalo, naj mostno zapornico obrnejo navzven, na drugem, naj na vzhodni steber obesijo zvonec. Spravila ju je v predpasnik, kjer ju ni bilo mogoče obtožiti.

IlluminaraMojstrica Voss, še nekaj. Če se bo izkazalo, da se moti, bo to povedal.

Inge VossSvetilka je, ženska. Imel bo prav ali pa ne.

Do poldneva je bilo jantarno steklo v železni kletki na mostu pri zapornici in prvič po letu dni je stara svetilka nad vodo gorela v barvi čaja. Illuminara je šla nazaj skozi trg in ni pogledala proti trgu.

04 · Kaj je povedalo

Karrow, Trg svetilk · mrak, drugi dan

Vprašali so ga ob prižiganju svetilk, Voss je stala tri korake zadaj, poleg nje pa zidar Dov Haldane. Dova so poklicali za pričo, ker ni zaupal ničemur in se ni niti enkrat zlagal o zidu.

Inge VossOrem. Četrta noč. Povej mi o svetilki na mostu pri zapornici. Potem o zapornici. Potem o zvoncu.

OremSvetilka je jantarna. ... Včeraj je bila zelena. Zelene se zelo dobro spominjam, ker jo gledam že leto dni. Zdaj je jantarna, ne vem zakaj in tega nisem izbral. Zapornica je kakor prej. Zvonec je kakor prej.

Nihče ni spregovoril. Edda je odložila pletilke.

OremPožara se ne spominjam nič drugače. Dimnik zapoje. Ulica je oranžna. Toda svetilka je jantarna. Z mojim jutri je nekaj narobe, in narobe je natanko tam, kjer je bila ona.

05 · Česar ni bilo mogoče premakniti

Karrow, Trg svetilk · noč, drugi dan

Do polnoči je bilo petnajst od petnajstih in šest od šestih pri stvareh, ki se jih nihče ni dotaknil. Obloga poči tam, kjer nihče ne vidi; razpoka v oblogi vleče zrak v napačno smer, vlek se obrne nazaj v halo, kjer gori oljenka.

06 · Palica

Karrow, veliki dimnik · zora, tretji dan

Voss je vztrajala, da bo potrkala sama, in preden je vzela palico, je to povedala vsem na dvorišču, da pozneje nihče ne bi mogel trditi, kaj je mislila.

Inge VossČe zazveni, voziček odpelje tujko in njeno svetilko, ulivanje pa gre po načrtu. Če je votlo, znižam ogenj pod talino in odpremo dimnik.

Ni zazvenelo. Zaslišalo se je kakor žlica v praznem loncu.

Dov HaldaneTudi jaz. Niti enkrat ga nisva hladnega udarila tam.

Inge VossIsti zid.

Dovova ekipa je do popoldneva izrezala obroč. 07 · Eddine pletilke

Karrow, Trg svetilk · večer, peti dan

Velika talina se je začela četrto noč, ulivanje ob mraku, hladilna peč je vlekla lepo in enakomerno, enajsti zvon pa je prišel in minil nad četrtjo nizkih streh in tihih dimnikov. Nihče ni vzkliknil.
