01 · The Relay Hut

Kesh Ridge relay hut · 14:02, fifty minutes before the east road burns

The repeater hummed on the bench like a trapped wasp. Emi Sar sat in front of it with her mother's old headset over one ear and her thumb on a brass key worn smooth by thirty years of other people's emergencies. Outside, smoke leaned across the ridge in a long brown shoulder.

EmiGalen Doss. Present, pump bay. Tuvi Marek. Present, side drift. Ilse Orr—

A voice answered through static, thin and flat with distance.

SeraIlse is here, baby. She is coughing but here.

EmiIlse Orr, present. Thirty-one. I am keeping thirty-one.

Illuminara had landed fifty paces down the road and walked the rest. She stopped in the open doorway with both hands visible and the light she normally wore dimmed to a lamp's worth. She had heard the girl's carrier across half the province, a single unbroken tone that had flattened every other call on the net, and she had expected a hijacker. She found a child with a notebook open on her knee.

IlluminaraI am Illuminara. I will not touch the equipment. I would like to listen before I ask for anything.

EmiEverybody listens first. Then somebody in a clean uniform asks me to stop.

IlluminaraThen I will be the one who does not ask yet.

She stayed in the doorway. Every few minutes Emi read the names again, from the top, in exactly the same order, and a voice underground answered each. Illuminara watched the girl's pencil tick against the page after every reply. It was not panic. Panic did not keep a column.

She is not guarding a signal. She is guarding a list.

IlluminaraWho is not on the mine's official roll?

Emi's pencil stopped.

EmiFourteen of them. Mama brought them from the border camp last spring and the owner never filed their papers. If the mine says thirty-one people are down there, it has to say who. So there is no thirty-one. There is seventeen, and seventeen is what the rescue crews have been told to expect.

IlluminaraAnd the reports?

EmiThree. The east gallery, the pump bay, the shaft collar. Mama wrote them in her own hand. Each came back stamped resolved.

She turned a page and showed it. Three carbon copies, each with a clean violet stamp over a crack someone had drawn in the margin.

EmiWhen I hold the channel, they keep saying her name. The moment I let go, she is a rumour again, and nobody sends a crew to a rumour.

Illuminara felt it land. She could have lifted the roof off the shaft in two breaths; the child had not asked for a roof. The child had asked to be counted.

IlluminaraWhat would you need to hear, in order to let one second of it go?

EmiNot a promise.

IlluminaraWhat, then?

EmiI would have to hear it happen, while it happens, on a line I can check.

02 · What the Fire Truck Heard

Orrel crossing, fire command truck · 14:19, thirty-three minutes before the east road burns

The truck's cab smelled of wet ash and lukewarm coffee. Fire Chief Odo Brenn had a map of six villages taped to the dashboard and a handset that had not spoken to any of them in forty minutes. Beside him sat Captain Mirel Wynn of the provincial gendarmerie, helmet on her knee, a triangulation printout creased in her fist.

BrennShe is sitting on the only repeater that reaches Tamsin, Hale, Quill, Ansel, Dunmere and Orrel. The wind turned at noon. I have nine hundred people downwind and no way to tell them which road.

Smoke watch had filled two school buses at Hale, and five Dunmere orchard trucks waited at the lower gate. A patrol car could carry the first order to Hale in four minutes, but the fire was closing forks one by one; the latest sheet gave the east fork seven minutes. What the other villages needed was not one order but the next road while they were moving.

WynnMy team is eleven minutes from her door. I have orders to cut the transmitter.

IlluminaraI have been in her door. I would like you to hear what the carrier is made of before you cut it.

She told them about the list. The fourteen unfiled names. The three stamped reports. Brenn's mouth thinned. Wynn did not move at all.

WynnWhich three?

IlluminaraEast gallery. Pump bay. Shaft collar.

WynnThe collar report came through my section. I forwarded it myself, six weeks ago. I was told it had been inspected and cleared.

The cab was quiet. Outside, a siren wound down without anyone touching it.

BrennCaptain, that does not make her carrier legal.

WynnNo. It makes it the only honest thing on that net.

Illuminara did not hurry them. She laid out what the girl could do and what she could not: Emi could not read a false assurance; she could read a name, answered live.

IlluminaraKeep one line. Give each of you a window on it, out loud, in the open. Your orders first. The roll call last. Everybody hears both. Nobody has to believe anybody. They only have to check.

BrennA car reaches Hale in four minutes. Why not send five more cars?

IlluminaraBecause the fork it reports burns in seven. A courier can start Hale; it cannot tell Dunmere's loaded trucks which turn is still open ten minutes later. Send the first car, and keep the repeater for the roads that change behind it.

Brenn looked at the seven-minute mark, then at five red arrows crossing five roads.

BrennFine. Thirty-eight seconds for fire orders. I can fit one route and one repeat if nobody decorates it.

WynnSouth patrol to Hale. One car continues to Dunmere's lower gate. The team approaching Emi holds at Orrel.

Wynn did not pocket the folded printout. She kept it visible against her knee.

WynnThe girl keeps her key. My team holds with this report still in my hand. Record that I gave the order.

IlluminaraShe keeps her key. Momentary, not latched: she can cut in whenever a name is skipped or a window runs long. If she cuts in without cause, she answers for it later. If she has cause, you left her the means.

03 · The Question No One Else Knew

Kesh Ridge relay hut · 14:34, eighteen minutes before the east road burns

Emi listened to the terms twice. Illuminara read them to her from a sheet Brenn's technician had typed on the truck's dashboard. She did not take her thumb off the key.

EmiYou are all on the same side of the table, and I am the only one who has been lied to by every one of you.

IlluminaraYes.

EmiThat is it? Yes?

IlluminaraYou are correct about the stamps. I cannot make that untrue by being polite.

Emi's knuckles whitened on the brass.

EmiDuring his thirty-eight seconds, how do I know the mine is quiet because it is Brenn's turn and not because the roof fell?

Illuminara looked at the typed sheet. The neat division she had brought back from the truck had made silence look like obedience.

IlluminaraYou do not. I missed that.

EmiThen the mine gets the first two seconds of every minute. Someone says present. Brenn gets thirty-eight. The roll gets twenty. If present does not come, the fire window waits.

BrennAgreed.

EmiAnd the key is not spent forever because one adult broke one window. It resets after every skipped name or proved overrun.

IlluminaraAgreed. You found the hole. We change the protocol before you move your thumb.

EmiThen prove the thing I can check. You say you will go down. Anyone can say they go down. I will ask my mother a question that only she and I know, and she answers on the open line. Then you tell me which gallery you are standing in when she says it.

IlluminaraGood. Ask it.

Emi pulled the headset tight. She spoke to the dark tone of the carrier as if it were a tunnel.

EmiMama. The morning of the ninth. What did you leave on the stove?

For eleven seconds nothing came back but hiss. Then a laugh, cracked and wet, from deep underground.

SeraThe good pot. The blue one. I left the lid off so you would be angry at me, not at the pot.

Emi shut her eyes. She did not cry. She wrote blue pot in the margin of the notebook, next to the first name, and her handwriting was steady.

IlluminaraI am going down at the pump-bay shaft. I will say the gallery number every time I change levels. You will hear my voice move.

EmiAnd if you stop saying it?

IlluminaraThen you have your key.

Emi eased her thumb off the brass. The carrier fell silent. For the first time in two hours, the net stayed open long enough for the new first check.

SeraMine present.

Brenn's tired voice came in without ceremony.

BrennMine rescue, amend your roll: thirty-one below, not seventeen. Stage the second cage and more air. The first set runs short at fifteen-thirty-two for thirty-one.

Mine-rescue leadSecond cage coming to the collar. Extra respirators with it. We search the side drift after the first lift.

Only then did Brenn give the road order.

BrennAll villages, this is Orrel command. East road closes at fourteen-fifty. Use the ridge track. Say again: ridge track.

Emi opened her mouth and closed it again. She had not expected the voice to sound so tired.

EmiHe sounds scared.

IlluminaraHe is. So are you. He is saying it anyway.

Another voice entered before the minute closed, older than Emi's and roughened by smoke.

MaraDunmere convoy. Five orchard trucks, forty-six people, lower gate. South patrol says the east fork is ash. We take mill lane. I am leaving the irrigation pumps.

BrennDunmere, mill lane is clear to the north culvert. Report when the fifth truck crosses.

MaraI will. Do not send anyone back for the trees.

04 · Shaft Three

Kesh cobalt mine, pump-bay shaft · 14:46, six minutes before the east road burns

The shaft smelled of wet iron and burnt rubber. Illuminara went down fast, calling the level aloud at each landing: level two, level three, east gallery. Her voice, and no one's else, crackled in the girl's headset at the top of the hill.

At 14:47, before the rock closed around her, the shared line carried Mara again.

MaraLast Dunmere house clear. Fifth truck over the north culvert. The pump shed stays.

BrennLogged. All Dunmere people clear.

At the sixth landing the rock stopped her. A slab the size of a barn door had come away from the gallery roof and settled across the passage on a crooked pair of timbers. Behind it, a lamp swung. Thirty-one tired people had made a small safe room in the place the owner had told them did not exist.

SeraWe heard you count down the levels. Is that her? Is that my daughter's light?

IlluminaraIt is mine. Your daughter's is on the other end of the line, and she is keeping your names.

She set her shoulder under the slab and took its weight. It was not the kind of strength she would have chosen to show, but the timbers would not have held through another tremor, and the people behind them were not the ones who had to prove anything today.

IlluminaraOne at a time. Say your name when you pass. Emi is writing it down.

They came through low, with lamps, with scarves over their mouths. The first was a man whose hands had been bandaged with a shirt.

GalenGalen Doss.

Up on the ridge, Emi's pencil dropped a line through the first name. Down in the dust, Illuminara kept her voice level and the slab off their backs.

At the collar, the first cage rose with seventeen people and went down empty again. The second cage and the extra respirators arrived because Brenn had changed the roll to thirty-one. The mine-rescue lead sent two people into the side drift for the fourteen names the official sheet had omitted. Illuminara held the slab through both runs.

At 14:49, Brenn's window ran long. He was repeating a village's route, and the forty-sixth second of the minute passed with his voice still on the line.

The headset snapped. Emi's voice came through hard, her thumb on the key.

EmiBrenn. You are over.

Everyone on the net heard it. For six seconds nobody breathed. Then:

BrennUnderstood. My window is yours. Ridge track. Out.

Emi let the cause-bound cut go early. When Brenn acknowledged the breach, the small reset lamp beside the key turned green again.

EmiIlse Orr. Tuvi Marek. Present, present.

The roll call went on. The tally grew in her notebook, one pencil stroke at a time, and each stroke was a voice she could check.

At the last landing, a girl younger than Emi squinted into Illuminara's dimmed light as if it were a lamp from a story. Illuminara turned the glow up a degree so the child could see her own hands.

Dust like snow, in the lamplight. She had not expected to feel wonder in the shaft, under a slab, with a clock running. It came anyway, small and clean, and she let it.

SeraThirty. One more behind me. Mine.

Sera Sar came through last, soot-blackened and gripping a hand-lamp she refused to put down. She did not say her name. Emi's voice said it for her, hoarse, over the channel.

EmiSera Sar. Present.

SeraNo, baby. Out.

05 · The Tally at Zero

Kesh Ridge relay hut · 15:41, forty-three minutes after the road burned

The south patrol had reached Hale at 14:23; the open channel moved its buses at 14:35, sent Dunmere by mill lane at 14:36 and confirmed the fifth truck over the culvert at 14:47. By 14:52 the last Hale bus had turned before the fire crossed the east road at 14:58. Nine hundred people were on the high pastures. Dunmere had lost one orchard.

Emi still sat in front of the repeater. The carrier was silent, the channel crowded with ordinary voices: fire crews, a village midwife, a dispatcher reading out vehicle plates.

EmiTwenty-eight. Twenty-nine. Thirty. Thirty-one.

The last pencil stroke closed the column. She laid the pencil down.

IlluminaraZero missing. Zero names without an answer.

EmiZero missing because they prepared for thirty-one, not seventeen.

Captain Wynn stood in the doorway with her cap in her hand and the folded printout still visible. She waited until the girl looked up.

WynnI need your statement about the carrier and the reports. You will answer for the net. I will answer for my section.

EmiI know.

WynnI will copy your three carbons here and read every stamp on this channel. You keep the originals.

Emi looked at her mother, who sat on an upturned crate with a blanket round her shoulders and a tin cup of tea going cold between both hands. Sera did not tell her what to do.

EmiI keep the key until the copying is done.

WynnAgreed.

At 15:52 Wynn read the report numbers, stamps and dates into the handset, including the section that had forwarded one and been told it was resolved. Her own.

MaraDunmere keeps its own household tally. Start with medicine, feed and wages. Three of mine rent their roofs, so their names are not on the property list. Titles come later.

WynnThe emergency account is open. Your copy governs the first payments.

The mine would not reopen. The fourteen unfiled workers would wait months for papers that should have existed a year ago. Emi would face a magistrate within the week. None of those costs was called a happy ending.

Illuminara stood at the hut door while the afternoon smoke thinned. Emi set the brass key on the bench beside the notebook, in plain view.

EmiWill you be there? At the inquiry?

IlluminaraIf you ask me.

Emi nodded, put the headset over one ear and found a clean page.

EmiMara Venn.

Static breathed. Then:

MaraPresent. Forty-six clear. One orchard gone. Start the next name.

Emi drew a line beside Mara's name.

What the critic still says doesn't work

Illuminara, called to a burning province where a fourteen-year-old holds the only emergency radio so that her mother and thirty others stay counted, listens until she hears the hole in her own plan, and the child's thirty-one-name roll and resettable key make one shared channel that every office can check, so a second cage, extra air and live road orders bring all thirty-one miners and nine hundred villagers out, at the price of a closed mine, a lost orchard and a hearing Emi will face.

Tonight's shape

Five scenes move from the relay hut, where Illuminara learns that Emi is guarding a list rather than a signal, through a fire truck where live road closures make the shared channel indispensable, into a protocol Emi repairs before releasing the carrier. Her manifest changes the mine rescue to two cage runs with extra air, while Mara Venn's Dunmere checkpoints make the evacuation audible. The aftermath leaves the mine closed, one orchard lost and Emi answerable, but every affected group keeps a voice, a record and a practical next step.

SeraIlse je tukaj, dete. Kašlja, vendar je tukaj.

Če rudnik reče, da je spodaj enaintrideset ljudi, mora povedati, kdo so. Zato enaintrideset ne obstaja. Obstaja sedemnajst, in reševalnim ekipam so rekli, naj pričakujejo sedemnajst.

IlluminaraKaj pa poročila?

EmiTri. Vzhodni rov, črpališče, ustje jaška. Mama jih je napisala lastnoročno. Vsako se je vrnilo z žigom rešeno.

Obrnila je stran in ji jo pokazala. Tri kopije na indigo papirju, vsaka s čistim vijoličnim žigom čez razpoko, ki jo je nekdo narisal na rob.

EmiKo držim kanal, ponavljajo njeno ime. V trenutku, ko ga spustim, spet postane govorica, ekipe pa nihče ne pošlje h govorici.

Illuminara je začutila težo teh besed. Streho jaška bi lahko dvignila v dveh vdihih; otrok ni prosil za streho. Otrok je prosil, naj ga preštejejo.

IlluminaraKaj bi morala slišati, da bi signal izpustila za eno sekundo?

EmiNe obljube.

IlluminaraKaj pa?

EmiMorala bi slišati, da se zgodi, medtem ko se dogaja, na zvezi, ki jo lahko preverim.

02 · Kar je slišalo gasilsko vozilo

Križišče Orrel, gasilsko poveljniško vozilo · 14:19, triintrideset minut, preden zgori vzhodna cesta

Kabina vozila je dišala po mokrem pepelu in mlačni kavi. Gasilski poveljnik Odo Brenn je imel na armaturno ploščo prilepljen zemljevid šestih vasi in slušalko, ki se z nobeno od njih ni oglasila že štirideset minut. Ob njem je sedela stotnica pokrajinske žandarmerije Mirel Wynn, s čelado na kolenu in zmečkanim izpisom triangulacije v pesti.

Patruljni avtomobil bi lahko prvo navodilo v Hale odpeljal v štirih minutah, toda ogenj je zapiral odcepe enega za drugim; najnovejši list je vzhodnemu odcepu dajal sedem minut. Druge vasi niso potrebovale enega navodila, temveč naslednjo prosto cesto med premikanjem.

WynnMoja ekipa je enajst minut od njenih vrat. Ukazano mi je prekiniti oddajnik.

IlluminaraBila sem v njenih vratih. Preden ga prekinete, bi rada, da slišite, iz česa je njen nosilni signal.

Povedala jima je o seznamu. O štirinajstih neprijavljenih imenih. O treh ožigosanih poročilih. Brenn je stisnil ustnice. Wynn se sploh ni premaknila.

WynnKatera tri?

IlluminaraVzhodni rov. Črpališče. Ustje jaška.

WynnPoročilo o ustju je šlo skozi moj oddelek. Pred šestimi tedni sem ga sama posredovala. Rekli so mi, da so ga pregledali in odobrili.

V kabini je zavladala tišina. Zunaj je sirena zamrla, ne da bi se je kdo dotaknil.

BrennStotnica, zaradi tega njen nosilni signal še ni zakonit.

WynnNe. Je pa zato edina poštena stvar na tem omrežju.

Illuminara ju ni priganjala. Pojasnila je, kaj deklica lahko stori in česa ne: Emi ne more prebrati lažnega zagotovila; lahko pa prebere ime, na katero nekdo v živo odgovori.

IlluminaraOhranite eno zvezo. Vsakemu dajte časovno okno, na glas in javno. Najprej vaša navodila. Nazadnje preverjanje imen. Vsi slišijo oboje. Nikomur ni treba nikomur verjeti. Samo preveriti morajo.

BrennAvtomobil prispe v Hale v štirih minutah. Zakaj ne bi poslali še petih?

IlluminaraKer odcep, o katerem poroča, zgori v sedmih. Kurir lahko sproži Hale; deset minut pozneje pa naloženim tovornjakom iz Dunmera ne more povedati, kateri zavoj je še odprt. Pošljite prvi avtomobil in ohranite repetitor za ceste, ki se za njim spreminjajo.

Brenn je pogledal sedemminutno oznako, nato pet rdečih puščic, ki so prečkale pet cest.

BrennPrav. Osemintrideset sekund za požarna navodila. Spravim noter eno pot in eno ponovitev, če nihče ne olepšuje.

WynnJužna patrulja v Hale. En avtomobil nadaljuje do spodnjih vrat Dunmera. Ekipa, ki se približuje Emi, obstane v Orrelu.

Wynn prepognjenega izpisa ni spravila v žep. Obdržala ga je vidnega ob kolenu.

WynnDeklica obdrži ključ. Moja ekipa počaka s tem poročilom še vedno v moji roki. Zabeležite, da sem jaz dala ukaz.

IlluminaraKljuč obdrži. Trenuten, ne zaklenjen: kadar koli lahko prekine, če je ime preskočeno ali okno prekoračeno. Če prekine brez razloga, bo pozneje odgovarjala. Če razlog ima, ste ji pustili sredstvo.

03 · Vprašanje, ki ga ni poznal nihče drug
