A forest that thinks in centuries holds the only veto over a failing dam, and seventy thousand people have eight days. Illuminara does not hurry the forest. She changes what it is asked to meet.

01 · Eight Days

Marren Dam, Wardens' hut · dusk, eight days before the flood

The spillway at Marren Dam had been built for a river that no longer existed. Forty years of silt had narrowed it, and the forecast for the autumn flood put the crest eleven percent above what the gates could pass. Joss Marrin, the chief operator, laid the numbers on the table of the Wardens' hut and did not dress them.

JossEight days. After that the water goes over the top, or it goes where it has always wanted to go, which is down the old floodway and through the lower stands of the Oren Wood.

Nobody in the hut needed the second half explained. The Compact of the Wood was a hundred and forty years old, and its first clause said that the dam stood by the leave of the forest. The Wardens signed for the forest; the valley signed for itself. Leave was asked once a generation. The last question, whether the dam might stay and the floodway be used in emergencies, had been put to the roots eleven years ago, and the forest had not yet answered.

Illuminara had come at dusk with Wren Odira because Tamsin Oke, the senior Warden, had written to her. She had been a court interpreter before she was anything else, and the habit had outlasted everything that came after it. Before she spoke she asked what the other party would have to believe for the sentence to be true.

Dorrit Hale, who ran the ferry and sat on the valley council, had no patience for that.

DorritCut the crest. Drain Marren to half and be done. We have spent eleven years asking a wood for permission to keep our own water.

JossA half-drained Marren is seventy thousand people without taps by February.

TamsinAnd a forest that learns its veto was theatre, will not ask again.

Illuminara let them finish. Then she asked Tamsin what the forest did between a question and an answer.

TamsinWaits. It answers in water. We ask it with water, and it takes as long as it takes to be sure.

IlluminaraWaiting is what you can see from outside. What does it need to meet before it is sure?

Tamsin thought about it longer than the question deserved.

TamsinEverything. It has never accepted a claim it has not been rained on by. A flood has to arrive in its roots before it believes in the flood.

IlluminaraOne flood at a time?

TamsinOne season at a time. The dam's future is two hundred years of seasons.

Wren, who kept the clock for her because someone had to, said quietly.

WrenSo it cannot decide in eight days.

IlluminaraI do not think it is slow at thinking. I think it is slow at being shown. The Wood is forty thousand hectares. Tamsin, you told me it has eleven thousand stands that sense separately and agree among themselves. Every question we have ever asked it arrived in one place, in order, a page a year. We have been handing a body that can read in parallel a single page at a time.

02 · The Counting Field

Counting Field beside the Oren Wood · the first three days

The plan took one night to say and two days to build. Joss's model ran the valley forward two hundred years under the proposed arrangement: the dam stays, and the floodway opens when a crest exceeds the gates. Each simulated year produced, at most, a few events a forest would care about, a crest or a drawdown or a dry spell. Tamsin was sure of that part.

TamsinIt counts the pulses. The quiet between them it already knows.

Illuminara proposed to stop making the forest meet the years in sequence, and she was exact about what that meant. Two hundred sentinel plots would be staked across the lower slopes, one plot for each simulated year. Into each plot would go only that year's pulses, at the strength the model gave them (the volume, the temperature, the load of silt), in the order the model gave them, and in their proper proportions to one another. What would not be kept was the quiet between pulses. Where the model had a long dry summer, the plot would get a single marked interval of rest, the same for every year, because Tamsin had said the forest already knew the quiet and counted only the pulses. Nothing in a plot lasted a year. Each took a day's irrigation, which meant that the forest, which read the whole slope at once, could meet two hundred years in a week, laid side by side instead of end to end. What each plot's roots would feel was a short, distinct signature of pulses, and the two hundred signatures together made a record that the roots could compare with each other the way they compared every neighbouring stand.

Joss objected first, and well.

JossMy model is honest to about thirty percent on sediment after year ninety. If you show it only the flattering years, the dam will pass.

IlluminaraThen it must not pass on flattery. Put in the worst years the model can produce, and mark them as the worst.

She saw him look at the table.

IlluminaraIncluding the year the dam fails it.

JossThat is not a plan for winning.

IlluminaraIt is a plan for being believed.

Wren asked the question Illuminara had been waiting for someone to ask.

WrenYou can open a window to it. You have done it for stranger minds. Why walk two hundred plots through mud?

IlluminaraBecause a window would show the forest what I understand. It has not asked what I understand. It has asked for water.

She looked out at the dark line of the Wood.

IlluminaraAnything I could place inside it, it could not check, and a veto it cannot exercise on checked evidence is not a veto. Every plot will be labelled with its year. Anyone can walk the Field with Joss's model in hand and confirm that plot one hundred and fourteen carries the pulses of year one hundred and fourteen, in the model's order and at the model's strength. If a plot lies, the forest will be able to say so.

By the third dawn the Counting Field stood in the rain, two hundred stakes in a grid across the slope, the irrigation lines laid, Joss's crews at the pumps and Tamsin's Wardens at the sap-gauge pillars that stood among the trees like a congregation. The pumps started at noon. Wren wrote the time in her book. Five days remained.

03 · The Field That Stayed Dry

Counting Field and Wardens' hut · fourth morning to fifth-day dusk

The forest's reply came on the fourth morning, and it was silence.

Tamsin brought the pillar readings up from the trees herself. She laid them on the table without comment, because she did not trust her own voice. Not one stand had reached across its boundary toward a plot. The roots along the Field's edge had sealed, closing in a way the Wardens' ledgers described as something that happened after a poisoning. Eleven thousand stands, and all of them had turned away.

DorritIt said no.

Dorrit said, not unkindly.

TamsinNo looks different. No has a shape. A no is a pattern of drawing down in the plots it dislikes.

Tamsin pressed her knuckles together.

TamsinThis is a door shut on the whole conversation.

Illuminara did not argue about what it meant. She asked what water they had used. Tamsin said river water, as in all the old watering letters. Joss said no, and the room changed. The model's numbers had come from the reservoir outlet, so the pumps had drawn from the outlet: forty metres down, four degrees, clear as glass, with almost no oxygen in it. Every old letter had been watered from the river above the lake. This was the first time anyone had drawn from the dam's own depths.

IlluminaraIt has met rivers for ten thousand years. A river is warm at the top and brown in a flood. It carries mud, and it smells of the hills it came from. We showed it water that is not a river at all.

She turned to Tamsin.

IlluminaraThere are two questions here and I will not let them run together. Why did it never answer you in eleven years? Because you handed it the future one page at a time, and that I think is still true. Why did it seal against us this morning? Because of the water. The first is an old fault in how we asked. The second is a new fault in what we carried. The forest did not refuse the dam. It refused a lie about what water is.

Joss was already looking at the spillway drawings pinned to the wall.

JossClear water. The dam sends the Wood clear water because the mud stays behind the wall.

IlluminaraAnd the mud is what is choking your gates.

She said it slowly, so he could take it as his own.

IlluminaraI do not yet know what the forest wants from silt. I know that it will not be asked with water it cannot recognise. Make the water a river's, and then we will see what it does with it.

They rebuilt in a day. Joss drew surface water from the upper layers, where the reservoir was warm, and dosed it with silt dredged from the delta at the head of the lake, the same silt that was narrowing his gates, mixed to the load the model gave for each simulated flood. At Joss's insistence one plot was left on clear outlet water as a control. Wardens carried the new water out to the plots in drums when the lines would not reach. Wren stood at the edge of the Field with her book open and said the time aloud at every hour, because nobody else wanted to. The second pumping began at dusk on the fifth day. Three days remained.

04 · What the Forest Wrote

Counting Field and Wardens' hut · sixth morning to seventh evening

The roots opened on the sixth morning. Tamsin heard it before she read it; the Wardens said the pillars made a sound when a stand woke, a faint tick like cooling metal. By noon the sap gauges were climbing in ranks down the slope.

The forest did not simply accept. It edited. In the plots for the early years, when the floodway opened under the proposed arrangement, the roots drew water in hard and held it. In others they drew it down and let it go. They drew hardest in the plots whose water carried the most silt, and least in the one plot left on clear water, the control Joss had insisted on. Tamsin plotted the pattern with her pencil crooked in her hand, and the shape that came up was a calendar. The forest wanted the water in spring, in the first weeks of the sap rise, and it wanted it to reach the Maren terraces below the dam.

The Maren terraces were Ines Valk's orchards, and those of three hundred and ninety other families.

She came to the hut that evening and read the plot herself. She was seventy, with the hands of a woman who pruned in winter and picked in summer, and she did not raise her voice.

InesEvery spring. You want four hundred families to drown their blossom on the word of a wood.

IlluminaraI want nothing of the kind. I do not decide this. The forest has told us what it needs. Whether the valley can give it, and how, is a question about your land. You know the terraces and I do not.

It would have been easy to say more. She said nothing, and she let Ines look at the plot until Ines's mind, which was a farmer's and therefore practical, began to move.

InesA flood that reaches is not a flood that reaches everything. The terraces sit at three heights.

JossThey do.

InesSo the crest only needs to climb one step a year.

Ines set her finger on the lowest terrace.

InesOne terrace floods and two rest. Each of us loses one spring in three and gains the silt on ground that has been going thin since my father's time. That I could live with. Four hundred families drowning together I could not.

It was a better design than anything Illuminara had in her head, and she said so. Then she said.

IlluminaraThe forest has to read it. We cannot decide for it that this is enough.

They staked sixty plots that night for the revised calendar, rotating, with the dosed warm water. The forest took a day and a half. On the seventh evening the stands woke again, and this time Tamsin did not trust her pencil. She held the paper up to the lantern and read it twice. The rotation was accepted. The roots had also marked every ninth year in the plots for a double pulse, an extra step of water to seed the alder stands on the far bank.

JossIt is bargaining.

TamsinIt is answering. It has just done in a week what usually takes it a generation.

Dorrit stood with his arms folded at the back of the hut.

DorritI still say a wood ought not to have a vote.

InesIt had one before there was a valley to complain. We are only late to the reading.

He did not sign. He said he would watch the first release. It was the best that could be hoped for, and Illuminara did not push for more.

05 · The First Release

Marren Dam and Maren terraces · the eighth night

The crest reached Marren Dam on the eighth night, thirteen percent over what the gates could pass.

Joss's crews opened the spillway, and the third gate jammed. Silt had packed the guide channels, and the gate stuck at a third of its travel. Under the weight of the rising water its steel frame had begun to bend. Joss's engineers gave it twenty minutes before the frame went, and it would take forty to cut the silt out by hand. Wren, standing in the control room with her book, said the numbers aloud so that everyone could hear them.

Illuminara walked out onto the gate apron in the rain. She was not in a window and she was holding no one's mind. The work was physical, and so she kept it exact. She laid her attention along the frame, found the line where the load was failing, and moved it. She took the weight off the bending members and passed it across the apron to the two concrete piers on either side, which had been built for more than they had ever been asked to carry. The frame stopped flexing. She held it there. It was not strength she needed so much as precision, and the knowledge, which she had taken from Joss's drawings that week, of which piers could take it.

WrenForty minutes.

Wren said into the radio, and then again at thirty, and again at twenty, and in the end she said only the numbers, one after another, in a level voice.

Joss's people cut the silt out of the guides in thirty-four. The third gate went to full travel. The floodway sluice at the foot of the dam opened for the first time in nine years, on the forest's terms, and the brown water that had been the dam's enemy for forty years went down the cut and onto the lowest of the Maren terraces.

Ines had asked to be first.

InesMine floods first. I will not ask the neighbours to trust a thing I have not stood in myself.

She stood at the edge of her orchard with the lantern at her feet, and the warm, silted water came across her old terrace and went around her boots. Beyond the terrace, along the slope, the sap pillars among the alders began to tick one after another in order, the way they had done in the Field.

The dam held. Marren came down seven percent, no more, and stopped. The valley's taps ran. The gates were clear for the first time in years, and the floodway ran its brown water down the cut for six days.

The amendment to the Compact was signed in the Wardens' hut on the seventh day after the crest. Tamsin signed for the forest, with the Counting Field as the instrument, and the valley signed for itself. Its central clause tied three things together so that none could be kept without the others: the valley would dredge the delta every autumn, the dredged silt would be dosed into warm surface water at the load the Field had tested, and that water, and no other, would carry the spring pulse onto the terraces, the rotation and the ninth-year double pulse included. The Wardens would draw a sample at the sluice at every release and set it beside the Field's readings, and the Field itself would be staked again every five years with the new data. Joss had sworn at the dredging bill until the clause was read back to him, and then he asked only that the survey crews be named in it. A second clause, which Illuminara had insisted on, said that the forest could refuse. If a pulse drifted from the water it had accepted, the Wardens would stake the plots again, and the forest would answer in its own time. Ines lost the next spring's blossom on her east terrace, and so would her children's families in their turn. Dorrit signed under protest, and then, to his own surprise, asked to carry the first sample bottle down to the sluice himself.

Wren found Illuminara on the dam wall at dawn on the morning of the signing. The last of the floodway's brown water was still going down the cut, and the lowest trees were dark with it.

WrenYou did not make it faster.

IlluminaraNo.

She watched the water reach the trunks.

IlluminaraI stopped making it wait for us.

Gozd, ki razmišlja v stoletjih, ima edini pravico zavrniti poseg ob jezu, ki mu grozi odpoved, sedemdeset tisoč ljudi pa ima le osem dni. Illuminara gozda ne priganja. Spremeni način, kako mu predstavijo dokaze.

01 · Osem dni

Jez Marren, koča varuhov · mrak, osem dni pred poplavo

Prelivni kanal jezu Marren je bil zgrajen za reko, ki je ni bilo več. Štirideset let naplavin ga je zožilo, napoved jesenske poplave pa je kazala, da bo vršni pretok za enajst odstotkov presegel zmogljivost zapornic. Joss Marrin, glavni upravljavec, je številke položil na mizo v koči Varuhov in jih ni olepševal.

JossOsem dni. Potem bo voda prelila jez. Ali pa bo stekla tja, kamor je že od nekdaj hotela: po stari poplavni strugi in skozi spodnje sestoje gozda Oren.

V koči nikomur ni bilo treba pojasnjevati drugega dela. Sporazum z Gozdom je bil star sto štirideset let in njegova prva določba je pravila, da jez stoji z dovoljenjem gozda. Varuhi so podpisovali v imenu gozda, dolina v svojem. Za dovoljenje so prosili enkrat na generacijo. Zadnje vprašanje — ali jez sme ostati in ali se poplavna struga lahko uporablja v sili — so koreninam zastavili pred enajstimi leti. Gozd še ni odgovoril.

Illuminara je ob mraku prispela z Wren Odiro, ker ji je pisala Tamsin Oke, glavna Varuhinja. Preden je postala karkoli drugega, je bila sodna tolmačka; ta navada je preživela vse, kar je prišlo pozneje. Preden je spregovorila, se je vprašala, kaj bi morala druga stran verjeti, da bi bil stavek zanjo resničen.

Dorrit Hale, ki je upravljal trajekt in sedel v svetu doline, za takšno previdnost ni imel potrpljenja.

DorritOdrežimo vrh vala. Izpraznimo Marren do polovice in končajmo. Enajst let že prosimo gozd za dovoljenje, da obdržimo lastno vodo.

JossČe Marren izpraznimo do polovice, bo do februarja sedemdeset tisoč ljudi brez vode iz pip.

TamsinIn gozd, ki ugotovi, da je bila njegova pravica do veta samo predstava, ne bo več pristal na pogovor.

Illuminara jih je pustila govoriti do konca. Nato je Tamsin vprašala, kaj gozd počne med vprašanjem in odgovorom.

TamsinČaka. Odgovarja z vodo. Sprašujemo ga z vodo, on pa si vzame toliko časa, kolikor ga potrebuje, da se prepriča.

IlluminaraČakanje je tisto, kar vidite od zunaj. Kaj mora izkusiti, preden se lahko prepriča?

Tamsin je premišljevala dlje, kot bi si vprašanje zaslužilo.

TamsinVse. Nikoli ni sprejel trditve, dokler ga ni dosegla kot dež. Poplava mora priti do njegovih korenin, preden ji verjame.

IlluminaraPo ena poplava naenkrat?

TamsinPo ena sezona naenkrat. Prihodnost jezu pomeni dvesto let letnih časov.

Wren, ki je za Illuminaro merila čas, ker ga je nekdo pač moral, je tiho rekla.

WrenTorej se v osmih dneh ne more odločiti.

IlluminaraNe mislim, da razmišlja počasi. Mislim, da mu dokaze kažemo prepočasi. Gozd obsega štirideset tisoč hektarov. Tamsin, rekla si mi, da ima enajst tisoč sestojev, ki zaznavajo vsak zase in se med seboj usklajujejo. Vsako vprašanje, ki smo mu ga kdaj zastavili, je prispelo na eno mesto, po vrsti, kakor ena stran na leto. Telesu, ki zna brati vzporedno, smo ves čas izročali le po eno stran.

02 · Števno polje

Števno polje ob gozdu Oren · prvi trije dnevi

Za razlago načrta so potrebovali eno noč, za izvedbo dva dni. Jossov model je prikazal prihodnjih dvesto let doline po predlagani ureditvi: jez ostane, poplavna struga pa se odpre, kadar vršni pretok preseže zmogljivost zapornic. Vsako simulirano leto je ustvarilo kvečjemu nekaj dogodkov, ki bi gozdu kaj pomenili: poplavni vrh, znižanje gladine ali sušno obdobje. O tem je bila Tamsin prepričana.

TamsinŠteje sunke. Tišino med njimi že pozna.

Illuminara je predlagala, naj gozdu let ne kažejo več zaporedoma. Pri tem je bila povsem natančna. Po spodnjih pobočjih bi zakoličili dvesto preizkusnih ploskev, po eno za vsako simulirano leto. Vsaka ploskev bi prejela samo sunke tistega leta, z močjo, ki jo je določal model — količino vode, temperaturo in vsebnostjo mulja —, v pravem vrstnem redu in v pravilnih medsebojnih razmerjih. Izpustili bi le dolge premore med sunki. Kjer je model predvidel dolgo sušno poletje, bi ploskev dobila en označen čas mirovanja, enak za vsa leta, saj je Tamsin pojasnila, da gozd tišino že pozna in šteje samo sunke. Na nobeni ploskvi se dogajanje ne bi raztezalo čez celo leto. Vsako bi namakali en dan. Gozd, ki bere vse pobočje hkrati, bi tako v enem tednu spoznal dvesto let — položenih drugo ob drugo, ne drugo za drugim. Korenine posamezne ploskve bi začutile kratko, značilno zaporedje sunkov; vseh dvesto zaporedij skupaj bi tvorilo zapis, ki bi ga korenine lahko primerjale med seboj, kakor primerjajo sosednje sestoje.

Joss je ugovarjal prvi — in upravičeno.

JossMoj model ima po devetdesetem letu pri naplavinah približno tridesetodstotno negotovost. Če mu pokažemo samo ugodna leta, bo jez dobil dovoljenje.

IlluminaraPotem dovoljenja ne sme dobiti zaradi olepševanja. Vključite najhujša leta, ki jih model lahko ustvari, in jih označite kot najhujša.

Opazila je, kako je pogledal proti mizi.

IlluminaraTudi leto, ko jez popusti.

JossTo ni načrt za zmago.

IlluminaraTo je načrt, kako doseči, da nam bodo verjeli.

Wren je postavila vprašanje, za katero je Illuminara čakala, da ga kdo izreče.

WrenLahko mu odpreš okno. To si storila tudi pri še bolj nenavadnih umih. Zakaj bi se torej prebijali skozi blato med dvesto ploskvami?

IlluminaraKer bi mu okno pokazalo tisto, kar razumem jaz. Gozd pa ni prosil za moje razumevanje. Prosil je za vodo.

Pogledala je proti temni črti dreves.

IlluminaraČesarkoli bi mu položila neposredno v zavest, ne bi mogel preveriti. Pravica do veta, ki je ni mogoče uveljaviti na podlagi preverjenih dokazov, pa ni prava pravica do veta. Vsaka ploskev bo označena s svojim letom. Vsakdo bo lahko prehodil Števno polje z Jossovim modelom v roki in preveril, da ploskev sto štirinajst nosi sunke leta sto štirinajst, v vrstnem redu in z močjo, ki ju določa model. Če bo katera ploskev lagala, bo gozd to lahko povedal.

Ob tretji zori je Števno polje stalo v dežju: dvesto količkov v mreži po pobočju, položene namakalne cevi, Jossove ekipe pri črpalkah in Tamsinini Varuhi pri stebrih za merjenje drevesnega soka. Med drevesi so stali kakor zbor, zbran k obredu. Črpalke so pognali opoldne. Wren je čas zapisala v beležnico. Ostalo je pet dni.

03 · Polje, ki je ostalo suho

Števno polje in koča varuhov · od četrtega jutra do mraka petega dne

Odgovor gozda je prišel četrto jutro. Bil je tišina.

Tamsin je iz gozda sama prinesla odčitke merilnih stebrov. Brez besed jih je razložila po mizi, ker ni zaupala lastnemu glasu. Niti en sestoj ni segel prek svoje meje proti kateri od ploskev. Korenine na robu Polja so se zapečatile, zaprle so se tako, kot so dnevniki Varuhov opisovali odziv po zastrupitvi. Enajst tisoč sestojev, in prav vsi so se obrnili stran.

DorritRekel je ne.

je rekel Dorrit, ne brez sočutja.

TamsinNe je videti drugače. Ne ima obliko: vzorec umikanja vode iz ploskev, ki mu niso všeč.

Tamsin je stisnila členke.

TamsinTo pa so vrata, zaprta pred celotnim pogovorom.

Illuminara ni ugovarjala razlagi. Vprašala je, katero vodo so uporabili. Tamsin je rekla, da rečno, kakor v vseh starih vodnih pismih. Joss je odvrnil: »Ne.« In razpoloženje v prostoru se je spremenilo. Podatki za model so prihajali iz iztoka akumulacije, zato so črpalke črpale od tam: štirideset metrov pod gladino, voda s štirimi stopinjami, bistra kot steklo in skoraj brez kisika. Vsa stara pisma so zalivali z rečno vodo nad jezerom. Tokrat so prvič črpali iz globin akumulacije za jezom.

IlluminaraReke pozna že deset tisoč let. Rečna voda je na površju topla, med poplavo pa rjava. Nosi blato in diši po hribih, iz katerih priteče. Mi smo mu pokazali vodo, ki sploh ni podobna reki.

Obrnila se je k Tamsin.

IlluminaraTu sta dve vprašanji in ne bom dovolila, da ju pomešamo. Zakaj vam v enajstih letih ni odgovoril? Ker ste mu prihodnost podajali po eno stran naenkrat; mislim, da to še vedno drži. Zakaj se je danes zaprl pred nami? Zaradi vode. Prva napaka je stara in zadeva način vprašanja. Druga je nova in zadeva to, kar smo mu prinesli. Gozd ni zavrnil jezu. Zavrnil je laž o tem, kaj je voda.

Joss je že gledal načrte preliva, pripete na steno.

JossBistra voda. Jez pošilja gozdu bistro vodo, ker se mulj zadržuje za pregrado.

IlluminaraIn prav ta mulj maši vaše zapornice.

Izgovorila je počasi, da bi do spoznanja lahko prišel sam.

IlluminaraNe vem še, kaj gozd potrebuje od mulja. Vem pa, da ga ne moremo spraševati z vodo, ki je ne prepozna. Pripravite vodo, kakršno nosi reka, potem pa bomo videli, kaj bo storil z njo.

V enem dnevu so vse postavili na novo. Joss je črpal površinsko vodo iz zgornjih, toplejših plasti akumulacije ter ji dodajal mulj, izkopan iz delte na začetku jezera — isti mulj, ki je ožil njegove zapornice —, za vsako simulirano poplavo v količini, ki jo je določal model.
