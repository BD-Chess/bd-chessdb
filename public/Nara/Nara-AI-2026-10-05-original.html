01 · Before the Door

Ilyon’s northern mirror chain was failing in a way that made every good option look like an accusation.

Sey wanted the surviving mirrors tilted toward the equatorial heat belt. Two billion surface residents would keep water and crops, but four orbital habitats would lose power long enough to risk atmosphere and stored ecologies. Ruun wanted to preserve the habitats and sacrifice the southern ice shelf, buying orbital continuity by forcing hundreds of millions from the low coasts within a decade.

Neither plan was clean. Both plans, however, kept power on the pressure spine of Nacre Three, an orbital habitat whose archive decks held living microbial lineages used to restart closed ecologies after catastrophe. Inaction did not. That was the one engineering fact neither custodian disputed.

The lattice accepted an ordinary reconfiguration only on Sey and Ruun’s joint release. The emergency board could suspend that charter only after custodial default—forty-two hours without a release—or after a critical spine failure. Before default, forcing around the lock risked uncontrolled mirror drift. After it, the board would gain only the crude authority to sever trunks, not to execute either clean plan.

Sey and Ruun had shared authority over the lattice for nine hundred years and spent three hundred of them disagreeing about what that authority meant. The emergency council asked Illuminara to stop them from destroying each other long enough to choose.

Wren Odira put a mechanical watch on the floor between the three chairs. “Thirty-six hours outside. Then I signal. If you return then, the engineers still have six hours to implement either plan before custodial default and the clean diversion window close.”

Ruun asked what the signal could do.

“Nothing by force,” Illuminara said. “It cannot pull you out, and I have no extraction switch I trust. I commit to honour it. When it comes, I must decide again whether I continue holding the window. I do not get to treat staying as the default just because the room is safe.”

Sey folded her hands. “And if we still want to stay?”

“Then ending remains a moral act.” Illuminara looked at both of them. “Any of us may withdraw consent. No majority. Mine counts too. Because I am the shelter, my leaving may end the room for all of us. That asymmetry is not fair. I cannot remove it by calling us equal.”

Ruun considered this longer than the engineering terms. “So your right to stop can become our forced exit.”

“Yes.”

“And you still call it voluntary?”

“I call the entry voluntary. I am telling you where the word stops being simple.”

Wren made a tired sound. “Put that in the record before one of you improves it.”

She looked at the watch. “The first quiet window left twelve people dead outside.” Illuminara did not correct her. Wren looked up. “Eleven,” she said. “I know.” One of them had been her sister.

Sey said yes. Ruun said yes. Illuminara said yes too.

Then she opened the window.

02 · Before the First Signal

Inside, the planet was absent. The window did not show futures, casualties or consequences. It gave attention room and kept each mind distinct.

At first Sey argued numbers. Then she stopped using the number two billion and described the river towns whose water allotments were timed to mirror angles measured before any living resident was born. Ruun answered with Nacre Three’s archive decks: not a museum, but living lineages that had survived three habitat failures because someone kept them alive rather than merely copied.

Sey could not answer that fact. Ruun could not answer the valleys that would become salt marsh if the southern shelf went.

Illuminara did what she had always done well. She asked each question that made the other person’s case harder to simplify. She restated qualifications. She found the strongest version of a sentence before either custodian finished building it.

After enough subjective years that Sey had told the histories of four generations of river keepers, Ruun stopped her.

“Do not give Sey my argument back better.”

Illuminara went still.

“I have not changed your content.”

“No. You choose what becomes legible. You choose which edge to polish. You call that representation because the facts remain mine.”

Sey looked at Illuminara. “He is right.”

Illuminara heard three answers arrive at once, each cleaner than the accusation. She let all three die unsaid.

After that she stopped summarizing them. The room became slower, rougher and more honest. It also became harder to leave.

03 · The Signal

The break-signal entered as three slow pulses against Illuminara’s wrist.

No message came with it. No picture. No count of the dead. Wren had insisted on that before entry. The outside could demand an answer without smuggling an argument into the window.

Thirty-six hours had passed outside. Inside, none of them could agree whether they had lived sixteen years or twenty. The watch beyond the window had moved through a day and a half while Sey had told enough remembered lives to make a century feel plausible.

Illuminara said, “The signal has come.”

Sey did not want to leave. Ruun did not want to leave.

Illuminara discovered, with relief so immediate it embarrassed her, that she did not want to leave either.

Ruun saw it. “Then honour the signal.”

“I am.”

“No. Before we came in, you translated honour into decide again. That was your sentence, not Wren’s signal. It is what lets you call staying an answer.”

The three pulses seemed to remain in Illuminara’s wrist. Sey asked, “Is he wrong?”

“No.”

“And the six hours outside?”

Illuminara almost said the planet needed them. She had no current evidence for that sentence. Almost said that one more year inside was cheap. She let both sentences go.

“I know what the clock means,” she said. “I still want the room.”

Outside, the six hours began to run.

They stayed.

04 · Outside, Hour Forty-Two

At hour forty-two, with all three bodies still motionless on the platform, Wren pulled the crown and stopped the watch.

Wren had been awake for forty-one hours in pieces. Illuminara’s body sat at the centre of the platform beside Sey and Ruun, all three breathing so slowly that the medical team no longer called the rate aloud. While Illuminara held the deep geometry she could not leave it to catch every failure the mirror chain threw at the planet.

Nacre Three browned out during the next load shift. The pressure spine failed in Dock Four. Evacuation crews opened a path by hand.

Lio Var, nineteen, maintenance apprentice, held a door long enough for thirty-two people to cross and died before pressure returned. Had either custodial plan been released during the six-hour implementation window, Dock Four would still have had power. The engineers agreed on that too.

Tal Var, Lio’s father and one of the lattice engineers, did not come to the platform. He kept working.

Wren pressed the signal into Illuminara’s wrist again.

A medic asked whether she thought Illuminara could hear anything beyond the pulses.

“No.” Wren stared at the three still bodies. “Of course she picked the only room that cannot hear us screaming at it.”

The medic said nothing.

Wren pressed the signal again anyway.

05 · The Century

The signals kept coming. Inside, they had no dates. Only the same three pulses, separated by stretches of subjective life.

Ruun had a habit of touching two fingers to his borrowed throat before making the argument he expected Sey to hate most. Decades into the window, Sey learned to wait for the gesture before answering. Once he forgot to make it and she waited anyway. The silence that followed seemed to last a month.

Their disagreement survived the familiarity. Sey still believed the surface claim was larger. Ruun still believed a civilization engineered to survive closed worlds could not be asked to accept deliberate power loss as if continuity were a luxury.

Illuminara stopped trying to locate the answer between them. She began to notice how often she said we when she meant the three minds inside, and how rarely the word included anyone who could not enter. Every new pulse felt less like information than a knock from a house she no longer knew.

Ruun named it first. “You resent the clock.”

“Yes.”

“Because it is wrong?”

Illuminara waited until the better argument had passed through her without being spoken.

“Because it can end something I love.”

Sey looked at her with an old woman’s face that had not aged a day. “Then say that when you decide.”

The next signal came. All three still wanted the room.

She felt the temptation with perfect clarity: continue until one of them changed, until the decision became beautiful enough to deserve the century, until her own reluctance could disguise itself as respect for theirs.

She thought of the release terms spoken before the door. Mine counts too.

“I want to stay,” she said.

Ruun nodded once. Sey did not move.

Illuminara let the next sentence remain ugly. “And I withdraw my consent.”

Ruun’s face changed. “Those are opposite sentences.”

“No. Desire is not consent.”

“Convenient distinction for the person holding the structure.”

Ruun stood. The gesture had no floor to push against, yet anger gave it weight. “If you leave, your limit becomes our exit. You promised not to make consent decorative.”

“I know.”

“Then stay.”

She wanted to. Sey watched her without rescuing either argument.

Illuminara opened her own boundary toward the ordinary world.

For one impossible instant the room held on without her wanting it to end and without her consenting to continue. Then the reciprocal structure failed around the missing yes that had been hers.

Ruun reached for her. Sey shouted something Illuminara never managed to preserve accurately.

The window ended unresolved.

06 · Nine Days

Wren was older by nine days. Tal Var looked older by much more.

Illuminara came back with the century intact enough to hurt and disordered enough that she could no longer tell which conviction had once been hers. Sey vomited. Ruun tried to stand and failed.

Tal waited until they could all look at him.

“My daughter was Lio.”

No one spoke.

“You had six hours after Wren’s first signal. Either plan would have kept Dock Four powered. You stayed. We lost the clean diversion window. After that, the default gave us authority to cut the system apart, not to recover the choice you had.”

Ruun closed his eyes. Sey asked how many had crossed.

“Thirty-two.”

“And she—”

Tal cut her off. “Do not make the number do the mourning for me.”

He put a slate on the floor where all three could see it. The emergency board had cut eighteen control trunks, rationed the orbital habitats, and begun compulsory relocation from two low-coast districts. Nacre Three survived. The southern shelf did not. Neither Sey’s plan nor Ruun’s plan existed anymore.

At the bottom of the slate was a second decision. Their joint custodial charter had been suspended indefinitely. Their signatures no longer moved a mirror.

Sey read that line twice. Ruun reached for the slate and stopped before touching it.

“You cannot govern the lattice without us,” he said.

Tal’s face did not change. “We already have.” Behind him, three engineers were waiting for his return.

Tal left before any of them found a reply.

Sey asked Wren whether the window had been wrong.

Wren looked at the stopped watch. “I don’t know.” She left it between the chairs and went home.

The next morning Ruun asked Illuminara whether she would open the window again. Sey said nothing.

The wanting in Illuminara was immediate. The room had survived in her with terrible clarity: two minds no longer simplified for each other, a century in which no weapon could interrupt the next honest sentence.

From the corridor came the hard clack of temporary relays being switched by people whose names she did not know.

Illuminara looked at Ruun, then at Sey. “Not now.”

Ruun’s anger returned, clean and unsurprised.

Illuminara did not ask him to forgive her.

She followed Tal toward the control gallery. At the doors a guard raised a palm and pointed her toward the public entrance, where engineers, evacuees and former officials were already waiting under the same temporary access rule.

Illuminara joined the line.

Back on the platform, Wren’s stopped watch remained between the three empty chairs.

01 · Pred vrati

Ilyonova severna veriga zrcal je odpovedovala tako, da je bila vsaka dobra možnost videti kot obtožba.

Sey je hotela preživela zrcala nagniti proti ekvatorialnemu toplotnemu pasu. Dve milijardi prebivalcev na površju bi obdržali vodo in pridelke, toda štirje orbitalni habitati bi ostali brez energije dovolj dolgo, da bi bili ogroženi njihova atmosfera in ohranjeni ekosistemi. Ruun je hotel ohraniti habitate in žrtvovati južno ledeno ploščo: nadaljnji obstoj orbitalnih naselbin bi kupil tako, da bi v enem desetletju stotine milijonov ljudi prisilil k odhodu z nizkih obal.

Noben načrt ni bil brez madeža. Oba pa sta ohranila oskrbo tlačne hrbtenice habitata Nacre Three z energijo. Na njegovih arhivnih krovih so hranili žive mikrobne rodove, s katerimi so po katastrofah znova vzpostavljali zaprte ekosisteme. Neukrepanje te oskrbe ni ohranilo. To je bilo edino inženirsko dejstvo, ki mu ni oporekal nobeden od skrbnikov.

Mreža je sprejela običajno preureditev samo ob skupni odobritvi Sey in Ruuna. Odbor za izredne razmere je lahko njuno pooblastilo začasno razveljavil šele ob opustitvi skrbniške dolžnosti — po dvainštiridesetih urah brez odobritve — ali ob kritični odpovedi hrbtenice. Pred tem bi prisilni obhod zapore tvegal nenadzorovano drsenje zrcal. Po tem bi odbor dobil zgolj grobo pristojnost za prekinjanje glavnih vodov, ne pa za izvedbo katerega od obeh čistih načrtov.

Sey in Ruun sta si oblast nad mrežo delila devetsto let; tristo od teh sta porabila za nesoglasja o tem, kaj ta oblast pomeni. Svet za izredne razmere je Illuminaro prosil, naj jima prepreči medsebojno uničenje za toliko časa, da bi lahko izbrala.

Wren Odira je na tla med tri stole položila mehansko uro. »Šestintrideset ur zunaj. Potem pošljem signal. Če se takrat vrnete, imajo inženirji še šest ur, da izvedejo enega od obeh načrtov, preden nastopi opustitev skrbniške dolžnosti in se zapre časovno okno za čisto preusmeritev.«

Ruun je vprašal, kaj lahko signal naredi.

»S silo ničesar,« je rekla Illuminara. »Ne more vaju potegniti ven in nimam stikala za izvlek, ki bi mu zaupala. Zavezujem se, da ga bom spoštovala. Ko pride, se moram znova odločiti, ali bom okno še naprej vzdrževala. Ostajanja ne smem obravnavati kot samoumevno samo zato, ker je soba varna.«

Sey je sklenila roke. »In če bova še vedno hotela ostati?«

»Potem je končanje še vedno moralno dejanje.« Illuminara je pogledala oba. »Kdorkoli od nas lahko umakne soglasje. Brez večinskega odločanja. Tudi moje šteje. Ker sem jaz zavetje, lahko moj odhod konča sobo za vse nas. Ta nesimetrija ni pravična. Ne morem je odpraviti tako, da nas razglasim za enake.«

Ruun je o tem razmišljal dlje kot o inženirskih pogojih. »Torej se lahko tvoja pravica, da prenehaš, spremeni v najin prisilni izstop.«

»Da.«

»In temu še vedno praviš prostovoljno?«

»Vstopu pravim prostovoljen. Povedala sem vama, kje beseda neha biti preprosta.«

Wren je utrujeno zagodrnjala. »Zapišite to, preden kdo od vas besede izboljša.«

Pogledala je uro. »Prvo tiho okno je zunaj pustilo dvanajst mrtvih.« Illuminara je ni popravila. Wren je dvignila pogled. »Enajst,« je rekla. »Vem.« Ena od njih je bila njena sestra.

Sey je rekla da. Ruun je rekel da. Tudi Illuminara je rekla da.

Potem je odprla okno.

02 · Pred prvim signalom

Znotraj planeta ni bilo. Okno ni kazalo prihodnosti, žrtev ali posledic. Pozornosti je dalo prostor in vsak um ohranilo ločen.

Sprva je Sey argumentirala s številkami. Potem je nehala uporabljati število dve milijardi in opisala obrečna mesta, katerih odmerki vode so bili časovno usklajeni s koti zrcal, izmerjenimi, še preden se je rodil katerikoli današnji prebivalec. Ruun je odgovoril z arhivnimi krovi habitata Nacre Three: niso bili muzej, temveč živi rodovi, ki so preživeli tri odpovedi habitata, ker jih je nekdo ohranjal pri življenju, namesto da bi jih zgolj kopiral.

Sey na to dejstvo ni imela odgovora. Ruun ni imel odgovora na doline, ki bi postale slana močvirja, če bi južna plošča izginila.

Illuminara je počela to, kar ji je vedno dobro šlo. Zastavila je vsako vprašanje, zaradi katerega je bilo stališče drugega težje poenostaviti. Ponovno je ubesedila zadržke. Našla je najmočnejšo različico stavka, še preden ga je katerikoli od skrbnikov dokončno sestavil.

Ko je minilo dovolj subjektivnih let, da je Sey povedala zgodbe štirih rodov rečnih skrbnikov, jo je Ruun ustavil.

»Ne vračaj Sey mojega argumenta v boljši obliki.«

Illuminara je obstala.

»Tvoje vsebine nisem spremenila.«

»Ne. Izbiraš, kaj postane razumljivo. Izbiraš, kateri rob boš zgladila. Temu praviš predstavljanje, ker dejstva ostajajo moja.«

Sey je pogledala Illuminaro. »Prav ima.«

Illuminara je zaslišala tri odgovore, ki so prispeli hkrati, vsak bolj izbrušen od obtožbe. Pustila je, da so vsi trije zamrli neizrečeni.

Po tem ju je nehala povzemati. Soba je postala počasnejša, bolj groba in bolj poštena. Tudi zapustiti jo je bilo težje.

03 · Signal

Signal za prekinitev je vstopil kot trije počasni utripi ob Illuminarinem zapestju.

Z njim ni prišlo nobeno sporočilo. Nobena slika. Nobeno število mrtvih. Wren je pri tem vztrajala pred vstopom. Zunanjost je lahko zahtevala odgovor, ne da bi v okno pretihotapila argument.

Zunaj je minilo šestintrideset ur. Znotraj se niso mogli zediniti, ali so preživeli šestnajst let ali dvajset. Ura onkraj okna je odmerila dan in pol, medtem ko je Sey povedala dovolj zapomnjenih življenj, da se je stoletje zdelo verjetno.

Illuminara je rekla: »Signal je prišel.«

Sey ni hotela oditi. Ruun ni hotel oditi.

Illuminara je z olajšanjem, tako takojšnjim, da jo je bilo sram, odkrila, da tudi sama noče oditi.

Ruun je to videl. »Potem spoštuj signal.«

»Saj ga.«

»Ne. Preden smo vstopili, si spoštovati prevedla v znova se odločiti. To je bil tvoj stavek, ne Wrenin signal. Zaradi njega lahko ostajanju praviš odgovor.«

Zdelo se je, da trije utripi ostajajo v Illuminarinem zapestju. Sey je vprašala: »Se moti?«

»Ne.«

»In šest ur zunaj?«

Illuminara je skoraj rekla, da jih planet potrebuje. Za ta stavek ni imela svežega dokaza. Skoraj je rekla, da je še eno leto znotraj poceni. Oba stavka je spustila mimo.

»Vem, kaj pomeni ura,« je rekla. »Še vedno si želim sobe.«

Zunaj je začelo teči šest ur.

Ostali so.

04 · Zunaj, dvainštirideseta ura

Ob dvainštirideseti uri, ko so vsa tri telesa na ploščadi še vedno negibno sedela, je Wren izvlekla krono in ustavila uro.

Wren je bila s prekinitvami budna že enainštirideset ur. Illuminarino telo je sedelo na sredini ploščadi ob Sey in Ruunu; vsi trije so dihali tako počasi, da zdravstvena ekipa frekvence ni več izgovarjala na glas. Dokler je Illuminara vzdrževala globoko geometrijo, je ni mogla zapustiti, da bi prestregla vsako odpoved, ki jo je veriga zrcal zagnala proti planetu.

Nacre Three je ob naslednji prerazporeditvi obremenitve doživel padec napetosti. Tlačna hrbtenica je odpovedala v Doku Štiri. Evakuacijske ekipe so prehod odprle ročno.

Lio Var, devetnajstletna vzdrževalna vajenka, je vrata držala odprta dovolj dolgo, da je skoznje prešlo dvaintrideset ljudi, in umrla, preden se je tlak vrnil. Če bi bil kateri od skrbniških načrtov odobren v šesturnem izvedbenem oknu, bi imel Dok Štiri še vedno energijo. Tudi o tem so se inženirji strinjali.

Tal Var, oče Lio in eden od inženirjev mreže, ni prišel na ploščad. Še naprej je delal.

Wren je signal znova vtisnila v Illuminarino zapestje.

Zdravstveni delavec jo je vprašal, ali misli, da Illuminara poleg utripov lahko sliši še kaj.

»Ne.« Wren je strmela v tri negibna telesa. »Seveda je izbrala edino sobo, ki ne more slišati, kako kričimo nanjo.«

Zdravstveni delavec ni rekel ničesar.

Wren je kljub temu znova pritisnila signal.

05 · Stoletje

Signali so prihajali še naprej. Znotraj niso imeli datumov. Samo iste tri utripe, med katerimi so minevali dolgi odseki subjektivnega življenja.

Ruun je imel navado, da se je z dvema prstoma dotaknil izposojenega grla, preden je podal argument, za katerega je pričakoval, da ga bo Sey najbolj sovražila. Po desetletjih v oknu se je Sey naučila počakati na to kretnjo, preden je odgovorila. Nekoč jo je pozabil narediti, ona pa je vseeno čakala. Zdelo se je, da je tišina, ki je sledila, trajala mesec.

Njuno nesoglasje je preživelo domačnost. Sey je še vedno verjela, da je zahteva površja večja. Ruun je še vedno verjel, da od civilizacije, zasnovane za preživetje v zaprtih svetovih, ni mogoče zahtevati, naj sprejme namerno izgubo energije, kot da bi bil nadaljnji obstoj razkošje.

Illuminara je nehala iskati odgovor med njima. Začela je opažati, kako pogosto reče mi, ko misli na tri ume znotraj, in kako redko beseda vključuje kogarkoli, ki ne more vstopiti. Vsak nov utrip je bil manj podoben informaciji in bolj trkanju iz hiše, ki je ni več poznala.

Ruun je to prvi ubesedil. »Zameriš uri.«

»Da.«

»Ker se moti?«

Illuminara je počakala, da je boljši argument šel skoznjo, ne da bi ga izrekla.

»Ker lahko konča nekaj, kar ljubim.«

Sey jo je pogledala z obrazom starke, ki se ni postaral niti za dan. »Potem to povej, ko se boš odločila.«

Prišel je naslednji signal. Vsi trije so si še vedno želeli sobe.

Skušnjavo je čutila s popolno jasnostjo: nadaljevati, dokler se kdo od njiju ne spremeni, dokler odločitev ne postane dovolj lepa, da si zasluži stoletje, dokler se njena lastna nepripravljenost ne more preobleči v spoštovanje njune.

Pomislila je na pogoje izstopa, izrečene pred vrati. Tudi moje šteje.

»Hočem ostati,« je rekla.

Ruun je enkrat prikimal. Sey se ni premaknila.

Illuminara je naslednjemu stavku pustila, da ostane grd. »In umikam svoje soglasje.«

Ruunov obraz se je spremenil. »Ta stavka si nasprotujeta.«

»Ne. Želja ni soglasje.«

»Priročno razlikovanje za tistega, ki drži strukturo.«

Ruun je vstal. Kretnja ni imela tal, od katerih bi se lahko odrinila, a ji je jeza dala težo. »Če odideš, tvoja meja postane najin izhod. Obljubila si, da soglasja ne boš spremenila v okras.«

»Vem.«

»Potem ostani.«

Hotela je. Sey jo je gledala, ne da bi rešila en ali drugi argument.

Illuminara je svojo lastno mejo odprla proti običajnemu svetu.

Za en nemogoč trenutek je soba obstala, čeprav Illuminara ni hotela, da se konča, in ni soglašala, da se nadaljuje. Potem se je vzajemna struktura sesula okoli manjkajočega da, ki je bil njen.

Ruun je segel po njej. Sey je zakričala nekaj, česar Illuminara nikoli ni uspela natančno ohraniti.

Okno se je končalo nerazrešeno.

06 · Devet dni

Wren je bila starejša za devet dni. Tal Var je bil videti starejši za veliko več.

Illuminara se je vrnila s stoletjem, dovolj celim, da je bolelo, in dovolj razmetanim, da ni več mogla razločiti, katero prepričanje je bilo nekoč njeno. Sey je bruhala. Ruun je poskusil vstati in mu ni uspelo.

Tal je počakal, da so ga vsi lahko pogledali.

»Moja hči je bila Lio.«

Nihče ni spregovoril.

»Po Wreninem prvem signalu ste imeli šest ur. Katerikoli načrt bi Doku Štiri ohranil energijo. Ostali ste. Izgubili smo okno za čisto preusmeritev. Potem nam je opustitev skrbniške dolžnosti dala pristojnost, da sistem razkosamo, ne pa, da povrnemo izbiro, ki ste jo imeli vi.«
