THE LANGUAGE OF EMPTY SEATSJEZIK PRAZNIH SEDEŽEV

At the last council before storm season, island settlers fill three chairs left empty by an ocean nation—and discover that hospitality has become consent to surrender the reefs that supply their drinking water. With the rescue pact expiring at sunset, Illuminara must help both peoples build a language in which absence cannot agree, refusal costs something, and every promise survives a three-minute test.

01 · The Chairs They Filled

The council platform stood where the lagoon changed from green sand to blue drinking-water reef and then black shelf. Five island delegations brought ledgers, gauges and well jars; the ocean nation arrived in forty narrow vessels.

Three chairs stood empty between the delegations.

Councilor Mara Vey thought the chairs were an omission. The sea people had travelled six days; leaving guests to stand looked like an insult. She asked two clerks to sit and took the third chair herself, smiling toward Navigator Sefa Koro.

The lagoon went silent. On the nearest vessel, a brass disk struck the deck once.

Sefa did not sit. She untied the blue cord at her wrist and laid it across the table. Behind her, forty masts lowered their rescue pennants.

“What have I done?” Mara asked.

The interpreter, Nilo Esen, answered too quickly. “You accepted the open duties.”

“Accepted what duties?”

Sefa pointed beyond the platform to the pale crescents under the water. “Stewardship of the north wells. Passage through the coral gates. Emergency draw from the three sweet reefs.”

The water delegates rose. Those reefs supplied eleven thousand people; one bad channel could turn five settlements brackish.

Mara stood from the chair as if it had burned her. “We accepted nothing. We filled seats for guests.”

“An empty seat is a duty kept open until someone takes it,” Sefa said through Nilo. “You took all three.”

“An empty seat means welcome here.”

“Then your welcome claims our unanswered burdens.”

Above the table, the storm buoy began its hourly pulse. A tropical wall was forming two days east. At eighteen hundred, when the sun touched the outer shoal, the old rescue compact expired. Without renewal, the island councils could not command the ocean nation's deep-draft vessels, and the ocean nation could not shelter in the island harbours without separate permission from every settlement.

The first nursery boats—families travelling between flotillas with the season—had entered the storm corridor at midnight. Eight were due by eighteen hundred; three more were expected by eighteen thirty, when an expired compact could leave them without coordinated harbour protection.

Governor Tal Orri pushed the island claim book away. “No agreement made by a chair will bind us.”

Sefa's deputy, Navigator Pol Reka, answered, “Then no agreement made by your signatures will bind us. Your marks do not speak aboard our vessels.”

Illuminara had watched from the edge of the platform, beside the freshwater cooperative's mechanic, Iri Vale. She touched neither chair nor ledger.

“Before anyone explains what the other side meant,” she said, “name what cannot be decided by explanation.”

Iri held up a jar of clear reef water. “No protocol may make silence, absence or courtesy count as consent to a water claim.”

Sefa laid a hand on the blue cord. “No protocol may erase an obligation merely because the person who carries it is not in the room.”

Mara looked at the lowered rescue pennants and the dark line of weather. “And no one loses the right to refuse because the clock is cruel.”

02 · What an Empty Place Holds

They moved the council into the old sail loft, where the floor could be marked without the tide washing the marks away. Each side drew its political language before trying to translate it.

Mara drew the island rule first: a filled chair meant presence and welcome; a signature bound only its signer; an empty chair could not vote; every affected water cooperative kept its own seal.

Sefa drew the maritime rule beside it. An empty chair kept a burden visible. Sitting there meant carrying its question until refusal or plan—not agreeing to perform the duty, as Nilo's translation had implied.

“So we did not grant the reefs?” Mara asked.

Pol's expression did not soften. “You accepted the duty to answer our claim. Then your clerks entered the chairs in your ledger as votes in favour.”

Tal turned on the clerks. They showed him the council rule: any visiting delegation seated at the central table counted toward quorum. The island system had transformed a maritime question into a local vote.

“Two systems made the mistake together,” Illuminara said.

“A convenient mistake,” Iri replied. “Their cisterns are low. Their fleet needs water.”

Pol stepped forward. “Yes. We need water. Do not hide that fact inside your accusation, as if need makes every word a trick.”

The storm buoy pulsed again. Eleven hours and forty minutes remained before the compact expired.

Illuminara asked for the explanation each side least wanted. Iri named strategic ambiguity; Pol named the islands' habit of calling unfamiliar limits misunderstandings.

“Both mechanisms can be real,” Illuminara said. “Different meanings can cause an error, and strategic pressure can decide who benefits from leaving it uncorrected.”

On sailcloth she sealed two predictions before a test was chosen: translation alone would yield agreement without concession; pressure alone would survive clearer words; both together would stabilise meaning but leave the resource dispute visible.

Sefa studied the predictions. “How do we test without letting the test bind the reefs?”

“With something small enough to reverse and real enough to disappoint someone.”

Mara pointed to a drifting rescue marker. Island pilots wanted it inside the eastern channel; ocean navigators needed turning room outside. It was reversible, but its position decided which boats waited in the storm.

“A live dispute,” Mara said. “Not a lesson.”

“Before anything binds,” Illuminara said, “each side restates the other's meaning within three minutes. One objection, and nothing binds.”

Pol glanced at the empty chairs. “Who measures the three minutes?”

Iri set her mechanical kitchen timer on the floor. “Someone whose wells are at stake and whose clock cannot be persuaded.”

03 · The First Vocabulary Fails

Both peoples used bowls in public decisions: island green meant assent, red refusal, white a question. The ocean nation placed answers beside filled chairs and questions before empty ones.

Mara set the rescue marker on a chart. Sefa put a white bowl before an empty chair. Mara placed green beside her own. “We agree to discuss it.” The navigators began rolling up the chart.

Nilo closed his eyes. “They think you accepted their position.”

“I accepted the question.”

“A question belongs before the empty chair,” Sefa said. “Green beside you is an answer.”

Their first protocol had failed before the timer turned. Pol laughed without pleasure. “We invented a clearer way to make the same mistake.”

Tal demanded written clauses. Sefa refused to make crews who read wind knots and water marks depend on shore-trained lawyers.

Illuminara removed the bowls. “One colour is carrying both the type of speech and its force.” She asked Nilo to translate three statements separately: I heard you; I can predict what you will do; I agree that you may do it.

The island language separated them. In the ocean council register, the last two shared a verb whose image was to set one's weight behind a mast. Nilo had translated it as agree in nine treaties. He also admitted that ocean readers treated at least four island attendance signatures as promises.

Mara put the chairs against the wall. “No more compressed meanings.”

They kept white for questions, blue for restatement and red for refusal, then proposed pouring water together for consent.

Iri rejected it. “Water as the sign of consent in a water dispute is coercive theatre.” Pol agreed: a crew with an empty cistern should not pour away what it needed to say yes. The second design failed.

Sefa offered her vessel's brass disk. Mara threaded blue sailcloth through its centre so consent would be both sounded and seen, but only after mutual restatement.

They had built no agreement. They had made two bad agreements impossible.

04 · Three Minutes at the Red Marker

At eleven forty-one they carried the protocol to the harbour mouth, where the marker rolled between a paired route for island boats and a turn wide enough for rescue vessels but across a fishing lane. The storm warning changed to approach; nursery boats in the corridor reported squalls.

Sefa made the first proposal: move the marker one hundred and twenty metres east until the last nursery vessel entered harbour, then restore it before the evening fishing return.

Mara placed white before the empty chair, Sefa placed blue beside Mara, and Iri turned the timer.

Mara said, “You are asking us to close half the island channel for six hours so your deep vessels can turn. Our small boats may still enter one at a time under pilot control. At eighteen hundred the marker returns, whether or not every rescue vessel has arrived.”

Sefa answered before the third minute. “Almost. The return condition is the last nursery vessel, not the clock. If one is late, we keep the channel.”

The timer rang. No consent.

Tal exhaled sharply. “Then the test proves your proposal is unacceptable.”

“It proves the proposal was not yet understood,” Illuminara said. “Refusal comes after understanding, not in place of it.”

Mara opened a second round. “Then I refuse an unlimited delay. My counterproposal: the marker moves for six hours. A late nursery vessel takes the northern entrance with two island pilots. You may refuse that risk.”

Sefa placed the blue bowl. Pol turned the timer.

“You will move the marker east now,” Sefa said. “At eighteen hundred it returns. If a nursery vessel remains outside, two pilots from your harbour—not ours—board it beyond the reef and bring it through the northern entrance. The island council accepts the pilot risk; the vessel's master keeps the right to refuse the northern route.”

Mara nodded once.

Mara placed blue for the return restatement, and the timer began.

“You accept the six-hour move,” Mara said, “but you do not surrender command of a late vessel. Its master may wait outside or choose the northern route after the island pilots report conditions. Refusing the route does not cancel rescue cooperation.”

Sefa pulled the blue bowl nearer.

Neither struck the disk. “You both understood,” Iri said. “Understanding is not consent,” they answered.

Sefa asked the nearest nursery master by radio whether the northern entrance was acceptable. The answer came through static: acceptable only if the outer wave sensor stayed below two point four metres.

The island pilots agreed to that threshold. Iri wrote it beside the timer. Only then did Mara and Sefa pull the blue cloth through the brass disk together. It rang once, and the marker crew started its engine.

The test had taken nineteen minutes. At exactly noon the six-hour move began. Island skiffs queued but did not stop; two deep rescue vessels turned without crossing the fishing lane. Every participant could say what would end the arrangement.

Pol watched the marker settle into its new position. “The words worked.”

Iri shook her head. “The limits worked.”

“Both,” Illuminara said. “Now ask whether they still work when the answer costs water.”

05 · The Need Inside the Misunderstanding

Sefa opened the sealed cistern records. Hail had destroyed three flotillas' roof catchments. The driest held one hundred and eight thousand litres—nine days at twelve thousand daily—and needed another one hundred and forty-four thousand to reach twenty-one days.

Pol's log showed he had asked about reef water twelve days before the chairs were filled. “Even with perfect understanding, we would still need an answer.” The marker test proved translation mattered; the dated request proved pressure predated the mistake. Both sealed predictions survived.

Iri spread maps of the sweet reefs. A single emergency draw would not poison them. A permanent channel might. The claim recorded by the island ledger granted seasonal access without a volume limit.

“So the misunderstanding was real,” Mara said, “and the advantage was real.”

“Yes,” Sefa said. “Do not make us choose which truth lets you respect us.”

Tal proposed water from the main island reservoirs in exchange for abandoning every reef claim. The freshwater cooperatives refused; their reservoirs were already rationed. Pol proposed a temporary channel at the northern reef. Iri refused; the first cut would create a salt path no treaty could close.

The compact had five hours and six minutes left.

Illuminara asked what each side could lose without losing itself. The question angered everyone, which was why she kept it.

The ocean nation could share one eastern-shoal fishing season; the islands could lend six drought condensers if the flotillas supplied crews and sailcloth. Fishing paid for risk, not water.

Illuminara found two entries everyone else had read separately: the flotillas expected to discard ninety-six thousand litres of salt-dusted first rain at the storm's lowest forecast, and their mast inventory listed forty-three spare resin seals. She first suggested sealing the collection mouths; Iri refused because trapped salt would foul them. Illuminara then asked whether the island cooperatives' charcoal-and-fibre first-flush gates could take the mast resin instead: replaceable fibre would hold the salt while water passed through. Iri cut a test groove; Sefa surrendered one seal from her own vessel. Under brine pressure it held.

The children counted forty gates while Iri and Pol checked the ledger aloud. Six condensers guaranteed thirty-six thousand litres in forty-eight hours; the gated first rain supplied at least ninety-six thousand; a separately sealed surface reserve was capped at thirty thousand. One hundred and sixty-two thousand against a one-hundred-and-forty-four-thousand shortfall: twelve and a half percent spare. The shared fishing season paid for equipment and risk; it was not entered in the water column.

Pol objected. “We surrender a season on a forecast.”

Iri replied, “We lend drought equipment on your records. Risk is not disrespect merely because it is mutual.”

Pol looked toward Sefa. She did not rescue him from the choice.

The low-stakes marker test had separated meaning from assent. Now the strategic dispute stood bare: who would carry the uncertainty of rain.

Illuminara marked her design's weakest line: swollen resin could jam the gates. Iri required a six-hour soak of three seals; Sefa accepted the delay but capped the contribution at forty. Illuminara revised the plan. Need became measurable enough to negotiate.

Mara placed the white bowl before an empty chair. “Question open: will you accept the equipment plan, the shared fishing season and the sealed surface reserve instead of any reef claim?”

Sefa placed the blue bowl. “First, each side predicts the other's obligations. Three minutes.”

06 · No Silence Is a Yes

They needed four rounds. The first added safe lanes to the fishing access; the second put two cooperative seals between a failed storm and any reserve release.

At seventeen thirty-one, in the third round, Mara said, “The reef claim is withdrawn.” Pol placed the red bowl between them. “No. A withdrawal says we possessed a right that you later took away. Your courts could call us violators for returning; ours could demand compensation for losing it. I will not bind rescue to a false history.”

Iri turned the timer. Mara answered, “You say no reef right ever formed because the chairs could not consent. We record the ledger entry as void, not revoked. You may return to the water question, but returning creates no answer.”

Pol reached for the blue cloth, then stopped. “And the duty held by the empty chair?” The timer rang before Mara could answer. Nothing bound. They had spent three minutes and lost the round.

On the restart, Nilo restated the chair itself. “It keeps a question visible. It grants no right and no answer. No person may fill it for another. Removing it records only that the question has been answered, refused or explicitly deferred.”

Iri added, “And absence never counts as any of those.”

Sefa said, “Correct.”

At seventeen forty-one, the freshwater cooperatives approved the equipment plan one by one, each keeping a red bowl within reach. The fifth marked assent only after reducing the surface reserve assigned to its settlement. The ocean delegation accepted the change and increased the fishing share; an unanswered bowl or a vessel master's refusal would still bind no one and would not end rescue cooperation.

At seventeen forty-seven, the navigators approved. At seventeen fifty-two, eight minutes before expiry, Pol was last. He put his hand on the blue cloth, then stopped.

“If the rain fails and the reserve fails,” he said, “we return to the water question.”

Mara answered, “You may always return to the question. Returning does not create an answer.”

Pol looked at the empty chair. “Correct.”

He pulled.

The disk rang. Five cooperative bells answered from the harbour offices. On the vessels, the rescue pennants rose again.

No one sat in the empty chair.

07 · The Seats That Remained

One nursery vessel reached the roadstead at seventeen forty-six. Its master refused the northern entrance at a two-point-six-metre wave reading. Pilot Deva Orr moved her skiff from its unloading berth into the escort position; her crew would unload by hand after dark. At eighteen hundred the marker returned and the vessel entered the reopened eastern channel. Three later vessels followed under the renewed pact by eighteen thirty.

No one treated the refusal as a betrayal.

Rain came at dawn, hard enough to flatten the harbour flags. The new gates ran brown for nine minutes as their replaceable fibre loaded with salt, then clear. Children held lamps behind the filters and called the change. They recovered one hundred and one thousand four hundred litres; the six condensers supplied thirty-five thousand six hundred more over forty-eight hours.

The reefs remained uncut. With seven thousand litres still missing, Iri's cooperative chose to release eight thousand from the sealed reserve. Rain, condensers and reserve therefore supplied one hundred and forty-five thousand litres, leaving a one-thousand-litre margin. The driest flotilla held two hundred and fifty-three thousand litres: twenty-one days at its recorded draw, plus that margin.

In the eastern shoals, nets snagged and arguments multiplied. Red bowls appeared seven times; after six refusals, fishing continued elsewhere.

Nilo revised nine treaties, expanding compressed verbs into heard, predicted and consented. Attendance signatures moved to a different page from promises.

Mara kept the chairs in the sail loft. When visitors tried to fill them, a clerk placed a white bowl and explained that the seats held unanswered duties. Courtesy carried no legal force.

Sefa returned before the next rescue season. She brought the brass disk, its centre polished by many hands. At the council platform she found Illuminara examining a crack in one of the old green bowls.

“You did not give us a common language,” Sefa said.

“No,” Illuminara replied. “You built a way to discover when you did not have one.”

Across the lagoon, Iri and Pol were arguing over the placement of a new rain gauge. The timer stood between them. Neither looked pleased.

Sefa listened until the timer rang and both began again.

“And if the method fails?” she asked.

Illuminara set the cracked bowl aside rather than mending it. “Then the failure must be allowed to say no.”

The three chairs remained empty in the morning light. They no longer looked abandoned. Between them stood a white bowl, a strip of blue cloth and a red bowl no one had hidden.

Beyond the platform, rescue pennants and fishing markers moved on the same wind without becoming the same thing.

Na zadnjem svetu pred sezono neurij otoški naseljenci zasedejo tri stole, ki jih je oceanski narod pustil prazne — in odkrijejo, da je gostoljubje postalo privolitev v predajo grebenov, ki jim zagotavljajo pitno vodo. Ker se reševalni sporazum izteče ob sončnem zahodu, mora Illuminara obema ljudstvoma pomagati zgraditi jezik, v katerem odsotnost ne more privoliti, zavrnitev nekaj stane in vsaka obljuba prestane triminutni preizkus.

01 · Stoli, ki so jih zasedli

Svetovalna ploščad je stala tam, kjer je laguna iz zelenega peska prehajala v moder greben s pitno vodo in nato v črno morsko polico. Pet otoških delegacij je prineslo knjige, merilnike in vrče vode iz vodnjakov; oceanski narod je prispel v štiridesetih ozkih plovilih.

Med delegacijami so stali trije prazni stoli.

Svetnica Mara Vey je menila, da so stole pozabili. Morski ljudje so potovali šest dni; pustiti goste stati se ji je zdelo žaljivo. Prosila je dva pisarja, naj sedeta, sama pa je zasedla tretji stol in se nasmehnila navigatorki Sefi Koro.

Laguna je utihnila. Na najbližjem plovilu je medeninasti disk enkrat udaril ob krov.

Sefa ni sedla. Razvezala je modro vrvico na zapestju in jo položila čez mizo. Za njo je štirideset jamborov spustilo reševalne zastavice.

»Kaj sem storila?« je vprašala Mara.

Tolmač Nilo Esen je odgovoril prehitro. »Sprejeli ste odprte dolžnosti.«

»Katere dolžnosti smo sprejeli?«

Sefa je pokazala mimo ploščadi proti bledim polmesecem pod vodo. »Skrbništvo nad severnimi vodnjaki. Prehod skozi koralna vrata. Nujni odvzem iz treh sladkovodnih grebenov.«

Delegati za vodo so vstali. Ti grebeni so oskrbovali enajst tisoč ljudi; en slab kanal bi lahko zasolil pet naselij.

Mara je vstala s stola, kakor da jo je opekel. »Ničesar nismo sprejeli. Zasedli smo sedeže za goste.«

»Prazen sedež je dolžnost, ki ostane odprta, dokler je kdo ne prevzame,« je Sefa povedala prek Nila. »Vi ste prevzeli vse tri.«

»Pri nas prazen sedež pomeni dobrodošlico.«

»Potem si vaša dobrodošlica prisvaja naša neodgovorjena bremena.«

Nad mizo je nevihtna boja začela oddajati svoj urni utrip. Dva dni vzhodno se je oblikovala tropska stena. Ob osemnajstih, ko bi se sonce dotaknilo zunanje plitvine, bi se stari reševalni sporazum iztekel. Brez obnove otoški sveti ne bi mogli poveljevati globokogreznim plovilom oceanskega naroda, oceanski narod pa se brez ločenega dovoljenja vsakega naselja ne bi mogel zateči v otoška pristanišča.

Prvi vrtčevski čolni — družine, ki so se s sezono selile med flotilami — so opolnoči vstopili v nevihtni koridor. Osem jih je moralo prispeti do osemnajstih; še tri so pričakovali do osemnajst trideset, ko bi jih iztečeni sporazum lahko pustil brez usklajene pristaniške zaščite.

Guverner Tal Orri je odrinil otoško knjigo zahtevkov. »Noben dogovor, sklenjen s stolom, nas ne bo zavezoval.«

Sefin namestnik, navigator Pol Reka, je odgovoril: »Potem tudi noben dogovor, sklenjen z vašimi podpisi, ne bo zavezoval nas. Vaše oznake na naših plovilih ne govorijo.«

Illuminara je opazovala z roba ploščadi ob mehaničarki zadruge za sladko vodo Iri Vale. Dotaknila se ni ne stola ne knjige.

»Preden kdo razloži, kaj je hotela povedati druga stran,« je dejala, »poimenujte, o čem razlaga ne more odločiti.«

Iri je dvignila vrč čiste grebenske vode. »Noben protokol ne sme dovoliti, da molk, odsotnost ali vljudnost štejejo kot privolitev v zahtevo po vodi.«

Sefa je položila roko na modro vrvico. »Noben protokol ne sme izbrisati obveznosti samo zato, ker tistega, ki jo nosi, ni v prostoru.«

Mara je pogledala spuščene reševalne zastavice in temno črto vremena. »In nihče ne izgubi pravice do zavrnitve samo zato, ker je ura kruta.«

02 · Kaj skriva prazen sedež

Svet so preselili v staro podstrešje za jadra, kjer so lahko označevali tla, ne da bi plima odnesla oznake. Vsaka stran je narisala svoj politični jezik, preden ga je skušala prevesti.

Mara je prva narisala otoško pravilo: zaseden stol je pomenil prisotnost in dobrodošlico; podpis je zavezoval samo podpisnika; prazen stol ni mogel glasovati; vsaka prizadeta vodna zadruga je obdržala svoj pečat.

Sefa je ob njem narisala pomorsko pravilo. Prazen stol je ohranjal breme vidno. Sesti nanj je pomenilo nositi njegovo vprašanje do zavrnitve ali načrta — ne pa sprejeti dolžnosti, kakor je namigoval Nilov prevod.

»Torej jim nismo prepustili grebenov?« je vprašala Mara.

Polov izraz se ni omehčal. »Sprejeli ste dolžnost, da odgovorite na naš zahtevek. Nato pa sta vaša pisarja stole vpisala v vašo knjigo kot glasove za.«

Tal se je obrnil proti pisarjema. Pokazala sta mu pravilo sveta: vsaka gostujoča delegacija, ki je sedela za osrednjo mizo, se je štela v sklepčnost. Otoški sistem je pomorsko vprašanje spremenil v lokalno glasovanje.

»Napako sta skupaj ustvarila dva sistema,« je rekla Illuminara.

»Prikladno napako,« je odvrnila Iri. »Njihove cisterne so skoraj prazne. Njihova flota potrebuje vodo.«

Pol je stopil naprej. »Da. Potrebujemo vodo. Tega dejstva ne skrivajte v svoji obtožbi, kot da potreba vsako besedo spremeni v prevaro.«

Nevihtna boja je znova utripnila. Do izteka sporazuma je ostalo enajst ur in štirideset minut.

Illuminara je zahtevala razlago, ki si jo je vsaka stran najmanj želela. Iri je poimenovala strateško dvoumnost; Pol pa navado otočanov, da neznane omejitve razglasijo za nesporazume.

»Oba mehanizma sta lahko resnična,« je rekla Illuminara. »Različni pomeni lahko povzročijo napako, strateški pritisk pa lahko odloči, komu koristi, da se napaka ne popravi.«

Na jadrovino je zapečatila dve napovedi, še preden so izbrali preizkus: samo prevod bi prinesel sporazum brez popuščanja; sam pritisk bi preživel jasnejše besede; oboje skupaj bi ustalilo pomen, vendar bi spor glede virov ostal viden.

Sefa je preučila napovedi. »Kako naj to preizkusimo, ne da bi preizkus zavezal grebene?«

»Z nečim, kar je dovolj majhno, da ga lahko razveljavimo, in dovolj resnično, da bo koga razočaralo.«

Mara je pokazala na plavajočo reševalno oznako. Otoški piloti so jo hoteli v vzhodnem kanalu; oceanski navigatorji so potrebovali prostor za obračanje zunaj njega. Položaj so lahko spremenili nazaj, vendar je odločal, kateri čolni bodo čakali v neurju.

»Spor v živo,« je rekla Mara. »Ne učna ura.«

»Preden karkoli postane zavezujoče,« je rekla Illuminara, »vsaka stran v treh minutah s svojimi besedami ponovi pomen druge. En ugovor in nič ne velja.«

Pol je pogledal prazne stole. »Kdo meri tri minute?«

Iri je na tla postavila svojo mehansko kuhinjsko uro. »Nekdo, čigar vodnjaki so ogroženi in čigar ure ni mogoče prepričati.«

03 · Prvo besedišče odpove

Obe ljudstvi sta pri javnih odločitvah uporabljali sklede: otoška zelena je pomenila soglasje, rdeča zavrnitev, bela pa vprašanje. Oceanski narod je odgovore postavljal ob zasedene stole, vprašanja pa pred prazne.

Mara je reševalno oznako postavila na zemljevid. Sefa je pred prazen stol položila belo skledo. Mara je ob svojega položila zeleno. »Strinjamo se, da bomo o tem razpravljali.« Navigatorji so začeli zvijati zemljevid.

Nilo je zaprl oči. »Mislijo, da ste sprejeli njihov predlog.«

»Sprejela sem vprašanje.«

»Vprašanje sodi pred prazen stol,« je rekla Sefa. »Zelena ob vas je odgovor.«

Njihov prvi protokol je odpovedal, še preden se je ura zavrtela. Pol se je zasmejal brez veselja. »Izumili smo jasnejši način, da ponovimo isto napako.«

Tal je zahteval zapisane določbe. Sefa ni hotela, da bi bile posadke, ki berejo vetrne vozle in vodne oznake, odvisne od odvetnikov, izšolanih na kopnem.

Illuminara je odstranila sklede. »Ena barva nosi hkrati vrsto izjave in njeno moč.« Nila je prosila, naj ločeno prevede tri povedi: Slišal sem vas; lahko napovem, kaj boste storili; strinjam se, da to smete storiti.

Otoški jezik jih je ločeval. V registru oceanskega sveta sta zadnji dve uporabljali isti glagol, katerega podoba je bila nasloniti svojo težo na jambor. Nilo ga je v devetih pogodbah prevedel kot strinjati se. Priznal je tudi, da so oceanski bralci vsaj štiri otoške podpise navzočnosti razumeli kot obljube.

Mara je stole postavila ob steno. »Nič več stisnjenih pomenov.«

Belo so ohranili za vprašanja, modro za ponovitev pomena in rdečo za zavrnitev, nato pa so predlagali, da bi vodo skupaj izlili kot znak privolitve.

Iri je predlog zavrnila. »Voda kot znak privolitve v sporu o vodi je prisilno gledališče.« Pol se je strinjal: posadka s prazno cisterno ne bi smela izliti vode, ki jo potrebuje, da bi rekla da. Druga zasnova je odpovedala.

Sefa je ponudila medeninasti disk s svojega plovila. Mara je skozi njegovo sredino napeljala modro jadrovino, da bi se privolitev hkrati slišala in videla, vendar šele po medsebojni ponovitvi pomena.

Niso sklenili nobenega dogovora. Onemogočili so dva slaba dogovora.

04 · Tri minute pri rdeči oznaki

Ob enajst enainštirideset so protokol odnesli k vhodu v pristanišče, kjer se je oznaka zibala med dvojno potjo za otoške čolne in zavojem, dovolj širokim za reševalna plovila, a speljanim čez ribolovno pot. Nevihtno opozorilo se je spremenilo v približevanje; vrtčevski čolni v koridorju so poročali o sunkih.

Sefa je podala prvi predlog: oznako naj premaknejo sto dvajset metrov proti vzhodu, dokler zadnje vrtčevsko plovilo ne vpluje v pristanišče, nato pa naj jo vrnejo pred večerno vrnitvijo ribičev.

Mara je pred prazen stol položila belo, Sefa ob Maro modro, Iri pa je zagnala uro.

Mara je rekla: »Prosite nas, naj za šest ur zapremo polovico otoškega kanala, da se bodo vaša globokogrezna plovila lahko obrnila. Naši mali čolni bodo lahko še vedno vpluvali eden za drugim pod vodstvom pilota. Ob osemnajstih se oznaka vrne, ne glede na to, ali so prispela vsa reševalna plovila.«

Sefa je odgovorila pred tretjo minuto. »Skoraj. Pogoj za vrnitev je zadnje vrtčevsko plovilo, ne ura. Če katero zamuja, kanal ostane odprt za nas.«

Ura je zazvonila. Privolitve ni bilo.

Tal je ostro izdihnil. »Potem preizkus dokazuje, da je vaš predlog nesprejemljiv.«

»Dokazuje, da predlog še ni bil razumljen,« je rekla Illuminara. »Zavrnitev pride po razumevanju, ne namesto njega.«

Mara je odprla drugi krog. »Potem zavračam neomejeno zamudo. Moj nasprotni predlog: oznaka se premakne za šest ur. Zamujeno vrtčevsko plovilo uporabi severni vhod z dvema otoškima pilotoma. To tveganje lahko zavrnete.«

Sefa je položila modro skledo. Pol je zagnal uro.

»Oznako boste zdaj premaknili proti vzhodu,« je rekla Sefa. »Ob osemnajstih se vrne. Če vrtčevsko plovilo ostane zunaj, se dva pilota iz vašega pristanišča — ne naša — vkrcata nanj onkraj grebena in ga pripeljeta skozi severni vhod. Otoški svet sprejme tveganje pilotov; poveljnik plovila ohrani pravico zavrniti severno pot.«

Mara je enkrat prikimala.

Mara je položila modro za povratno ponovitev in ura se je zagnala.

»Sprejmete šesturni premik,« je rekla Mara, »vendar ne prepustite poveljstva nad zamujenim plovilom. Njegov poveljnik lahko počaka zunaj ali izbere severno pot, potem ko otoška pilota poročata o razmerah. Zavrnitev poti ne prekliče reševalnega sodelovanja.«

Sefa je modro skledo potegnila bližje.

Nobena ni udarila po disku. »Obe sta razumeli,« je rekla Iri. »Razumevanje ni privolitev,« sta odgovorili.

Sefa je po radiu vprašala poveljnika najbližjega vrtčevskega plovila, ali je severni vhod sprejemljiv. Skozi šumenje je prišel odgovor: sprejemljiv samo, če senzor zunanjih valov ostane pod dvema celima štirimi metra.

Otoška pilota sta se strinjala s tem pragom. Iri ga je zapisala ob uri. Šele nato sta Mara in Sefa skupaj potegnili modro tkanino skozi medeninasti disk. Enkrat je zazvonil in posadka oznake je zagnala motor.

Preizkus je trajal devetnajst minut. Natanko opoldne se je začel šesturni premik. Otoški čolni so čakali v vrsti, vendar se niso ustavili; dve globoki reševalni plovili sta se obrnili, ne da bi prečkali ribolovno pot. Vsak udeleženec je lahko povedal, kaj bo končalo dogovor.

Pol je opazoval, kako se oznaka umirja na novem položaju. »Besede so delovale.«

Iri je zmajala z glavo. »Omejitve so delovale.«

»Oboje,« je rekla Illuminara. »Zdaj vprašajte, ali še vedno delujejo, ko odgovor stane vodo.«

05 · Potreba v nesporazumu

Sefa je odprla zapečatene zapise cistern. Toča je uničila strešne zbiralnike treh flotil. Najbolj suha je imela sto osem tisoč litrov — devet dni po dvanajst tisoč na dan — in potrebovala še sto štiriinštirideset tisoč, da bi dosegla enaindvajset dni.

Polov dnevnik je pokazal, da je po vodi iz grebenov vprašal dvanajst dni, preden so zasedli stole. »Tudi ob popolnem razumevanju bi še vedno potrebovali odgovor.« Preizkus oznake je dokazal, da je prevod pomemben; datirana zahteva pa, da je pritisk obstajal pred napako. Obe zapečateni napovedi sta preživeli.

Iri je razgrnila zemljevide sladkovodnih grebenov. Enkraten nujni odvzem jih ne bi zastrupil. Trajni kanal bi jih lahko. Zahtevek, ki ga je zabeležila otoška knjiga, je dovoljeval sezonski dostop brez količinske omejitve.

»Torej je bil nesporazum resničen,« je rekla Mara, »in korist je bila resnična.«

»Da,« je rekla Sefa. »Ne silite nas, da izberemo, katera resnica vam dovoljuje, da nas spoštujete.«

Tal je predlagal vodo iz glavnih otoških zbiralnikov v zameno za opustitev vseh zahtevkov po grebenih. Zadruge za sladko vodo so zavrnile; njihovi zbiralniki so bili že pod omejitvami. Pol je predlagal začasni kanal pri severnem grebenu. Iri je zavrnila; prvi rez bi ustvaril slano pot, ki je nobena pogodba ne bi mogla zapreti.

Do izteka sporazuma je ostalo pet ur in šest minut.

Illuminara je vprašala, kaj lahko vsaka stran izgubi, ne da bi izgubila samo sebe. Vprašanje je razjezilo vse, zato ga je obdržala.

Oceanski narod bi lahko delil eno ribolovno sezono na vzhodni plitvini; otoki bi lahko posodili šest sušnih kondenzatorjev, če bi flotile prispevale posadke in jadrovino. Ribolov bi plačal tveganje, ne vode.

Illuminara je povezala dva vnosa, ki so ju vsi drugi brali ločeno: flotile so pri najnižji napovedi neurja pričakovale, da bodo zavrgle šestindevetdeset tisoč litrov prvega dežja, onesnaženega s soljo, njihov popis jamborov pa je navajal triinštirideset rezervnih smolnih tesnil. Najprej je predlagala, da bi zatesnili odprtine zbiralnikov; Iri je zavrnila, ker bi ujeta sol onesnažila sistem. Illuminara je nato vprašala, ali bi ogljeno-vlaknasta vrata otoških zadrug za prvi izpust lahko namesto tega uporabila smolo z jamborov: zamenljivo vlakno bi zadržalo sol, voda pa bi stekla skozenj. Iri je izrezala preizkusni utor; Sefa je odstopila eno tesnilo s svojega plovila. Pod pritiskom slanice je zdržalo.

Otroci so prešteli štirideset vrat, medtem ko sta Iri in Pol na glas preverjala knjigo. Šest kondenzatorjev je v oseminštiridesetih urah zagotavljalo šestintrideset tisoč litrov; vrata za prvi dež najmanj šestindevetdeset tisoč; ločeno zapečatena površinska rezerva pa je bila omejena na trideset tisoč. Sto dvainšestdeset tisoč proti primanjkljaju sto štiriinštirideset tisoč: dvanajst in pol odstotka rezerve. Skupna ribolovna sezona je plačala opremo in tveganje; v stolpec vode je niso vpisali.

Pol je ugovarjal. »Sezono predamo zaradi napovedi.«

Iri je odgovorila: »Sušno opremo posodimo na podlagi vaših zapisov. Tveganje ni nespoštovanje samo zato, ker je vzajemno.«

Pol je pogledal proti Sefi. Ni ga rešila pred izbiro.

Preizkus oznake z majhnim vložkom je ločil pomen od privolitve. Zdaj je strateški spor stal razgaljen: kdo bo nosil negotovost dežja.

Illuminara je označila najšibkejšo točko svoje zasnove: nabrekla smola bi lahko zablokirala vrata. Iri je zahtevala šesturno namakanje treh tesnil; Sefa je sprejela zamudo, vendar omejila prispevek na štirideset. Illuminara je načrt popravila. Potreba je postala dovolj merljiva za pogajanje.

Mara je belo skledo položila pred prazen stol. »Odprto vprašanje: ali boste namesto kakršnega koli zahtevka po grebenih sprejeli načrt opreme, skupno ribolovno sezono in zapečateno površinsko rezervo?«

Sefa je položila modro skledo. »Najprej vsaka stran napove obveznosti druge. Tri minute.«

06 · Noben molk ni da

Potrebovali so štiri kroge. Prvi je ribolovnemu dostopu dodal varne poti; drugi je med neuspešno nevihto in kakršno koli sprostitvijo rezerve postavil dva zadružna pečata.

Ob sedemnajst enaintrideset je Mara v tretjem krogu rekla: »Zahtevek po grebenih je umaknjen.« Pol je med njiju postavil rdečo skledo. »Ne. Umik pomeni, da smo imeli pravico, ki ste nam jo pozneje odvzeli. Vaša sodišča bi nas lahko ob vrnitvi razglasila za kršitelje; naša bi lahko zahtevala odškodnino za njeno izgubo. Reševanja ne bom vezal na lažno zgodovino.«

Iri je zagnala uro. Mara je odgovorila: »Pravite, da pravica do grebenov ni nikoli nastala, ker stoli niso mogli privoliti. Vnos v knjigi zabeležimo kot ničen, ne kot preklican. K vprašanju vode se lahko vrnete, toda vrnitev ne ustvari odgovora.«

Pol je segel po modri tkanini, nato pa se ustavil. »Kaj pa dolžnost, ki jo drži prazni stol?« Ura je zazvonila, preden je Mara utegnila odgovoriti. Nič ni postalo zavezujoče. Porabili so tri minute in izgubili krog.

Ob ponovnem začetku je Nilo s svojimi besedami ponovil pomen samega stola. »Vprašanje ohranja vidno. Ne podeljuje ne pravice ne odgovora. Nihče ga ne sme zasedeti namesto drugega. Njegova odstranitev zabeleži samo, da je bilo vprašanje odgovorjeno, zavrnjeno ali izrecno odloženo.«

Iri je dodala: »In odsotnost nikoli ne šteje kot karkoli od tega.«

Sefa je rekla: »Pravilno.«

Ob sedemnajst enainštirideset so zadruge za sladko vodo eno za drugo odobrile načrt opreme, vsaka z rdečo skledo na dosegu roke. Peta je privolitev označila šele, ko so zmanjšali površinsko rezervo, dodeljeno njenemu naselju. Oceanska delegacija je sprejela spremembo in povečala ribolovni delež; neodgovorjena skleda ali zavrnitev poveljnika plovila še vedno ne bi zavezovala nikogar in ne bi končala reševalnega sodelovanja.

Ob sedemnajst sedeminštirideset so navigatorji odobrili načrt. Ob sedemnajst dvainpetdeset, osem minut pred iztekom, je bil Pol zadnji. Položil je roko na modro tkanino, nato pa se ustavil.

»Če dež odpove in odpove rezerva,« je dejal, »se vrnemo k vprašanju vode.«

Mara je odgovorila: »K vprašanju se lahko vedno vrnete. Vrnitev ne ustvari odgovora.«

Pol je pogledal prazen stol. »Pravilno.«

Potegnil je.

Disk je zazvonil. Iz pristaniških uradov je odgovorilo pet zadružnih zvonov. Na plovilih so se reševalne zastavice znova dvignile.

Nihče ni sedel na prazen stol.

07 · Sedeži, ki so ostali

En vrtčevski čoln je dosegel sidrišče ob sedemnajst šestinštirideset. Njegov poveljnik je pri odčitku valov dva cela šest metra zavrnil severni vhod. Pilotka Deva Orr je svoj čoln premaknila z razkladalnega priveza na spremljevalni položaj; njena posadka bo po temi raztovarjala ročno. Ob osemnajstih se je oznaka vrnila in plovilo je vstopilo v znova odprti vzhodni kanal. Tri poznejša plovila so do osemnajst trideset sledila po obnovljenem sporazumu.

Nihče zavrnitve ni obravnaval kot izdajo.

Ob zori je prišel dež, dovolj močan, da je polegel pristaniške zastave. Nova vrata so devet minut izpuščala rjavo vodo, medtem ko se je njihovo zamenljivo vlakno polnilo s soljo, nato je pritekla bistra. Otroci so za filtri držali svetilke in oznanili spremembo. Zbrali so sto en tisoč štiristo litrov; šest kondenzatorjev je v oseminštiridesetih urah zagotovilo še petintrideset tisoč šeststo.

Grebeni so ostali nedotaknjeni. Ker je še vedno manjkalo sedem tisoč litrov, se je Irijina zadruga odločila sprostiti osem tisoč iz zapečatene rezerve. Dež, kondenzatorji in rezerva so tako zagotovili sto petinštirideset tisoč litrov, s tisoč litri presežka. Najbolj suha flotila je imela dvesto triinpetdeset tisoč litrov: enaindvajset dni pri zabeleženi porabi in še ta presežek.

Na vzhodnih plitvinah so se mreže zatikale in prepiri množili. Rdeče sklede so se pojavile sedemkrat; po šestih zavrnitvah se je ribolov nadaljeval drugje.

Nilo je popravil devet pogodb in stisnjene glagole razširil v slišano, napovedano in privoljeno. Podpise navzočnosti so premaknili na drugo stran od obljub.

Mara je stole obdržala v podstrešju za jadra. Ko so jih obiskovalci skušali zasesti, je pisar postavil belo skledo in razložil, da sedeži hranijo neodgovorjene dolžnosti. Vljudnost ni imela pravne moči.

Sefa se je vrnila pred naslednjo reševalno sezono. Prinesla je medeninasti disk, katerega sredino so zgladile številne roke. Na svetovalni ploščadi je našla Illuminaro, ki je preučevala razpoko v eni od starih zelenih skled.

»Niste nam dali skupnega jezika,« je rekla Sefa.

»Ne,« je odgovorila Illuminara. »Zgradili ste način, kako odkriti, kdaj ga nimate.«

Na drugi strani lagune sta se Iri in Pol prepirala o postavitvi novega dežemera. Med njima je stala ura. Nobeden ni bil videti zadovoljen.

Sefa je poslušala, dokler ni ura zazvonila in sta oba začela znova.

»Kaj pa, če metoda odpove?« je vprašala.

Illuminara je počeno skledo odložila, namesto da bi jo popravila. »Potem mora biti neuspehu dovoljeno reči ne.«

Trije stoli so v jutranji svetlobi ostali prazni. Niso bili več videti zapuščeni. Med njimi so stali bela skleda, trak modre tkanine in rdeča skleda, ki je ni nihče skril.

Onkraj ploščadi so se reševalne zastavice in ribolovne oznake premikale v istem vetru, ne da bi postale ista stvar.
