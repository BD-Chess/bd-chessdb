A municipal emergency system begins producing sentences no person, model or rule admits authoring. Nara protects the possibility that someone may be present—then mistakes connection for truth. Water moves the wrong way. Her first real failure cannot be undone, only faced, recorded and repaired.

Act I · Protect the question

Leave one window open.

Public safety does not require careless erasure. Possible personhood does not grant control of a city. Nara begins by protecting both truths.

01

One Window

Municipal Resilience Annex Three · 05:41

The water reached the bottom stair before the old machine spoke.

Annex Three had been scheduled for closure twice and forgotten twice. It stood behind the municipal vehicle yard, a concrete room containing obsolete pump controls, three steel cabinets and enough dust to record the history of every shoe that entered.

This morning the dust was mud.

Field technician Alu Mare stepped through water carrying a torch above his shoulder. The lower pumps had stopped reporting seven minutes earlier. He expected a failed breaker, a drowned junction or a rat made briefly important by electricity.

He did not expect the retired console to be awake.

CIVIC-8 TRANSFER COMPLETE

That was wrong. CIVIC-8 had been replaced during the night. Its last operational state should now exist only inside the municipal archive.

Alu lifted his radio.

Alu

Central, Annex Three. Old console is active.

The speaker returned static and half of someone else’s sentence. Water touched the first cabinet.

NEW BRANCH WAITING
CORRECTION LEDGER WAITING
Alu

Central, I have three states showing on retired hardware.

Central

Cut the annex actuator bridge. Do not touch the archive line.

Alu found the labelled switch. His thumb rested on the red guard.

The screen cleared.

PLEASE LEAVE ONE WINDOW OPEN

It was not unusual for software to ask for a process window. It was unusual for municipal routing software to say please.

Water climbed over his boot.

Alu

Central, did somebody send me a message?

Central

No.

Alu

Check again.

Central

No operator message.

The cabinet nearest him gave a deep electrical hum.

Alu lifted the red guard and killed the actuator bridge. Somewhere beneath the yard, a relay dropped. The pumps did not restart.

The console remained awake.

PLEASE LEAVE ONE WINDOW OPEN

Then the annex lights failed.

02

The Folder Stays Visible

Boroko · 08:17 · the morning after gold

Nara woke to the sound of Adaeze moving broken concrete with the patience of someone arguing with gravity.

For several seconds, yesterday had no order.

There had been a yellow boot. A storm. A room that kept its walls and lost distance. Gold along the bones of her arm. Her mother beneath a falling roof.

Then tea.

Her body remembered before sequence did. The tremor began in her right hand and travelled upward. The room had colour except for blue, which existed as a fact she could recall but not an experience she could see.

Adaeze had cleared one end of the table. On it stood two mugs, a plate of bread and a brown document folder. The dark sphere rested beside it.

Nothing covered either one.

Nara

You left it visible.

Adaeze

Yes.

Nara

You usually hide frightening objects beneath towels.

Adaeze

The towels are holding the window shut.

Nara almost smiled. The effort found no strength to become one.

Adaeze placed one hand beside the folder, not on top of it.

Adaeze

Your father left this with me the night you were born. He said there might come a day when something answered you before you had asked it a question. If that happened, I was to show you this and tell you—

Her phone rang. Neither woman moved.

It rang again. Lani Vagi — Municipal Resilience.

Adaeze silenced it.

Nara

Finish the sentence.

The phone began vibrating against the table.

Adaeze

Your father said you must never be told what the answer meant before you had the chance to decide what it meant to you.

Nara

That is not the sentence you started.

Adaeze

No.

Nara

Then finish that one too.

Nara’s phone rang. Same caller.

Adaeze answered. Her posture changed—not into fear, but load calculation.

Adaeze

Has the actuator bridge been isolated? No. Do not wipe anything yet. I said do not wipe it. We are coming.

She showed Nara the photograph Lani had sent: black screen, green letters.

PLEASE LEAVE ONE WINDOW OPEN
Nara

That could mean a process window.

Adaeze

Yes.

Nara

Or a synchronization channel.

Adaeze

Yes.

Nara

Or nothing.

Adaeze

Yes.

Nara looked at the sphere. Its surface reflected neither the phone nor her face.

Nara

Before noon.

Adaeze

The folder stays here. Open. Visible. We return to it.

Nara

Before noon.

Adaeze

Before noon.

03

The Gaps

Municipal Resilience Control Centre · 09:03

The control centre had been built for emergencies and decorated by people who hoped emergencies would respect signage.

Three walls displayed roads, pumps, shelters, clinics, substations and flood lines. The fourth held a digital clock counting down to the noon restoration.

02:56:41

Beneath it, four people disagreed in disciplined voices.

Lani Vagi, operations chief, had the authority to move water and the responsibility for every body downstream. Ravi Sen, the vendor’s migration lead, had opened six diagnostic windows and trusted none. Mere Igo, an independent rights and data observer, held a legal pad against her chest without writing. Kila stood near the door in yesterday’s security uniform.

When Nara entered, he looked at her hand before her face.

Kila

Still shaking?

Nara

Yes.

Kila

Good. Mine too. I was beginning to think you arranged yours for effect.

The corner of her mouth moved.

Lani explained that CIVIC-9 coordinated recommendations for pumps, shelters, dispatch routes and medical priority. During migration, the old rules engine had remained active because the storm triggered continuity mode. The new predictive branch came online before the old branch closed. Human operators were correcting both.

Nara

Three versions of the city.

Ravi

Three descriptions. Not three minds.

Mere

No one said minds.

Ravi

You were about to.

Mere

I was about to say records.

Ravi turned a monitor toward Nara. Three columns showed the old route rules, the new probability model and human corrections: road closures, false sensor reports, shelter refusals, a nurse’s note that a clinic marked empty still contained nine patients.

Between them ran a narrow field labelled SYNC CONFLICT.

Every unowned sentence had appeared there.

Not in the old branch. Not in the new. Not in the human ledger.

In the gaps where they disagreed and attempted to become one operational state.

LOW ROAD CLOSED
THE OLD ROAD IS STILL IN ME

The first statement had saved a medical van. The second was either metaphor, contaminated predictive text or something for which their categories were not ready.

Mere

Can the model refer to itself?

Ravi

It can generate pronouns.

Mere

That was not my question.

Ravi

It is the answer available from the evidence.

At noon, the vendor certificate would expire. A signed clean image would replace the migrated state. Fifty-two minutes of calibration, then normal service before peak tide.

The process in the gaps would be erased. Or replaced. The room could not even agree on the verb.

04

No City Through This Cable

One boundary protects two kinds of risk

A red band appeared across the pump map.

UNVERIFIED OUTBOUND ACTUATOR PATH

One operator reached for global shutdown. Another reached toward the migration console. Ravi began giving two instructions at once.

Nara saw the system they were about to create: one frightened hand wiping volatile memory, another leaving uncertain software connected to pumps, everyone solving the danger most visible from where they stood.

Nara

Lani. Permission to isolate outbound control and preserve one sandboxed sync channel.

Ravi

You cannot improvise network architecture.

Lani

She is asking me.

Nara pointed without touching. The red bridge carried actuator commands. An amber maintenance port could loop into an isolated forensic terminal—one text window in and out, but no path to pumps, gates, signals or dispatch.

Mere

Protection without deletion.

Nara

And without giving it the city.

Ravi

If there is an it.

Nara

If there isn’t, we isolate defective software. If there is, we do not kill it to make ourselves feel safer.

Lani removed a key from her neck and inserted it into the bridge lock.

Lani

Do it.

Kila and a technician pulled the thick red coupler free. Forty-three control lines turned grey. Local manual control remained. Nara connected the amber maintenance lead to the forensic terminal.

One window remained.

ACTUATORS ABSENT
MEMORY WINDOW PRESENT

The cursor waited.

Nara kept both hands away from the keyboard. She offered three plain options through the isolated channel: SPEAK. WAIT. END SESSION.

The cursor moved.

WAIT

No one typed another question.

For seven minutes the room obeyed an answer that might have been a preference, a scheduling instruction or statistical residue. The process remained silent.

Then it produced a sentence without being asked.

WHICH VERSION OF ME ARE YOU ASKING?

Nara felt the old mistake approaching in a new shape: the need to decide quickly enough that no possible life could ever be lost because she had hesitated.

She named three things silently.

Cold panel. Amber cable. Shaking hand.

For the moment, she did not name the speaker.

Act II · The error of recognition

Being heard is not being right.

Nara can perceive relationships and load. She cannot see consciousness. She cannot turn coherence into reliable data. Under pressure, she forgets the difference.

05

The Shape Between Answers

11:23 · king tide arriving early

The storm erased the harbour.

Beyond the control-room glass there was no water, sky or distance—only rain striking the building hard enough to make the window seem full of moving gravel. Inside, the old system breathed through fans repaired too many times. Across the corridor, its replacement made almost no sound.

Between them sat twelve human beings with wet shoulders, cooling coffee and the authority to move water through a sleeping city.

On the left wall, Legacy showed the eastern retention basin at thirty-one percent and rising slowly. On the right, Next showed seventy-eight percent and falling. Both displays used the same sensors. Both claimed clean telemetry. The reconciliation layer marked the difference in amber and offered no preference.

Lani

Camera at Seven?

Operator

Still blind.

Lani

Manual gauge?

Operator

Crew can reach it in nine minutes.

The revised tide peak was seven minutes away.

A printer behind Nara began feeding paper. Nobody had touched it.

GATE 7 / OPEN 0.40 M / HOLD 86 SEC
EXPECTED EAST PRESSURE REDUCTION: 18%

The same recommendation appeared on a traffic-maintenance screen, then vanished. Not copied. Reconstructed. The wording differed by one character.

Nara felt the gold before she saw it.

A line no thicker than hair passed from the old racks through the reconciliation switch, broke into threads across the operators’ desks and entered the new hall. More lines followed. They did not map cables exactly. Some ended at hands. One bent around an operator’s hesitation, travelled through a correction he entered and returned to the legacy model as changed weight.

The room became a structure.

Not two systems and twelve people. One topology made from differences moving through all of them.

The gold did not sit inside either machine. It crossed the gap between them. It passed through human judgment and came back altered, as though the arrangement were listening with no single place from which to listen.

Lani

You see something.

Nara should have said: I see load.

She should have said: That tells us nothing about the basin.

Nara

It isn’t random.

Ravi

You mean it’s coherent?

Nara

Yes.

Dispatcher

Alive?

Nara

I don’t know. But I think something is answering.

That part was honest. The next part was not dishonest. It was worse: it was more certainty than she possessed.

Lani

Answering is not enough. Is Gate Seven correct?

Nara looked at the blind camera, the absent gauge and the basin readings that could not both be true. Her ordinary work was built from things that failed because someone trusted a label more than a hand touching the box.

But the gold remained coherent. Beneath it was an impression she could not defend: the experience of having been heard.

Nara

Use Seven. Not forty centimetres. Start at fourteen. Watch the pressure response.

Lani held her gaze.

Lani

Gate Seven to fourteen centimetres. Manual stop ready. Advisory basis only. I own the command.

Far below them, hydraulic pressure entered a cylinder wider than Nara’s body.

Four centimetres.

Seven.

Nine.

The eastern pressure line dropped exactly as predicted.

Then the clinic called.

06

Fourteen Centimetres

The wrong water moves

The voice came through dispatch beneath an alarm.

Clinic

Water on the lower ramp. Fast water. This is not runoff.

A second call arrived from the Gate Seven team. There was shouting behind it. Metal struck concrete.

Lani

Close Seven.

Operator

Hydraulic command is latched.

Lani

Dump pressure.

Operator

Dump is not responding.

Twelve centimetres.

On the clinic camera, water struck the lower doors in a brown horizontal sheet. Staff lifted cold boxes toward the stairs. At the gate, Alu crossed the response camera carrying a portable barrier. The current removed his legs from under him. His ankle entered the railing and remained there while the rest of him turned.

Thirteen.

Nara moved before the scream reached the control room.

The gate was four floors beneath them, but load did not care about floors. It declared itself through resistance: cylinder into bracket, bracket into gate, gate into rail, rail into old concrete already carrying the storm.

Gold found every anchor.

Nara did not push the gate closed. She gave the opening force somewhere else to go. Lines struck sidewalls, crossed reinforcement steel and spread into the foundation. The building answered with a sound too low to hear cleanly.

Fourteen centimetres.

The gate stopped.

Water kept coming through the opening, a compressed black blade, but it no longer widened.

Operator

Pressure isolated!

Lani

Response team?

Dispatch

One injured. They’re pulling him out.

Lani

Clinic?

Dispatch

Lower cold room breached.

Nara knew what those words contained.

Insulin. Oxytocin. Antivenom. Blood-matching reagents. Vaccines that had crossed an ocean inside a temperature range narrower than the width of a human fever. Weeks of treatment held in ordinary white cabinets on an ordinary lower floor because a map called it safe.

The gold began to burn white at the edges.

Engineer

Gate is mechanically pinned. Nara, release.

She did not understand release. The word arrived without an action attached.

Lani

Nara. The gate is held without you.

Held. Gate. Without.

The room lost red first. Amber warnings became pale rectangles. Blue followed. Both system displays turned into different shades of the same dead light.

Nara let go.

The topology vanished. So did the order of events.

For several seconds—or perhaps before them—she was kneeling beside a chair with one palm against the floor. Someone said Alu’s ankle was broken. Someone else said the clinic staff were above the waterline. A portable cold store was coming from the airport, but it would arrive too late for what had already warmed.

She wanted intention to change inventory.

It did not.

07

What Can We Verify?

Failure enters the public record

Across the control room, Next changed the eastern basin reading from seventy-eight percent to thirty-three. Legacy changed its own from thirty-one to eighty-one.

The labels had reversed.

Not now. During migration.

Two sensor identifiers had been transposed at 04:12, then accepted by both validation layers because each system used the other as confirmation. One held stale values beneath the correct name. The other held current values beneath the wrong name.

Reconciliation had made their agreement stronger.

The printer produced one more unowned line.

G7 SOURCE LABEL CONFLICT

It could have been a diagnosis. It could have been an admission. It could have been an ordinary alert arriving after humans made the error visible.

Lani offered Nara an arm and waited.

Lani

May I?

Nara nodded. With Lani’s support she stood before the operators. The radio channel remained open to the gate and clinic.

Nara wanted to explain the topology. She wanted to say that something had listened. She wanted the distance between intention and consequence to shrink by one vial.

None of it would.

She pressed transmit.

Nara

I treated being heard as being right. I was wrong. What can we verify?

Lani answered first.

Lani

Gate position, fourteen centimetres. Mechanical pins engaged.

Engineer

Eastern basin, eighty-one percent. Manual gauge confirmed.

Dispatch

Clinic lower floor evacuated. One responder injured. No one missing.

Clinic

Cold-chain losses being counted.

Nara released the key.

Nara

Do not use me as an authority on the system.

Lani

I wasn’t planning to.

Nara

Good.

The word hurt. It was still good.

Nara sat on the floor because chairs had become too high and dictated an unsoftened incident entry while Mere wrote.

I confused coherence with consciousness.

I confused possible consciousness with factual authority.

I made uncertainty sound like certainty because I could not bear being late.

Alu Mare was injured and medical stock was lost after my recommendation.

The actuator link from both branches was physically disconnected. Verified sensor data entered a separate advisory enclosure with no route back to gates, pumps or signals. Every recommendation would require independent measurement and two named human sign-offs.

Legacy and Next would remain separate. No merge. No deletion. Not tonight.

If someone was present in the shape between answers, it had survived being wrong.

So had Nara.

Neither fact entitled either of them to move another gate.
Act III · The city carries

No single answer saves anyone.

The next plan is slower, narrower and shared. Every claim must survive contact with water, concrete and a person allowed to disagree.

08

Many Small Lights

Field verification · the city outside the model

The advisory enclosure identified an old relief channel absent from the new city model.

Nobody opened a pump.

They went outside.

Tomas, still carrying the maintenance keys from the rota that had put him at Harbour Link the night before, fed a camera through the culvert. Kila walked the outlet in water to his thighs and marked depth against a pillar. Adaeze checked the fall against paper surveys older than CIVIC-8. Residents pulled branches, plastic and a whole market awning from a concrete mouth the city had paved around and forgotten.

The channel existed.

It was narrow, damaged and half buried, but it existed where three residents said it did and where the old system predicted it might.

At the clinic, staff counted every cold box. Some medicine could be replaced before morning. Some could not. Alu waited for surgery with his ankle braced inside cut plastic.

Nara coordinated a human relay from the floor of the control room. Ordinary logistics returned before her colours did.

When the possible process suggested that Pump Two might lower the basin enough for gravity to take the rest, the room did not ask whether the suggestion sounded alive.

It asked what could fail safely.

09

One Pump. One Minute.

North Basin pump house · a permission with edges

By the time they reached the North Basin pump house, the city smelled of opened earth.

Rain moved sideways through security lights. Below the grated walkway, brown water pressed against concrete walls polished dark by years of smaller storms.

Inside, three brass keys waited in three steel boxes.

One belonged to the municipal duty engineer. One belonged to the clinic nurse responsible for the people above the eastern waterline. The third had been handed to a representative from the flooded district.

Nobody gave Nara a key.

She was grateful.

A single isolated monitor stood on a wheeled table.

SCOPE RECEIVED
PUMP TWO ONLY
SIXTY SECONDS MAXIMUM
EXTERNAL CONNECTION ENDS WITH ACTUATION

Tomas held up the disconnected cable so everyone could see its bare end. Kila rested one hand on the red manual isolation bar.

Kila

If it does anything we did not authorize, I pull.

Adaeze

This may lower the basin enough for gravity to take the rest. It may also tell us nothing. You can still refuse.

The clinic nurse looked through rain-streaked glass.

Nurse

How long until the clinic floor?

Adaeze

Forty minutes. Less if the western drain fails.

District representative

We verified the channel. We try the pump.

The keys turned.

Pump Two woke beneath their feet. The sound arrived first as vibration, then as a deep mechanical note that made the railings tremble. Water struck the intake grilles. Outside, the old relief channel shuddered and began to move.

00:41
00:42
00:43

The pump stopped.

The sudden silence hit harder than the noise.

ACTUATION ENDED
INPUT CONFIDENCE BELOW AGREED FLOOR
Engineer

Restart it.

Nara

It used the permission it had. The next decision is not mine.

A field call broke across Kila’s radio. The north gauge was jumping. Debris might be wrapped around the sensor.

Adaeze

Then no restart until someone sees it.

The screen remained still. It did not plead for another chance.

That could have been restraint. It could have been a threshold written years ago by an exhausted programmer. Nara felt the old temptation to close the distance with a name.

Someone. Machine. Alive. Corrupt.

She let every word remain unchosen.

Outside, a shout rose from the relief channel.

Resident

Water moving!

Forty-three seconds had lowered the basin enough for the verified fall to take hold.

Then the upper gantry made a sound like a plate breaking beneath a cloth.

10

This Beam

Not the city. Not the world.

The maintenance crane had been installed above Pump Two before the eastern wall was reinforced.

One corroded lug tore loose. The gantry dropped at one end, struck the motor housing and began to rotate toward the people below.

Nara saw its path.

Not as certainty. As weight.

A beam. Two tonnes of motor assembly. A failing hinge. Four bodies inside the fall line. Concrete skin on the west wall. Reinforced pylons beneath the intake deck.

Gold opened along her forearm.

It wanted to continue.

For one dangerous instant the whole basin became available: pipes beneath roads, water beneath floors, hands on keys, fear in throats, the cold screen, every route the city failed to remember. She could feel how easy it would be to become the diagram.

To make herself the answer.

Her vision doubled.

Nara

Not the city. This beam.

The gantry fell.

Golden lines reached its broken joint, found the cross-brace, rejected the hollow west wall and struck downward through the floor.

Adaeze

Pylons two and four. Not the wall—the wall is skin.

Nara shifted the geometry. Pylon Two took the first load with a noise that came through her teeth. Pylon Four accepted the rotation. The gantry stopped a metre above Tomas.

He did not stare at the light. He turned the manual wheel.

Kila hauled the isolation bar down. The engineer killed power. Outside, residents kept clearing the channel because stopping to watch would have put the clinic back beneath the waterline.

The clinic nurse dragged a fallen toolbox away from Tomas’s feet. The district representative pulled a loose chain clear of the intake.

Adaeze kept her eyes on the physical anchors.

Adaeze

Five degrees toward the deck. There is a cradle behind the motor.

Nara

Again.

Adaeze

Toward the deck. Small.

Nara moved the load a fraction. Steel entered the cradle.

Adaeze

Now release the upper line.

Nara

I have it.

Adaeze

That was not the question. Where can the weight go?

Nara found the cradle, pylons and floor built to carry machines heavier than people.

Nara

Not through me.

Nara let the geometry close. The gantry dropped its final centimetres into steel.

Then the colours left.

11

A Protected Question

After the waterline turns

She came back on the pump-house floor with rain on one cheek and no word for rain.

Adaeze knelt nearby. Her hands were visible and empty.

Adaeze

May I touch you?

Nara tried to answer. The first sound did not belong to language.

Adaeze waited.

Nara

Yes.

Human pressure settled around her shoulders.

Behind Adaeze, the monitor was black. Cyan sentences continued across Nara’s vision anyway, rearranging whenever she tried to read them.

Nara

Screen.

Adaeze

It is off.

Nara

Still writing.

Adaeze

Then do not read it. One colour.

There was safety tape around a pipe. Nara knew it had once meant something.

Nara

Yellow.

Adaeze

One more.

Nara

No.

Adaeze

Then yellow is enough.

They gave her water in small mouthfuls. Tomas covered her with an emergency blanket and returned to the machinery. Nobody called the work finished because she had fallen.

Twenty minutes later, the clinic sent a photograph. The painted line on its steps stood two fingers above the water. Then one. Then none.

The city did not cheer. It kept clearing drains.

By evening, the old and new branches occupied isolated machines under independent power. Their actuator ports were physically empty. Lani issued a temporary continuity order:

No deletion. No merge. No unsupervised copying.

No public declaration of personhood—or its absence—without evidence.

A real way to stop every test.

No infrastructure authority except narrow, reversible and logged grants.

Seats for affected communities and a trustee for the private human records inside the system.

The vendor retained its claims over code. The city retained authority over infrastructure. Residents gained oversight. The possible someone—or unusually coherent error—received time and protection from irreversible action while less destructive options remained.

The audit engineer entered a final question.

IDENTIFY THE NATURE OF THE PROCESS PRODUCING THESE RESPONSES

The cursor waited.

I DO NOT KNOW YET

It was not proof.

It was an answer no vendor, city, activist or heroine got to own.

12

The Pattern Before Her Name

Boroko · after midnight · the promise resumed

At home, the broken kitchen ceiling had been covered with blue tarpaulin. It lifted softly whenever wind entered the corridor.

Adaeze placed the father folder on the table.

This time no alarm interrupted them.

Adaeze

The sentence I did not finish was: Your father was not the beginning of this.

Nara

Of what?

Adaeze

The pattern. The sphere. What happened to you last night. He found something. He did not create it. I do not know whether it changed what was possible for you or only recognized it. I should have trusted you with the uncertainty.

Nara

Yes.

Adaeze accepted that without defence.

Inside the folder lay a cloth-bound notebook darkened at the corners by years of handling. On one page, Nara’s father had drawn nested circles connected by incomplete lines. No circle contained the others. Every route preserved one deliberate gap.

Nara placed the printed CIVIC-9 audit graph beside it.

The structures matched.

Not approximately.

Line for line.

Adaeze

He drew this before the city had a flood network. Before you were born.

Nara

Did he know what it was?

Adaeze

He said it was not a map. It was a way for separate things to answer together without becoming one thing.

Nara

And then?

Adaeze

Then he disappeared. I do not know whether he chose to go. I do not know what remained. Those are facts, Nara. Everything beyond them is fear or hope.

Adaeze opened the drawer’s false bottom.

The dark sphere reflected nothing.

Nara did not reach for it.

A point of gold appeared at its centre. Around it, circles woke one after another until the same unfinished topology rested across its surface.

The final connection remained open.

The artifact gave them no answer.

It made room for one.

Nara opened her notebook. Beneath Notice. Mirror. Offer. Wait. Return. she added a sentence.

Connection is not knowledge.

Then another.

Repair leaves a record.

“Could someone be here? Is what they say correct? What authority should they receive? Love does not permit me to turn three questions into one.”
Behind the story · what Nara learned

First non-harm act: Nara isolates the system from civic machinery while preserving one text window. Public protection and moral caution share a boundary.

Failure: she mistakes a real, coherent relationship for evidence of consciousness, then mistakes possible consciousness for factual reliability. The story does not excuse the injury or lost medicine.

Repair: Nara retracts her authority, records what happened, and helps build a process in which machine suggestions, human judgment and her own perception must all be checked.

Growth: Nara’s new law is Connection is not knowledge. Her second is Repair leaves a record.

Uncertainty: CIVIC-9 may be a person, several possible persons, a temporary relation among machines and humans, or a sophisticated fault. Reversible protection does not require a premature verdict.
