01 · Burns by the Wall

Sorrel Gate, upper terraces · 12:40

Smoke stood on the western ridge, six kilometres off and leaning toward them. The district had given Sorrel Gate until three o'clock. Illuminara had spent the morning walking the upper terraces with a tablet of creek gauges, which was what the regional water office had sent her to do, and she had stopped doing it at the first black patch.

There were forty-one of them now. Each was the size of a tablecloth. Each was still warm when she held her palm an inch above it. None lay in open grass. Every one lay on the sheltered side of a drystone wall or the lip of a terrace, and not one had crossed the stone.

Someone is counting the wind, she thought. Someone is lighting only where the wind has been refused.

Taavi Brandt came up the track with a drip torch hanging unlit from his belt and a young crew lead named Joss Marren a pace behind. He was fifty-odd and broad, with the stillness of a man who had learned to spend his words last.

Taavi BrandtSomebody has been lighting my hill since six this morning. Eleven fires I can account for. I'd put the rest on the same hand.

IlluminaraForty-one. I've walked them all.

Taavi BrandtThen you've walked forty-one places where a fire could have got away from a person. Constable Hale is at the church with an order for Ivet Corrandon. She's eighty-one, she won't open her door to my crews, and at dark I carry her down that road myself. I would sooner she walked.

IlluminaraLook at where they are, Chief. Not at who.

He looked, because he was fair and because she had said it quietly. He looked at the nearest patch and the wall above it, and for the length of a breath he said nothing.

Taavi BrandtCalder Fold. Nine years ago. I lit a backburn, lawful and signed, in a wind that behaved for four hours. In the fifth it veered and the fire took the track and then a barn, and there was a man asleep in the barn. Aldous Penn. Careful fires are the ones that kill people. The careless ones don't last long enough.

IlluminaraThen I won't argue that she's careful. I'll ask whether she's right, and look for an instrument that can answer without either of us having to believe anything. Let me speak to her first.

Taavi BrandtUntil two. At two the buses load, and she's on one or she's on my shoulder.

02 · What the Hill Was For

Corrandon cottage, kitchen · 13:15

The door was not locked. Illuminara knocked anyway, and a voice told her through the wood that it never was, that people simply believed it was, and that she could come in if she had the sense to wipe her boots.

Ivet Corrandon sat at the table with ash to the elbows, a match tin at her right hand and a blackthorn stick against her chair. Across her left palm ran a blister the width of a finger. She had the face of someone who had been right for a long time without anyone's help.

Ivet CorrandonYou're the water girl. They said a water girl was on my walls with a machine.

IlluminaraI'm a water-modeller. The machine is a tablet and mostly it counts creeks. May I sit?

Ivet CorrandonIf you've come to take me to the bus you can sit on the floor.

IlluminaraI've no authority to take you anywhere. I came to ask about the wall at the top of Pellam's field.

The old woman looked at her for some time. Illuminara took the clean cloth from the dresser while she waited and asked, with her eyes, about the hand. A question can wait and a blister cannot. Ivet let her do it, watching with open suspicion and then, slowly, with less.

Ivet CorrandonIn nineteen eighty-four a forestry man stood in this kitchen and told my Edrin the hill must not be burned, not by anyone, not ever, and he took the paper that said we could. Edrin was fined. He didn't speak for a week. Forty years we've lived under a hill with nothing to do but grow.

IlluminaraAnd you went on burning.

Ivet CorrandonSmall. At the end of the wet, on the lee of the walls. Edrin called it combing. A fire that comes over a ridge is hungry, and it eats everything between itself and the next thing to eat. Comb the lee of a wall black and the headfire reaches the stone, looks over, and finds nothing for its mouth. A wall with no black behind it is only a stack of stones.

IlluminaraThen why not tell the chief?

Ivet CorrandonHe lit a hill once and a man died. I read it in the paper. He'll hear old woman, and he'll hear liability, and he will not hear wall.

She said it without cruelty, as she might have given the weight of a loaf. Illuminara had been wrong about nothing in this kitchen so far, and she had the feeling that would not last.

Ivet CorrandonAnd you. Your machine has never smelled a slope at four o'clock. It's never stood where the air changes its mind.

IlluminaraNo, it hasn't. So tell me where your map is wrong. I would rather find that here than on the ridge.

Ivet CorrandonNowhere.

The silence went on long enough for the kettle to tick. Ivet looked at the dressed hand, and at the stick, and at the window.

Ivet CorrandonThe north. The gully above the Marrick cottages. I ran out of knee. Edrin never combed the gully. He said it burns itself. Then he never said another word about it, and I never asked.

Something turned over in Illuminara, quietly, like a key in a lock she had been leaning on for three weeks. The gully was the old stone leat, the flood channel she had modelled for the water office. Every storm she had run went down that cut like thread into a needle. Water finds it. So does anything else that wants to go downhill.

IlluminaraMrs Corrandon, would you tell me the order you burn in? Not the reasons. The order. I'll carry it to the chief as something he can test, with his own torch, on his own say.

Ivet CorrandonAnd if his test says I'm a fool?

IlluminaraThen a fool's mistake is corrected in thirty metres, instead of a village's in three hundred. Either way the hill is the winner.

03 · A Test He Could Stop

Sorrel Gate school, command post · 14:05

Two buses idled in the car park with their doors open. Brandt stood over a trestle table with the district map pinned under four stones, and he did not look up until she had laid her tablet on top of his county. Over his shoulder, Joss Marren was loading a hose reel and trying not to listen.

IlluminaraShe isn't lighting fires. She's combing the lee of every wall so a headfire has nothing to eat behind the stone. It's a firebreak drawn in fire. Forty years old, and the state forbade it for all of them.

Taavi BrandtThat's what she says.

IlluminaraIt's what the walls say. I read them as load paths, and a load path is an interpretation, and she could be right by accident. So I'm not asking you to believe her. I'm asking for one segment. Thirty metres of the east wall, your crew, your torch, your hose behind it. If the burn does not die at the lee by itself, you put it out and we've lost twenty minutes.

Taavi BrandtAnd if it dies by itself, I've learned about one wall. The headfire isn't one wall.

IlluminaraNo. But it's a way of turning a coincidence into a measurement. And I'd ask you to hold the second thing against it as well, because I think she's right about the walls and I'm afraid of the place she hasn't burned.

He took a long time. A bus driver coughed. Outside, a woman was counting children from the school into the first coach, name by name, getting each name right.

Taavi BrandtThen these are mine. The buses go at three, all of them, nobody comes off. Anyone not on a bus is crew or on a tanker. The wind goes southwest at about twenty to six. I have one number and it does a different job in each hour. Before a torch is lit, thirty-five on the ridge gauge cancels the burn. After it's lit and the strip has gone cold, a gust past thirty-five or one ember across a wall, and nobody lights anything more: every crew comes off the walls and the Marrick track and stands behind the car park wall, and the black and the wet are left to do the work. I don't chase a headfire with a hose. And the woman stays where I can see her. Not in cuffs. At my shoulder.

IlluminaraShe'll want that. She'll want to be told what she's looking at.

Taavi BrandtHale takes her statement afterwards. She isn't pardoned. If this goes wrong at Calder's rate, I'll want it said that I heard her and chose it, not that she tricked me.

Ivet had arrived at the car park door without being noticed, which at eighty-one and with a stick took talent. She stood in the frame and heard the last of it.

Ivet CorrandonI never asked to be pardoned, Chief.

Taavi BrandtNo. I'm told you never asked anyone for anything. That's most of what's wrong with this.

04 · The Gully

East wall, then the gully head · 16:05

Joss Marren lit thirty metres of the lee at ten past four, with Brandt crouched at his elbow and Ivet in a borrowed folding chair on the track above, her stick across her knees like a sword she had decided not to draw. The flame crept three paces along the grass. It lay down against the stone and went out.

Nobody said anything. A second pass, a little further along: the same. Joss looked at his chief. Brandt put two fingers into the black and held them there.

Taavi BrandtCold.

Ivet CorrandonOf course it's cold. It had nothing to ask for.

Then the smoke from the last small patch did something that Illuminara had been waiting for and did not enjoy being right about. It did not lift. It slid sideways along the ground, thin and grey, leaning north, and went into the mouth of the gully above the Marrick cottages the way water goes into a drain.

IlluminaraChief, look at the smoke. That's my creek. The gully is the old leat, and it is the same shape for air that it is for water. When the wind goes southwest at twenty to six, the headfire won't come at the village over the walls she's combed. It'll come to the one place that has no black behind it, and the leat will take it straight down the cut and out over the top terrace.

Taavi BrandtInto the school and the Marrick cottages.

IlluminaraInto both. And it won't stop at the wall, because no one has burned anything behind that wall for forty years.

Ivet CorrandonEdrin said it burns itself.

IlluminaraWhen did he say it?

Ivet CorrandonThe year after the goats went. Hennie Marrick kept sixty goats on that cut and they ate everything to the clay. Edrin said what he saw. He didn't say the goats had been doing the burning for us.

It was the first time Illuminara had seen the old woman look surprised, and she did not look away from it, out of courtesy. Forty years of a rule that had been right on the day it was spoken.

Taavi BrandtI can't backburn it dry. Not with a headfire two hours out. Dry fuel in a cut that draws like that, I'd be lighting my own chimney.

IlluminaraThen don't burn it dry. The leat has a sluice at the head, and the creek is still running. Forty litres a second for the wet band, down the floor of the cut and over both banks, and a margin either side that your crew combs slow and downhill, Mrs Corrandon's way. A wet strip, a black strip, a wall. The fire has a river on one side and a stone on the other and nowhere to go but out.

Taavi BrandtDownhill. Not up. You said up in your notes.

IlluminaraI did.

Taavi BrandtUp and you're feeding it a ramp. Thirty years I've never lit upslope. Down, a flame has to work for every metre.

IlluminaraDown. Thank you.

She put her finger on his map, on the thin blue cut above the Marrick cottages, and gave him the numbers one at a time so that he could argue with each.

IlluminaraTwo hundred and forty metres from the sluice to the Marrick fence, three terraces, the third at a hundred and eighty. Eleven minutes a hundred metres. Open the sluice at ten to five and the wet front is past the third terrace at ten past. If it isn't, nobody lights the margins, and the crews go to the car park wall. That's the one failure signal, and it comes before any fire does. The wet and the black can run as far as the fence and no further. The two cottages sit forty metres beyond it, past any water I can carry. I would rather say that now than have you find it at six.

Taavi BrandtAnd the wall we just burned?

IlluminaraProves one wall. It's why I trust the margins and not the hill. The hill I'll know when the gully tells us, and I'll tell you what to look for. If I'm right, the headfire enters the head of the cut and slows to a crawl on the wet band. If I'm wrong, it won't slow, and you'll see flame standing on a bank. Either of us can see that from the car park wall.

The wheel at the sluice head had not turned since before Illuminara had been in the district. It took Joss and two of Brandt's volunteers and a length of scaffold pipe for a lever, and she stood by with her watch and counted them through it aloud, as one counts a woman in labour. At ten to five the stone throat filled and the cut began to talk.

At ten past five the wet front shone past the third terrace and the ridge gauge read eighteen. Brandt looked at the one number and then at the other.

Taavi BrandtLight.

Joss went down the east bank with the slow torch and the second crew down the west, walking downhill, a flame having to work for every metre.

05 · The Wind Comes Round

Car park wall, below the gully · 17:39

The wind turned a minute early.

It came off the west like a door opening in a long house, and the smoke that had leaned north for three hours stood up, thought about it, and leaned the other way. The ridge gauge on Brandt's radio read twenty-nine, then thirty-three. His crews were still in their lines: black behind every wall, a wet band shining down the bottom of the cut, a combed margin either side with the last embers of Joss's slow torch still ticking in it.

Taavi BrandtThirty-four. Nothing left to light, so the other half of my number is the one that counts now. One more and they come off, walking, not asking. Joss, tell them the figure. I want them already turned.

At a quarter to six the gauge read thirty-six.

Taavi BrandtThat's my line. All crews off the walls, off the Marrick track, behind the car park wall. Leave the strip. Go.

They came off in the order he had given them, the wet band and the black walls left standing behind them like a stage with nobody on it, and were over the car park wall with a few minutes in hand. Nobody went up the Marrick track.

The headfire arrived at six minutes to six. It did not come as a wave. It came as a noise, then as a colour on the underside of the smoke, then as a line of brightness across the whole west face of the hill with embers going over it like thrown seed. It reached the first wall. It looked over. Illuminara watched it do exactly what an old woman had said it would do, and find nothing for its mouth.

In the gully it was worse and better. The fire went down into the cut because the cut drew it. Illuminara kept her eyes on the head of the cut and on the two banks, as she had told him she would, and said what she saw aloud so that he could hear it.

IlluminaraSlowing. Banks clear.

The wet band took it to a crawl; it tried both banks and found them combed and black, and no flame stood on either one for longer than it took to be seen. For forty minutes it hissed and leaned and spat at the stone. Then it ran out of road.

Not everything held. The two Marrick cottages lay forty metres beyond the fence where the break ended, exactly where she had marked the edge of her water, and there had been neither time nor crew to carry it further. A gust took an ember across the last wall at ten past six. Joss started along the wall toward the tanker parked at the foot of the Marrick track.

Taavi BrandtNo. They're empty. Let them go.

Joss MarrenChief, they're Hennie's—

Taavi BrandtThey're stone and slates. Hennie's in the school hall with his dog. This wall is the line, and that track is the wrong side of it. If I send one lad up it and the wind veers again, I've done Calder in a new place. Let them go, Joss.

They went up like paper lanterns, one and then the other, and the whole crew watched them in a silence that Illuminara did not interrupt. Below them the village lay dark and standing. Three hundred roofs. A school with its windows reflecting something that was no longer a threat.

06 · What Remained

Corrandon cottage, kitchen · 21:40

Constable Hale took the statement at the kitchen table with her hat on her knee and was careful to write only what she was told. Ivet gave it with her bandaged hand flat on the wood. When it was done Hale put away her pen and said nothing about permits, because there was a chief in the doorway and it was his to say.

Taavi BrandtThe Marrick cottages are ash. The test burn was lit without a licence, on my say, against the district's written order. I'll answer for both. I'll answer, too, for the look I gave an old woman at midday.

Ivet CorrandonYou gave me a perfectly good look. I'd have given it to you.

Taavi BrandtMy report starts with your map and ends with my abort line. The review can read it in that order.

Ivet CorrandonThey'll fine me.

Taavi BrandtThey may. I'll stand beside you at the hearing and carry your map in with me, with the gauge log and the times, and say what I saw on that wall and in that cut. That isn't a pardon. I can't give you one. It isn't a promise they'll adopt anything, either.

Ivet CorrandonI don't want a pardon. I want it taught.

Brandt turned his hat once in his hands. It was the nearest thing to hesitation the night had left him.

Taavi BrandtThen a trial, not a promise. Two of your walls first, my crews, in the dry month, my gauge in my hand and my line on the radio. The regional office can quarrel about the paper with a hillside that's still standing.

Ivet said nothing for a while. Edrin's map lay open under the lamp, drawn forty years ago in pencil on the back of a seed catalogue: every wall of Sorrel Gate, each lee shaded.

Ivet CorrandonThen I'll say what I'm giving, since you've said what you are. I don't light alone again. I light with your crew at my elbow, and when you call your signal my torch stops, and I won't need telling twice. And the gully goes on the map, in ink. Edrin said it burns itself. It burned itself because Hennie's goats kept it bare, and when the goats went nobody wrote the date on the rule. I want the date on it.

Illuminara drew one more line on the map at the north, a thin blue cut with an arrow, and wrote beside it, in her small precise hand, leat and wet band, comb downhill; one hill, one wind, one day. She turned the paper toward the old woman.

IlluminaraWould you sign it with his name as well? It's the part he never had time to finish.

Ivet took the pencil in her bandaged hand, and wrote, slowly and without once pressing too hard, Edrin Corrandon, and under it, and Ivet.

01 · Ožigi ob zidu

Sorrel Gate, zgornje terase · 12:40

Dim je stal na zahodnem grebenu, šest kilometrov stran, in se nagibal proti njim. Okrožje je Sorrel Gatu dalo čas do treh. Illuminara je dopoldne hodila po zgornjih terasah s tablico meritev potokov, zaradi česar jo je tja poslal regionalni urad za vode, pri prvem črnem madežu pa je to nehala početi.

Zdaj jih je bilo enainštirideset. Vsak je bil velik kakor namizni prt. Vsak je bil še topel, ko je dlan držala dva centimetra nad njim. Noben ni ležal v odprti travi. Vsak je bil na zavetrni strani suhozida ali ob robu terase in niti eden ni prešel kamna.

Nekdo šteje veter, je pomislila. Nekdo neti samo tam, kjer je bil veter zavrnjen.

Taavi BrandtNekdo mi od šestih zjutraj zažiga hrib. Za enajst ognjev vem, kaj so. Preostale bi pripisal isti roki.

IlluminaraEnainštirideset. Vse sem prehodila.

Taavi BrandtPotem ste prehodili enainštirideset krajev, kjer bi ogenj človeku lahko ušel. Policistka Hale je pri cerkvi z nalogom za Ivet Corrandon. Stara je enainosemdeset let, mojim ekipam noče odpreti vrat, ob mraku pa jo sam odnesem po tej cesti. Raje bi videl, da gre peš.

IlluminaraPoglejte, kje so, načelnik. Ne kdo jih je naredil.

IlluminaraPotem ne bom trdila, da je previdna. Vprašala bom, ali ima prav, in poiskala merilo, ki lahko odgovori, ne da bi moral kdor koli od naju čemur koli verjeti. Naj najprej govorim z njo.

Taavi BrandtDo dveh. Ob dveh začnejo polniti avtobuse in takrat bo v enem od njih ali na moji rami.

02 · Čemu je bil namenjen hrib

Koča Corrandonovih, kuhinja · 13:15

Vrata niso bila zaklenjena. Illuminara je vseeno potrkala in glas za lesom ji je povedal, da nikoli niso, da ljudje samo verjamejo, da so, ter da lahko vstopi, če je dovolj pametna, da si obriše čevlje.

Ivet Corrandon je sedela za mizo s pepelom do komolcev, škatlico vžigalic ob desni roki in črnotrnovko ob stolu. Čez njeno levo dlan je potekal za prst širok žulj. Imela je obraz nekoga, ki je imel dolgo prav brez pomoči kogar koli.

Ivet CorrandonVi ste vodno dekle. Rekli so, da je na mojih zidovih vodno dekle z napravo.

IlluminaraSem modelarka voda. Naprava je tablica in večinoma šteje potoke. Smem sesti?

Ivet CorrandonČe ste me prišli odpeljat na avtobus, lahko sedete na tla.

Stara ženska jo je nekaj časa gledala. Illuminara je med čakanjem vzela čisto krpo s kredence in z očmi vprašala za roko. Vprašanje lahko počaka, žulj pa ne. Ivet ji je dovolila, sprva odkrito sumničavo, nato pa počasi manj.

Ivet CorrandonLeta štiriinosemdeset je v tej kuhinji stal gozdar in mojemu Edrinu povedal, da se hriba ne sme požigati, da tega ne sme nihče in nikoli, ter mu vzel papir, ki je govoril, da smeva. Edrina so oglobili. Teden dni ni spregovoril. Štirideset let živimo pod hribom, ki ne more početi drugega kot rasti.

IlluminaraVi pa ste požigali naprej.

Ivet CorrandonPo malem. Ob koncu mokrega obdobja, na zavetrni strani zidov. Edrin je temu rekel česanje. Zavetrje zidu počesite do črnega in čelni požar pride do kamna, pogleda čez ter ne najde ničesar za svoja usta. Zid brez črnine za seboj je samo kup kamenja.

IlluminaraZakaj potem niste povedali načelniku?

Ivet CorrandonNekoč je zažgal hrib in človek je umrl. Prebrala sem v časopisu. Slišal bo stara ženska, slišal bo odgovornost, zidu pa ne bo slišal.

Rekla je brez krutosti, kakor bi povedala težo štruce. Illuminara se v tej kuhinji doslej ni motila o ničemer in slutila je, da to ne bo trajalo.

Ivet CorrandonPa vi. Vaša naprava še nikoli ni vohala pobočja ob štirih popoldne. Nikoli ni stala tam, kjer si zrak premisli.

IlluminaraNe, ni. Zato mi povejte, kje je vaš zemljevid napačen. Raje bi to odkrila tukaj kakor na grebenu.

Ivet CorrandonNikjer.

Ivet CorrandonNa severu. Jarek nad Marrickovima kočama. Zmanjkalo mi je kolena. Edrin jarka nikoli ni česal. Rekel je, da se požge sam. Nato o njem ni rekel niti besede več, jaz pa nisem nikoli vprašala.

Jarek je bil stari kamniti razbremenilni kanal, poplavna struga, ki jo je modelirala za urad za vode. Vsaka nevihta, ki jo je simulirala, je šla po tistem rezu kakor nit skozi šivankino uho. Ivet CorrandonIn če njegov preizkus pokaže, da sem neumna?

03 · Preizkus, ki ga je lahko ustavil
