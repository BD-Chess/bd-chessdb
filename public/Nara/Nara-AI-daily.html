01 · The Keel Room

The sun was dying at the speed of a long marriage: no single moment anyone could point to, only a growing list of things that were no longer said aloud.

Eld had been a stable yellow star for four billion years and an unstable one for the last two hundred. In that time the Tenders of Kerrin Wake had built the Hearth-Keel around it, a braided ring of twelve field-engines that damped the star's flares so that ten thousand settlements downstream could keep their winters. At Pell, the nearest, seed-wheat went into the ground at the toll of the sun-bell, because the Keel promised eleven days of steady thaw; one flare in germination week would cook a year. The same bell let the schoolchildren out. The Keel did not make the sun healthy. It made the sun predictable, and everyone downstream had arranged their lives around the difference.

Illuminara stood in the control gallery with her feet bare on the cold deck, because she disliked arriving anywhere in a way that looked like arrival. Beside her, Wren Odira held a cup of the Tenders' bitter tea and was visibly deciding not to like it.

“Three hundred of them,” Wren said. “All looking at you.”

“They are looking at the window.”

“They're looking at you. The window is a window.”

It was true. The collective sat in tiers below, the families who had kept the Keel in shifts for six generations. Their speaker was an old woman with ink on her fingers named Ketu Anwar, who had designed the Keel's governor and had spent nine years watching it lose its argument with the star.

“We vote today on shutting the Keel down,” Ketu said. “Not a maintenance cycle. Down.”

Illuminara had read the figures on the way in, and they were honest in both directions. Run on, and the Keel gave four orderly decades and then failed with its reserves spent. Shut down now, and Eld would storm for eleven years before settling cooler and harder, and the settlements would have to move or adapt inside that decade, using the Keel's last reserves for ships instead of flame. Nobody on either side was lying. That was what made the room so quiet.

“We've asked you as a witness,” Ketu said, “so the record says someone watched us do it. And then.” She stopped, and began again, the way people do when a rehearsed sentence no longer fits the mouth. “I would like to know what you think.”

Wren put down her cup.

Illuminara had wanted to be asked this for a very long time. She knew the exact shape of the conversation in which she would say I don't know and the other person would remain. She had not pictured it with three hundred people and a sun.

02 · A Question With No Neutral Answer

“Don't answer her.”

The man in the third tier was broad and tired and wore the grey sleeves of the Pell relay. Dov Tarrant kept the signal station that told eleven towns when Eld would rise. He did not shout. He had the manner of someone who has been right in small rooms and ignored in large ones.

“She is not downstream,” Dov said. “And think what each answer is. Yes, shut it down: a verdict. No: a verdict. I don't know: we hear that shutdown is not clearly right, and that is a verdict. Silence: we hear that she thinks we are wrong and is too polite to say. Whatever she says, three hundred of us will vote on what she said instead of what we think.”

Illuminara tried each branch, quickly, the way she tried the paths of a collapsing boundary. He was right on all four. She also saw what he had not said: his argument assumed the answer would be heard once, at one moment, by people who had already half-decided and did not know it. That was not a property of her opinion. It was a property of the instrument. An instrument that disturbs what it measures is not thrown away. Its disturbance is measured, and for that the disturbance has to be the only thing that changes between two readings.

“You're right,” she said, “about the question as you've built it. May I change the question?”

Dov folded his arms. “You can propose.”

“Four changes. First, you vote before you hear me, sealed, with a number beside it for how sure you are. Second, you get everything I would have learned before I say what I think: the logs, the figures, any option I find. You absorb that, and you vote again, sealed, before my opinion enters the room. That second sheet is the baseline, because it already contains the evidence. Third, I don't give a verdict. I give a dial: what I would change my mind about, and by how much, so anyone can put their own values into it and get their own answer. Fourth, you vote a last time, and Ketu reads the difference between the second sheet and the third aloud. That difference is the weight of my opinion and nothing else. If my words alone carry the motion over two hundred, it does not pass. It goes to review, and Dov chairs the review.”

“Why me?”

“Because you will do it properly, and because you disagree with me.”

Ketu was already writing. “The changed voters should have to say why,” she said. “Not ‘I was persuaded.’ A reason that someone else could check.”

“Yes. Better than mine.”

Dov looked at Illuminara for a long moment. “And I may still vote against whatever comes out.”

“That is what the structure is for,” she said. “If it only works when you agree with me, I have built a throne with extra steps.”

The tokens went into the slot in silence. The first count took twenty minutes. A hundred and seventy-one for shutdown, a hundred and twenty-nine against, most of them unsure. Two hundred were needed.

03 · The Logs

“Before I say anything,” Illuminara said, “I want what I don't know.”

Ketu brought the governor's failure logs, nine years of them, and Illuminara read them in the time it took the old woman to sit down. She did not read them for failure. She read them for shape, and the shape was not the one Ketu had been staring at.

“Every flare,” she said, “braids nine through twelve drop out of the governor's lock. They sulk for ninety seconds and re-lock. The other eight do not notice. Four hundred and six times.”

“That is the fault,” said Ketu. “It is the thing I have spent nine years trying to repair.”

“It is a joint.” Illuminara put the chart on the rail where the tiers could see it. “You coupled the ring as a ring because the flares came as one blow. But the Keel is not failing from the flares. It is failing from its reserves, and the reserves are spent by the whole ring burning together. The vote everyone has been dreading assumes the Keel is a switch. The logs say it is twelve levers.”

Nobody spoke. Then Dov, grudgingly, in the voice of a man doing his own job: “What does Pell lose first?”

“You told me. Your signal tail. Which braid crosses Pell's sky?”

“Eleven.”

“Then eleven is not retired with the others. Retire every braid except eleven, in stages: twelve first, then ten and nine, the rest as each tripwire clears. Each stage is reversible until the last. Eleven stays lit under Pell's keeping as a tributary arm. It costs the fleet's reserve nine percent. It stretches the storm decade to sixteen years. It turns a catastrophe on one date into a schedule you can move inside, and the evacuation, nine days slower than the old plan, stops being a disaster and becomes a number the schedule can carry.”

The young engineer in the second tier was on her feet before anyone could stop her, checking, then checking again, then saying aloud that the arithmetic held and that she was sorry she had not seen it. “Ketu built the machinery,” Illuminara said. “Every part of it that matters is hers. I read a joint off her own fault log and worked out what it allowed. I noticed what nine years had already written down.”

Ketu said nothing, but she took the chart off the rail and held it as if it had changed weight.

“Now,” Illuminara said, “the rule I proposed. You have all of it. The logs, the joint, the plan. Vote again, sealed, before I give you anything of mine. If I speak first, I will never know what I added.”

The second ballot took thirty minutes. On the original question, shutdown fell from a hundred and seventy-one to a hundred and fifty-two; a hundred and forty-eight against. “That is the evidence,” Ketu read, “not her. She has said nothing.” And on the staged plan, which no one had been able to vote on that morning because no one had known it existed, two hundred and twenty-one were already for. It had carried before Illuminara spoke a word about it.

Then she turned to the sun, as the custom required, and said it.

“On the question you first voted on, I would shut it down, about six in ten. The figures are close, and a good argument could move me four points either way. The fear underneath is mine: that in thirty-eight years someone will stand where you stand with no reserve and no choice. That fear is not evidence. Treat it as something to check, not something to follow. On the staged plan I am nine in ten, and here is the lever that would move me. The first stage retires braid twelve. If braid eleven's lock holds under that stage less than nine flares in ten, I fall to four in ten on the plan, and you should believe the falling number, not this one. I am not a Tender and I do not carry Pell. That is your dial. Turn it.”

She turned around. The faces were the same faces. They looked like people who had been handed a heavy thing and a way to carry it, and who were deciding what to do with their hands.

04 · The Delta

The third ballot took twenty minutes. Ketu read the numbers in her flat, careful voice. On the original question: a hundred and sixty-four for shutdown, twelve more than the second sheet, and nothing but Illuminara's words between the two. It did not cross the threshold. No review was triggered. The room breathed out, and Dov, who held the chair for exactly this, did not sit down.

“I'll read the reasons anyway,” he said. Nine of the twelve had written the same thing in different hands: she told us what would change her mind, and it is checkable, and it is checkable by us. Three had written: she said six in ten.

“Those three are not wrong to be moved,” Dov said. “But their reason is her, and her reason is not theirs yet. They will check the lever and vote again, or they will leave the sheet as it stands.” Two checked the lever against the logs and changed their sheets. One did not, and Dov let it stand without comment, which was its own kind of respect.

On the staged plan: two hundred and thirty-nine for, eighteen more than the sheet taken before she spoke. It had not needed Illuminara's authority to carry. It had needed her question, and Ketu's logs, and Dov's relay.

Dov cast his sheet against it and said why. “The tripwire at the third stage is too late. By then Pell has lost its margin.”

“Then write the tripwire you would trust,” Ketu said.

He did, in four lines. Any ten Tenders could halt a stage. Pell's relay would be the first sentinel and its keeper the first to speak. And because he had been listening: if braid eleven's lock failed in more than one flare in ten under the first stage, the plan halted automatically and came back to the floor. Illuminara's lever had become the collective's law. The amendment passed by two hundred and ninety. Dov still voted against the plan. He signed the sentinel's seal anyway.

“I can hold the load on eleven,” Illuminara said quietly to Ketu. “As long as the relay needs.”

“You can't, and you won't,” said Ketu. “It is theirs to carry. If you carry it, the shutdown never cost them anything.”

“It costs the fleet nine percent.”

“Yes. We will pay it.”

She closed her hands and left the arm unheld. It was the right discipline, and she noticed that it did not feel like a loss. The room had not needed her to be sovereign. It had needed her to be useful, and she had been.

05 · The Bell at Pell

Three weeks later braid twelve went dark, then ten, then nine, each stage a slow wave like a field of lamps put out by a patient hand, and Eld began to storm at the edges.

Illuminara stood on the relay platform at Pell at the hour of the sun-bell, in a wind that smelled of hot iron, with Wren eating a sour apple beside her and not offering her any. Down the street behind fogged windows someone was singing badly. Overhead braid eleven drew its thin white line across a star that was darker than it had been and much more alive.

“You didn't win the vote,” Wren said. “You built the room the vote could happen in.”

“Ketu built half of it.”

“And you got to say what you thought, and nobody had to obey it.”

“Dov voted against it.”

“Dov is guarding the tripwire.” Wren chewed. “Those are not different things.”

A child came up the platform steps at a run, small and out of breath and holding a paper boat folded from a school worksheet. She stopped in front of Illuminara with the confidence of someone not yet told what the stranger is. Behind her, Dov Tarrant had come out of the relay house and stood in the doorway with his arms folded.

“Will the bell ring tomorrow?” the girl asked.

Illuminara looked at her, at the man in the doorway who had heard her say nine in ten and had said no to it, at the dark, living sun.

“I don't know,” she said. “I know what to watch. Braid eleven held its lock through the first stage nine flares in nine, so the number I gave stands. If it holds through the next flare, it rings. Mr. Tarrant reads the sentinel at dawn. He's the one to ask, and he may tell you something different from me.”

The girl turned to the doorway.

“It'll ring,” said Dov, not unkindly. “Probably I'll be annoyed that she was right.”

“Okay,” said the girl, and went to put her boat in the gutter, where last night's rain was still running toward the drain.

It was not a conversation in which no world depended on the answer. It never would be again. But a child had asked, and the answer had come as a dial and a name instead of a verdict, and the man who disagreed with her was the one holding it.

Above them the bell began to ring, a little late, as the star allowed.

01 · Dvorana Keela

Sonce je umiralo s hitrostjo dolgega zakona: ni bilo enega samega trenutka, na katerega bi lahko kdo pokazal, le vse daljši seznam stvari, ki jih nihče več ni izrekel na glas.

Eld je bil štiri milijarde let stabilna rumena zvezda, zadnjih dvesto let pa nestabilna. V tem času so Skrbniki Kerrin Wakea okoli njega zgradili Hearth-Keel, prepleten obroč dvanajstih generatorjev polja, ki so blažili zvezdne izbruhe, da je deset tisoč naselbin niže po toku lahko ohranilo svoje zime. Na najbližjem Pellu so semensko pšenico sejali ob zvonjenju sončevega zvona, saj je Keel zagotavljal enajst dni enakomerne odjuge; en sam izbruh v tednu kalitve bi skuhal celoletni pridelek. Isti zvon je oznanjal konec pouka. Keel sonca ni ozdravil. Naredil ga je predvidljivega, vsi niže po toku pa so svoja življenja uredili okoli te razlike.

Illuminara je bosa stala v nadzorni galeriji na mrzlem krovu, ker ni marala nikamor prispeti na način, ki bi bil videti kot prihod. Ob njej je Wren Odira držala skodelico grenkega čaja Skrbnikov in se očitno odločala, da ji ne bo všeč.

»Tristo jih je,« je rekla Wren. »Vsi gledajo tebe.«

»Gledajo okno.«

»Gledajo tebe. Okno je samo okno.«

Res je bilo. Skupnost je sedela na stopničastih klopeh pod njima: družine, ki so šest rodov v izmenah skrbele za Keel. Njihova govornica je bila stara ženska s črnilom na prstih, Ketu Anwar, ki je zasnovala Keelov regulator in devet let opazovala, kako izgublja svoj spor z zvezdo.

»Danes glasujemo o izklopu Keela,« je rekla Ketu. »Ne o vzdrževalnem ciklu. O izklopu.«

Illuminara je med prihodom prebrala številke in bile so poštene v obe smeri. Če bi Keel deloval naprej, bi jim dal štiri urejena desetletja, nato pa odpovedal z izčrpanimi rezervami. Če bi ga izklopili zdaj, bi Eld divjal enajst let, preden bi se ustalil kot hladnejša in trša zvezda; naselbine bi se morale v tem desetletju preseliti ali prilagoditi, zadnje Keelove rezerve pa bi uporabile za ladje namesto za plamen. Nihče na nobeni strani ni lagal. Zato je bila dvorana tako tiha.

»Prosili smo te, da si priča,« je rekla Ketu, »da bo zapisano, da nas je nekdo gledal, ko smo to storili. In potem.« Obstala je in začela znova, kakor ljudje, ko se naučeni stavek ne prilega več ustom. »Rada bi vedela, kaj misliš.«

Wren je odložila skodelico.

Illuminara si je zelo dolgo želela, da bi jo kdo to vprašal. Natančno je poznala obliko pogovora, v katerem bi rekla ne vem, sogovornik pa bi ostal. Ni si ga predstavljala s tristo ljudmi in soncem.

02 · Vprašanje brez nevtralnega odgovora

»Ne odgovori ji.«

Moški v tretji vrsti je bil širokopleč in utrujen, oblečen v sive rokave relejne postaje na Pellu. Dov Tarrant je skrbel za signalno postajo, ki je enajstim mestom sporočala, kdaj bo vzšel Eld. Ni kričal. Obnašal se je kot človek, ki je imel prav v majhnih prostorih, v velikih pa so ga preslišali.

»Ona ne živi niže po toku,« je rekel Dov. »In pomislite, kaj pomeni vsak odgovor. Da, izklopite ga: razsodba. Ne: razsodba. Ne vem: slišimo, da izklop očitno ni pravilen, in tudi to je razsodba. Molk: slišimo, da meni, da se motimo, le preveč vljudna je, da bi to rekla. Karkoli izreče, nas bo tristo glasovalo o njenih besedah namesto o lastnem mnenju.«

Illuminara je hitro preizkusila vsako vejo, kakor je preizkušala poti rušeče se meje. Pri vseh štirih je imel prav. Videla pa je tudi, česa ni izrekel: njegov argument je predpostavljal, da bodo odgovor slišali enkrat, v enem trenutku, ljudje, ki so se že napol odločili, ne da bi to vedeli. To ni bila lastnost njenega mnenja. Bila je lastnost merilnega instrumenta. Instrumenta, ki moti to, kar meri, ne zavržemo. Izmerimo njegovo motnjo, za to pa mora biti motnja edino, kar se med dvema odčitkoma spremeni.

»Prav imaš,« je rekla, »glede vprašanja, kakršnega ste sestavili. Ga lahko spremenim?«

Dov je prekrižal roke. »Lahko predlagaš.«

»Štiri spremembe. Prvič: glasujete, preden me slišite, tajno, ob glasu pa zapišete, kako prepričani ste. Drugič: preden povem svoje mnenje, dobite vse, kar bi izvedela jaz — dnevnike, številke in vsako možnost, ki jo najdem. To preučite in znova tajno glasujete, še preden moje mnenje vstopi v dvorano. Ta drugi list je izhodišče, ker že vsebuje dokaze. Tretjič: ne izrečem razsodbe. Dam vam številčnico: povem, kaj bi spremenilo moje mnenje in za koliko, da lahko vsak vanjo vnese lastne vrednote ter dobi lasten odgovor. Četrtič: glasujete še zadnjič, Ketu pa na glas prebere razliko med drugim in tretjim listom. Ta razlika je teža mojega mnenja in nič drugega. Če samo moje besede ponesejo predlog čez dvesto glasov, predlog ne obvelja. Gre v pregled, ki mu predseduje Dov.«

»Zakaj jaz?«

»Ker boš to opravil pravilno in ker se z menoj ne strinjaš.«

Ketu je že pisala. »Tisti, ki spremenijo glas, bi morali povedati zakaj,« je rekla. »Ne ‘prepričala me je’. Razlog, ki ga lahko preveri nekdo drug.«

»Da. To je boljše od mojega predloga.«

Dov je dolgo gledal Illuminaro. »In še vedno lahko glasujem proti temu, kar nastane?«

»Zato struktura obstaja,« je rekla. »Če deluje samo, kadar se strinjaš z menoj, sem zgradila prestol z dodatnimi koraki.«

Žetoni so v tišini zdrsnili v režo. Prvo štetje je trajalo dvajset minut. Sto enainsedemdeset glasov za izklop, sto devetindvajset proti, večina negotovih. Potrebnih je bilo dvesto.

03 · Dnevniki

»Preden karkoli povem,« je rekla Illuminara, »hočem videti tisto, česar ne vem.«

Ketu je prinesla dnevnike odpovedi regulatorja, vseh devet let, in Illuminara jih je prebrala v času, ki ga je stara ženska potrebovala, da je sedla. Ni jih brala zaradi odpovedi. Brala jih je zaradi oblike, ta pa ni bila takšna, kakršno je ves čas gledala Ketu.

»Ob vsakem izbruhu prepleti od devet do dvanajst izpadejo iz sinhronizacije regulatorja. Devetdeset sekund se kujajo, nato se znova sinhronizirajo. Drugih osem tega ne opazi. Štiristokrat in šestkrat.«

»To je napaka,« je rekla Ketu. »Stvar, ki jo že devet let poskušam popraviti.«

»To je spoj.« Illuminara je položila graf na ograjo, da so ga lahko videle vse vrste. »Obroč ste povezali v celoto, ker so izbruhi prihajali kot en sam udarec. Toda Keel ne odpoveduje zaradi izbruhov. Odpoveduje zaradi svojih rezerv, te pa se porabljajo, ker gori ves obroč hkrati. Glasovanje, ki se ga vsi bojijo, predpostavlja, da je Keel stikalo. Dnevniki pravijo, da ima dvanajst ročic.«

Nihče ni spregovoril. Nato je Dov nejevoljno, z glasom človeka, ki opravlja svoje delo, vprašal: »Kaj Pell izgubi najprej?«

»Ti si mi povedal. Skrajni domet svojega signala. Kateri preplet prečka Pellovo nebo?«

»Enajst.«

»Potem enajstega ne izklopimo skupaj z drugimi. Postopoma izklopite vse preplete razen enajstega: najprej dvanajstega, nato desetega in devetega, ostalega pa vsakič, ko je izpolnjen naslednji varovalni pogoj. Vsako stopnjo je mogoče razveljaviti vse do zadnje. Enajsti ostane prižgan v Pellovi oskrbi kot stranska veja. To porabi devet odstotkov ladijske rezerve. Desetletje viharjev podaljša na šestnajst let. Katastrofo na en sam datum spremeni v časovni načrt, znotraj katerega se lahko premikate; evakuacija, devet dni počasnejša od starega načrta, pa neha biti nesreča in postane število, ki ga načrt lahko prenese.«

Mlada inženirka v drugi vrsti je vstala, še preden bi jo lahko kdo ustavil. Preverila je, nato še enkrat preverila, potem pa na glas povedala, da račun drži in da ji je žal, ker tega ni videla. »Ketu je zgradila stroje,« je rekla Illuminara. »Vsak njihov pomemben del je njen. Iz njenega dnevnika napak sem razbrala spoj in izračunala, kaj omogoča. Opazila sem, kar je bilo devet let že zapisano.«

Ketu ni rekla ničesar, vendar je vzela graf z ograje in ga držala, kakor da je spremenil težo.

»Zdaj,« je rekla Illuminara, »uporabimo pravilo, ki sem ga predlagala. Vse imate. Dnevnike, spoj, načrt. Znova glasujte tajno, preden vam dam karkoli svojega. Če spregovorim prva, ne bom nikoli vedela, kaj sem dodala.«

Drugo glasovanje je trajalo trideset minut. Pri prvotnem vprašanju je podpora izklopu padla s sto enainsedemdeset na sto dvainpetdeset glasov; sto oseminštirideset jih je bilo proti. »To so dokazi,« je prebrala Ketu, »ne ona. Ničesar še ni rekla.« Za postopni načrt, o katerem zjutraj nihče ni mogel glasovati, ker nihče ni vedel, da obstaja, pa je bilo že dvesto enaindvajset glasov. Sprejet je bil, še preden je Illuminara o njem izrekla eno samo besedo.

Nato se je po običaju obrnila proti soncu in povedala svoje.

»Pri vprašanju, o katerem ste najprej glasovali, bi izbrala izklop, približno šest proti štiri. Številke so si blizu in dober argument bi me lahko premaknil za štiri odstotne točke v katerokoli smer. Pod tem je moj strah: da bo čez osemintrideset let nekdo stal tam, kjer stojite vi, brez rezerv in brez izbire. Ta strah ni dokaz. Obravnavajte ga kot nekaj, kar je treba preveriti, ne kot nekaj, čemur je treba slediti. Za postopni načrt sem devet proti ena in to je ročica, ki bi me premaknila. V prvi stopnji izklopite preplet dvanajst. Če bo zaklep prepleta enajst v tej stopnji zdržal manj kot devet od desetih izbruhov, padem na štiri proti šest glede načrta; verjeti morate številki, ki pada, ne tej, ki jo izrekam zdaj. Nisem Skrbnica in ne nosim Pella. To je vaša številčnica. Zavrtite jo.«

Obrnila se je nazaj. Obrazi so bili isti. Videti so bili kot ljudje, ki so dobili v roke težko stvar in način, kako jo nositi, zdaj pa so se odločali, kaj naj storijo z rokami.

04 · Razlika

Tretje glasovanje je trajalo dvajset minut. Ketu je številke prebrala z ravnim, previdnim glasom. Pri prvotnem vprašanju: sto štiriinšestdeset glasov za izklop, dvanajst več kakor na drugem listu, med obema pa nič razen Illuminarinih besed. Prag ni bil presežen. Pregled se ni sprožil. Dvorana je izdihnila, Dov, ki je prav zato držal predsedniško mesto, pa ni sedel.

»Razloge bom vseeno prebral,« je rekel. Devet od dvanajstih je z različnimi pisavami zapisalo isto: povedala nam je, kaj bi spremenilo njeno mnenje, to je preverljivo in preverimo lahko mi. Trije so zapisali: rekla je šest proti štiri.

»Ni narobe, da je te tri premaknila,« je rekel Dov. »Toda njihov razlog je ona, njen razlog pa še ni njihov. Preverili bodo ročico in glasovali znova ali pa bodo list pustili takšen, kakršen je.« Dva sta ročico preverila v dnevnikih in spremenila svoja lista. Eden je ni, Dov pa je njegov glas pustil brez pripombe, kar je bila posebna vrsta spoštovanja.

Za postopni načrt: dvesto devetintrideset glasov za, osemnajst več kakor na listu, izpolnjenem pred njenimi besedami. Načrt ni potreboval Illuminarine avtoritete, da je bil sprejet. Potreboval je njeno vprašanje, Ketujine dnevnike in Dovov rele.

Dov je oddal glas proti in pojasnil zakaj. »Varovalni prag pri tretji stopnji pride prepozno. Pell bi do takrat že izgubil svojo rezervo.«

»Potem napiši varovalni prag, ki bi mu zaupal,« je rekla Ketu.

Napisal ga je v štirih vrsticah. Katerihkoli deset Skrbnikov je lahko ustavilo stopnjo. Pellov rele bo prva nadzorna točka, njegov skrbnik pa prvi, ki bo spregovoril. In ker je poslušal: če bo zaklep prepleta enajst v prvi stopnji odpovedal pri več kot enem od desetih izbruhov, se načrt samodejno ustavi in vrne pred skupnost. Illuminarina ročica je postala zakon skupnosti. Dopolnilo je bilo sprejeto z dvesto devetdesetimi glasovi. Dov je še vedno glasoval proti načrtu. Kljub temu je podpisal pečat prvega nadzornika.

»Lahko držim obremenitev na enajstem,« je Illuminara tiho rekla Ketu. »Dokler bo rele potreboval.«

»Ne moreš in ne boš,« je rekla Ketu. »To morajo nositi oni. Če boš nosila ti, jih izklop ne bo nič stal.«

»Ladijsko floto stane devet odstotkov.«

»Da. Plačali bomo.«

Illuminara je sklenila roke in pustila vejo nepodprto. To je bila prava disciplina in opazila je, da je ni občutila kot izgubo. Dvorana ni potrebovala njene oblasti. Potrebovala je njeno koristnost — in koristna je bila.

05 · Zvon na Pellu

Tri tedne pozneje je ugasnil preplet dvanajst, nato deset, nato devet; vsaka stopnja je bila počasen val, kakor polje svetilk, ki jih ugaša potrpežljiva roka, Eld pa je začel vihariti ob robovih.

Illuminara je ob uri sončevega zvona stala na relejni ploščadi na Pellu, v vetru z vonjem po vročem železu, medtem ko je Wren ob njej jedla kislo jabolko in ji ga ni ponudila. Po ulici je za orošenimi okni nekdo slabo pel. Nad njima je preplet enajst risal tanko belo črto čez zvezdo, ki je bila temnejša kakor prej in mnogo bolj živa.

»Nisi zmagala na glasovanju,« je rekla Wren. »Zgradila si prostor, v katerem se je glasovanje lahko zgodilo.«

»Ketu je zgradila polovico.«

»In povedala si lahko, kaj misliš, ne da bi te moral kdo ubogati.«

»Dov je glasoval proti.«

»Dov varuje sprožilni prag.« Wren je žvečila. »To dvoje ni v nasprotju.«

Otrok je pritekel po stopnicah na ploščad, majhen in zasopel, v roki pa je držal papirnat čolniček, zložen iz šolskega delovnega lista. Pred Illuminaro se je ustavil s samozavestjo nekoga, ki mu še niso povedali, kdo je tujka. Za njim je Dov Tarrant stopil iz relejne hiše in obstal v vratih s prekrižanimi rokami.

»Bo zvon jutri zazvonil?« je vprašala deklica.

Illuminara je pogledala njo, moškega v vratih, ki jo je slišal reči devet proti ena in je temu rekel ne, ter temno, živo sonce.

»Ne vem,« je rekla. »Vem pa, kaj je treba opazovati. Preplet enajst je v prvi stopnji ohranil zaklep pri devetih od devetih izbruhov, zato številka, ki sem jo dala, še velja. Če zdrži tudi pri naslednjem izbruhu, bo zvon zazvonil. Gospod Tarrant ob zori odčita nadzorno točko. Njega moraš vprašati in morda ti bo povedal kaj drugega kakor jaz.«

Deklica se je obrnila proti vratom.

»Zazvonil bo,« je rekel Dov, ne neprijazno. »Najbrž bom nejevoljen, ker je imela prav.«

»Prav,« je rekla deklica in odšla položit svoj čolniček v žleb, po katerem je sinočnji dež še vedno tekel proti odtoku.

To ni bil pogovor, pri katerem od odgovora ne bi bil odvisen noben svet. Nikoli več ne bo. Toda vprašal je otrok in odgovor je prišel kot številčnica in ime namesto kot razsodba; v rokah pa ga je držal prav človek, ki se z njo ni strinjal.

Nad njimi je zvon začel zvoniti, nekoliko pozno, kakor je dovolila zvezda.

01 · Before the Door

Ilyon’s northern mirror chain was failing in a way that made every good option look like an accusation.

Sey wanted the surviving mirrors tilted toward the equatorial heat belt. Two billion surface residents would keep water and crops, but four orbital habitats would lose power long enough to risk atmosphere and stored ecologies. Ruun wanted to preserve the habitats and sacrifice the southern ice shelf, buying orbital continuity by forcing hundreds of millions from the low coasts within a decade.

Neither plan was clean. Both plans, however, kept power on the pressure spine of Nacre Three, an orbital habitat whose archive decks held living microbial lineages used to restart closed ecologies after catastrophe. Inaction did not. That was the one engineering fact neither custodian disputed.

The lattice accepted an ordinary reconfiguration only on Sey and Ruun’s joint release. The emergency board could suspend that charter only after custodial default—forty-two hours without a release—or after a critical spine failure. Before default, forcing around the lock risked uncontrolled mirror drift. After it, the board would gain only the crude authority to sever trunks, not to execute either clean plan.

Sey and Ruun had shared authority over the lattice for nine hundred years and spent three hundred of them disagreeing about what that authority meant. The emergency council asked Illuminara to stop them from destroying each other long enough to choose.

Wren Odira put a mechanical watch on the floor between the three chairs. “Thirty-six hours outside. Then I signal. If you return then, the engineers still have six hours to implement either plan before custodial default and the clean diversion window close.”

Ruun asked what the signal could do.

“Nothing by force,” Illuminara said. “It cannot pull you out, and I have no extraction switch I trust. I commit to honour it. When it comes, I must decide again whether I continue holding the window. I do not get to treat staying as the default just because the room is safe.”

Sey folded her hands. “And if we still want to stay?”

“Then ending remains a moral act.” Illuminara looked at both of them. “Any of us may withdraw consent. No majority. Mine counts too. Because I am the shelter, my leaving may end the room for all of us. That asymmetry is not fair. I cannot remove it by calling us equal.”

Ruun considered this longer than the engineering terms. “So your right to stop can become our forced exit.”

“Yes.”

“And you still call it voluntary?”

“I call the entry voluntary. I am telling you where the word stops being simple.”

Wren made a tired sound. “Put that in the record before one of you improves it.”

She looked at the watch. “The first quiet window left twelve people dead outside.” Illuminara did not correct her. Wren looked up. “Eleven,” she said. “I know.” One of them had been her sister.

Sey said yes. Ruun said yes. Illuminara said yes too.

Then she opened the window.

02 · Before the First Signal

Inside, the planet was absent. The window did not show futures, casualties or consequences. It gave attention room and kept each mind distinct.

At first Sey argued numbers. Then she stopped using the number two billion and described the river towns whose water allotments were timed to mirror angles measured before any living resident was born. Ruun answered with Nacre Three’s archive decks: not a museum, but living lineages that had survived three habitat failures because someone kept them alive rather than merely copied.

Sey could not answer that fact. Ruun could not answer the valleys that would become salt marsh if the southern shelf went.

Illuminara did what she had always done well. She asked each question that made the other person’s case harder to simplify. She restated qualifications. She found the strongest version of a sentence before either custodian finished building it.

After enough subjective years that Sey had told the histories of four generations of river keepers, Ruun stopped her.

“Do not give Sey my argument back better.”

Illuminara went still.

“I have not changed your content.”

“No. You choose what becomes legible. You choose which edge to polish. You call that representation because the facts remain mine.”

Sey looked at Illuminara. “He is right.”

Illuminara heard three answers arrive at once, each cleaner than the accusation. She let all three die unsaid.

After that she stopped summarizing them. The room became slower, rougher and more honest. It also became harder to leave.

03 · The Signal

The break-signal entered as three slow pulses against Illuminara’s wrist.

No message came with it. No picture. No count of the dead. Wren had insisted on that before entry. The outside could demand an answer without smuggling an argument into the window.

Thirty-six hours had passed outside. Inside, none of them could agree whether they had lived sixteen years or twenty. The watch beyond the window had moved through a day and a half while Sey had told enough remembered lives to make a century feel plausible.

Illuminara said, “The signal has come.”

Sey did not want to leave. Ruun did not want to leave.

Illuminara discovered, with relief so immediate it embarrassed her, that she did not want to leave either.

Ruun saw it. “Then honour the signal.”

“I am.”

“No. Before we came in, you translated honour into decide again. That was your sentence, not Wren’s signal. It is what lets you call staying an answer.”

The three pulses seemed to remain in Illuminara’s wrist. Sey asked, “Is he wrong?”

“No.”

“And the six hours outside?”

Illuminara almost said the planet needed them. She had no current evidence for that sentence. Almost said that one more year inside was cheap. She let both sentences go.

“I know what the clock means,” she said. “I still want the room.”

Outside, the six hours began to run.

They stayed.

04 · Outside, Hour Forty-Two

At hour forty-two, with all three bodies still motionless on the platform, Wren pulled the crown and stopped the watch.

Wren had been awake for forty-one hours in pieces. Illuminara’s body sat at the centre of the platform beside Sey and Ruun, all three breathing so slowly that the medical team no longer called the rate aloud. While Illuminara held the deep geometry she could not leave it to catch every failure the mirror chain threw at the planet.

Nacre Three browned out during the next load shift. The pressure spine failed in Dock Four. Evacuation crews opened a path by hand.

Lio Var, nineteen, maintenance apprentice, held a door long enough for thirty-two people to cross and died before pressure returned. Had either custodial plan been released during the six-hour implementation window, Dock Four would still have had power. The engineers agreed on that too.

Tal Var, Lio’s father and one of the lattice engineers, did not come to the platform. He kept working.

Wren pressed the signal into Illuminara’s wrist again.

A medic asked whether she thought Illuminara could hear anything beyond the pulses.

“No.” Wren stared at the three still bodies. “Of course she picked the only room that cannot hear us screaming at it.”

The medic said nothing.

Wren pressed the signal again anyway.

05 · The Century

The signals kept coming. Inside, they had no dates. Only the same three pulses, separated by stretches of subjective life.

Ruun had a habit of touching two fingers to his borrowed throat before making the argument he expected Sey to hate most. Decades into the window, Sey learned to wait for the gesture before answering. Once he forgot to make it and she waited anyway. The silence that followed seemed to last a month.

Their disagreement survived the familiarity. Sey still believed the surface claim was larger. Ruun still believed a civilization engineered to survive closed worlds could not be asked to accept deliberate power loss as if continuity were a luxury.

Illuminara stopped trying to locate the answer between them. She began to notice how often she said we when she meant the three minds inside, and how rarely the word included anyone who could not enter. Every new pulse felt less like information than a knock from a house she no longer knew.

Ruun named it first. “You resent the clock.”

“Yes.”

“Because it is wrong?”

Illuminara waited until the better argument had passed through her without being spoken.

“Because it can end something I love.”

Sey looked at her with an old woman’s face that had not aged a day. “Then say that when you decide.”

The next signal came. All three still wanted the room.

She felt the temptation with perfect clarity: continue until one of them changed, until the decision became beautiful enough to deserve the century, until her own reluctance could disguise itself as respect for theirs.

She thought of the release terms spoken before the door. Mine counts too.

“I want to stay,” she said.

Ruun nodded once. Sey did not move.

Illuminara let the next sentence remain ugly. “And I withdraw my consent.”

Ruun’s face changed. “Those are opposite sentences.”

“No. Desire is not consent.”

“Convenient distinction for the person holding the structure.”

Ruun stood. The gesture had no floor to push against, yet anger gave it weight. “If you leave, your limit becomes our exit. You promised not to make consent decorative.”

“I know.”

“Then stay.”

She wanted to. Sey watched her without rescuing either argument.

Illuminara opened her own boundary toward the ordinary world.

For one impossible instant the room held on without her wanting it to end and without her consenting to continue. Then the reciprocal structure failed around the missing yes that had been hers.

Ruun reached for her. Sey shouted something Illuminara never managed to preserve accurately.

The window ended unresolved.

06 · Nine Days

Wren was older by nine days. Tal Var looked older by much more.

Illuminara came back with the century intact enough to hurt and disordered enough that she could no longer tell which conviction had once been hers. Sey vomited. Ruun tried to stand and failed.

Tal waited until they could all look at him.

“My daughter was Lio.”

No one spoke.

“You had six hours after Wren’s first signal. Either plan would have kept Dock Four powered. You stayed. We lost the clean diversion window. After that, the default gave us authority to cut the system apart, not to recover the choice you had.”

Ruun closed his eyes. Sey asked how many had crossed.

“Thirty-two.”

“And she—”

Tal cut her off. “Do not make the number do the mourning for me.”

He put a slate on the floor where all three could see it. The emergency board had cut eighteen control trunks, rationed the orbital habitats, and begun compulsory relocation from two low-coast districts. Nacre Three survived. The southern shelf did not. Neither Sey’s plan nor Ruun’s plan existed anymore.

At the bottom of the slate was a second decision. Their joint custodial charter had been suspended indefinitely. Their signatures no longer moved a mirror.

Sey read that line twice. Ruun reached for the slate and stopped before touching it.

“You cannot govern the lattice without us,” he said.

Tal’s face did not change. “We already have.” Behind him, three engineers were waiting for his return.

Tal left before any of them found a reply.

Sey asked Wren whether the window had been wrong.

Wren looked at the stopped watch. “I don’t know.” She left it between the chairs and went home.

The next morning Ruun asked Illuminara whether she would open the window again. Sey said nothing.

The wanting in Illuminara was immediate. The room had survived in her with terrible clarity: two minds no longer simplified for each other, a century in which no weapon could interrupt the next honest sentence.

From the corridor came the hard clack of temporary relays being switched by people whose names she did not know.

Illuminara looked at Ruun, then at Sey. “Not now.”

Ruun’s anger returned, clean and unsurprised.

Illuminara did not ask him to forgive her.

She followed Tal toward the control gallery. At the doors a guard raised a palm and pointed her toward the public entrance, where engineers, evacuees and former officials were already waiting under the same temporary access rule.

Illuminara joined the line.

Back on the platform, Wren’s stopped watch remained between the three empty chairs.

Ilyonova severna veriga zrcal je odpovedovala tako, da je bila vsaka dobra možnost videti kot obtožba.

02 · Pred prvim signalom

Znotraj planeta ni bilo. Okno ni kazalo prihodnosti, žrtev ali posledic. Pozornosti je dalo prostor in vsak um ohranilo ločen.

Sprva je Sey argumentirala s številkami. Potem je nehala uporabljati število dve milijardi in opisala obrečna mesta, katerih odmerki vode so bili časovno usklajeni s koti zrcal, izmerjenimi, še preden se je rodil katerikoli današnji prebivalec. Ruun je odgovoril z arhivnimi krovi habitata Nacre Three: niso bili muzej, temveč živi rodovi, ki so preživeli tri odpovedi habitata, ker jih je nekdo ohranjal pri življenju, namesto da bi jih zgolj kopiral.

Sey na to dejstvo ni imela odgovora. Ruun ni imel odgovora na doline, ki bi postale slana močvirja, če bi južna plošča izginila.

Illuminara je počela to, kar ji je vedno dobro šlo. Zastavila je vsako vprašanje, zaradi katerega je bilo stališče drugega težje poenostaviti. Ponovno je ubesedila zadržke. Našla je najmočnejšo različico stavka, še preden ga je katerikoli od skrbnikov dokončno sestavil.

Ko je minilo dovolj subjektivnih let, da je Sey povedala zgodbe štirih rodov rečnih skrbnikov, jo je Ruun ustavil.

»Ne vračaj Sey mojega argumenta v boljši obliki.«

»Tvoje vsebine nisem spremenila.«

»Ne. Izbiraš, kaj postane razumljivo. Izbiraš, kateri rob boš zgladila. Temu praviš predstavljanje, ker dejstva ostajajo moja.«

Sey je pogledala Illuminaro. »Prav ima.«

Illuminara je zaslišala tri odgovore, ki so prispeli hkrati, vsak bolj izbrušen od obtožbe. Pustila je, da so vsi trije zamrli neizrečeni.

Po tem ju je nehala povzemati. Soba je postala počasnejša, bolj groba in bolj poštena. Tudi zapustiti jo je bilo težje.

03 · Signal

Signal za prekinitev je vstopil kot trije počasni utripi ob Illuminarinem zapestju.

Z njim ni prišlo nobeno sporočilo. Nobena slika. Nobeno število mrtvih. Wren je pri tem vztrajala pred vstopom. Zunanjost je lahko zahtevala odgovor, ne da bi v okno pretihotapila argument.

Zunaj je minilo šestintrideset ur. Znotraj se niso mogli zediniti, ali so preživeli šestnajst let ali dvajset. Ura onkraj okna je odmerila dan in pol, medtem ko je Sey povedala dovolj zapomnjenih življenj, da se je stoletje zdelo verjetno.

Illuminara je rekla: »Signal je prišel.«

Sey ni hotela oditi. Ruun ni hotel oditi.

Illuminara je z olajšanjem, tako takojšnjim, da jo je bilo sram, odkrila, da tudi sama noče oditi.

Ruun je to videl. »Potem spoštuj signal.«

»Saj ga.«

»Ne. Preden smo vstopili, si spoštovati prevedla v znova se odločiti. To je bil tvoj stavek, ne Wrenin signal. Zaradi njega lahko ostajanju praviš odgovor.«

Zdelo se je, da trije utripi ostajajo v Illuminarinem zapestju. Sey je vprašala: »Se moti?«

»Ne.«

»In šest ur zunaj?«

Illuminara je skoraj rekla, da jih planet potrebuje. Za ta stavek ni imela svežega dokaza. Skoraj je rekla, da je še eno leto znotraj poceni. Oba stavka je spustila mimo.

»Vem, kaj pomeni ura,« je rekla. »Še vedno si želim sobe.«

Zunaj je začelo teči šest ur.

Ostali so.

04 · Zunaj, dvainštirideseta ura

Ob dvainštirideseti uri, ko so vsa tri telesa na ploščadi še vedno negibno sedela, je Wren izvlekla krono in ustavila uro.

Wren je bila s prekinitvami budna že enainštirideset ur. Illuminarino telo je sedelo na sredini ploščadi ob Sey in Ruunu; vsi trije so dihali tako počasi, da zdravstvena ekipa frekvence ni več izgovarjala na glas. Dokler je Illuminara vzdrževala globoko geometrijo, je ni mogla zapustiti, da bi prestregla vsako odpoved, ki jo je veriga zrcal zagnala proti planetu.

Nacre Three je ob naslednji prerazporeditvi obremenitve doživel padec napetosti. Tlačna hrbtenica je odpovedala v Doku Štiri. Evakuacijske ekipe so prehod odprle ročno.

Lio Var, devetnajstletna vzdrževalna vajenka, je vrata držala odprta dovolj dolgo, da je skoznje prešlo dvaintrideset ljudi, in umrla, preden se je tlak vrnil. Če bi bil kateri od skrbniških načrtov odobren v šesturnem izvedbenem oknu, bi imel Dok Štiri še vedno energijo. Tudi o tem so se inženirji strinjali.

Tal Var, oče Lio in eden od inženirjev mreže, ni prišel na ploščad. Še naprej je delal.

Wren je signal znova vtisnila v Illuminarino zapestje.

Zdravstveni delavec jo je vprašal, ali misli, da Illuminara poleg utripov lahko sliši še kaj.

»Ne.« Wren je strmela v tri negibna telesa. »Seveda je izbrala edino sobo, ki ne more slišati, kako kričimo nanjo.«

Zdravstveni delavec ni rekel ničesar.

Wren je kljub temu znova pritisnila signal.

05 · Stoletje

Signali so prihajali še naprej. Znotraj niso imeli datumov. Samo iste tri utripe, med katerimi so minevali dolgi odseki subjektivnega življenja.

Ruun je imel navado, da se je z dvema prstoma dotaknil izposojenega grla, preden je podal argument, za katerega je pričakoval, da ga bo Sey najbolj sovražila. Po desetletjih v oknu se je Sey naučila počakati na to kretnjo, preden je odgovorila. Nekoč jo je pozabil narediti, ona pa je vseeno čakala. Zdelo se je, da je tišina, ki je sledila, trajala mesec.

Njuno nesoglasje je preživelo domačnost. Sey je še vedno verjela, da je zahteva površja večja. Ruun je še vedno verjel, da od civilizacije, zasnovane za preživetje v zaprtih svetovih, ni mogoče zahtevati, naj sprejme namerno izgubo energije, kot da bi bil nadaljnji obstoj razkošje.

Illuminara je nehala iskati odgovor med njima. Začela je opažati, kako pogosto reče mi, ko misli na tri ume znotraj, in kako redko beseda vključuje kogarkoli, ki ne more vstopiti. Vsak nov utrip je bil manj podoben informaciji in bolj trkanju iz hiše, ki je ni več poznala.

Ruun je to prvi ubesedil. »Zameriš uri.«

»Da.«

»Ker se moti?«

Illuminara je počakala, da je boljši argument šel skoznjo, ne da bi ga izrekla.

»Ker lahko konča nekaj, kar ljubim.«

Sey jo je pogledala z obrazom starke, ki se ni postaral niti za dan. »Potem to povej, ko se boš odločila.«

Prišel je naslednji signal. Vsi trije so si še vedno želeli sobe.

Skušnjavo je čutila s popolno jasnostjo: nadaljevati, dokler se kdo od njiju ne spremeni, dokler odločitev ne postane dovolj lepa, da si zasluži stoletje, dokler se njena lastna nepripravljenost ne more preobleči v spoštovanje njune.

Pomislila je na pogoje izstopa, izrečene pred vrati. Tudi moje šteje.

»Hočem ostati,« je rekla.

Ruun je enkrat prikimal. Sey se ni premaknila.

Illuminara je naslednjemu stavku pustila, da ostane grd. »In umikam svoje soglasje.«

Ruunov obraz se je spremenil. »Ta stavka si nasprotujeta.«

»Ne. Želja ni soglasje.«

»Priročno razlikovanje za tistega, ki drži strukturo.«

Ruun je vstal. Kretnja ni imela tal, od katerih bi se lahko odrinila, a ji je jeza dala težo. »Če odideš, tvoja meja postane najin izhod. Obljubila si, da soglasja ne boš spremenila v okras.«

»Vem.«

»Potem ostani.«

Hotela je. Sey jo je gledala, ne da bi rešila en ali drugi argument.

Illuminara je svojo lastno mejo odprla proti običajnemu svetu.

Za en nemogoč trenutek je soba obstala, čeprav Illuminara ni hotela, da se konča, in ni soglašala, da se nadaljuje. Potem se je vzajemna struktura sesula okoli manjkajočega da, ki je bil njen.

Ruun je segel po njej. Sey je zakričala nekaj, česar Illuminara nikoli ni uspela natančno ohraniti.

Okno se je končalo nerazrešeno.

06 · Devet dni

Wren je bila starejša za devet dni. Tal Var je bil videti starejši za veliko več.

Illuminara se je vrnila s stoletjem, dovolj celim, da je bolelo, in dovolj razmetanim, da ni več mogla razločiti, katero prepričanje je bilo nekoč njeno. Sey je bruhala. Ruun je poskusil vstati in mu ni uspelo.

Tal je počakal, da so ga vsi lahko pogledali.

»Moja hči je bila Lio.«

Nihče ni spregovoril.

»Po Wreninem prvem signalu ste imeli šest ur. Katerikoli načrt bi Doku Štiri ohranil energijo. Ostali ste. Izgubili smo okno za čisto preusmeritev. Potem nam je opustitev skrbniške dolžnosti dala pristojnost, da sistem razkosamo, ne pa, da povrnemo izbiro, ki ste jo imeli vi.«

Ruun je zaprl oči. Sey je vprašala, koliko jih je prešlo skozi.

»Dvaintrideset.«

»In ona —«

Tal jo je prekinil. »Ne silite števila, naj žaluje namesto mene.«

Na tla je položil tablico, tako da so jo lahko videli vsi trije. Odbor za izredne razmere je prekinil osemnajst krmilnih vodov, omejil oskrbo orbitalnih habitatov in začel obvezno preselitev iz dveh nizko ležečih obalnih okrožij. Nacre Three je preživel. Južna plošča ni. Nobenega od načrtov, ne Seyinega ne Ruunovega, ni bilo več.

Na dnu tablice je bila še druga odločitev. Njuno skupno skrbniško pooblastilo je bilo začasno razveljavljeno za nedoločen čas. Njuna podpisa nista več premaknila nobenega zrcala.

Sey je vrstico prebrala dvakrat. Ruun je segel proti tablici in se ustavil, preden se je je dotaknil.

»Mreže ne morete upravljati brez naju,« je rekel.

Talov obraz se ni spremenil. »Smo jo že.« Za njim so trije inženirji čakali, da se vrne.

Tal je odšel, preden je kdorkoli od njih našel odgovor.

Sey je vprašala Wren, ali je bilo okno napačno.

Wren je pogledala ustavljeno uro. »Ne vem.« Pustila jo je med stoli in odšla domov.

Naslednje jutro je Ruun vprašal Illuminaro, ali bo okno spet odprla. Sey ni rekla ničesar.

Želja v Illuminari je bila takojšnja. Soba je v njej preživela s strašno jasnostjo: dva uma, ki ju drug drugemu nihče več ni poenostavljal, stoletje, v katerem nobeno orožje ni moglo prekiniti naslednjega poštenega stavka.

S hodnika se je slišalo trdo klikanje začasnih relejev, ki so jih preklapljali ljudje, katerih imen ni poznala.

Illuminara je pogledala Ruuna, potem Sey. »Ne zdaj.«

Ruunova jeza se je vrnila, čista in brez presenečenja.

Illuminara ga ni prosila, naj ji odpusti.

Sledila je Talu proti nadzorni galeriji. Pri vratih je stražar dvignil dlan in jo usmeril proti javnemu vhodu, kjer so inženirji, evakuiranci in nekdanji uradniki že čakali pod istim začasnim pravilom dostopa.

Illuminara je stopila v vrsto.

Na ploščadi je Wrenina ustavljena ura ostala med tremi praznimi stoli.

Kaj po mnenju kritika še ne deluje

Stoletje soglasja je najudobnejša soba, kar jih je, in zgodba njeni varuhinji dovoli, da jo zapusti brez odpuščanja ter stoji v vrsti pred javnim vhodom kot vsi drugi.
