A habitat circling a white dwarf lives by a shield that must be open for forty-seven seconds of lethal light, and the shield has started to open late. Every motor passes its test, the next pass is nine hours away, and the only fast fix is wired into the telescope Illuminara came aboard to look through.

01 · The Count

Ninefold Station, Commons and observatory gallery · nine hours and four minutes before periapsis

Nine hours and four minutes before periapsis, forty children in the Commons of Ninefold Station practised the count with the lights on, which everyone agreed was cheating.

On the night of a pass the lights went out for forty-seven seconds. The shield closed over the sky and over Ember, and two hundred and twenty thousand people sang the numbers to one another in the dark so that nobody had to spend those seconds alone. The very young took the low numbers. The old took the high ones, where the singing thinned, and the children had learned to lean toward the old voices and carry them.

Illuminara had come aboard two days earlier as the guest of Chief Astronomer Rashida Quill, to watch the Kettle cross the face of Ember: a ragged rock, once a moon, that did it once in eleven years and would do it again in two hours and fourteen minutes. She had spent the morning in the observatory gallery above the Commons, listening to the choir through the floor and to the station through the soles of her feet. Ninefold carried its loads like a held breath. She had been learning its grammar for two days: the long groan of the spine as the wheel turned, the small bright tick of the shield's folded petals at their hinges.

The choir mistressAgain, from the thirties. Don’t rush the thirties.

Rashida’s wrist chimed. She listened, said.

RashidaThat is your shield, Teodor.

listened again, and her mouth went flat.

RashidaIt is also my spine. Yes.

She looked at Illuminara.

RashidaThe Flight Director has a timing problem and wants someone who reads structure. Come. If it touches the spine, I will want you to have heard it first.

Below them forty voices reached thirty-one and rushed it.

02 · Everything Passes

Ninefold Station, Operations · before the heater test

The shield hung in the middle of Operations as a model of light: twenty-four petals around the spine, folded like the ribs of a closed umbrella, each with its own motor, its own controller, its own small green lamp. All twenty-four lamps were green.

Teodor AnsahEvery actuator test passes.

said Flight Director Teodor Ansah. He did not sound relieved.

Teodor AnsahEvery controller test passes. Dov, show her the part that doesn’t.

Dov Kalani, who ran controls and had audited every pass for nineteen years, put four numbers on the glass. They were the spread between the first petal to fire and the last on each of the previous four passes: 0.9 seconds, 2.6, 5.1, 8.2.

DovThe lean burn finishes thirty seconds before the flux arrives. No petal may move before that, or the wheels saturate. A petal needs nineteen seconds to open, so none may start later than nineteen seconds before the flux. That leaves eleven seconds in which all twenty-four have to start. The spread is the width of the group. The window is eleven.

He drew the fit through his four points, and it climbed past the line.

DovEleven point nine, next pass. Last time the slowest petals finished less than three seconds before the light. This time they won’t, and the wheels will be fighting the opening of a shield that’s lopsided by more than they can hold.

Someone at the backCommand it earlier.

IlluminaraThat slides the group along the line. It is already wider than the window. Starting earlier only chooses which edge breaks first.

Ansah ticked off what was left. Replacing the controllers would take four weeks. The lifeboats held twelve thousand. He said the second number the way a man says something he has already said to himself in the dark.

Illuminara asked for the force records from the last pass, not the timing logs. A shield opening is a set of loads travelling through a spine, and the spine remembered them.

IlluminaraThey did not open in a scatter.

she said after a minute.

IlluminaraThey opened in order. Petal one, then two, then three. Every petal a little later than its neighbour, all the way round.

DovRadiation. The offset registers get hit when we cross the belt. I scrubbed eleven of them two orbits ago.

He said it without heat. He had been right about registers before.

IlluminaraThen let’s each say what we would see.

Illuminara said, and wrote it on the glass where it could not be revised. If registers were flipping, the delays would come in the register’s own steps, jumps of a half, a whole, two seconds. They would belong to particular petals and wander from run to run. If it was the chain that carried the trigger, the delay would be a smooth ramp that followed the direction the trigger travelled, and turned round when the trigger did.

IlluminaraThe cheapest question is which way the ramp leans when we run it backwards.

Illuminara looked at the lamps.

IlluminaraWhat is the same for every petal?

DovThe order.

Dov said, as if she were rhetorical.

EwaIt’s the wave.

said a voice from the doorway.

Pilar Ndoye, who led the outside crews, had come in with a dosimeter case; her daughter had come in behind her, because the school had been cleared for the alert. Ewa was nine and did not think the room belonged to anyone in particular.

EwaIn the stadium, you stand up when the person next to you stands up. So the ones far from the start are late. They’re not slow. They’re waiting. If you start it from the other side the late ones are the other ones.

Dov opened his mouth.

DovEach controller verifies its neighbour’s order before it forwards it.

DovSo no petal can fire on a corrupted command.

IlluminaraThen it is a wave. Thank you, Ewa. That is the mechanism and the test, and I had only the test.

03 · Twenty-Six Minutes

Ninefold Station · the twenty-six-minute reserve window

The trace heaters could bring the ring up to its approach temperature in fourteen minutes. The station’s reserve would pay for twenty-six minutes of them and not one more, because after that the reserve fell below what the shield motors needed at periapsis. Ansah put that on the wall as a countdown. Nobody argued with it.

Dov wrote his own prediction beside Illuminara’s, in his own hand: if registers, the delay will not reverse. He signed it.

The runs were dry. No petal moved; the controllers only logged when the pulse arrived. At fourteen minutes the ring was warm, and Dov launched the pulse clockwise from the hub. Petal one logged at zero. Petal twenty-four logged at thirteen point four seconds. Between them the delays climbed in a ramp with small stairs in it, one stair at every connector. The shape favoured a travelling chain, but a fixed staircase of damaged register offsets could still imitate it. They left the retry counters and connector diagnostics sealed until the directional test was complete.

The second run, clockwise again, came back at thirteen point one, with the same stairs in the same places. Repeatability weakened the idea of fresh random flips, but a stable set of corrupted offsets could still have produced the pattern. The two written causes remained on the glass.

At minute twenty-one Ansah said.

Teodor AnsahYou have five minutes, and a run takes three and a half. I would rather bank them.

Dov looked at the glass, at his signature, at the fit he had drawn with such care. He knew what the third run was for. If the ramp leaned the other way, his registers were innocent and he had been wrong in front of the room. If it did not, he was right and the station had a much worse night coming.

DovSpend them.

he said, and launched the pulse counter-clockwise.

Petal twenty-four logged at zero. Petal one logged at thirteen point three.

Nobody said anything. Dov rubbed his eyes with the heel of one hand and then picked up the marker and drew a line through his own prediction.

DovIt is the chain.

he said. Only then did he unseal the retry counters. At each of the twenty-three forwards, the warmed connector had required an average of six repeat reads at ninety-five milliseconds apiece, plus twelve milliseconds to verify and forward: twenty-three times five hundred and eighty-two milliseconds, thirteen point three eight six seconds. Pilar checked the multiplication and circled thirteen point four.

DovMy registers are fine. Thank you for making me prove it.

They ended the run at twenty-four minutes and thirty seconds, with ninety seconds of reserve to spare, and turned the heaters off.

04 · The Quiet Spokes

Operations and observatory gallery · seven hours before periapsis

Teodor AnsahThere is no such trigger on this station.

Rashida looked at the shield, at the twenty-four lamps, at the four numbers on the glass, and then, for some reason, at the girl in the doorway, who had gone quiet.

RashidaMy conditions. The star comes back to me the moment the shield can stand without it. The permanent repair is paid for out of the shield’s own budget, so that the array is never borrowed a second time. And I turn the key myself.

Teodor AnsahAgreed.

before Illuminara could, and added his own. The old chain would stay in place as a backup, and each petal would fire on whichever trigger reached it first. Every jumper would be pulse-tested twice before it was accepted. Pilar said her crews would decide for themselves, and went to ask them. They decided in four minutes. Rashida turned the key at seven hours before periapsis, ten minutes before the Kettle touched the edge of Ember. The array’s mirrors let go of one another with a single soft note, like a hand leaving a table. She stood in the gallery listening to it die and did not look at the sky.

05 · Forty-Seven Seconds

Ninefold Station, hub and Commons · before and during periapsis

The jumpers went in by hand, in the glare and the cold, one station at a time. Two failed their first pulse test and were done again. By one hour and ten minutes before periapsis all twenty-four had passed twice, and Dov ran the full rehearsal on the real clock. The spread across the star came back at four tenths of a second.

That was the part that could be tested. There was another part that could not. The chain had always staggered the petals, a slow ripple that spread their reaction torque over eight seconds. The star would start all twenty-four together. For the first time since the shield was commissioned, every petal would push on the spine in the same instant, and every push would come down through a single place: the hub bearing, where the shield’s twenty-four torques added up.

DovOn paper the bearing holds. With eleven per cent to spare. Nobody has seen that load. Not in rehearsal, not ever.

IlluminaraI can feel that bearing from here. It is the one place everything sums, which makes it the only place I have to be. I won’t touch the shield, and I won’t touch the wheels. For the first four seconds, while the torque peaks, I will lend the bearing some stiffness. After four seconds the wheels have it. If they read over sixty per cent saturation by then, nothing I could add would matter.

Teodor AnsahFour seconds. Not five.

She stood with one hand on the bearing housing, which was cold and faintly alive, and Pilar’s outside crews watched from the lock with their helmets off. In the Commons the choir sat in the dark early, because the children had insisted.

At thirty seconds before the flux the lean burn ended and the star pulsed. Twenty-three lamps went amber together, and twenty-three petals leaned out of their folds on the same breath. Station seventeen stayed dark and folded. The bearing took the other twenty-three like a fist taking a blow. Illuminara closed on it, felt the load climb and give, a sound that was almost a word. At four seconds the saturation read forty-one per cent. The reading did not prove her stiffness mattered; it proved only that the wheels stayed inside the abort bound.

She let go.

DovStation seventeen. Station seventeen is dark.

The jumper had held through every test. It had lost its seat in the shudder of the lean burn. Petal seventeen stood folded while twenty-three opened around it, and Dov’s eyes were on the old chain, the backup nobody had wanted to rely on. A trigger was crawling toward seventeen, hop by hop and retry by retry.

It reached the petal nine point three seconds after the first. The window was eleven. Seventeen began to open at twenty and seven tenths seconds before the flux, and finished at one point seven seconds before it.

The sky closed over Ember with one point seven seconds of margin.

In the dark, the Commons sang. The very young took the low numbers and the old held the high ones, and somewhere around thirty-one the children did not rush.

06 · The Loop

Ninefold Station · the following orbit and repair passes

The dosimeters read what they read on a quiet night. Two hundred and twenty thousand people had sat in the dark for forty-seven seconds, and the light that would have found them had been stopped by twenty-four petals that opened in time.

The station spent the next orbit paying for it. The heaters and the outside work had eaten the reserve, and for nine hours the Commons ran at half lighting and the recyclers ran in shifts. People complained, and then lined up to carry water to the old ones on the upper decks. Nobody was hurt.

The Kettle crossed Ember with no one watching it. Rashida never said what that was like, and Illuminara did not ask. They found each other in the gallery the next morning, and Rashida said only.

RashidaEleven years. Bring a chair.

Dov found the permanent repair at four in the morning and brought it to Illuminara.

DovThe chain takes about thirteen point four seconds. So give it fourteen seconds to carry an appointment, not a trigger.

He drew the return fibre as a ruler. Paired pulses before each pass would calibrate every controller around the warm ring. Fourteen seconds before burn end—six tenths longer than the measured delay—the hub would name burn end.

DovEvery petal knows when before it has to act. No one waits for a signal after firing time.

IlluminaraWhat did you ask yourself?

DovWhat is the same for every petal.

He frowned at it.

DovYou asked me that, in Operations. I thought you were being rhetorical.

The return fibre was a spare two-kilometre coil. Pilar rested her crews, then spliced it along the hot side. Ansah demanded a shadow pass: its appointments and the star trigger agreed within a tenth of a second, and every packet arrived early. On the third pass it flew. The star stayed dark, the jumpers came out, the telescope got its sky back, and the shield opened on the calibrated ring with a spread of six tenths.

Illuminara watched it from the Commons, in the dark, with a small girl’s hand in hers, holding nothing at all. That had been the point of the work.

At forty-six, Ewa let go to clap, and the choir lost the count, and found it, and finished.

In the morning there was a drawing pinned to the observatory door: a ring of petals in blue crayon, a very long line of stick people with their arms up, and, in careful capitals, THE WAVE STARTS WHERE YOU PUT IT. Dov read it twice, tapped the words, and said.

DovThat was the problem.

03 · Šestindvajset minut
