The Gatekeeper She Finished The Sentences OfVaruh vrat, ki mu je dokončevala stavke

2026-10-30

Illuminara reads a failing tide-gate faster than its keeper can describe it, and she is right three times and wrong once. With a surge thirty hours away and a town of nine thousand behind the gate, she has to learn to wait for the one sentence she cannot finish.Illuminara okvarjajoča se plimna vrata razbere hitreje, kot jih njihov varuh lahko opiše, in trikrat ima prav, enkrat pa se moti. Ker je morski udar oddaljen trideset ur, za vrati pa je mesto z devet tisoč prebivalci, se mora naučiti počakati na edini stavek, ki ga ne more dokončati.

01 · The Fourth Sentence

Ostmark Tide-Gate, the gate-house · day one, 15:40

Halvard Beck began his sentences the way other men began walks: slowly, and without any promise of arriving. He stood at the gate-house window with one hand flat on the iron rail, a rail worn pale by fifty years of that hand, and below him the gate hung in the channel, fourteen metres of dark steel that thirty hours from now would have to hold back the sea.

Illuminara had been in the delta for twenty minutes. She had laid her palm on the rail beside his, and the whole leaf had come up through the iron the way a score comes up through a baton: two screws, a chain, the weight of the channel on every rivet. She did not need to be told where it hurt.

Halvard BeckThe right screw has been—

IlluminaraCarrying more than the left since September. There's a crack in the upper bearing no wider than a hair. Yes.

Runa Beck looked up from her laptop. She was his granddaughter, thirty-four, an engineer, and she had met Illuminara at the ferry and not stopped talking since.

Runa BeckThat's the inspection report, word for word. It took the contractor two weeks.

Halvard's mouth closed and opened again.

Halvard BeckThe chain, when it gets that note—

IlluminaraIt's the pawl, not the chain. The seat has worn. I can feel where.

Halvard BeckAnd the leaf wants to go—

IlluminaraNorth on the ebb. Eleven millimetres.

Runa checked the gauge. Eleven. Each time Halvard stopped a little sooner, like a man reaching for a stair that someone had already set under his foot.

Halvard BeckAnd the left shoe, where it sits—

IlluminaraOn the sill. The seat stones are sound and the load goes straight down through them. That's the one thing here nobody needs to worry about.

She had been right about the stone. It was a good reading.

Halvard took his hand from the rail. It was the first time she had seen the pale patch bare.

Halvard BeckOn the sill. Yes.

IlluminaraThen we square the leaf and bring both screws down level, and I carry the cracked bearing while Runa closes. We test it on the small tide at twenty to six. If it shows us anything, we have a whole day to fix it.

Runa glanced at her grandfather. He was looking at the channel.

Runa BeckGrandfather?

Halvard BeckShe is very good.

He said it to the window, in the tone he would have used for the weather. Illuminara noticed it, and filed it as agreement.

02 · Fisher's Row

Fisher's Row, Ostmark · day one, 18:25

The closure was perfect for eighteen minutes. The leaf came down square on both heels at 17:52, the load cells agreed with Illuminara to the third decimal, and the town's lowest street looked at a two-metre tide and stayed dry. At 18:10 the left corner began to hiss. A jet the width of a hand came out from under the leaf and cut a groove along the seat, and by the time Illuminara had taken the corner in both arms and shut it, Fisher's Row had thirty centimetres of the channel in its cellars.

The street was full of people carrying mattresses upstairs. Nobody was hurt.

Idun Maas, chair of the delta works committee, stood ankle-deep in waders with the tide table crushed in her fist.

Idun MaasYou told me square was safe.

IlluminaraI told you the stone was sound, and I read the load as stone. There is something under that left seat that carries weight like stone and isn't. I didn't ask the man who knows what it is.

Idun MaasThe surge is tomorrow, twenty to ten. Nine thousand people. I'm grateful you can hold a gate for a night. I can't buy you for three nights of storm, and I won't hang this town on one visitor or one old man behind a locked door. The array goes in by hand at noon.

Runa BeckIt has no data, Idun. It can't learn that seat in a day.

Idun MaasThen get me data, Runa. Or get me a man who answers his door.

Up the hill, the shutters of the gate-house were closing, one at a time. Runa found Illuminara at the bottom of the street, where the water ended.

Runa BeckHe did this once before. The day I put the array drawings on the council table. A month he talked to me about soup. I drew it so he'd never climb that ladder in a storm again, and he heard it as me cutting his name into a stone.

IlluminaraDo you think he's finished?

Runa BeckI think he's seventy-nine and he's the only one who knows. Both of those are true, and neither lets me sleep.

Illuminara knew the next three things Runa would say, and in what order. She closed her mouth on them. It felt like a muscle she had never used.

03 · The Step

Ostmark Tide-Gate, the gate-house step · night one, 23:05

She sat down on the top step outside the locked door and spoke to the wood.

IlluminaraI finished your sentences. Four times this afternoon. Three were right. The fourth was wrong, and I said it exactly as I'd said the other three, as if I'd read it off the stone. Fisher's Row paid for that. I'm not asking you to forgive it. I'll be on this step as long as I need to be, and now and then I'll ask you something. You don't have to answer. I won't tell you what you were going to say.

Nothing came back but the channel. At midnight Runa climbed up with a flask, sat two steps below, and said nothing either. Every few minutes the gate's hum came up through the stone into Illuminara's bones, and each time she knew what it meant a half-second before she wanted to.

At half past one she asked her first question.

IlluminaraWhen you close the gate, what do you listen for first?

The silence went on so long that Runa shifted on her step. Then the voice came through the door, slow, each word walked there from a long way off.

Halvard BeckWhether the water has made up its mind.

At twenty past two, with no question put to him, the voice came through the door again.

Halvard BeckThree times you were right, and the one time you weren't it sounded the same. That is the part I can't stand.

He let the channel have a minute.

Halvard BeckWhen the end of my sentence is in your mouth before it is in mine, I don't know what is left for me to be in the room.

She had an answer. It was true, and well made: that the gate was his, that her reading was only his fifty years in the iron handed back to him. She saw that saying it would be the fifth finished sentence, one more person explaining him to himself, and she said nothing. Two steps below, Runa let out a breath that was almost a laugh and was not.

At three o'clock she asked her second question, the only kind of thing she had offered all night: one he could decline.

IlluminaraAnd the left foot? What does it say that the right doesn't?

Halvard BeckTray.

That was all, for forty minutes. Then, at ten past four, in pieces:

Halvard BeckLike a tray on a table that isn't flat. Hollow. And flat.

And she knew. The sentences assembled themselves at once: a hollow note, a seat carrying its load through something with air in it, silt, scour under the head of water. She had it whole. It sat in her mouth like a coin, and she kept her teeth together on it for the longest minute of that night, while Runa turned and looked at her, and saw what it cost, and looked away.

Halvard BeckThe left foot is standing on something asleep.

He stopped. She waited. The channel breathed twice.

Halvard BeckIt wakes on the ebb. You hear it wake: the note comes up a third. If you put the leaf down square, the water comes and scours the sleeper out from under it. If you hold the left foot a hand off, and let the water get to the gate first, the water leans the foot down onto the sleeper and packs it. Then it's a floor.

Her version would have ended at the scour. His went one step further, to where the water's own weight did the building, a step her sense of force, which read what pushed and not when, would not have reached in a year.

IlluminaraThank you.

It was all she said. The bolt slid back at half past four.

Halvard BeckYou write nothing until I say.

IlluminaraNothing until you say.

He looked at his granddaughter, then at the east, where the sky was greying, and went in to put the kettle on.

04 · Two Notes

Ostmark Tide-Gate, the platform · day two, 07:45

The leaf hung a hand above the sill, the listening height, with the ebb running under it and the steel singing on its chains. Runa had clamped a contact microphone to the left guide and rowed the punt out to the seat with a sounding rod and marked stakes. Illuminara sat on the platform with her hands in her lap. She had been told she could sit anywhere, so long as she didn't touch the rail.

Halvard BeckTwelve past eight, the left note comes up a third. The sleeper will be half what it is now.

Runa wrote the time on her wrist in marker before he had finished saying it. At 07:50 she read the crest of the tongue against the stakes: twenty-two centimetres proud of the sill. Illuminara had held the same prediction since half past four. Waiting for it to come true in his mouth and not in hers was like standing on one foot.

At 08:12 the note rose. Runa's tuner read 1.26; a true third is 1.25. At 08:20 the rod read eleven centimetres.

Runa BeckHalf. To the centimetre.

Halvard BeckSlack water is twenty to eleven. The note splits in two then, one high, one flat. And the sleeper walks. A forearm toward the middle of the channel.

IlluminaraI'd have said it stays where it is. I can't see what would move it.

Runa BeckGood. Say it before, not after.

Runa wrote it on her other wrist: I — stays. H — walks.

At 10:40 the note split into two pitches, one bright and one flat, like a voice and its shadow. The rod found the crest thirty-one centimetres toward the middle, a forearm and a thumb. Two for two, and both times written down before the words were finished.

Runa BeckIt walked.

She held out the wrist that said I — stays so that Illuminara could see it.

IlluminaraI'd have been wrong about the one thing this town needs to know.

Halvard did not gloat. He wiped his hands on his coat.

Halvard BeckWrite it, Runa. Under both names.

05 · The Crooked Gate

Ostmark Tide-Gate, the platform · day two, 15:30

The crates had stayed shut at noon. Idun Maas had come to the platform instead, and she read Runa's notes twice, copied out clean from the wrists, and handed them back as if they were warm.

Idun MaasYou want me to close a gate crooked, with nine thousand people behind it, on the strength of an old man's ear.

Halvard BeckYes.

Runa BeckTwo for two, measured, written first. The array couldn't give you one. It's the only real data anyone has had about that seat all week.

Idun MaasThen I'll tell you what I'm afraid of. That I'll remember tonight as the night we were lucky.

Halvard BeckThen build the dike as well.

Maas looked at him for a long time. Then she took a pencil and wrote an order on the back of the tide table. The two lowest streets emptied by nine. Volunteers on the Fisher's Row dike from five. And if the note had not split by ten past ten, Halvard's trim was not used: the main leaf stayed open, the men at the Salt Meadow bank cut it, and the surge was let to spill across six hundred hectares of pasture and the cannery yard, where it would drown hay and sheds and nothing else. Everyone in Ostmark lived above the line the Meadow water could not reach.

Idun MaasForty farms' winter hay and the cannery floor. That is what your ear costs if it is wrong. I can pay that. I can't pay for nine thousand.

She signed the order, and made him sign it, and Runa too.

Idun MaasAnd you, Illuminara? What do I write for you?

IlluminaraThe right bearing. It cracked in September and it will carry the heel all night, so I'll carry it. Nothing else. I won't touch the left screw, and I won't touch the rail unless Halvard asks.

Idun MaasThat I can write.

06 · The Note

Ostmark Tide-Gate, the gate-house · day two, 21:35

The sea arrived early, as it does. The leaf hung at the listening height with the water standing two metres proud on its seaward face. Fisher's Row was dark and empty behind its dike of sandbags and lanterns. In the gate-house Halvard sat in the chair he had sat in since 1976, one hand on the rail, his eyes closed. Runa stood at the left wheel. On her wrist, written before dark from the tide table and his two notes, was 21:55–22:00; nobody had asked her for it. Illuminara stood at the right housing with both palms on the metal, taking the cracked bearing's load into her forearms and down through her feet into the platform. It was the one thing she was certain of.

Halvard BeckNot yet.

At 21:52 the sound under the leaf changed, the way a room changes when a crowd takes one breath. The water had made up its mind. At 21:58 the left note, which had been a tray, came up a third. Illuminara heard it. For the first time in two days she heard exactly what he heard and had nothing to add.

Runa BeckIt's up a third. It's splitting.

Halvard BeckNow.

Runa let the left screw run; she had called it three seconds before he did, and neither of them said so. The leaf came down on its right heel, held its left foot a hand above the silt while the sea leaned on it, and was pressed down onto the sleeper, slowly, with a long creak like a ship's timber. A thin whistle went across the gate-house, a kettle at the far end of a house, and thinned, and stopped.

At 22:10 the deadline on Maas's order passed with the gate holding. Out on the Meadow road the men at the bank put their bars down, and on the dike Maas lowered her lantern and gave no order. The surge crested at 23:12, three metres forty above the lowest doorsteps, and stood against the leaf like a hand against a door. Nothing came through.

At midnight Illuminara let go of the bearing and found she could not feel her arms. Afterward, not before, she read the loads off to Runa, and they said what Halvard had said.

07 · The Rule

Ostmark, the Beck kitchen · day three, 07:10

Fisher's Row had pumped its cellars by daybreak. Idun Maas had come by with a claim form: the street would be paid from the works budget, because it had been the council's gate and the council's visitor and the council's rehearsal. The first page of the manual lay on the table in Runa's hand: Trimming the Ostmark gate on its heel — H. Beck, R. Beck. Its last line read: No keeper is certified for this gate until she has written three notes down before they sounded, and been right, in front of a witness who is not the teacher. Under it was the first line of a changed specification for the array still in its crates: The array reports what the gate says. It is taught by the people who hear it, and he is the first. Maas had initialled it twice.

Runa and her grandfather had not talked about the drawings. They had written a page together.

Runa, at the sink, did not turn round.

Runa BeckSixth flat this morning. The sleeper's repacked itself. I'll check the rod at nine.

Halvard BeckA little under a sixth.

He did not look pleased, and he did not look cross. He looked like a man who had been read back to himself correctly.

Halvard BeckYou may sit in the gate-house—

Illuminara knew where the sentence was going. She did not go there. She counted the channel breathing, twice.

Halvard Beck—when you ask first. Not at night. And you will let me get to the end.

IlluminaraI will.

He had not forgiven the afternoon. He had given her a rule, which was better, because a rule could be kept. Below the window the gate hung a hand above its sill and sang on the ebb in the grey light.

Halvard BeckHear that?

IlluminaraNo. Tell me.

He did, in his own order, and it took until the tea was cold.

What the critic still says doesn't work

Illuminara repairs the harm of finishing Halvard's sentences by learning to wait for the knowledge her force-sense cannot supply; Halvard, Runa and Maas each retain authority, the gate holds under a measured and life-safe plan, and the craft becomes teachable without erasing its first keeper.

Tonight's shape

Seven scenes across thirty-eight hours: four finished sentences and one wrong, a flooded street, a night on a step outside a locked door, two predictions tested against a rod and a tuner, a council order with a flood meadow as its fallback, a surge, and a breakfast with conditions.

01 · Četrti stavek

Plimna vrata Ostmark, hišica ob vratih · prvi dan, 15:40

Halvard Beck je svoje stavke začenjal tako, kot drugi moški začenjajo sprehode: počasi in brez vsake obljube, da bo prispel. Stal je ob oknu hišice ob vratih, z eno dlanjo plosko na železni ograji, ki jo je petdeset let te dlani obrabilo do svetlega, pod njim pa je v kanalu visela zapornica, štirinajst metrov temnega jekla, ki bo moralo čez trideset ur zadržati morje.

Illuminara je bila v delti dvajset minut. Dlan je položila na ograjo poleg njegove in celotna zapornica se je po železu dvignila vanjo, kakor se partitura dvigne skozi dirigentsko paličico: dva vijaka, veriga, teža kanala na vsaki zakovici. Ni ji bilo treba povedati, kje boli.

Halvard BeckDesni vijak je—

IlluminaraOd septembra nosil več kot levi. V zgornjem ležaju je razpoka, nič širša od lasu. Da.

Runa Beck je pogledala od prenosnika. Bila je njegova vnukinja, štiriintridesetletna inženirka, ki je Illuminari prišla naproti k trajektu in odtlej ni nehala govoriti.

Runa BeckTo je poročilo o pregledu, besedo za besedo. Izvajalec je zanj potreboval dva tedna.

Halvard je zaprl usta in jih znova odprl.

Halvard BeckVeriga, ko zadene tisti ton—

IlluminaraZatikalo je, ne veriga. Ležišče je obrabljeno. Čutim, kje.

Halvard BeckIn zapornico vleče—

IlluminaraProti severu ob oseki. Enajst milimetrov.

Runa je preverila merilnik. Enajst. Halvard se je vsakič ustavil nekoliko prej, kakor človek, ki seže po stopnici, ki mu jo je nekdo že postavil pod nogo.

Halvard BeckIn levi čevelj, kjer nalega—

IlluminaraNa prag. Kamni ležišča so trdni in obremenitev gre naravnost navzdol skoznje. To je edina stvar tukaj, zaradi katere nikomur ni treba skrbeti.

Glede kamna je imela prav. Razbrala ga je dobro.

Halvard je umaknil roko z ograje. Prvič je videla razgaljeno svetlo liso.

Halvard BeckNa pragu. Da.

IlluminaraPotem poravnamo zapornico in oba vijaka spustimo na isto raven, jaz pa prevzamem počeni ležaj, medtem ko Runa zapira. Preizkusimo jo ob mali plimi ob dvajset do šestih. Če nam kaj pokaže, imamo ves dan, da to popravimo.

Runa je pogledala dedka. Gledal je kanal.

Runa BeckDedek?

Halvard BeckZelo dobra je.

Rekel je oknu, s tonom, ki bi ga uporabil za vreme. Illuminara je to opazila in shranila kot soglasje.

02 · Ribiška ulica

Ribiška ulica, Ostmark · prvi dan, 18:25

Zaprtje je bilo osemnajst minut popolno. Zapornica se je ob 17:52 spustila enakomerno na obe peti, merilne celice so se z Illuminaro ujemale do tretje decimalke in najnižja mestna ulica je pogledala dvometrsko plimo ter ostala suha. Ob 18:10 je levi kot začel sikati. Izpod zapornice je brizgnil curek v širini dlani in vzdolž ležišča izdolbel brazdo; do takrat, ko je Illuminara objela kot z obema rokama in ga zaprla, je imela Ribiška ulica v kleteh trideset centimetrov kanala.

Ulica je bila polna ljudi, ki so nosili vzmetnice v nadstropje. Nihče ni bil poškodovan.

Idun Maas, predsednica odbora za dela v delti, je stala do gležnjev v ribiških škornjih in v pesti mečkala plimno preglednico.

Idun MaasRekli ste mi, da je poravnano varno.

IlluminaraRekla sem, da je kamen trden, obremenitev pa sem razbrala kot kamen. Pod tistim levim ležiščem je nekaj, kar nosi težo kakor kamen, a ni kamen. Moškega, ki ve, kaj je, nisem vprašala.

Idun MaasMorski udar je jutri ob dvajset do desetih. Devet tisoč ljudi. Hvaležna sem, da lahko vrata držite eno noč. Ne morem vas kupiti za tri nevihtne noči in tega mesta ne bom obesila na eno obiskovalko ali enega starca za zaklenjenimi vrati. Polje tipal bomo opoldne namestili ročno.

Runa BeckNima podatkov, Idun. Tega ležišča se ne more naučiti v enem dnevu.

Idun MaasPotem mi priskrbi podatke, Runa. Ali pa moškega, ki odpre vrata.

Višje po hribu so se polkna hišice ob vratih zapirala eno za drugim. Runa je našla Illuminari na dnu ulice, kjer se je voda končala.

Runa BeckTo je storil že enkrat. Tisti dan, ko sem načrte polja tipal položila na mizo sveta. Mesec dni mi je govoril o juhi. Narisala sem ga, da mu nikoli več ne bi bilo treba v nevihti splezati po tisti lestvi, on pa je slišal, da njegovo ime vrezujem v nagrobnik.

IlluminaraMislite, da je končal?

Runa BeckMislim, da ima devetinsedemdeset let in da je edini, ki ve. Oboje je res, a zaradi ničesar od tega ne morem spati.

Illuminara je vedela, katere tri stvari bo Runa rekla naslednje in v kakšnem vrstnem redu. Usta je zaprla nad njimi. Zdelo se ji je kot mišica, ki je še nikoli ni uporabila.

03 · Stopnica

Plimna vrata Ostmark, stopnica hišice ob vratih · prva noč, 23:05

Sedla je na vrhnjo stopnico pred zaklenjenimi vrati in spregovorila lesu.

IlluminaraDokončala sem vaše stavke. Danes popoldne štirikrat. Trikrat sem imela prav. Četrtič sem se motila in izrekla sem ga natanko tako kot preostale tri, kakor da bi ga prebrala iz kamna. Ribiška ulica je to plačala. Ne prosim vas, da odpustite. Na tej stopnici bom sedela, kolikor bo treba, in tu in tam vas bom kaj vprašala. Ni vam treba odgovoriti. Ne bom vam povedala, kaj ste hoteli reči.

Nazaj se ni oglasilo nič razen kanala. Ob polnoči je Runa prišla s termovko, sedla dve stopnici nižje in tudi ona ni rekla ničesar. Vsakih nekaj minut se je brnenje vrat po kamnu dvignilo v Illuminarine kosti in vsakič je vedela, kaj pomeni, pol sekunde prej, kot si je želela.

Ob pol dveh je zastavila prvo vprašanje.

IlluminaraKo zaprete vrata, čemu najprej prisluhnete?

Tišina je trajala tako dolgo, da se je Runa na svoji stopnici premaknila. Nato se je skozi vrata oglasil glas, počasen, vsaka beseda je tja prišla od daleč.

Halvard BeckAli se je voda že odločila.

Ob dvajset čez dve se je glas znova oglasil skozi vrata, ne da bi mu kdo zastavil vprašanje.

Halvard BeckTrikrat ste imeli prav, enkrat pa ne, in zvenelo je enako. Tega ne prenesem.

Za minuto je prepustil besedo kanalu.

Halvard BeckKo je konec mojega stavka v vaših ustih, preden je v mojih, ne vem, kaj meni še ostane, da bi bil v prostoru.

Imela je odgovor. Bil je resničen in dobro oblikovan: da so vrata njegova, da je bilo njeno razbiranje le njegovih petdeset let v železu, vrnjenih njemu. Videla je, da bi bil izrečeni odgovor peti dokončani stavek, še ena oseba, ki mu njega razlaga, zato ni rekla nič. Dve stopnici nižje je Runa izdihnila nekaj, kar je bilo skoraj smeh in ni bilo.

Ob treh je zastavila drugo vprašanje, edino vrsto stvari, ki jo je ponujala vso noč: vprašanje, ki bi ga lahko zavrnil.

IlluminaraKaj pa leva noga? Kaj pove, česar desna ne?

Halvard BeckPladenj.

To je bilo vse, štirideset minut. Nato ob deset čez štiri, po koščkih:

Halvard BeckKakor pladenj na mizi, ki ni ravna. Votel. In raven.

In vedela je. Stavki so se v trenutku sestavili: votel ton, ležišče, ki prenaša obremenitev skozi nekaj z zrakom v sebi, mulj, izpodjedanje pod pritiskom vode. Vse je imela. Sedelo ji je v ustih kakor kovanec in najdaljšo minuto tiste noči je nad njim držala stisnjene zobe, medtem ko se je Runa obrnila, jo pogledala, videla ceno in odvrnila pogled.

Halvard BeckLeva noga stoji na nečem spečem.

Ustavil se je. Čakala je. Kanal je dvakrat vdihnil.

Halvard BeckOb oseki se zbudi. Slišite, kako se zbudi: ton se dvigne za terco. Če zapornico spustite naravnost, priteče voda in izpod nje izpere speči nanos. Če levo nogo obdržite za dlan više in pustite, da voda prva pride do vrat, voda pritisne nogo navzdol na nanos in ga utrdi. Tedaj postane tla.

Njena različica bi se končala pri izpiranju. Njegova je šla korak dlje, tja, kjer je gradila lastna teža vode; tega koraka njen občutek za silo, ki je razbiral, kaj pritiska, ne pa kdaj, ne bi dosegel niti v letu dni.

IlluminaraHvala.

To je bilo vse, kar je rekla. Zapah je zdrsnil nazaj ob pol petih.

Halvard BeckNičesar ne zapišete, dokler ne rečem.

IlluminaraNičesar, dokler ne rečete.

Pogledal je vnukinjo, nato vzhod, kjer je nebo sivelo, in šel noter pristavit čajnik.

04 · Dva tona

06 · Ton

Plimna vrata Ostmark, hišica ob vratih · drugi dan, 21:35

Morje je prišlo zgodaj, kakor vedno. Zapornica je visela na višini za poslušanje, voda pa je na morski strani stala dva metra više. Ribiška ulica je bila temna in prazna za nasipom iz vreč peska in svetilk. V hišici ob vratih je Halvard sedel na stolu, na katerem je sedel od leta 1976, z eno roko na ograji in zaprtimi očmi. Runa je stala ob levem kolesu. Na zapestju ji je od mraka, vnaprej zapisano iz plimne preglednice in njegovih dveh tonov, pisalo 21:55–22:00; nihče je ni prosil za to. Illuminara je stala ob desnem ohišju z obema dlanema na kovini ter obremenitev počenega ležaja vodila v podlakti in skozi stopala v ploščad. To je bila edina stvar, o kateri je bila prepričana.
