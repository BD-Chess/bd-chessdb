Two half-bridges stood out over the river like the hands of people who had agreed to shake and then thought better of it. Between the tips lay nine metres of brown water. A string stretched from rail to rail would also have shown the north span pointing nine centimetres left of the south, and that was the part that made the villages shout.

Illuminara walked out along Odo's side over decking so new it still smelled of larch. Odo of Upper Wend followed with a notebook held against his chest, the way a man holds a shield he expects to be asked to put down.

OdoI set my line from the Tithe Stone. The same stone Hanne Sull used. I closed it to a centimetre, twice. You are going to tell me she sighted wrong. I will carry you over the river on my back if you can show it.

IlluminaraI am not going to tell you that yet. I want to know what each of you would have to be wrong about, and I want to see it myself. Ten days to the flood. After it the ferry will not run until April. Upper Wend has eleven women due before then, and one midwife, who lives on the other bank.

OdoSeren Ashby. I have known Seren since she was nine. Do you think I need reminding?

IlluminaraNo. I think you needed someone to say it, so you would not have to say it first.

He looked at her, then at the water, the way you look at a debt.

OdoIf you say we were both right, you are saying the river lied. Rivers do not lie. Somebody sighted wrong, and it was not us.

She did not argue. Behind the anger stood a man who had spent two summers lifting beams to a line he trusted with his name, and she would not take it from him before there was something to put in its place.

IlluminaraThen show me how you sighted. Slowly, as though I were new. I will do exactly what you say.

Nobody had asked Odo to teach anything since the closing day. He set down the notebook, took the sighting vane out of his coat, and began.

The ferry was a flat raft on a rope, poled by its keeper, Pell Garrow, with the patience of a man paid by the crossing. Halfway over, the rope stopped taking the weight and a length of old anchor chain took over, rattling through the sheave. IlluminaraThen I will try to get you something to walk on.

Hanne Sull ran her survey the way she ran her tea, strong and measured to the gram. Each page of her survey book began with the weather drawn small in ink: a sun, a cloud, one grim little rain. She taught Illuminara the chain, the plumb, the vane and the arithmetic of closing a line, and watched with folded arms while the visitor did everything wrong and then right.

By evening Illuminara had re-run both crews' work with the crews' own hands. The north line closed to eight millimetres, the south to six. She laid the two books side by side on the shed table and could find no error in either larger than a thumbnail.

HanneI told Odo that in July. He said it was impossible, which is what people say when it is not impossible.

IlluminaraIt is not. Both lines are good, so the lines are not what moved. You each began from one place. What was it?

HanneThe Tithe Stone, on the north terrace. On rock. It cannot have moved.

She stopped, pen lifted.

HanneLast April it came out nine centimetres off my line. I wrote it down as chain stretch. Chains stretch.

Alone in the dusk, Illuminara laid her palm on the south footing and then, crossing, on the north. Both gave the same message, the way a rope tells you someone is pulling the other end: two great blocks either side of nothing, each drawn gently away from the other. A feeling was a hypothesis, and nobody was going to hang a bridge from her palm.
