01 · The Placard

Marrow Station, surface of the moon Tavret · Hour 0

The air clock for Halcyon was a magnetic placard on the control-room wall, and someone had been changing its digits by hand. It read 31:00. Underneath, in marker, a second number: 23.

Ofelia BrandtHalcyon sits on the sea floor, four thousand and ten metres below this floor: three thousand three hundred and fifty of ice, then six hundred and sixty of water. Twenty-three people, thirty-one hours of air at the rate they breathe now, saturated at six bar, so they cannot simply be pulled up. The shaft froze round the lift cage at 03:40. We have one vessel that can go down: Curlew, a drone-sub, six seats, nine hours from launch to Halcyon's door.

Illuminara stood at the placard rather than the screens. Nobody had yet asked her to sit.

Imre SolanoThe hull is certified for one excursion below three thousand metres. Down and back, to anywhere past that line. Two of twelve test hulls cracked on the second. I won't fly a second one, and you shouldn't ask me to.

Ofelia BrandtSo six come up. Seventeen don't. I have been told to draw lots, and I would rather resign.

IlluminaraWhat exactly does the certificate count?

Imre SolanoPressure excursions. Full cycles past the line.

IlluminaraThen the excursion is the scarce thing, not the seats. I'd like to ask what it is best spent on before we ask who sits in it.

Ofelia BrandtIt carries six. That's what it is for.

IlluminaraWho says the cage is dead?

Ofelia BrandtIt stopped moving, and the shaft is ice around it.

IlluminaraStopped is not dead. May I see what it has been saying since 03:40?

02 · The Trace

Halcyon, 4,010 m below · Hour 2

The habitat's voice came up the cable thin and a little late. Illuminara spoke to the commander first and the engineer second.

Marit OseiI've heard about the lots. I will not run them. If someone has a plan that is not a plan to choose who dies, say it quickly, because my people are watching me listen.

IlluminaraI have a question rather than a plan. Maud Okonkwo logged the cage's frame strain. Maud, there is a wobble in it.

Maud OkonkwoSensor noise. It rises and falls, a third or so. I flagged it and wrote it off.

Illuminara laid the trace beside a second line: the swell and slackening of the ice shell as Tavret's orbit pulled it. The peaks of the wobble sat on the peaks of the flex, every three hours and fourteen minutes.

IlluminaraNoise does not keep the moon's time. I think the cage is held by ice that is squeezed against it, and every time the shell relaxes, the squeeze eases. That gives us a door that opens for a few minutes every three hours and fourteen.

Maud OkonkwoThen you have the wrong picture, and it matters. The shaft is clear above and below. The cage climbs on pinions, on a rack; the cable is only voice and power. The ice is on three roller carriages and fourteen metres of rack at the collar, glazed over the teeth. Free those and the cage is free.

Fourteen metres, not a plug a hundred long. Illuminara took the correction the way she took a good measurement, with gratitude and without ceremony. It made the plan smaller. It also made it possible.

Marit OseiAnd you want my people to bet their lives on a wobble.

IlluminaraI want them to measure it, which costs only waiting. The next peak is at Hour 6 and twenty-six minutes. If the strain does not fall by at least a third and stay down for five minutes, I am wrong, and the director's lots are the plan. I'll say so myself.

03 · One Excursion

Marrow Station, control room · Hour 6, 26 minutes

Maud Okonkwo read the number up the cable as it fell, and the room, which had not been quiet, became so.

Maud OkonkwoDown thirty-eight percent. Holding. Five minutes. Nine. It's coming back up now.

Ofelia BrandtSo the door exists. What holds it?

IlluminaraI do, partly, and I want you to hear my limit rather than guess it. At a peak I can carry the cage's whole weight for eight minutes. At a trough, ninety seconds. On glazed rack the pinions skip and the carriages carry nothing, so for those fourteen metres someone must lift all of it. I can lift that, or I can keep the ice walls from closing on a cut lane. Not both for more than ninety seconds.

Ofelia BrandtSeats save six and spend the hull.

IlluminaraAnd leave seventeen with air and nothing left to come for them. A second excursion is the other road, and Imre has told us what it costs.

Imre SolanoThen the walls need something else.

IlluminaraA frame. Four titanium struts to jack the lane open to a metre, and a hot-water lance for the carriages. The struts hold the walls and I hold the weight, and neither of us holds the other's share. Curlew carries it in the seat bay; Imre began swapping seats for struts at Hour 3, under protest, as a job that can be undone. It goes down once, to the collar, and comes home. It never touches a passenger.

Imre SolanoEach strut seats in a socket drilled into the collar casting, is good for a hundred and forty kilonewtons, and is rated for forty troughs. At a trough the ice leans on each with about eighty. The frame runs on its own cells for sixty hours and reads up the cable's data pair, so it does not need Curlew once it is placed. The cancel line is a hundred and twenty kilonewtons on any strut, or three centimetres of total walk in any socket, counted from installation. Past it the frame is finished and so is every crossing after. I read them at every trough, and nobody boards until I have read one with the cage gone.

Ofelia BrandtAnd if your eight minutes fail?

IlluminaraThen Curlew is spent and so are we, and I will have cost you the six. I am sure of the arithmetic, not of the ice. So the first window frees an empty cage. If I fail there, nobody is in it.

Maud OkonkwoThe cage runs two-tenths of a metre a second. Fifty-five minutes between the collar and us, four hours thirty-nine between the collar and the surface. Windows from 9:40, every three-fourteen. Which can we afford to miss?

IlluminaraThe frame at 9:40, the cage free by 9:48 and with you at 10:43. If twelve are aboard by 11:55 it meets the collar at 12:50 and crosses at the 12:54 window, and they dock at the surface at 17:40. Empty, it is back at the collar at 22:40, four minutes after the 22:36 window. So it crosses at 25:50, reaches you at 26:52, and the eleven cross at 29:04 and dock at 33:50.

Maud OkonkwoAir is the other clock, and it's mine. Thirty-one hours at twenty-three people. The twelve you take first are the stretcher cases and the over-fifties, and they are fifty-nine percent of what Halcyon breathes. When they go at 11:55 there are nineteen hours and five minutes left at the old rate. The eleven stretch that to forty-six and a half. Halcyon is dead at 58:27.

IlluminaraThe cage carries eight hours of its own air and needs under six from the landing to the surface, so the eleven can seal themselves into it as late as an hour before Halcyon dies. The last window that works is 58:10. Anything before it, we can afford to miss.

Imre SolanoThree hours to the collar, and ten minutes to deploy. The other six hours of the nine are open water and a blind dock, and we are not going there. I leave at 6:30, and I fly it once.

Ofelia BrandtThen fly.

04 · The Order

Halcyon, crew deck and wet porch · Hour 7

The cage was rated for twelve, by weight on the rack. That left a number nobody wanted to say, and Commander Osei said it.

Marit OseiTwelve now. Eleven later. Which of you goes first is our decision, not the surface's, and it is not going to be a draw.

Anselm DubeI'll propose, and you can tear it up. First lift: the stretcher cases, the over-fifties, the three who feel the cold worst. Everyone whose body gets less safe the longer it waits. They also breathe fastest, so when they go, the rest have forty-six hours.

Kofi MensahAnd who is last?

Anselm DubeThe ones who can still work. Maud, those of us who know the rack, the commander.

Kofi MensahThat isn't an order, that's a sentence. You're making the strongest wait because they can.

Marit OseiYes. So here is what goes with the sentence. The last eleven choose the hour. Imre's line is the line: past a hundred and twenty kilonewtons or three centimetres of walk, nobody crosses and nobody argues. Inside it, any one of us can refuse a crossing, and nobody is carried against a refusal. The surface does not get a vote.

Anselm DubeOne voice can cost all eleven of us a window.

Marit OseiA window costs three hours fourteen of our own air, which we can count. A crossing costs what it costs, which we can't.

Then she asked the harder thing as a question. The cage would land on six metres of iced rack, and someone had to go out through the porch and clear it with a hot hose before 10:40.

Marit OseiNobody is ordered. If nobody goes, we clear it from inside the cage, which costs eighteen minutes of boarding. The first lift misses 12:54 and waits for 16:08. I am telling you the price so that you can say no knowing it.

Anselm DubeEveryone in this room owes the table three days of decompression. A body that has worked the porch owes forty hours more. Nobody should pretend that is nothing.

Two people said no, plainly, and Marit marked their names down without a word. Maud Okonkwo went because she alone knew the rack, Anselm because he had worked a porch before, and Kofi Mensah because he had asked what the strong owed the sick and had not liked the silence that followed. Maud was back through the lock by 9:25 with ice in her cuffs, and took the headset.

05 · Eight Minutes

The collar, 3,350 m down · Hour 9, 40 minutes

Illuminara had come down beside the cable, past the point where light was an idea, and the cold and the pressure meant what they always meant to her, which was information. She could read where it carried load, and where it was about to close.

Curlew hung at the collar with the frame folded in its belly. Solano's voice in the water was calm and slightly hoarse.

Imre SolanoStruts deploying. Lane walls at one metre. Lance in two minutes. Peak window opens now.

The struts took the walls. That was the arrangement, and it was a relief to be only the other half of it. She took the cage's weight. It came into her like a held note, fourteen tonnes of rack and steel that the ice was trying to keep. The first carriage let go at minute two. The second at minute four. The third did not.

Imre SolanoCarriage three isn't releasing. Minute five. Three left.

Maud OkonkwoThat carriage has a shear pin on the east face. It's the one I always said we should replace. Hit it from the east.

Solano swung the lance. Illuminara felt the pin go, a small bright failure, and the cage dropped out of the ice onto clean rack and its own pinions for the first time since 03:40. She released it at minute seven and fifty seconds.

IlluminaraThat is as long as I can hold. If it had needed ten seconds more, it would not have been me who could give them.

The cage ran down toward the habitat, empty, at a stately two-tenths of a metre a second, and the voices from Halcyon began to cheer, and she let them. She did not go up. The windows would not wait for her to come back down, so she stayed in the lee of the frame, in the dark.

At 11:17, in the first trough, the struts took the ice alone, and Solano, rising in Curlew, read them off the telemetry: fifty-eight percent of rating, eleven millimetres of walk.

Imre SolanoInside. Board them.

They were aboard at 11:55, twelve of them, with the stretchers latched. At 12:50 the cage came up to the collar, and this time there was weight in it, and the weight had names. She held it across the fourteen metres for seven minutes and thirty seconds. The struts held the walls. Curlew broke the surface at 12:55, its excursion spent, flown once as promised.

The cage went on up the open bore, four hours and thirty-nine minutes of it, with six bar held inside. At 17:40 its door mated to the chamber door at Marrow Station and the first twelve began to come down to surface pressure at the pace their bodies allowed.

06 · The Second Ride

The collar, 3,350 m down · Hour 22, 40 minutes

The empty cage came down the bore on schedule, and that was the trouble. It stopped at the glazed rack four minutes after the 22:36 peak, and Illuminara felt the squeeze already coming back through the ice.

Maud OkonkwoFour minutes. Say it plainly so we can stop doing sums. Can you hold it?

IlluminaraNot across. I would have under three minutes and the crossing needs seven. The ice would close on the cage in the middle of the rack. The next window is 25:50.

Nobody on the cable suggested she try. Her being sure of a limit had never cost anyone anything before; now it cost three hours and fourteen minutes of air that Halcyon could count.

The frame stood alone through the long wait, on its own cells, reading up the cable's data pair, and Maud logged every trough. Walk of 1.4 centimetres at 14:31. Two point zero at 17:45. Two point six at 20:59, inside the line. Then, at 24:13, with the cage parked above it and nothing moving, two point two.

At 25:50 Illuminara carried the empty cage across. At 26:52 it settled on the landing the porch team had cleared, and eleven people mustered at the cage door at the pace of people who could walk.

Imre SolanoTrough, 27:27. One hundred and four kilonewtons, walk two point six centimetres. The line is one-twenty and three. It is inside the line, and I would let you cross.

Marit OseiInside the line, then it's ours. One voice is enough. Who refuses?

Kofi MensahI do. One window. Look at the log: at 20:59 it read two point six, and at 24:13 it was back to two point two with nobody touching it. I would rather pay in air than cross on two point six with eleven people in the cage.

Maud OkonkwoHe's reading the trace correctly. It does relax.

Anselm DubeThree hours fourteen.

Marit OseiCounted. We have twenty-nine hours of air and the crossing needs five. Refusal stands. We cross at 32:18.

They waited. At 30:41 the trough read two point three centimetres. At 31:15 the cage left the landing, and at 32:18 Illuminara took its weight, tired, and carried it across in seven minutes and fifteen seconds, fifteen seconds under what she had said. Then she rose beside it for four hours and thirty-nine minutes at two-tenths of a metre a second, a speed she found tedious and kept to.

At 37:04 the door mated. Marit Osei was the last one through it.

Marit OseiThat's the eleven. You can tell your director the habitat is switched off.

Ofelia BrandtThe company will want to know whose fault the freeze was.

Marit OseiThe company can write it on whatever placard it likes. Twenty-three people are in a chamber with a door.

Maud, Anselm and Kofi would spend their extra forty hours on the table without complaint. Illuminara sat on the floor of the control room for the first time since she had come up. She did not pretend to be anything but tired. She said so, plainly, to the room, and to Solano.

On the wall, someone had finally wiped off the digits. In their place, in marker, was the number 23, and a line under it.

04 · Vrstni red

Halcyon, paluba posadke in mokra preddverna komora · Ura 7

Kletka je bila po nosilnosti zobate letve predvidena za dvanajst ljudi. Tako je ostalo število, ki ga nihče ni želel izreči, in poveljnica Osei ga je izrekla.
