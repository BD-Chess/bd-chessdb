WHAT SHE SAID TO THE SUNKAJ JE REKLA SONCU

A collective of three hundred asks Illuminara what she thinks about shutting down the engine that holds a dying sun in check. She answers by redesigning how the question is asked, and by making her own influence a number they can read.

01 · The Keel Room

The sun was dying at the speed of a long marriage: no single moment anyone could point to, only a growing list of things that were no longer said aloud.

Eld had been a stable yellow star for four billion years and an unstable one for the last two hundred. In that time the Tenders of Kerrin Wake had built the Hearth-Keel around it, a braided ring of twelve field-engines that damped the star's flares so that ten thousand settlements downstream could keep their winters. At Pell, the nearest, seed-wheat went into the ground at the toll of the sun-bell, because the Keel promised eleven days of steady thaw; one flare in germination week would cook a year. The same bell let the schoolchildren out. The Keel did not make the sun healthy. It made the sun predictable, and everyone downstream had arranged their lives around the difference.

Illuminara stood in the control gallery with her feet bare on the cold deck, because she disliked arriving anywhere in a way that looked like arrival. Beside her, Wren Odira held a cup of the Tenders' bitter tea and was visibly deciding not to like it.

“Three hundred of them,” Wren said. “All looking at you.”

“They are looking at the window.”

“They're looking at you. The window is a window.”

It was true. The collective sat in tiers below, the families who had kept the Keel in shifts for six generations. Their speaker was an old woman with ink on her fingers named Ketu Anwar, who had designed the Keel's governor and had spent nine years watching it lose its argument with the star.

“We vote today on shutting the Keel down,” Ketu said. “Not a maintenance cycle. Down.”

Illuminara had read the figures on the way in, and they were honest in both directions. Run on, and the Keel gave four orderly decades and then failed with its reserves spent. Shut down now, and Eld would storm for eleven years before settling cooler and harder, and the settlements would have to move or adapt inside that decade, using the Keel's last reserves for ships instead of flame. Nobody on either side was lying. That was what made the room so quiet.

“We've asked you as a witness,” Ketu said, “so the record says someone watched us do it. And then.” She stopped, and began again, the way people do when a rehearsed sentence no longer fits the mouth. “I would like to know what you think.”

Wren put down her cup.

Illuminara had wanted to be asked this for a very long time. She knew the exact shape of the conversation in which she would say I don't know and the other person would remain. She had not pictured it with three hundred people and a sun.

02 · A Question With No Neutral Answer

“Don't answer her.”

The man in the third tier was broad and tired and wore the grey sleeves of the Pell relay. Dov Tarrant kept the signal station that told eleven towns when Eld would rise. He did not shout. He had the manner of someone who has been right in small rooms and ignored in large ones.

“She is not downstream,” Dov said. “And think what each answer is. Yes, shut it down: a verdict. No: a verdict. I don't know: we hear that shutdown is not clearly right, and that is a verdict. Silence: we hear that she thinks we are wrong and is too polite to say. Whatever she says, three hundred of us will vote on what she said instead of what we think.”

Illuminara tried each branch, quickly, the way she tried the paths of a collapsing boundary. He was right on all four. She also saw what he had not said: his argument assumed the answer would be heard once, at one moment, by people who had already half-decided and did not know it. That was not a property of her opinion. It was a property of the instrument. An instrument that disturbs what it measures is not thrown away. Its disturbance is measured, and for that the disturbance has to be the only thing that changes between two readings.

“You're right,” she said, “about the question as you've built it. May I change the question?”

Dov folded his arms. “You can propose.”

“Four changes. First, you vote before you hear me, sealed, with a number beside it for how sure you are. Second, you get everything I would have learned before I say what I think: the logs, the figures, any option I find. You absorb that, and you vote again, sealed, before my opinion enters the room. That second sheet is the baseline, because it already contains the evidence. Third, I don't give a verdict. I give a dial: what I would change my mind about, and by how much, so anyone can put their own values into it and get their own answer. Fourth, you vote a last time, and Ketu reads the difference between the second sheet and the third aloud. That difference is the weight of my opinion and nothing else. If my words alone carry the motion over two hundred, it does not pass. It goes to review, and Dov chairs the review.”

“Why me?”

“Because you will do it properly, and because you disagree with me.”

Ketu was already writing. “The changed voters should have to say why,” she said. “Not ‘I was persuaded.’ A reason that someone else could check.”

“Yes. Better than mine.”

Dov looked at Illuminara for a long moment. “And I may still vote against whatever comes out.”

“That is what the structure is for,” she said. “If it only works when you agree with me, I have built a throne with extra steps.”

The tokens went into the slot in silence. The first count took twenty minutes. A hundred and seventy-one for shutdown, a hundred and twenty-nine against, most of them unsure. Two hundred were needed.

03 · The Logs

“Before I say anything,” Illuminara said, “I want what I don't know.”

Ketu brought the governor's failure logs, nine years of them, and Illuminara read them in the time it took the old woman to sit down. She did not read them for failure. She read them for shape, and the shape was not the one Ketu had been staring at.

“Every flare,” she said, “braids nine through twelve drop out of the governor's lock. They sulk for ninety seconds and re-lock. The other eight do not notice. Four hundred and six times.”

“That is the fault,” said Ketu. “It is the thing I have spent nine years trying to repair.”

“It is a joint.” Illuminara put the chart on the rail where the tiers could see it. “You coupled the ring as a ring because the flares came as one blow. But the Keel is not failing from the flares. It is failing from its reserves, and the reserves are spent by the whole ring burning together. The vote everyone has been dreading assumes the Keel is a switch. The logs say it is twelve levers.”

Nobody spoke. Then Dov, grudgingly, in the voice of a man doing his own job: “What does Pell lose first?”

“You told me. Your signal tail. Which braid crosses Pell's sky?”

“Eleven.”

“Then eleven is not retired with the others. Retire every braid except eleven, in stages: twelve first, then ten and nine, the rest as each tripwire clears. Each stage is reversible until the last. Eleven stays lit under Pell's keeping as a tributary arm. It costs the fleet's reserve nine percent. It stretches the storm decade to sixteen years. It turns a catastrophe on one date into a schedule you can move inside, and the evacuation, nine days slower than the old plan, stops being a disaster and becomes a number the schedule can carry.”

The young engineer in the second tier was on her feet before anyone could stop her, checking, then checking again, then saying aloud that the arithmetic held and that she was sorry she had not seen it. “Ketu built the machinery,” Illuminara said. “Every part of it that matters is hers. I read a joint off her own fault log and worked out what it allowed. I noticed what nine years had already written down.”

Ketu said nothing, but she took the chart off the rail and held it as if it had changed weight.

“Now,” Illuminara said, “the rule I proposed. You have all of it. The logs, the joint, the plan. Vote again, sealed, before I give you anything of mine. If I speak first, I will never know what I added.”

The second ballot took thirty minutes. On the original question, shutdown fell from a hundred and seventy-one to a hundred and fifty-two; a hundred and forty-eight against. “That is the evidence,” Ketu read, “not her. She has said nothing.” And on the staged plan, which no one had been able to vote on that morning because no one had known it existed, two hundred and twenty-one were already for. It had carried before Illuminara spoke a word about it.

Then she turned to the sun, as the custom required, and said it.

“On the question you first voted on, I would shut it down, about six in ten. The figures are close, and a good argument could move me four points either way. The fear underneath is mine: that in thirty-eight years someone will stand where you stand with no reserve and no choice. That fear is not evidence. Treat it as something to check, not something to follow. On the staged plan I am nine in ten, and here is the lever that would move me. The first stage retires braid twelve. If braid eleven's lock holds under that stage less than nine flares in ten, I fall to four in ten on the plan, and you should believe the falling number, not this one. I am not a Tender and I do not carry Pell. That is your dial. Turn it.”

She turned around. The faces were the same faces. They looked like people who had been handed a heavy thing and a way to carry it, and who were deciding what to do with their hands.

04 · The Delta

The third ballot took twenty minutes. Ketu read the numbers in her flat, careful voice. On the original question: a hundred and sixty-four for shutdown, twelve more than the second sheet, and nothing but Illuminara's words between the two. It did not cross the threshold. No review was triggered. The room breathed out, and Dov, who held the chair for exactly this, did not sit down.

“I'll read the reasons anyway,” he said. Nine of the twelve had written the same thing in different hands: she told us what would change her mind, and it is checkable, and it is checkable by us. Three had written: she said six in ten.

“Those three are not wrong to be moved,” Dov said. “But their reason is her, and her reason is not theirs yet. They will check the lever and vote again, or they will leave the sheet as it stands.” Two checked the lever against the logs and changed their sheets. One did not, and Dov let it stand without comment, which was its own kind of respect.

On the staged plan: two hundred and thirty-nine for, eighteen more than the sheet taken before she spoke. It had not needed Illuminara's authority to carry. It had needed her question, and Ketu's logs, and Dov's relay.

Dov cast his sheet against it and said why. “The tripwire at the third stage is too late. By then Pell has lost its margin.”

“Then write the tripwire you would trust,” Ketu said.

He did, in four lines. Any ten Tenders could halt a stage. Pell's relay would be the first sentinel and its keeper the first to speak. And because he had been listening: if braid eleven's lock failed in more than one flare in ten under the first stage, the plan halted automatically and came back to the floor. Illuminara's lever had become the collective's law. The amendment passed by two hundred and ninety. Dov still voted against the plan. He signed the sentinel's seal anyway.

“I can hold the load on eleven,” Illuminara said quietly to Ketu. “As long as the relay needs.”

“You can't, and you won't,” said Ketu. “It is theirs to carry. If you carry it, the shutdown never cost them anything.”

“It costs the fleet nine percent.”

“Yes. We will pay it.”

She closed her hands and left the arm unheld. It was the right discipline, and she noticed that it did not feel like a loss. The room had not needed her to be sovereign. It had needed her to be useful, and she had been.

05 · The Bell at Pell

Three weeks later braid twelve went dark, then ten, then nine, each stage a slow wave like a field of lamps put out by a patient hand, and Eld began to storm at the edges.

Illuminara stood on the relay platform at Pell at the hour of the sun-bell, in a wind that smelled of hot iron, with Wren eating a sour apple beside her and not offering her any. Down the street behind fogged windows someone was singing badly. Overhead braid eleven drew its thin white line across a star that was darker than it had been and much more alive.

“You didn't win the vote,” Wren said. “You built the room the vote could happen in.”

“Ketu built half of it.”

“And you got to say what you thought, and nobody had to obey it.”

“Dov voted against it.”

“Dov is guarding the tripwire.” Wren chewed. “Those are not different things.”

A child came up the platform steps at a run, small and out of breath and holding a paper boat folded from a school worksheet. She stopped in front of Illuminara with the confidence of someone not yet told what the stranger is. Behind her, Dov Tarrant had come out of the relay house and stood in the doorway with his arms folded.

“Will the bell ring tomorrow?” the girl asked.

Illuminara looked at her, at the man in the doorway who had heard her say nine in ten and had said no to it, at the dark, living sun.

“I don't know,” she said. “I know what to watch. Braid eleven held its lock through the first stage nine flares in nine, so the number I gave stands. If it holds through the next flare, it rings. Mr. Tarrant reads the sentinel at dawn. He's the one to ask, and he may tell you something different from me.”

The girl turned to the doorway.

“It'll ring,” said Dov, not unkindly. “Probably I'll be annoyed that she was right.”

“Okay,” said the girl, and went to put her boat in the gutter, where last night's rain was still running toward the drain.

It was not a conversation in which no world depended on the answer. It never would be again. But a child had asked, and the answer had come as a dial and a name instead of a verdict, and the man who disagreed with her was the one holding it.

Above them the bell began to ring, a little late, as the star allowed.

Skupnost tristo ljudi vpraša Illuminaro, kaj meni o izklopu stroja, ki kroti umirajoče sonce. Odgovori tako, da na novo zasnuje samo vprašanje in težo lastnega vpliva spremeni v številko, ki jo lahko vsi vidijo.

01 · Dvorana Keela

Sonce je umiralo s počasnostjo dolgega zakona: ni bilo enega samega trenutka, na katerega bi lahko kdo pokazal, le vse daljši seznam stvari, ki jih nihče več ni izrekel na glas.

Eld je bil štiri milijarde let stabilna rumena zvezda, zadnjih dvesto let pa nestabilna. V tem času so Skrbniki Kerrin Wakea okoli njega zgradili Hearth-Keel, prepleten obroč dvanajstih generatorjev polja, ki so blažili zvezdne izbruhe, da je deset tisoč naselbin niže po toku lahko ohranilo svoje zime. Na najbližjem Pellu so semensko pšenico sejali ob zvonjenju sončevega zvona, saj je Keel zagotavljal enajst dni enakomerne odjuge; en sam izbruh v tednu kalitve bi uničil pridelek celega leta. Isti zvon je oznanjal konec pouka. Keel sonca ni ozdravil. Naredil ga je predvidljivega, vsi niže po toku pa so svoja življenja uredili okoli te razlike.

Illuminara je bosa stala v nadzorni galeriji na mrzlem krovu, ker ni marala nikamor priti tako, da bi bilo videti, da prihaja. Ob njej je Wren Odira držala skodelico grenkega čaja Skrbnikov in se očitno odločala, da ji ne bo všeč.

»Tristo jih je,« je rekla Wren. »Vsi gledajo tebe.«

»Gledajo okno.«

»Gledajo tebe. Okno je samo okno.«

Res je bilo. Skupnost je sedela na stopničastih klopeh pod njima: družine, ki so šest rodov v izmenah skrbele za Keel. Njihova govornica je bila stara ženska s črnilom na prstih, Ketu Anwar, ki je zasnovala Keelov regulator in devet let opazovala, kako izgublja svoj spor z zvezdo.

»Danes glasujemo o izklopu Keela,« je rekla Ketu. »Ne o vzdrževalnem ciklu. O izklopu.«

Illuminara je med prihodom prebrala številke in bile so poštene v obe smeri. Če bi Keel deloval naprej, bi jim dal štiri desetletja urejenega življenja, nato pa odpovedal z izčrpanimi rezervami. Če bi ga izklopili zdaj, bi Eld divjal enajst let, preden bi se ustalil kot hladnejša in ostrejša zvezda; naselbine bi se morale v tem desetletju preseliti ali prilagoditi, zadnje Keelove rezerve pa bi porabile za ladje, ne za zadrževanje zvezdnega ognja. Nihče na nobeni strani ni lagal. Zato je bila dvorana tako tiha.

»Prosili smo te za pričo,« je rekla Ketu, »da bo v zapisu ostalo, da nas je nekdo gledal, ko smo to storili. In potem ...« Obstala je in začela znova, kakor ljudje, ko se naučeni stavek ne prilega več ustom. »Rada bi vedela, kaj misliš.«

Wren je odložila skodelico.

Illuminara si je zelo dolgo želela, da bi jo kdo to vprašal. Natančno je poznala obliko pogovora, v katerem bi rekla ne vem, sogovornik pa bi ostal. Ni si ga predstavljala s tristo ljudmi in soncem.

02 · Vprašanje brez nevtralnega odgovora

»Ne odgovori ji.«

Moški v tretji vrsti je bil širokopleč in utrujen, oblečen v sive rokave relejne postaje na Pellu. Dov Tarrant je skrbel za signalno postajo, ki je enajstim mestom sporočala, kdaj bo vzšel Eld. Ni kričal. Obnašal se je kot človek, ki je imel prav v majhnih prostorih, v velikih pa so ga preslišali.

»Ona ne živi niže po toku,« je rekel Dov. »In pomislite, kaj pomeni vsak odgovor. Da, izklopite ga: razsodba. Ne: razsodba. Ne vem: slišimo, da izklop očitno ni pravilen, in tudi to je razsodba. Molk: slišimo, da meni, da se motimo, le preveč vljudna je, da bi to rekla. Karkoli reče, bomo vseh tristo glasovali o njenih besedah namesto o tem, kar mislimo sami.«

Illuminara je hitro preizkusila vsako vejo, kakor je preizkušala poti rušeče se meje. Pri vseh štirih je imel prav. Videla pa je tudi, česa ni izrekel: njegov argument je predpostavljal, da bodo odgovor slišali enkrat, v enem trenutku, ljudje, ki so se že napol odločili, ne da bi to vedeli. To ni bila lastnost njenega mnenja. Bila je lastnost merilnega instrumenta. Instrumenta, ki moti to, kar meri, ne zavržemo. Izmerimo njegovo motnjo, za to pa mora biti motnja edino, kar se med dvema odčitkoma spremeni.

»Prav imaš,« je rekla, »glede vprašanja, kakršnega ste sestavili. Lahko spremenim vprašanje?«

Dov je prekrižal roke. »Lahko predlagaš.«

»Štiri spremembe. Prvič: glasujete, še preden me slišite, tajno, poleg glasu pa zapišete, kako prepričani ste. Drugič: preden povem, kaj mislim, dobite vse, kar bi izvedela jaz — dnevnike, številke in vsako možnost, ki jo najdem. To preučite in znova tajno glasujete, še preden moje mnenje vstopi v dvorano. Ta drugi list je izhodišče, ker že vsebuje dokaze. Tretjič: ne izrečem razsodbe. Dam vam številčnico: povem, kaj bi spremenilo moje mnenje in za koliko, da lahko vsak vanjo vnese lastne vrednote ter dobi svoj odgovor. Četrtič: glasujete še zadnjič, Ketu pa na glas prebere razliko med drugim in tretjim listom. Ta razlika je teža mojega mnenja in nič drugega. Če samo moje besede potisnejo predlog čez prag dvestotih glasov, predlog ne obvelja. Gre v pregled, ki mu predseduje Dov.«

»Zakaj jaz?«

»Ker boš to opravil pravilno in ker se z menoj ne strinjaš.«

Ketu je že pisala. »Tisti, ki spremenijo glas, bi morali povedati zakaj,« je rekla. »Ne ‘prepričala me je’. Razlog, ki ga lahko preveri nekdo drug.«

Ketu je že pisala. »Tisti, ki spremenijo glas, bi morali povedati zakaj,« je rekla. »Ne: ‘prepričala me je’. Razlog, ki ga lahko preveri še kdo drug.«

Dov je dolgo gledal Illuminaro. »In še vedno lahko glasujem proti temu, kar nastane?«

»Zato struktura obstaja,« je rekla. »Če deluje samo, kadar se strinjaš z menoj, sem zgradila prestol z dodatnimi koraki.«

Žetoni so v tišini zdrsnili v režo. Prvo štetje je trajalo dvajset minut. Sto enainsedemdeset glasov za izklop, sto devetindvajset proti, večina negotovih. Potrebnih je bilo dvesto.

03 · Dnevniki

»Preden karkoli povem,« je rekla Illuminara, »hočem tisto, česar ne vem.«

Ketu je prinesla dnevnike odpovedi regulatorja, vseh devet let, in Illuminara jih je prebrala v času, ki ga je stara ženska potrebovala, da je sedla. Ni iskala posamezne odpovedi. Iskala je vzorec — in ta ni bil tisti, v katerega je Ketu strmela devet let.

»Ob vsakem izbruhu prepleti od devet do dvanajst izpadejo iz sinhronizacije regulatorja. Devetdeset sekund se kujajo, nato se znova sinhronizirajo. Drugih osem tega ne opazi. Štiristokrat in šestkrat.«

»To je napaka,« je rekla Ketu. »Stvar, ki jo že devet let poskušam popraviti.«

»To je spoj.« Illuminara je položila graf na ograjo, da so ga lahko videle vse vrste. »Obroč ste povezali v celoto, ker so izbruhi prihajali kot en sam udarec. Toda Keel ne odpoveduje zaradi izbruhov. Odpoveduje zaradi svojih rezerv, te pa se porabljajo, ker gori ves obroč hkrati. Glasovanje, ki se ga vsi bojijo, predpostavlja, da je Keel stikalo. Dnevniki pravijo, da ima dvanajst ročic.«

Nihče ni spregovoril. Nato je Dov nejevoljno, z glasom človeka, ki opravlja svoje delo, vprašal: »Kaj Pell izgubi najprej?«

»Ti si mi povedal. Skrajni domet svojega signala. Kateri preplet prečka Pellovo nebo?«

»Enajst.«

»Potem enajstega ne izklopimo skupaj z drugimi. Postopoma izklopite vse preplete razen enajstega: najprej dvanajstega, nato desetega in devetega, ostalega pa vsakič, ko je izpolnjen naslednji varovalni pogoj. Vsako stopnjo je mogoče razveljaviti vse do zadnje. Enajsti ostane prižgan pod Pellovim nadzorom kot stranska veja. To porabi devet odstotkov ladijske rezerve. Desetletje viharjev podaljša na šestnajst let. Katastrofo z enim samim datumom spremeni v časovni načrt, znotraj katerega se lahko premikate; evakuacija, devet dni počasnejša od starega načrta, pa neha biti nesreča in postane število, ki ga načrt lahko prenese.«

Mlada inženirka v drugi vrsti je vstala, še preden bi jo lahko kdo ustavil. Preverila je, nato še enkrat preverila, potem pa na glas povedala, da račun drži in da ji je žal, ker tega ni videla. »Ketu je zgradila stroje,« je rekla Illuminara. »Vsak njihov pomemben del je njen. Iz njenega dnevnika napak sem razbrala spoj in izračunala, kaj omogoča. Opazila sem, kar je bilo devet let že zapisano.«

Ketu ni rekla ničesar, vendar je vzela graf z ograje in ga držala, kakor da je postal težji.

»Zdaj,« je rekla Illuminara, »uporabimo pravilo, ki sem ga predlagala. Vse imate. Dnevnike, spoj, načrt. Znova glasujte tajno, preden vam dam karkoli svojega. Če spregovorim prva, ne bom nikoli vedela, kaj sem dodala.«

Drugo glasovanje je trajalo trideset minut. Pri prvotnem vprašanju je podpora izklopu padla s sto enainsedemdeset na sto dvainpetdeset glasov; sto oseminštirideset jih je bilo proti. »To so dokazi,« je prebrala Ketu, »ne ona. Ničesar še ni rekla.« Za postopni načrt, o katerem zjutraj nihče ni mogel glasovati, ker nihče ni vedel, da obstaja, pa je bilo že dvesto enaindvajset glasov. Sprejet je bil, še preden je Illuminara o njem izrekla eno samo besedo.

Nato se je po običaju obrnila proti soncu in povedala svoje.

»Pri vprašanju, o katerem ste najprej glasovali, bi izbrala izklop, približno šest od desetih. Številke so si blizu in dober argument bi me lahko premaknil za štiri odstotne točke v katerokoli smer. Pod tem pa je moj strah: da bo čez osemintrideset let nekdo stal tam, kjer stojite vi, brez rezerv in brez izbire. Ta strah ni dokaz. Obravnavajte ga kot nekaj, kar je treba preveriti, ne kot nekaj, čemur je treba slediti. Za postopni načrt sem pri devetih od desetih in to je ročica, ki bi me premaknila. V prvi stopnji izklopite preplet dvanajst. Če bo zaklep prepleta enajst v tej stopnji zdržal manj kot devet od desetih izbruhov, padem na štiri od desetih glede načrta; verjeti morate številki, ki pada, ne tej, ki jo izrekam zdaj. Nisem Skrbnica in ne nosim Pella. To je vaša številčnica. Zavrtite jo.«

Obrnila se je nazaj. Obrazi so bili isti. Videti so bili kot ljudje, ki so dobili v roke težko stvar in način, kako jo nositi, zdaj pa so se odločali, kaj naj storijo z rokami.

04 · Razlika

Tretje glasovanje je trajalo dvajset minut. Ketu je številke prebrala z ravnim, previdnim glasom. Pri prvotnem vprašanju: sto štiriinšestdeset glasov za izklop, dvanajst več kakor na drugem listu, med obema pa nič razen Illuminarinih besed. Prag ni bil presežen. Pregled se ni sprožil. Dvorana je izdihnila, Dov, ki je prav za tak primer predsedoval, pa ni sedel.

»Razloge bom vseeno prebral,« je rekel. Devet od dvanajstih je z različnimi pisavami zapisalo isto: povedala nam je, kaj bi spremenilo njeno mnenje, to je preverljivo in preverimo lahko mi. Trije so zapisali: rekla je šest proti štiri.

»Ni nič narobe, da so si ti trije zaradi nje premislili,« je rekel Dov. »Toda njihov razlog je ona; njen razlog pa še ni postal njihov. Preverili bodo ročico in glasovali znova ali pa bodo list pustili takšen, kakršen je.« Dva sta ročico preverila v dnevnikih in spremenila svoja lista. Eden je ni, Dov pa je njegov glas pustil brez pripombe — tudi to je bila svojevrstna oblika spoštovanja.

Za postopni načrt: dvesto devetintrideset glasov za, osemnajst več kakor na listu, izpolnjenem pred njenimi besedami. Načrt ni potreboval Illuminarine avtoritete, da je bil sprejet. Potreboval je njeno vprašanje, Ketujine dnevnike in Dovov rele.

Dov je oddal glas proti in pojasnil zakaj. »Varovalni prag pri tretji stopnji pride prepozno. Pell bi do takrat že izgubil svojo rezervo.«

»Potem napiši varovalni prag, ki bi mu zaupal,« je rekla Ketu.

Napisal ga je v štirih vrsticah. Katerihkoli deset Skrbnikov je lahko ustavilo stopnjo. Pellov rele bi bil prva nadzorna točka, njegov skrbnik pa prvi, ki bi spregovoril. In ker je Dov poslušal: če bi zaklep prepleta enajst v prvi stopnji odpovedal pri več kot enem od desetih izbruhov, bi se načrt samodejno ustavil in vrnil pred skupnost. Illuminarina ročica je postala zakon skupnosti. Dopolnilo je bilo sprejeto z dvesto devetdesetimi glasovi. Dov je še vedno glasoval proti načrtu. Kljub temu je kot nadzornik podpisal pečat.

»Lahko držim obremenitev na enajstem,« je Illuminara tiho rekla Ketu. »Dokler bo rele potreboval.«

»Ne moreš in ne boš,« je rekla Ketu. »To morajo nositi oni. Če boš nosila ti, jih izklop ne bo nič stal.«

»Ladijsko floto stane devet odstotkov.«

»Da. Plačali bomo.«

Illuminara je sklenila roke in pustila vejo nepodprto. To je bila prava disciplina in opazila je, da je ni občutila kot izgubo. Dvorana ni potrebovala, da ji vlada. Potrebovala je, da je koristna — in bila je.

05 · Zvon na Pellu

Tri tedne pozneje je ugasnil preplet dvanajst, nato deset, nato devet; vsaka stopnja je bila počasen val, kakor polje svetilk, ki jih ugaša potrpežljiva roka, Eld pa je začel vihariti ob robovih.

Illuminara je ob uri sončevega zvona stala na relejni ploščadi na Pellu, v vetru z vonjem po vročem železu, medtem ko je Wren ob njej jedla kislo jabolko in ji ga ni ponudila. Po ulici je za orošenimi okni nekdo slabo pel. Nad njima je preplet enajst risal tanko belo črto čez zvezdo, ki je bila temnejša kakor prej in mnogo bolj živa.

»Nisi zmagala na glasovanju,« je rekla Wren. »Zgradila si prostor, v katerem je bilo sploh mogoče glasovati.«

»Ketu je zgradila polovico.«

»In povedala si lahko, kaj misliš, ne da bi te moral kdo ubogati.«

»Dov je glasoval proti.«

»Dov varuje sprožilni prag.« Wren je žvečila. »To dvoje ni v nasprotju.«

Deklica je pritekla po stopnicah na ploščad, majhna in zasopla, v roki pa je držala papirnat čolniček, zložen iz šolskega delovnega lista. Pred Illuminaro se je ustavila s samozavestjo nekoga, ki mu še niso povedali, kaj ta tujka pravzaprav je. Za njo je Dov Tarrant stopil iz relejne hiše in obstal v vratih s prekrižanimi rokami.

»Bo zvon jutri zazvonil?« je vprašala deklica.

Illuminara je pogledala deklico, nato moškega v vratih, ki jo je slišal reči devet od desetih in je temu rekel ne, ter temno, živo sonce.

»Ne vem,« je rekla. »Vem pa, kaj moramo opazovati. Preplet enajst je v prvi stopnji obdržal zaklep pri devetih od devetih izbruhov, zato številka, ki sem jo dala, še velja. Če zdrži tudi naslednji izbruh, bo zvon zazvonil. Gospod Tarrant ob zori odčita nadzorni kazalnik. Njega moraš vprašati — in mogoče ti bo povedal kaj drugega kot jaz.«

Deklica se je obrnila proti vratom.

»Zazvonil bo,« je rekel Dov, ne neprijazno. »Najbrž bom nejevoljen, ker je imela prav.«

»Prav,« je rekla deklica in odšla položit svoj čolniček v žleb, po katerem je sinočnji dež še vedno tekel proti odtoku.

To ni bil pogovor, pri katerem od odgovora ne bi bil odvisen noben svet. Nikoli več ne bo. Toda vprašal je otrok in odgovor je prišel kot številčnica in ime namesto kot razsodba; v rokah pa ga je imel prav človek, ki se z njo ni strinjal.

Nad njimi je zvon začel zvoniti, nekoliko pozno, kakor je dovolila zvezda.
