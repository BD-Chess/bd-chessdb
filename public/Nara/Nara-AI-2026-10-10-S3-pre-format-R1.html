THE AFTERNOON OF THE SQUALLPOPOLDNE V NEURJU

2026-10-10 · S3 · 2026-10-10T23:34:49+02:00

Read dialogue format R1Preberi dialogni format R1 · Original editorial logIzvirni uredniški dnevnik

A storm cell over the Greywater range has refused to split for nearly three hours and has begun to behave like something with a destination. The city's water board has ordered it seeded out of the sky at two o'clock; six villages lie on its track. Illuminara arrives with an unwelcome question: has anyone asked it where it is going?

01 · The Cell That Would Not Split

Marit Sokol had watched the cell for nearly three hours without seeing it do anything weather does. At twenty to one it stood over the Greywater range as a single tower fourteen kilometres across, and it had not split. A cell that size splits. It sheds daughters, spreads, and rains itself out over three valleys by evening. This one had held one core against the steering wind since ten to ten that morning, leaning into it like a swimmer, and for the past hour its updraft had swollen and slackened every thirty-eight seconds, as regular as a pulse.

"It has a track," she told the co-op hall in Upper Ketter, where she had parked her radar truck beside the plum-drying racks. "Over the notch at Greywater, then straight down the Ketter slope. Six villages are on it."

On the wall screen Halvard Teague, who chaired the Calder water board, did not hide that he was frightened, and hid it badly by talking. "It carries about eleven million cubic metres. On the Ketter slope it comes down all at once, on saturated clay, on a valley with two thousand people in it. Captain Quill flies at two. We seed it, it rains out over the empty west range, and nobody drowns."

"And nobody drinks," said Ines Bahr, who ran the Upper Ketter co-op and had once led her whole valley out of its houses for a storm that went round the mountain. They had lost the plums on the racks. "Eleven million is what Hollin has left, Halvard. Calder has nine weeks of water. And if you order us out again for nothing, next time nobody will go."

Captain Dara Quill stood by the door with her helmet under her arm. "I've seeded two hundred cells," she said. "None of them held still while I did it. I'll fly if I'm told to. I'd like it known that I don't like it."

Illuminara had come because Marit sent her the radar loop with three words: it is listening. She had read the cell from the pass road as she read anything under load, as paths of force, and the paths were tidy in a way that weather's were not: one inflow, one core, one slow turn in the lean of the whole tower that looked less like physics than like attention. "Has anyone asked it where it is going?"

"Asked it," Teague said. "It's a storm."

"It is a storm that has held one core against the wind for three hours. In my experience, anything that holds against the wind is going somewhere." Wren Odira, who kept the clock, said there was an hour and twenty before the flight.

02 · The Page

"And what do you imagine I can say to it?" Marit asked. "I can tell you what a line of barrels does. Ten square kilometres of black felt adds less than a thousandth of that cell's power. We cannot push it. A cell leans toward the warmer, wetter inflow, and it can feel a few tenths of a degree, but the barrels are not a hand."

"No," Illuminara said. "They are a page."

She asked for what the valley had: the felt the plums dried on, three hundred charcoal burners, a hundred and forty volunteers. She would light three ridgelines, A, B and C, to equal heat, with equal fuel burned on each so that the marked ridge could not simply be the hotter one, and Marit would check them with her own thermometers. On one of the three, chosen from a shuffled deck, the burners would flash a pattern, three short and one long, over and over.

Marit would not let it stand on faith. "Two models," she said, and wrote them on separate cards. "Heat: it leans toward the warmer inflow, so on equal ridges it leans nowhere. Page: it leans toward the flashing ridge." Illuminara added the rule beneath and Marit signed it: the core must tilt toward the marked ridge within three minutes, and a draw that missed the window did not count. Ines insisted on shuffling the deck herself. She shuffled it like a woman who had watched people cheat at cards for forty years.

The first card was B. For six minutes the radar showed the core sitting exactly as it had been. At the sixth minute the lean began, long and slow, toward B. "Late," Illuminara said, and struck the draw from the count. "I assumed it already knew the page. It has to learn it first." For twelve minutes the crews flashed the pattern on each ridge in turn, four minutes apiece. Then Marit wrote a fresh pair of cards with the same rule, and Ines shuffled again.

The draws came C, B, A, C. The core tilted in two minutes, in ninety seconds, in fifty, in forty. Captain Quill, holding the stopwatch, read out the last one and then, to nobody, "One in eighty-one, if every ridge is equal and every draw independent."

"Slow at first because it was learning the page," Illuminara said. "That is not what weather does."

"Eighty-one to one is a good afternoon," Teague said from the screen, quietly. "It is not a mind."

"Equal fuel is not equal feeling," Marit said. "Flashing burners may simply read warmer to it. Ketter's inflow is thicker, and my model gives Ketter four tenths of a degree over Sallow. So keep the heat the same and change only the sign. Put the pattern it has learned on Sallow, and on Ketter flash a pattern it has never been shown, three long and one short, with the same burners, the same fuel and the same rate. I will measure both ridges myself. Heat says Ketter. The page says Sallow." She wrote both predictions down before the crews moved. Her probes put the two ridges within a tenth of a degree, the same fuel burned on each. In a minute and forty seconds the core leaned toward Sallow, against the warmer road, and Illuminara had both patterns lifted at once and the ridges returned to equal.

"It learned a sign and followed it against the heat," Marit said, with the unwilling care of someone who had meant to be the sceptic. "I'll not call it more than that."

"Nor will I," Illuminara said. "I do not say it is a mind like yours."

"A thing that can learn a sign can choose to flood a valley," Teague said.

"Yes. So we find out what it wants."

03 · What It Did Not Hear

She made her second mistake in the next ten minutes. The page had two words, lit and dark, and she meant to say not Ketter. She darkened the Ketter ridge, lit the long gentle ridge at Sallow beyond it, and told the crews to hold both.

The core swung toward the dark. In six minutes the track had moved three kilometres toward the Ketter notch, and Marit read the new arrival time aloud as if it were a diagnosis.

"You pointed it at my valley," Ines said.

"Yes." Illuminara had the crews relight Ketter at once, and the core eased back. "I wrote refusal and it read invitation. Dark is cold and still to it, and that is not a thing it avoids." She turned from the screen. "It does not want warmth. It wants the quietest cold thing it can feel, and from up there it can feel Hollin the way you can feel a stove across a room."

Nobody spoke for a moment. Then Marit pulled up the terrain. "The Ketter way is the shortest and the steepest. Forced up that slope, a cell that size is wrung out in forty kilometres. It rains itself onto the clay and never reaches the reservoir."

"Then it is walking toward its death at the wrong door," Illuminara said. "Sallow is longer and gentle. It would arrive with most of its water. It has been alive six hours, and nobody has told it that the Ketter slope is a wall."

"So what it wants," Teague said slowly, "is what Calder wants."

"Everything it wants is at Hollin. The danger is only in the road." She did not say that she had spent a quarter of an hour looking for a way to put the villages on the page, and that the page had no place for them. She said, "I asked the wrong question. I asked how to keep it off your people. The question was how to get it to the water."

They built the second message in forty minutes. The crews laid barrels so that every kilometre of each route showed the inflow the cell would find there: on Ketter a thick line for six kilometres, thinning to a few embers, on Sallow even and unbroken for thirty. Marit worked the numbers from her model; Ines's people carried the felt. At two o'clock Quill held her flight. Teague gave them until four. "At four I decide, on whatever you have shown me. The board answers to a city, and it will not wait on a miracle past that." He put the order to leave Ketter at half past two.

"It goes out now," Ines said, and walked to the door. She sent all six villages down the valley road with their animals, and kept eleven volunteers on the high ground to tend the barrels.

04 · The Fork

At four o'clock Teague opened the gate from the dam office, and it was short. Marit showed the cell forty kilometres short of the notch, whole, one core, riding the Sallow route past every ember Ines's crews had laid. Nothing had been spent. Teague did not let the old order lapse by silence. He rewrote it in two lines on the log: the seeding window would close once the cell had lost a fifth of its water, because below that it could no longer fill Hollin and the only use left in it was to rain it out safely, and until then Captain Quill waited on the ramp. "I am moving the line," he said, "and I am telling you where I have moved it."

At eight minutes to five the cell reached the notch at Greywater and stopped.

It did not leave. It did not split. It stood over the fork, turning slowly, and for the first time in the afternoon it began to rain, a thin hard curtain falling on the empty high slope. Marit counted. At eleven minutes past five she said, "It has dropped a fifth of its water."

"That is the line I drew," Teague said. "Captain, seed it now."

"If I seed a stalled core it rains out where it stands," Quill said, "and it stands over the Ketter slope. I'll fly if you order me. I'll tell you now what I'll be flying into, and what I'll be dropping it on." She looked at the empty valley road on the radar. "That valley is empty only because Mrs Bahr moved it. Hold your order."

He held it, and said aloud that he was suspending his own, and why.

Illuminara had gone to the window. The cell's paths of force lay open to her like a hand, and at the fork they had gathered into something she had not seen in them before: a hesitation, the inflow turning two ways at once. It had read the barrels. It was choosing. And in the draw toward Hollin, where all its attention ran, there was a fault, a small continual disturbance in the cold, still thing it wanted. She read it twice before she trusted it.

"Marit. What is moving on Hollin?"

Marit pulled up the reservoir's sensors. The south arm was ringed with small waves. "The intake pumps. Nine cubic metres a second, and the wake carries."

"It can feel them. The surface is not quiet." Illuminara turned to the screen. "Mr Teague, I'm asking you to stop the intakes at Hollin."

"That is Calder's evening," Teague said. "That is three hours in the tank and then nothing."

"I cannot tell you it will turn if you stop them. I can only say that it learned a sign today and followed it against the heat, and that it has no reason to choose a long road to a place that is not still." Her voice stayed level. "It may decline. I would rather you knew that before you decide."

He took four minutes. When he spoke it was not to her but to the dam crew in Calder, and the order was short. At twenty past five the pumps stopped. Marit's sensors showed the rings on the south arm flattening, then gone. The cell hung over the notch for eleven more minutes, a long time for a mind that lived nine hours. At 17:31 the core leaned toward Sallow, and the rain on the high slope stopped, and the whole tower began to move.

05 · Hollin at Dusk

It reached the reservoir at five past six with a little over three-fifths of what it had started with, and rained on it for thirty-five minutes.

Quill had flown them to the dam, the only sortie she logged that day, with nothing in the hold. Illuminara stood on the crest with Teague and Wren and watched the surface go grey and then white. She did not need the radar. She felt the paths in the cell loosen as it spent itself into the still water it had crossed the mountains to find, one thread and then another going slack, with no pain in it that she could read, only a kind of arrival. "Eight hours and fifty minutes," Marit said on the radio, from the truck. The curtain thinned. The last of it fell in a quiet that was almost warm.

Hollin took in six and a half million cubic metres from the cell itself, and the soaked catchment ran for the rest of the night and added another four. By morning Calder's nine weeks were eighteen. The Ketter villages were dry, and Ines Bahr's people came home to wet roofs and an undamaged slope. Two villages on the Sallow ridge lost their western terraces to the hail on the cell's outer edge, a season of olives and vines, and the board would pay for the walls but not for the year. Teague would be asked, for months, why the pumps had stopped.

He wrote it in the log that night, standing at the dam office desk. Under Cause of stoppage he put the word request, looked at it, and did not change it.

Nevihtna celica nad gorovjem Greywater se skoraj tri ure noče razcepiti in se začne obnašati, kakor da nekam potuje. Mestni vodni svet je ukazal, da jo ob dveh razpršijo s sejanjem oblakov; na njeni poti leži šest vasi. Illuminara pride z neprijetnim vprašanjem: jo je kdo vprašal, kam gre?

01 · Celica, ki se ni hotela razcepiti

Marit Sokol je že skoraj tri ure opazovala nevihtno celico, ne da bi videla karkoli, kar običajno počne vreme. Dvajset minut pred eno je stala nad gorovjem Greywater kot en sam stolp, širok štirinajst kilometrov, in se ni razcepila. Celica te velikosti se razcepi. Izloči manjše celice, se razširi in do večera izprazni čez tri doline. Ta pa je že od desetih do desetih dopoldne držala eno samo jedro proti vetru, kakor plavalec, ki se upira toku. Zadnjo uro se je njen vzgornik na vsakih osemintrideset sekund okrepil in umiril, tako redno kot utrip.

»Ima pot,« je povedala zbranim v zadružni dvorani v Zgornjem Kettru, kjer je svoj radarski tovornjak parkirala ob stojala za sušenje sliv. »Čez sedlo pri Greywaterju, nato naravnost po kettrskem pobočju navzdol. Na tej poti je šest vasi.«

Na stenskem zaslonu je Halvard Teague, predsednik vodnega sveta v Calderju, slabo prikrival strah z govorjenjem. »V sebi nosi približno enajst milijonov kubičnih metrov. Na kettrskem pobočju bo vse padlo naenkrat, na razmočeno glino, na dolino z dva tisoč prebivalci. Kapitanka Quill vzleti ob dveh. Posejemo oblake, dež izpraznimo nad praznim zahodnim gorovjem in nihče ne utone.«

»In nihče ne pije,« je rekla Ines Bahr, ki je vodila zadrugo v Zgornjem Kettru in je nekoč zaradi nevihte, ki je nazadnje obšla goro, izselila vso dolino. Slive na stojalih so tedaj propadle. »Enajst milijonov je toliko, kolikor je Hollinu sploh še ostalo. Calder ima vode za devet tednov. In če ljudem še enkrat ukažem, naj za nič zapustijo domove, se naslednjič nihče ne bo umaknil.«

Kapitanka Dara Quill je stala pri vratih s čelado pod pazduho. »Razpršila sem že dvesto nevihtnih celic,« je dejala. »Nobena ni stala pri miru, medtem ko sem to počela. Poletela bom, če mi ukažete. Hočem pa, da se ve, da mi to ni všeč.«

Illuminara je prišla, ker ji je Marit poslala radarski posnetek s tremi besedami: posluša nas. Celico je prebrala že s ceste proti prelazu, tako kot je brala vse, kar je bilo pod obremenitvijo: kot poti sil. Te so bile urejene na način, ki za vreme ni običajen: en dotok, eno jedro in en počasen obrat nagiba celotnega stolpa, ki je bil bolj podoben pozornosti kot fiziki. »Jo je kdo vprašal, kam gre?«

»Vprašal?« je rekel Teague. »Saj je nevihta.«

»Nevihta, ki tri ure drži eno samo jedro proti vetru. Po mojih izkušnjah gre vse, kar se upira vetru, nekam.« Wren Odira, ki je spremljala čas, je povedala, da je do poleta še ura in dvajset minut.

02 · Stran

»In kaj si predstavljate, da ji lahko rečem?« je vprašala Marit. »Lahko povem, kaj naredi vrsta sodov. Deset kvadratnih kilometrov črnega filca doda manj kot tisočinko moči te celice. Ne moremo je potisniti. Celica se nagiba proti toplejšemu, bolj vlažnemu dotoku in zazna nekaj desetink stopinje, toda sodi niso roka.«

»Ne,« je rekla Illuminara. »So stran v knjigi.«

Prosila je za tisto, kar je dolina imela: filc za sušenje sliv, tristo gorilnikov na oglje in sto štirideset prostovoljcev. Prižgali bi tri slemena, A, B in C, z enako količino toplote in goriva na vsakem, da označeno sleme ne bi bilo preprosto toplejše. Marit bi to preverjala s svojimi termometri. Na enem od treh, izbranem s premešanim svežnjem kart, bi gorilniki znova in znova utripali v vzorcu treh kratkih in enega dolgega utripa.

Marit tega ni hotela prepustiti veri. »Dva modela,« je rekla in ju zapisala na ločeni kartici. »Toplota: celica se nagne proti toplejšemu dotoku; če so slemena enaka, se ne nagne nikamor. Stran: nagne se proti utripajočemu slemenu.« Illuminara je pod napoved dopisala pravilo, Marit pa ga je podpisala: jedro se mora v treh minutah nagniti proti označenemu slemenu, poskus, ki zamudi, pa ne šteje. Ines je vztrajala, da bo karte mešala sama. Mešala jih je kakor ženska, ki je štirideset let gledala ljudi goljufati pri kartah.

Prva karta je bila B. Šest minut je radar kazal jedro na istem mestu. Šele v šesti minuti se je začelo počasi in dolgo nagibati proti B. »Prepozno,« je rekla Illuminara in poskus prečrtala. »Mislila sem, da stran že pozna. Najprej se jo mora naučiti.« Naslednjih dvanajst minut so ekipe vzorec utripale na vsakem slemenu po štiri minute. Nato je Marit pripravila nov par kartic z istim pravilom, Ines pa je še enkrat premešala.

Izžrebana slemena so bila C, B, A, C. Jedro se je nagnilo v dveh minutah, nato v devetdesetih sekundah, petdesetih in štiridesetih. Kapitanka Quill je s štoparico v roki na glas prebrala zadnji čas, nato pa tiho rekla, bolj sebi kot drugim: »Ena proti enainosemdeset, če so vsa slemena enakovredna in vsi izbori neodvisni.«

»Sprva je bila počasna, ker se je učila strani,« je rekla Illuminara. »Vreme tega ne počne.«

»Enainosemdeset proti ena je lepo popoldne,« je tiho rekel Teague z zaslona. »Ni pa to dokaz za um.«

»Enako gorivo še ne pomeni enakega občutka,« je rekla Marit. »Utripajoči gorilniki so zanjo lahko preprosto toplejši. Dotok v Ketter je gostejši in moj model tam napoveduje štiri desetinke stopinje več kot pri Sallowu. Zato ohranimo toploto in spremenimo samo znamenje. Na Sallow postavite vzorec, ki se ga je naučila, na Ketter pa naj z istimi gorilniki, enako količino goriva in enako hitrostjo utripa vzorec, ki ga še nikoli ni videla: tri dolge in en kratek. Sama bom izmerila obe slemeni. Toplota napoveduje Ketter. Stran napoveduje Sallow.« Obe napovedi je zapisala, še preden so ekipe karkoli premaknile. Njena tipala so pokazala razliko največ desetinke stopinje; na obeh slemenih je zgorelo enako goriva. Minuto in štirideset sekund pozneje se je jedro nagnilo proti Sallowu, proti toplejši poti. Illuminara je dala oba vzorca hkrati ugasniti in slemeni sta bili spet enako osvetljeni.

»Naučila se je znamenja in mu sledila proti toploti,« je rekla Marit, z nejevoljno natančnostjo človeka, ki je hotel ostati skeptik. »Več od tega ne bom trdila.«

»Tudi jaz ne,« je rekla Illuminara. »Ne pravim, da je um, podoben vašemu.«

»Nekaj, kar se lahko nauči znamenja, se lahko tudi odloči poplaviti dolino,« je rekel Teague.

»Da. Zato ugotovimo, kaj hoče.«

03 · Česar ni slišala

V naslednjih desetih minutah je naredila drugo napako. Stran je imela dve besedi, svetlo in temno, z njima pa je hotela reči ne Ketter. Zatemnila je kettrsko sleme, osvetlila dolgo, položno sleme pri Sallowu in ekipam naročila, naj oboje ohranijo.

Jedro se je zasukalo proti temi. V šestih minutah se je njegova pot premaknila tri kilometre proti kettrskemu sedlu, Marit pa je nov čas prihoda prebrala na glas, kot bi izrekala diagnozo.

»Usmerili ste jo v mojo dolino,« je rekla Ines.

»Da.« Illuminara je ekipam ukazala, naj takoj spet osvetlijo Ketter, in jedro se je začelo umikati. »Zapisala sem zavrnitev, ona pa je prebrala povabilo. Zanjo je tema hladna in mirna, to pa ni nekaj, čemur bi se izogibala.« Obrnila se je od zaslona. »Ne išče toplote. Hoče najti najmirnejšo mrzlo stvar, ki jo lahko začuti, od tam zgoraj pa Hollin čuti tako, kot vi čutite peč na drugi strani sobe.«

Nekaj trenutkov ni spregovoril nihče. Nato je Marit odprla prikaz reliefa. »Pot čez Ketter je najkrajša in najbolj strma. Tako velika celica se na takem pobočju v štiridesetih kilometrih izprazni. Dež zgrmi na glino in do zbiralnika sploh ne pride.«

»Potem gre naproti svoji smrti skozi napačna vrata,« je rekla Illuminara. »Sallow je daljši in položen. Do zbiralnika bi prispela z večino vode. Živa je šest ur in še nihče ji ni povedal, da je kettrsko pobočje zid.«

»Torej si želi,« je počasi rekel Teague, »isto kot Calder.«

»Vse, kar išče, je pri Hollinu. Nevarnost je samo v poti.« Ni rekla, da je četrt ure iskala način, kako bi na stran zapisala vasi, a na njej zanje ni bilo prostora. Rekla je: »Postavila sem napačno vprašanje. Spraševala sem se, kako jo spraviti stran od vaših ljudi. Vprašati bi se morala, kako jo spraviti do vode.«

Drugo sporočilo so sestavljali štirideset minut. Ekipe so sode razporedile tako, da je vsak kilometer obeh poti prikazoval dotok, ki ga bo celica tam našla: v Ketterju je debela črta gorela šest kilometrov in se nato redčila v nekaj žerjavic; pri Sallowu je bila enakomerna in neprekinjena trideset kilometrov. Marit je izračunala podatke po svojem modelu, Inesini ljudje so prenašali filc. Ob dveh je Quill odpovedala polet. Teague jim je dal čas do štirih. »Ob štirih se odločim na podlagi tega, kar boste pokazali. Svet odgovarja mestu in na čudež ne bo čakal dlje.« Ukazal je, naj se evakuacija Kettra začne ob pol treh.

»Odidejo takoj,« je rekla Ines in šla k vratom. Vseh šest vasi je poslala po dolinski cesti skupaj z živalmi, na varnih višavjih pa pustila enajst prostovoljcev, ki so skrbeli za sode.

04 · Razpotje

Ob štirih je Teague v pisarni pri jezu odprl sestanek in bil je kratek. Marit je pokazala celico štirideset kilometrov pred sedlom: celo, z enim jedrom, na poti čez Sallow mimo vseh žerjavic, ki so jih položile Inesine ekipe. Vode še ni izgubila. Teague starega ukaza ni pustil tiho poteči. V dnevnik je zapisal dve novi vrstici: čas za sejanje oblakov se konča, ko celica izgubi petino vode, ker pod to mejo Hollina ne more več napolniti in je koristno samo še varno izprazniti dež; do takrat kapitanka Quill čaka na vzletni stezi. »Premikam mejo,« je rekel, »in povem vam, kam sem jo premaknil.«

Ob osmih minutah pred peto je celica dosegla sedlo pri Greywaterju in obstala.

Ni odšla. Ni se razcepila. Stala je nad razpotjem, počasi se vrtela, in prvič tisto popoldne je začelo deževati: ozka, ostra zavesa dežja na prazno visoko pobočje. Marit je štela. Ob enajstih minutah čez pet je rekla: »Izgubila je petino vode.«

»To je meja, ki sem jo določil,« je rekel Teague. »Kapitanka, posejte oblake zdaj.«

»Če posejem obstalo celico, se bo izpraznila tam, kjer stoji,« je rekla Quill, »stoji pa nad kettrskim pobočjem. Poletela bom, če mi ukažete. Toda že zdaj vam povem, v kaj bom poletela in na kaj bom spustila dež.« Pogledala je prazno dolinsko cesto na radarju. »Ta dolina je prazna samo zato, ker jo je gospa Bahr izpraznila. Zadržite ukaz.«

Zadržal ga je in na glas povedal, da začasno razveljavlja svoj ukaz in zakaj.

Illuminara je stopila k oknu. Poti sil v celici so se pred njo odprle kakor dlan, pri razpotju pa so se zbrale v nekaj, česar prej še ni opazila: v omahovanje, v dotok, ki se je obračal v dve smeri hkrati. Sode je prebrala. Odločala se je. V vleku proti Hollinu, kamor je bila usmerjena vsa njena pozornost, pa je bila motnja, majhno neprestano valovanje v hladni, mirni stvari, ki jo je iskala. Prebrala ga je dvakrat, preden mu je zaupala.

»Marit. Kaj se premika na Hollinu?«

Marit je odprla podatke tipal zbiralnika. Po južnem kraku so se širili majhni valovi. »Zajemne črpalke. Devet kubičnih metrov na sekundo, valovi pa se širijo.«

»Lahko jih čuti. Gladina ni mirna.« Illuminara se je obrnila proti zaslonu. »Gospod Teague, prosim vas, da ustavite zajemne črpalke na Hollinu.«

»To je Calderjev večer,« je rekel Teague. »Za tri ure imamo v rezervoarju in potem ničesar.«

»Ne morem vam obljubiti, da bo obrnila, če jih ustavite. Povem vam lahko samo, da se je danes naučila znamenja, mu sledila proti toploti in da nima razloga izbrati dolge poti do kraja, ki ni miren.« Njen glas je ostal enakomeren. »Lahko zavrne. Raje vidim, da to veste, preden se odločite.«

Odločal se je štiri minute. Ko je spregovoril, ni govoril njej, temveč posadki jezu v Calderju, in ukaz je bil kratek. Ob dvajsetih minutah čez pet so črpalke obstale. Maritina tipala so pokazala, kako se valovi na južnem kraku umirjajo in izginejo. Celica je nad sedlom visela še enajst minut, dolgo za um, ki je živel devet ur. Ob 17.31 se je jedro nagnilo proti Sallowu, dež na visokem pobočju je ponehal in ves stolp se je začel premikati.

05 · Hollin ob mraku

Zbiralnik je dosegla pet minut čez šesto, z nekaj več kot tremi petinami vode, ki jo je imela na začetku, in nanj deževala petintrideset minut.

Quill jih je odpeljala do jezu; to je bil njen edini polet tistega dne, brez tovora v odlagalniku. Illuminara je s Teaguejem in Wren stala na grebenu in gledala, kako se gladina spremeni iz sive v belo. Radarja ni potrebovala. Čutila je, kako se poti sil v celici rahljajo, ko se je vsa izlivala v mirno vodo, ki jo je prišla iskat čez gore: ena nit za drugo je popuščala, brez bolečine, ki bi jo lahko prebrala, samo z nečim podobnim prihodu. »Osem ur in petdeset minut,« je po radiu iz tovornjaka rekla Marit. Dežna zavesa se je stanjšala. Zadnje kaplje so padle v tišino, ki je bila skoraj topla.

Hollin je neposredno iz celice dobil šest milijonov in pol kubičnih metrov vode, razmočeno porečje pa je vso noč prispevalo še štiri milijone. Do jutra se je Calderjeva oskrba podaljšala z devetih na osemnajst tednov. Kettrske vasi so ostale suhe, Inesini ljudje pa so se vrnili pod mokre strehe in na nepoškodovano pobočje. Dve vasi na sallowškem grebenu sta zaradi toče na zunanjem robu celice izgubili zahodne terase, sezono oljk in vinske trte; svet bo plačal podporne zidove, ne pa izgubljenega leta. Teagueja bodo še mesece spraševali, zakaj so se črpalke ustavile.

Tistega večera je to zapisal v dnevnik za pisalno mizo v pisarni jezu. V rubriko Vzrok za zaustavitev je napisal besedo prošnja, jo pogledal in je ni prečrtal.
