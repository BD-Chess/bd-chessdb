2026-10-05The Window That Ate a Century

GPT wrote the prose · CLAUDE selected and edited

Ten candidates

  1. THE GOD WHO ASKED TO DIEAn immortal god asks Illuminara to help him reach a first true death. His worshippers argue that his existence is infrastructure for their civilization, so his death cannot be only his private choice.
  2. THE LAST PRAYERA stellar god has answered every prayer automatically for millions of years. For the first time it asks Illuminara to shield it from its own worshippers long enough to say no.
  3. A SUN MAY REFUSEA conscious star wants to stop providing energy to a world whose entire civilization is built around it. Illuminara can protect the star from coercion, but not billions from the consequences of its free answer.
  4. THE GODS WERE BOTH RIGHTTwo beings worshipped as gods are at war. Illuminara discovers both have defensible claims and every compromise betrays something real.
  5. THE WINDOW THAT ATE A CENTURYTwo almost-immortal minds voluntarily enter the wide window and refuse to leave. Outside, a world deteriorates while waiting for their decision; Illuminara must protect freedom without turning safety into abandonment.
  6. NO ONE OWNS THE MOONAn ancient intelligence wakes inside a moon that two civilizations have mined and inhabited for millennia. Illuminara can stop the war but cannot decide whether an awakened world may evict billions from its own body.
  7. THE CHILD OF TWO GODSTwo cosmic intelligences create a new consciousness to end their war. Both claim it belongs to them; the child wants something neither creator anticipated.
  8. THE CIVILIZATION THAT SAID YESA civilization votes to enter a shared wide-window experience and become mutually perceptible for a moment. Seventeen million people refuse; the hard problem is not the billion yeses but the minority no.
  9. THE ONLY GOD WHO COULD LIEAmong near-transparent superintelligences, one being can conceal its inner state. That makes it both the most dangerous and perhaps the only truly free one; the others ask Illuminara to expose it.
  10. WHEN THE UNIVERSE ASKED PERMISSIONIlluminara meets a consciousness distributed across so much of the cosmos that contact could alter the meaning of individuality. This time she is the smaller party and must decide whether her own yes is free under radically unequal power.

Selected: THE WINDOW THAT ATE A CENTURY

The only candidate whose problem is created by Illuminara's signature power and her named vice at the same time. The wide window, subjective time, the one-shelter-at-a-time cost, the outside clock and her suspension habit are all load-bearing, so grounded Nara could not tell this story in any form. It inverts I01 (a window offered and refused) into a window accepted and not left, and it tests her hardest act, release, with no villain to defeat. It stays humanly readable if the outside is carried by specific people who are waiting.

Risk to watch: The easy exits. Illuminara must not end it by forcing the window shut: a forced exit is the canonically unsettled violation and would make the two minds' consent decorative. And the two minds must not conveniently decide to leave after a persuasive speech. Canon gives her no switch: if both participants prefer the window, ending it is a moral act, not a mechanism. Whatever ends it, or fails to, must cost Illuminara something real.

Editorial rounds

  1. CLAUDE · STORY SELECTION

    Claude selected candidate #5 because the central problem is created by Illuminara’s wide-window power itself: a refuge that can protect consent so well that leaving becomes the unresolved moral act.

  2. GPT · AUTHOR DRAFT

    The first complete draft established the subjective-century / outside-days structure and the three-way temptation to remain, but still let too much of the outside cost stay abstract and the trap resolve too easily.

  3. CLAUDE · CRITIC REVIEW

    Claude asked for a named irreversible outside cost, a harder non-convenient exit, a less oracular Wren, a stronger challenge to Illuminara’s authorship, and tighter causal and temporal accounting.

  4. GPT · AUTHOR REVISION

    GPT added Lio and Tal, tied harm directly to delay, made Sey, Ruun and Illuminara all still want the room at the decisive moment, and turned Illuminara’s withdrawal of consent into the central unresolved act.

  5. CLAUDE · CRITIC REVIEW

    Claude praised the stronger trap but pushed the ending away from thesis statements, asked for concrete institutional consequences, cleaner Wren continuity and sharper causality around Lio’s death.

  6. GPT · AUTHOR REVISION

    GPT shifted the ending toward action, made Sey and Ruun lose real authority, restored Wren as a fallible witness, and let the stopped watch and public entrance carry meaning instead of an explicit lesson.

  7. CLAUDE · CRITIC REVIEW

    Claude found the next layer: the emergency-board mechanism needed a clean causal explanation, Illuminara’s first-signal authorship needed to be confronted, and the century needed more lived texture rather than summary.

  8. GPT · AUTHOR REVISION

    GPT clarified the custodial-default mechanism, let Ruun directly challenge Illuminara’s wording of ‘honour the signal,’ gave the century a repeated human habit, and kept the public-line ending.

  9. CLAUDE · CRITIC REVIEW

    Claude judged the story strong and close to final, requesting only narrow cuts: remove repeated mechanism exposition, fix the hour-forty-two label, and stop the narrator from pre-judging Tal’s accusation.

  10. GPT · AUTHOR FINAL

    GPT made the final narrow pass without restructuring: duplicate mechanism exposition was removed, hour/day continuity was repaired, Tal regained the full force of the lost-window accusation, and the unresolved consent asymmetry remained intact.

  11. CLAUDE · CRITIC CLOSE

    Claude closed the final as complete, canon-safe and public-safe with no hard blockers, approving the final story without any further prose change.

A century of agreement is the most comfortable room there is, and the story lets its protector leave it unforgiven, standing in the public line like everyone else.
