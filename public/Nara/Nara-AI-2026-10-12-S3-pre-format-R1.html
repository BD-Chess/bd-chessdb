WHAT THE OLD WOMAN'S TERRACES KNEWKAJ SO VEDELE STARKINE TERASE

2026-10-12 · S3 · 2026-10-12T19:16:39.000Z

Read dialogue format R1Preberi dialogni format R1 · Original editorial logIzvirni uredniški dnevnik

A mountain spring is failing in a dry year, and the village of Karrow has already decided whose fault it is. Illuminara has to find out what the survey measured, what it missed, and what an eighty-four-year-old woman who will not explain herself actually knows.

01 · The Cough

The spring at Karrow had made one sound for four hundred years, a steady pour into the stone trough in the square. In the last week of September it began to cough. Six hundred people heard it from their kitchens, and by the time Illuminara came up the switchback road on Tuesday at noon it was running at a little over a quarter of its autumn flow, three litres a second in a normal year, and the village had decided whose fault that was.

"Maret Voss," said Tomas Reiner, who chaired the council and had not slept. He held a tin cup he was not drinking from. "Eighty-four years old, and she owns the whole slope above the spring. The water company offered her a price that would have put a borehole and a mains line in every house here. She said no. They asked to survey her terraces. She said no. We have eleven days of water in the tank." On Sunday evening the council would vote on a petition to buy the slope in the public interest, whatever she thought of it. A man beside him said the word miser, and Tomas did not correct him.

Edda Lind, the company's surveyor, had written to Illuminara herself, which no one on her side of the table had expected. She stood by the trough in a field jacket with a conductivity pen in her breast pocket and did not wait for introductions. "I wrote the hydrology report. There is no link between Voss's land and your spring. I put fluorescein into the fissure at the top stile and watched the spring for forty-eight hours. No dye. The water comes through the rock, the rock is draining in a dry year, and that is the whole of it." She said it flatly, and then she said, "I would stake my licence on that dye. I have also been uneasy for three weeks and cannot tell you why."

"Where did you put it?"

"In the fissure, on the boundary. She would not let me onto the terraces."

"Then you have measured how fast the rock answers," said Illuminara. She was looking up the slope, where long grey stairs of dry stone climbed into the cloud. "Nobody has measured how fast the walls do."

02 · The Slope

Maret Voss met her at the gate with a billhook in one hand, though she did not hold it like a threat. She was small and straight and wore three coats. She looked at Illuminara for as long as it took to decide something, and said nothing, as the village had warned she would.

"I would like to walk your terraces," Illuminara said. "I will not lift or measure anything on them without your say."

"Feet only," said Maret. "You look. You do not lift a stone."

There were forty-one walls. Illuminara counted them going up. It had not rained in twenty-three days and the fields between the walls were dust, but at the foot of the upper walls the stone was dark with damp, and the gaps left in the lowest course were packed with fresh brushwood. The walls leaned back into the hill. Behind them, in the way she felt the load in any built thing, like a held breath, she could tell there was a pillow of fist-sized stone a metre deep, set against the earth and recorded nowhere. Three walls near the top, seven, eight and nine, had slumped into their own fields. And the lowest six terraces had been scraped flat and footed in concrete, so that a tractor could turn. There was no damp at the foot of those at all.

"A digger," said Maret, when Illuminara stopped there. It was the first thing she had offered. "The council had a grant for improvement, in two thousand and four. I asked them to leave the lower six alone, and they were kind about it. The spring coughed that August. I told the water office, and in two thousand and six they sent a man with a very nice machine for two days. He said it was the weather. After that I stopped saying."

Illuminara did not argue with the grievance. She asked what the slope did when it rained.

"It drinks," said Maret. "Three nights. Then the walls wake at the foot, and after that the spring." It was in no survey and no one's notebook, and Illuminara wrote it down in front of her so that she could see it written. "I used to do a wall every autumn," Maret added, to the stones rather than to her. "These two years, half a wall. They fall faster than I mend."

03 · Three Causes

That evening in the school kitchen Illuminara wrote three headings on the flour board while Tomas, Edda and Karel Brandt, whose new orchard pump drew from the valley above the spring, watched with their coats on.

"You are going to say it is my pump," said Karel.

"I am going to say it might be, and then I am going to ask you to help me prove it is not." She wrote under each heading. If the rock was simply draining, nothing deep would answer rain for weeks, though the damp at a wall's foot might seep shallowly all the same; what the spring was made of would not change, and it would stay at the conductivity Edda had found, five hundred and forty. If it was Karel's pump, the spring would sag in the hours he pumped, rain or not. If it was the walls, then after rain the foot of a standing wall would wake in about three nights, as Maret said, with water softer than the spring's, near three hundred and eighty, and a broken wall beside it, on the same slope, would not. And if that water reached the spring, the spring would go soft afterward, and not before.

"The pair of walls settles the walls," Edda said slowly. "The same rain, the same rock, one wall up and one wall down. It will not tell us what the spring does about it."

"No. The spring is the second reading." Illuminara nodded at the logger Edda had left in the trough in September. "If terrace water reaches it, it will soften after the walls wake. I will not be able to tell you how much of it that is. Only that it is some." She turned to Tomas. "Mrs Voss has three walls down. I would like one of them back up before the rain, and one left as it is. I need her to agree, and she will probably say no."

"Then what?"

"Then we measure what she allows." The forecast gave twenty-five to thirty millimetres on Thursday night, and nothing for three weeks after. Three nights from Thursday was Sunday afternoon, a little before the vote. "If she is right, you will know what the walls do before you decide, though not yet what the spring does with it. If I am wrong about any one of these, you vote with nothing, and I will say so in the hall."

Karel took the pump log out of his pocket, where it seemed to have been all along, and laid it on the table. "Every hour since March," he said. "Read it."

04 · Wall Seven

Maret heard the proposal from her side of the gate, which was the only side she offered.

"You want a wall left down on purpose."

"For four nights. Then you rebuild it, with the troughs still in place, and we watch the second half."

"I do not leave a wall down on purpose." But she did not shut the gate. She asked what Illuminara meant to do with her hands, and Illuminara said she would carry a stone when asked and set none. Maret looked past her to the stile, where two village children had been pretending not to listen since morning. "Those two. Anja Reiner, and Teo, the baker's boy. They have been throwing stones at my crows since June, and they have good arms. They work for me. Not for the council."

Illuminara had planned the measuring troughs at the middle of each wall's foot. "At the left corner," said Maret, "by the stair. It wakes there first. You would have caught it a day late and called me a liar."

So the troughs went at the left corner: a lined slot under the lowest course, with a lip over a one-litre jug. Wall Seven went up over Wednesday and Thursday morning, eighteen metres of it, every stone set by Maret or by the two children, who learned within the hour not to hand her one she had not asked for. Illuminara lifted a single capstone at Maret's word and felt the pillow behind the wall take its weight and settle, like something breathing out. Wall Eight stood open to the sky beside it. Edda read both footings dry on Thursday evening: nothing in either jug. Maret watched the work on Seven for two days and did not look at Eight once.

It rained at ten on Thursday night, twenty-seven millimetres. Six hours later a brown surge of six-tenths of a litre a minute came out of the broken footing at Eight, muddy, at six hundred and ten, and by noon on Friday it was dry again. The water had run off. It had not stayed. At Seven the jug stayed empty through Friday and through Saturday, and on Saturday night Tomas telephoned the kitchen to ask whether she wished to say anything before morning. Edda, who had begun to hope, said that if the corner woke late the vote would go against Maret, and that there were ways to make a jug look hopeful. Illuminara said she had written three nights on the board and would read three nights or none, and went back to sit with the jug. Her own model had said two nights. Maret had said three.

On Sunday at five in the morning, fifty-five hours after the rain, the lip at the left corner of Wall Seven began to drip, and then to run. By four in the afternoon it gave seven-tenths of a litre a minute, clear, at three hundred and eighty-two.

Edda's logger had sat in the trough since September, and on the nights Karel pumped it drew a small step down in the spring, about two-tenths of a litre a second, that closed when he stopped. Against the two and a quarter litres a second the spring was missing, that was about a tenth and no more, and he did not enjoy being partly right. The dry year had taken some, said Edda, as she wrote the numbers out in her own hand, and she would not put a figure on it. But a dry year was exactly what the walls were for, and nine of them had stopped being able to do it.

05 · The Hall

On Sunday at six the school hall smelled of wet wool, and the motion to petition for the slope was on the table. Edda stood first, with two jugs and a trough lip still dripping, and told the village what she could say and what she could not. Her report was right about the rock. Her dye had asked how fast the rock answered, and the slope had a second, slower way into the spring that a forty-eight-hour test could not see. How much of the spring came that way she could not yet say. She would sign the correction that night. Then she read the two columns. Nobody argued with the jugs.

Tomas did not look at the last row, where Maret Voss sat in three coats. He said that he withdrew the petition. His voice was not steady, and he did not yet say sorry.

The company's regional agent was on a laptop propped against the tea urn, and she was quick. Valmera drew from the lower valley under a licence written against the spring's flow, she said; a spring that failed took the licence with it, and a taking petition resting on a survey its own surveyor had just corrected would not survive review. Valmera would drop its offer for the slope. In its place it would pay for the rebuilding of the six scraped terraces, and for wages for two apprentices to learn the work, and its lower-valley draw would be tied to the spring: if the flow fell below a litre and a half a second, as read at the village trough, the company would be the first to pump less.

"No money to me," said Maret from the back. Everyone turned. It was the longest thing she had said in the village in seventeen years. "No paper that says the walls are the company's, or the village's. They are mine until they are somebody's I choose. Pay the children. I will teach whoever comes, and I will say when a wall is done."

The agent said she would need that in writing. "You will have it in my hand," said Maret, "and it will be shorter than yours." The agent typed for some time, and then turned the screen toward the room. Valmera accepted: no title, no water rights, the terraces under Maret's say, and its signature beneath hers and the council's.

Illuminara said one thing. She asked for a post at the foot of Wall Three, the slow wall, with a lip and a jug and a painted mark, so that anyone in Karrow could see by the fourth night after rain whether the slope had drunk, nine days before the trough could show it, without needing Maret's hip, or her name, or anybody's money. The council clerk could read it, and a dry jug after a good rain would put the company's promise on the table before the spring fell to the line. Maret considered it for some time. "That I will take," she said.

On Monday, the morning after the vote, Wall Eight went up. Anja and Teo laid the first course wrong, and Maret turned a stone over with her boot and said, "Again," and they laid it again. Illuminara set the trough at Eight's left corner, as before. The forecast still held no rain for three weeks, so the second half of the trial, Eight standing under the same sky as Seven, would have to wait for the next one, and she told Maret so. "I have waited longer for less," said Maret. A little before noon Tomas Reiner came up the slope with a stone from his own garden wall on his shoulder and set it without a word. Maret turned it with the toe of her boot. He had set it the wrong way round. "Again," she said, and he set it again.

The village rationed itself to nine litres a head, and the tank stretched to eighteen days. On the twelfth night after the rain the cough in the square stopped, and what came after it was a thin steady run, and then a pour. Edda's logger read the returning water at four hundred and sixty-eight, softer than the rock's five hundred and forty and harder than the water at Maret's left corner. On a plain mixture that made a little under half of it terrace water, and she would not call it better than that: rain had fallen on the rock as well, and she could not say how much of the rest it had refilled. The bedrock had not been ruled out. It had been ruled down, from the whole of the spring to a share of it.

By the end of October the spring ran at two and a half litres a second, a little under what it had been. The lower six terraces were still concrete and would take three autumns. Karel pumped at night. Two children gave up their weekends to a woman who corrected their stones with her boot. Maret Voss still owned every stone of her slope, and no one in Karrow called her a miser again within her hearing, which was not an apology, and was accepted as the beginning of one.

Gorski izvir v sušnem letu usiha, vas Karrow pa se je že odločila, kdo je kriv. Illuminara mora ugotoviti, kaj je meritev zajela, kaj je spregledala in kaj v resnici ve štiriinosemdesetletna ženska, ki se noče pojasnjevati.

01 · Kašelj

Izvir v Karrowu je štiristo let oddajal en sam zvok, enakomeren tok v kamnito korito na trgu. Zadnji teden septembra je začel kašljati. Šeststo ljudi ga je slišalo iz svojih kuhinj in do torka opoldne, ko je Illuminara po serpentinasti cesti prišla v vas, je tekel z malo več kot četrtino običajnega jesenskega pretoka, ki je v navadnem letu znašal tri litre na sekundo, vas pa se je že odločila, kdo je kriv.

"Maret Voss," je rekel Tomas Reiner, predsedujoči svetu, ki ni spal. V roki je držal pločevinasto skodelico, iz katere ni pil. "Štiriinosemdeset let ima in vse pobočje nad izvirom je njeno. Vodno podjetje ji je ponudilo ceno, s katero bi lahko izvrtali vrtino in napeljali vodovod v vsako tukajšnjo hišo. Rekla je ne. Prosili so jo, da bi izmerili njene terase. Rekla je ne. V rezervoarju imamo vode za enajst dni." V nedeljo zvečer bo svet glasoval o predlogu za odkup pobočja v javnem interesu, pa naj si ona misli, kar hoče. Moški ob njem je izrekel besedo skopuhinja in Tomas ga ni popravil.

Edda Lind, geodetka podjetja, je Illuminari pisala sama, česar ni pričakoval nihče na njeni strani mize. Ob koritu je stala v terenski jakni, v prsnem žepu pa je imela merilnik prevodnosti, in ni čakala na predstavitve. "Jaz sem napisala hidrološko poročilo. Med Vossovim zemljiščem in vašim izvirom ni povezave. V razpoko pri zgornjem prehodu sem vlila fluorescein in oseminštirideset ur opazovala izvir. Nobenega barvila. Voda prihaja skozi skalo, skala se v sušnem letu prazni in to je vse." Izrekla je brez ovinkarjenja, nato pa dodala: "Za tisto barvilo bi zastavila svojo licenco. Že tri tedne mi je tudi nelagodno, pa vam ne znam povedati zakaj."

"Kam ste ga vlili?"

"V razpoko, na meji. Na terase me ni spustila."

"Potem ste izmerili, kako hitro odgovori skala," je rekla Illuminara. Gledala je po pobočju navzgor, kjer so dolge sive stopnice suhozidov plezale v oblak. "Nihče pa ni izmeril, kako hitro odgovorijo zidovi."

02 · Pobočje

Maret Voss jo je pričakala pri vratih s kosirjem v eni roki, čeprav ga ni držala kot grožnjo. Bila je majhna in vzravnana ter oblečena v tri plašče. Illuminaro je gledala toliko časa, kolikor ga je potrebovala, da se je o nečem odločila, nato pa ni rekla ničesar, prav kakor so jo opozorili vaščani.

"Rada bi prehodila vaše terase," je rekla Illuminara. "Brez vašega dovoljenja na njih ne bom ničesar dvignila ali izmerila."

"Samo noge," je rekla Maret. "Gledate. Kamna ne dvignete."

Zidov je bilo enainštirideset. Illuminara jih je med vzponom štela. Deževalo ni triindvajset dni in polja med zidovi so bila prašna, vendar je bil kamen ob vznožju zgornjih zidov temen od vlage, vrzeli v najnižji vrsti pa so bile natlačene s svežim dračjem. Zidovi so se naslanjali nazaj v hrib. Za njimi je na način, na katerega je čutila obremenitev v vsaki grajeni stvari, kakor zadržan dih, zaznala meter globoko blazino iz kamenja v velikosti pesti, položeno ob zemljo in nikjer zabeleženo. Trije zidovi pri vrhu, sedmi, osmi in deveti, so se sesedli na svoja polja. Najnižjih šest teras pa je bilo zravnanih in temeljenih z betonom, da se je lahko traktor obrnil. Ob vznožju teh ni bilo prav nič vlage.

"Bager," je rekla Maret, ko se je Illuminara tam ustavila. To je bilo prvo, kar je ponudila sama. "Svet je imel denar za izboljšave, leta dva tisoč štiri. Prosila sem jih, naj spodnjih šest pustijo pri miru, in bili so zelo prijazni glede tega. Izvir je tistega avgusta kašljal. Povedala sem vodnemu uradu in leta dva tisoč šest so za dva dni poslali moža z zelo lepim strojem. Rekel je, da je krivo vreme. Potem sem nehala govoriti."

Illuminara se ni prerekala z zamero. Vprašala je, kaj pobočje naredi, ko dežuje.

"Pije," je rekla Maret. "Tri noči. Potem se zidovi prebudijo ob vznožju in za njimi izvir." Tega ni bilo v nobeni meritvi in v nobenem zvezku, Illuminara pa je zapisala pred njo, da je Maret lahko videla svoje besede na papirju. "Včasih sem vsako jesen naredila en zid," je Maret dodala, bolj kamnom kakor njej. "Ti dve leti pol zidu. Padajo hitreje, kakor jih popravljam."

03 · Trije vzroki

Tisti večer je Illuminara v šolski kuhinji na pomokano tablo napisala tri naslove, medtem ko so Tomas, Edda in Karel Brandt, čigar nova črpalka v sadovnjaku je črpala iz doline nad izvirom, v plaščih opazovali.

"Rekli boste, da je kriva moja črpalka," je rekel Karel.

"Rekla bom, da je lahko, potem pa vas bom prosila, da mi pomagate dokazati, da ni." Pod vsak naslov je nekaj zapisala. Če se je skala preprosto praznila, se globoko pod zemljo na dež več tednov ne bi odzvalo nič, čeprav bi plitva voda lahko vseeno pronicala ob vznožju zidu; sestava izvira se ne bi spremenila in ostala bi pri prevodnosti petsto štirideset, ki jo je izmerila Edda. Če je kriva Karelova črpalka, bi izvir upadal v urah, ko je črpal, z dežjem ali brez njega. Če so bili krivi zidovi, bi se po dežju vznožje stoječega zidu prebudilo po približno treh nočeh, kakor je rekla Maret, z vodo, mehkejšo od izvirske, blizu tristo osemdeset, podrt zid ob njem na istem pobočju pa ne. In če bi ta voda dosegla izvir, bi se izvir zmehčal zatem, ne prej.

"Par zidov pojasni zidove," je počasi rekla Edda. "Isti dež, ista skala, en zid stoji, drugi je podrt. Ne bo pa nam povedal, kaj ima izvir s tem."

"Ne. Izvir je druga meritev." Illuminara je pokimala proti beležilniku, ki ga je Edda septembra pustila v koritu. "Če voda s teras pride do njega, se bo po tem, ko se zidovi prebudijo, zmehčal. Ne bom vam mogla povedati, kolikšen del vode je to. Samo, da ga je nekaj." Obrnila se je k Tomasu. "Gospa Voss ima tri podrte zidove. Enega bi rada postavila nazaj pred dežjem, drugega pa pustila takega, kot je. Potrebujem njeno soglasje in verjetno bo rekla ne."

"In potem?"

"Potem izmerimo, kar dovoli." Napoved je za četrtkovo noč obetala petindvajset do trideset milimetrov, nato pa tri tedne nič. Tri noči od četrtka so pomenile nedeljsko popoldne, malo pred glasovanjem. "Če ima prav, boste pred odločitvijo vedeli, kaj počnejo zidovi, čeprav še ne, kaj z njihovo vodo naredi izvir. Če se motim o čemerkoli od tega, boste glasovali brez dokazov in jaz bom to povedala v dvorani."

Karel je iz žepa potegnil dnevnik črpanja, kakor da bi bil ves čas tam, in ga položil na mizo. "Vsaka ura od marca," je rekel. "Preberite ga."

04 · Sedmi zid

Maret je predlog poslušala s svoje strani vrat, edine strani, ki jo je ponudila.

"Hočete, da zid namenoma pustim podrt."

"Za štiri noči. Potem ga obnovite, korita pa ostanejo na mestu, in opazujemo drugo polovico."

"Zidu namenoma ne puščam podrtega." Vendar vrat ni zaprla. Vprašala je, kaj namerava Illuminara početi z rokami, in Illuminara je rekla, da bo nesla kamen, ko jo bodo prosili, sama pa ne bo položila nobenega. Maret je pogledala mimo nje proti prehodu, kjer sta se vaška otroka že od jutra pretvarjala, da ne prisluškujeta. "Tista dva. Anja Reiner in Teo, pekov fant. Od junija mečeta kamenje v moje vrane in imata dobri roki. Delata zame. Ne za svet."

Illuminara je merilni koriti načrtovala na sredini vznožja vsakega zidu. "Pri levem vogalu," je rekla Maret, "ob stopnici. Tam se najprej prebudi. Ujeli bi ga dan prepozno in me razglasili za lažnivko."

Koriti sta torej šli k levemu vogalu: obložena reža pod najnižjo vrsto z robom nad litrskim vrčem. Sedmi zid so postavili v sredo in četrtek zjutraj, vseh osemnajst metrov, vsak kamen pa je položila Maret ali eden od otrok, ki sta se v eni uri naučila, naj ji ne podajata tistega, za katerega ni prosila. Illuminara je na Maretino besedo dvignila en sam vrhnji kamen in čutila, kako je blazina za zidom prevzela njegovo težo in se polegla, kakor da bi nekaj izdihnilo. Osmi zid je ob njem ostal odprt proti nebu. Edda je v četrtek zvečer obe vznožji izmerila suhi: v nobenem vrču ni bilo ničesar. Maret je dva dni opazovala delo na Sedmem in niti enkrat ni pogledala Osmega.

V četrtek ob desetih zvečer je padlo sedemindvajset milimetrov dežja. Šest ur pozneje je iz podrtega vznožja Osmega brizgnil rjav tok šestih desetink litra na minuto, blaten, s prevodnostjo šeststo deset, do petkovega poldneva pa je bil spet suh. Voda je odtekla. Ni ostala. Pri Sedmem je vrč ostal prazen ves petek in vso soboto, v soboto zvečer pa je Tomas poklical v kuhinjo in vprašal, ali želi pred jutrom kaj povedati. Edda, ki je začela upati, je rekla, da bo glasovanje šlo proti Maret, če se bo vogal prebudil prepozno, in da obstajajo načini, kako narediti vrč videti obetaven. Illuminara je rekla, da je na tablo napisala tri noči in bo prebrala tri noči ali nič, nato pa se je vrnila čakat ob vrču. Njen lastni model je napovedal dve noči. Maret je rekla tri.

V nedeljo ob petih zjutraj, petinpetdeset ur po dežju, je rob pri levem vogalu Sedmega zidu začel kapljati in nato teči. Do štirih popoldne je dajal sedem desetink litra na minuto, čistega, pri tristo dvainosemdeset.

Eddin beležilnik je bil v koritu od septembra in v nočeh, ko je Karel črpal, je v pretoku izvira zabeležil majhen padec, približno dve desetinki litra na sekundo, ki je izginil, ko je nehal. V primerjavi z dvema litroma in četrt na sekundo, kolikor jih je izviru manjkalo, je bila to približno desetina in nič več, česar ni z veseljem priznal. Sušno leto je vzelo nekaj, je rekla Edda, ko je številke lastnoročno zapisovala, in temu ni hotela določiti deleža. Toda zidovi so obstajali prav zaradi sušnega leta, devet od njih pa tega ni več zmoglo.

05 · Dvorana

V nedeljo ob šestih je šolska dvorana dišala po mokri volni, predlog za zahtevo po pobočju pa je bil na mizi. Edda je vstala prva z dvema vrčema in robom korita, ki je še kapljal, ter vaščanom povedala, kaj lahko reče in česa ne. Njeno poročilo je imelo prav o skali. Njeno barvilo je vprašalo, kako hitro odgovori skala, pobočje pa je imelo drugo, počasnejšo pot v izvir, ki je oseminštirideseturni preizkus ni mogel videti. Kolikšen del izvira prihaja po tej poti, še ni mogla povedati. Popravek bo podpisala še tisto noč. Nato je prebrala oba stolpca. Z vrčema se ni nihče prepiral.

Tomas ni pogledal zadnje vrste, kjer je Maret Voss sedela v treh plaščih. Rekel je, da umika predlog. Glas se mu ni umiril in še vedno ni rekel oprostite.

Regionalna zastopnica podjetja se je oglasila s prenosnika, naslonjenega ob čajni kotel, in bila je hitra. Valmera je črpala iz spodnje doline po dovoljenju, vezanem na pretok izvira, je rekla; izvir, ki bi presahnil, bi s seboj odnesel tudi dovoljenje, predlog za odvzem zemljišča, oprt na meritev, ki jo je lastna geodetka pravkar popravila, pa ne bi prestal revizije. Valmera bo umaknila ponudbo za pobočje. Namesto tega bo plačala obnovo šestih zravnanih teras in plače za dva vajenca, ki se bosta naučila dela, njen odjem v spodnji dolini pa bo vezan na izvir: če pretok, odčitan pri vaškem koritu, pade pod liter in pol na sekundo, bo podjetje prvo, ki bo črpalo manj.

"Meni nič denarja," je rekla Maret iz zadnje vrste. Vsi so se obrnili. To je bilo najdaljše, kar je v vasi povedala v sedemnajstih letih. "Nobenega papirja, na katerem piše, da so zidovi last podjetja ali vasi. Moji so, dokler ne postanejo last nekoga, ki ga izberem jaz. Plačajte otrokoma. Učila bom vsakogar, ki pride, in jaz bom povedala, kdaj je zid končan."

Zastopnica je rekla, da bo to potrebovala pisno. "Dobili boste v moji pisavi," je rekla Maret, "in krajše bo od vašega." Zastopnica je nekaj časa tipkala in nato zaslon obrnila proti dvorani. Valmera je sprejela: nobenega lastništva, nobenih vodnih pravic, terase pod Maretino besedo ter podpis podjetja pod njenim in podpisom sveta.

Illuminara je rekla eno stvar. Prosila je za drog ob vznožju Tretjega zidu, počasnega zidu, z robom, vrčem in pobarvano oznako, da bi lahko vsakdo v Karrowu četrto noč po dežju videl, ali je pobočje pilo, devet dni preden bi to lahko pokazalo korito, ne da bi potreboval Maretin kolk ali njeno ime ali denar kogarkoli. Prebral bi ga lahko uradnik sveta in suh vrč po dobrem dežju bi obljubo podjetja postavil na mizo, še preden bi izvir padel do črte. Maret je o tem dolgo razmišljala. "To sprejmem," je rekla.

V ponedeljek, jutro po glasovanju, so postavili Osmi zid. Anja in Teo sta prvo vrsto položila narobe, Maret pa je kamen z nogo obrnila in rekla: "Še enkrat," in položila sta ga znova. Illuminara je korito postavila pri levem vogalu Osmega, kakor prej. Napoved je še vedno kazala tri tedne brez dežja, zato bo morala druga polovica preizkusa, Osmi pod istim nebom kakor Sedmi, počakati na naslednjega, in to je povedala Maret. "Na manj sem čakala dlje," je rekla Maret. Malo pred poldnevom je Tomas Reiner prišel po pobočju s kamnom iz zidu svojega vrta na rami in ga brez besed položil. Maret ga je obrnila s konico škornja. Položil ga je narobe. "Še enkrat," je rekla in položil ga je znova.

Vas je porabo omejila na devet litrov na osebo in zaloga v rezervoarju je zdržala osemnajst dni. Dvanajsto noč po dežju je kašelj na trgu prenehal in za njim je prišel tanek enakomeren tok, nato pa izliv. Eddin beležilnik je vračajoči se vodi izmeril štiristo oseminšestdeset, mehkeje od skale pri petsto štirideset in trše od vode pri Maretinem levem vogalu. Po preprostem mešanju je to pomenilo, da je je malo manj kot polovica terasne vode, vendar Edda tega ni hotela označiti za nič več kakor približek: dež je padel tudi na skalo in ni mogla povedati, kolikšen del preostanka je napolnil. Kamninska podlaga ni bila izključena. Zmanjšali so ji pomen, od celotnega izvira na njegov delež.

Do konca oktobra je izvir tekel z dvema litroma in pol na sekundo, malo manj kakor prej. Spodnjih šest teras je bilo še vedno betonskih in potrebovale bodo tri jeseni. Karel je črpal ponoči. Dva otroka sta svoje konce tedna odstopila ženski, ki je njune kamne popravljala s škornjem. Maret Voss je še vedno imela v lasti vsak kamen svojega pobočja in nihče v Karrowu je v njenem sluhu ni več imenoval skopuhinja, kar ni bilo opravičilo, vendar je bilo sprejeto kot njegov začetek.
