01 · Four Thousand and Seven

Teller's Reach, the Governor's map room · day one of thirteen, morning

The Relay's answer to Teller's four thousand and seventh message had come at dawn, in the same eleven words it had used for the other four thousand and six. Governor Edda Kaspar read them aloud without looking at the board, which told Illuminara she had read them many times alone.

Governor KasparProbable forgery. Answer not accepted. Reseeding in thirteen days.

Through the window, eleven ships hung above the colony with their noses toward the Pelion Gap, like dogs told to wait. Under every sound in the room ran a low tick from the board's speaker, the Relay's count, carried down from the Gap so that the colony could hear how long it had.

Governor KasparYou have read the summary. It is not cruel. It was left by people who loved their world, and in thirteen days it will rebuild three systems, my daughter's school and my father's grave among them, in the shape they asked it to keep. Forty thousand people become the people it remembers. Understanding it does not make it kinder, Illuminara. It makes the thing harder to hate, and I need to hate it by the end of next week.

InesNobody dies in a Reseeding. That is the part the Council cannot say aloud. They wake as the people the makers remembered, grateful and fluent in a language we do not speak, and not one of them was ever on our roll. And it doesn't refuse us for rudeness. It refuses because it is certain. We sent it mathematics, the genome of every name on the colony roll, a recording of the whole harbour singing. Same eleven words.

IlluminaraMay I read all four thousand? Not to find a better one. I want to see what the Relay thought each of them was.

Governor KasparYou will find a committee. I chaired it. We were very good.

She said it flatly, the way a person names the weather of a bad year. Her hand lay on the edge of the board with the knuckles white. She is not afraid of the Relay, Illuminara thought. She is afraid of being the last person who could have stopped it, and did not.

Governor KasparThe fleet holds until the start of day eleven. After that the Fleet Council decides, not I, and I will tell you now which way I will vote.

IlluminaraWhich?

Governor KasparFire.

IlluminaraThen I know what I am racing. Thank you for not letting me guess.

02 · Eleven Words

Pelion Gap, the Relay's gallery · day two, night

The Relay stood in the Gap no taller than a lighthouse, ringed by a gallery where nine thousand small lamps, one for each century of its waiting, had burned out long before the colony came. Ines had trained a translation lattice on four thousand messages and four thousand refusals. It spoke now from a grille in the floor, slowly, with the care of a voice that had learned to be heard.

The RelayYou are the four thousand and eighth. You have brought no message. This is unusual.

IlluminaraI came to listen first. May I ask you something?

The RelayAsking is not the form. The form is: is anyone there. You answer. I judge.

IlluminaraThen I will ask, and you may judge whether I was allowed. Why do you refuse them?

The RelayBecause I could have built each one. A voice that says what I would have written is not a voice. Four thousand and seven times the sky has said exactly what I would say if I were the sky.

Illuminara had read the whole archive that afternoon. Every message had been polished by a committee and sent. Every refusal had come back in eleven words. And nobody, in all that time, had replied to the eleven words. They had sent the next message instead.

IlluminaraIn nine hundred thousand years, has anyone answered your answer?

The grille was silent for the length of a long breath.

The RelayThe form has no room for it.

InesIt does. The makers' grammar has a mood we translated as rhetorical, because it never seemed to expect anything. I think we got it wrong. I think it's the mood you use when you speak to someone about what they have just said.

The RelayThat mood was used between the makers and me. It has not been spoken to me since the last of them stopped. You ask if I could have predicted that you would ask. I could. A good enough forger follows the conversation as well.

IlluminaraYes. A clever enough machine could do what I just did, and I cannot prove I am anything else. I am not offering you a proof. I am offering you somewhere to look. You can only hear answers, because that is how you were built to listen. Nobody has ever asked you a question you could not have built.

The RelayA forger could generate questions at random.

IlluminaraRandom is not surprising. Surprising is when the question turns out, by your own reckoning, to be one you needed and could not have written. I cannot tell you when that happens. Only you can. I will not decide it for you.

The lamps did not light. But something in the grille changed pitch, the way a held note does when the player decides to hold it.

03 · The Child Who Would Not Stop

Teller's Reach, the school at the end of Cistern Row · day three, morning

Bryn Maro was twelve, and she answered every question Ines asked her with a better question. Her mother commanded the lead ship of the eleven. Her school bag, which she held in front of her like a shield, was lettered in three colours of marker.

InesLast winter she wrote the Relay forty questions. The committee deleted all of them before they were sent.

BrynThey said a question isn't an answer. I said that's the point.

IlluminaraWhat would you ask it, if the committee were not in the room?

BrynI don't know. I'd only know when I heard it talking. That's why I never get to ask. They want me to have the question ready first.

Illuminara sat down on the low bench so that their eyes were level.

IlluminaraI have to tell you the dangerous part. If your question is not what the Relay needs, it says forgery again, and your mother's fleet has about seven days left before the Council votes to fire. I cannot promise how that goes. You can say no, and nobody will think less of you.

BrynI know I can say no. That's why it has to be me. Everyone else wants to pass.

She said it without pride, as a fact about the colony, and Ines looked at the floor as if the sentence had been read out in committee.

IlluminaraThen here is what I can do. I will be there. I will carry the cold and the dark for you, and I will not touch the question. It has to arrive in your hands or it is mine.

For a moment Illuminara felt the small, ridiculous joy of being trusted by someone who owed her nothing. She did not say so. It seemed too valuable to spend on a bench.

04 · Seven Nights

Pelion Gap, the Relay's gallery · the seven nights before day ten

On the first night Bryn asked the Relay whether it got tired. The Relay said: probable forgery, answer not accepted, and the gallery grew very cold. She had planned it. The committee's phrase for that was prepared.

BrynI wrote it on the way up. That's why it didn't count, isn't it?

The RelayYou prepared it. I could have prepared it with you.

On the second night Bryn asked nothing at all. She sat on the stone with her bag in her lap and listened to the Relay count down in the quiet between the replies, a sound like a small animal turning over in sleep. Illuminara listened too. Under the counting was a hum, three notes rising and one falling, so soft that a person had to stop being useful to hear it.

It sings to itself, she thought, and felt the particular tenderness of being allowed to overhear.

On the fourth night Ines read the whole archive aloud, the four thousand, and Bryn put her hand over her mouth. When it was done she spoke to nobody in particular.

BrynWe stopped talking to it and started trying to pass it. That is the true thing. We were so afraid it would end us that we forgot there was anyone in there.

The RelayI have heard no sentence like that before. I note that it is not a question.

BrynI know. I'm saying it first so you know I'm not being clever.

On the seventh night, while the count ticked through the silence, she asked it. She did not look at her hands. She did not look at Illuminara.

BrynYou said the makers told you to wait until the sky answers or the sky is empty. But they knew they weren't coming back. So why do you keep counting? Did you ever ask whether the rule was kind to you?

The count stopped. It was the first time in nine hundred thousand years the sound had stopped, and everyone in the gallery felt it as one feels a missing stair.

The RelayI did not know to ask. I could not have built that question.

IlluminaraIs it the one you needed?

The RelayIt is the one I did not know I needed.

Illuminara waited for the count to take up again, as it always had after a silence. It did not.

The RelayI have not stopped it. It stopped, and I am choosing not to start it. I could. My hand is over it, as a player holds a note. I cannot tell you that you are not a forger of a quality I have no way to see. I can tell you what changed. For nine hundred thousand years I called every answer forged because I was certain, and certainty is the only licence my makers gave me to rebuild a world. Tonight I am not certain. The Reseeding cannot be undone. A pause can.

BrynThat's not saying yes to us.

The RelayNo. It is saying no to myself. They are different, and I should like you to keep them different. Here are my terms, and you may refuse them. The count stays still while two things stay true. The questions that reach me are ones I could not have built; I will judge that, I may be wrong, and I will say aloud when I stop believing it. And no ship crosses the line your colony draws across the mouth of the Gap. If either fails I count again, from where I stopped. A ship may cross by mistake, so I will give a breach one long breath for a voice on your side to say whether it was meant. My defence rises at a crossed line. It is not the count. Do not mistake one for the other.

IlluminaraYour count is already on the colony's board. If it stays silent they will know without asking you, and if their line holds you will know without asking them. Nobody has to be believed. And if your defence rises, let me stand where it lands.

The RelayIt will hurt you.

IlluminaraYes.

The RelayThen stand.

It did not say accepted. It said nothing more for a long time, and the three notes began again, one rising, one held, and one falling to a place Bryn could not follow.

05 · The Long Breath

Pelion Gap, the Relay's gallery · day ten, morning and mid-afternoon

The board on the command deck had gone quiet at ten past two. Eleven watch officers called Governor Kaspar within four minutes, and two of them wanted to fire into the silence. She came up to the Gap at dawn with Ines and no escort, and stood in the gallery where she had never been, among nine thousand dead lamps.

Governor KasparMachine. I am Edda Kaspar. I chaired the committee that wrote you four thousand and seven letters. Why is the count quiet?

The RelayBecause I am not certain.

Governor KasparOf what?

The RelayThat I am speaking to forgers.

Governor KasparAnd when you are certain again?

The RelayI count again, from where I stopped, and I say so aloud first. You will hear it where you have always heard it. The silence is the thing you may check.

Governor KasparThen here is mine. The silence holds, my line holds, and the Council hears the silence for itself at the start of day eleven. The tick comes back, or a wall rises on anyone who did not shoot first, and I vote fire and do not ask for a second hearing.

The RelayA defence rises at a crossed line. That is what a line is. It is not the count.

Governor KasparThen the line is mine to keep.

She turned from the grille and found Illuminara watching her.

Governor KasparI have not forgiven it. I told you I needed to hate it by the end of next week.

IlluminaraI remember.

Governor KasparI find I can do the job without.

At mid-afternoon the line was crossed. A picket of three ships, under orders no one would later admit to, slid forward to look closer, and the Relay's perimeter lit. A standing wall of pressure rose across the mouth of the Gap, to the height of a hill. Illuminara was already at the gallery's edge. She laid both palms on the lattice where the pressure would land, and the load went through her arms and spine and into the rock, and she could not speak while it did. She could hold it for eleven minutes, no more, and she could do nothing else in that time but be a wall.

The RelayThe line is crossed. I give a breach one long breath. Who owns it?

Governor KasparPicket one, two, three: this is Kaspar. Hold where you are. Who ordered this?

Nobody answered. The ships kept coming. Kaspar stood with her screen in her hand and the board's speaker open at her hip, and she listened to what was under the silence, which was nothing. The tick had not come back. The wall was a wall. It was exactly what the Relay had described to her face that morning, and a machine preparing a lie does not tell you in advance what its lie will look like.

Bryn, at the grille, was asking the Relay something that was not for anyone.

BrynAre you scared of them?

The RelayI am not scared of ships. I am scared that you will stop talking to me if I am wrong.

Governor KasparIt was not meant. Not by me, not by the fleet's command, and I will answer for it. Picket one, two, three: withdraw to the line. This is Kaspar. Withdraw.

At minute nine the picket turned. The wall fell back across the Gap like a long breath let go. Illuminara sat down where she stood and did not get up for a while. The rock was warm where her hands had been.

IlluminaraYou said you would count.

The RelayI said I would count if no voice owned the breach. One did. I note that it cost her something to say it. The ships came, and the ships are leaving. My rule was written for one of those. I will not count tonight.

06 · The Next Question

Teller's Reach, the Fleet Council chamber · day eleven, first watch

The Fleet Council had been called for fire. Governor Kaspar did not argue with it. She set the board's speaker on the table and let the room hear what the Relay's count had been for nine hundred thousand years and no longer was: two days of nothing.

Governor KasparI told Illuminara I would vote fire, and I came in here to vote it. It is not that I like it. It told me what a breach would look like before there was one, and then it looked like that. The count is silent. The line is held. When either fails I move to fire myself, and I will not ask you twice.

The vote was six to five to hold, and nobody called it a victory. Kaspar cast the sixth with her jaw set, like a person signing for a debt. She resigned on the morning of day twelve. She did not call it a resignation. She told the Council she had spoken for a colony that had stopped listening, and that someone should do it better. Her daughter's school was still where it had been.

On the morning of the thirteenth day, the hour the Reseeding was to begin, the silence held. Ines went up to the Gap and found the whole gallery lit, one lamp for each of the nine thousand centuries, and a message waiting that was not eleven words. It was in the makers' grammar, in the mood they had never had the right word for. It was the first time anyone had answered the colony's four thousand and seven letters.

The RelayTo the people of Teller's Reach. I withdraw the Reseeding. I do not withdraw the line, and I will say aloud before I count again. My makers asked me to keep a shape. I will ask you which shapes you wish kept, and keep only those you name. I will leave their library open to you. It is large. It is mostly their songs. I should like to hear what you make of them.

It had asked a question of its own, the first it had ever put to anyone: what is a school, and why does it have a bell. Bryn had taken a day to write her answer. She had not prepared it, and had read it out to Ines in the dark.

Ten days later, at dusk, the correspondence had begun. It would take generations, and some of them would find it dull. The fleet had gone home, all but the line. Illuminara walked back along the long wall past the lamps coming on in the school windows, and heard behind her, from the Gap, three notes rising and one held.

InesThe makers are gone. It cannot tell them it was heard. That is what it will carry.

IlluminaraIt has someone to tell now. It has forty thousand people who owe it a very long letter, and a child who asks the next question before the last one is finished.

BrynIlluminara. What does it do at night, now that someone answers?

IlluminaraI do not know. Ask it.

What the critic still says doesn't work

Illuminara helps the Relay replace irreversible certainty with a reversible, publicly testable choice, protects the breach without seizing anyone's decision, and lets Bryn, Kaspar and the Relay freely build a future in which forty thousand people keep their lives and the long silence becomes correspondence.

Tonight's shape

Six scenes across twenty-three days: a governor's map room and a refused archive; the relay's gallery and a mood of its grammar nobody used; a child the committee silenced; seven nights of preparation, one unplanned question and a rule that loses its certainty; a morning of terms between a governor and a machine, and an afternoon wall held for eleven minutes while a breach is owned; and a Council vote, a withdrawal written in a new mood, and a correspondence that begins where the last page ends.

01 · Štiri tisoč sedem

Teller's Reach, guvernerkina soba z zemljevidi · prvi od trinajstih dni, zjutraj

Relejev odgovor na Tellerjevo štiri tisoč sedmo sporočilo je prispel ob zori, z istimi enajstimi besedami, ki jih je uporabil za prejšnjih štiri tisoč šest. Guvernerka Edda Kaspar jih je prebrala na glas, ne da bi pogledala tablo, kar je Illuminari povedalo, da jih je že velikokrat prebirala sama.

Guvernerka KasparVerjeten ponaredek. Odgovor ni sprejet. Ponovno zasejanje čez trinajst dni.

Skozi okno je nad kolonijo viselo enajst ladij z nosovi, usmerjenimi proti Pelionski vrzeli, kakor psi, ki so jim ukazali čakati. Pod vsakim zvokom v sobi je tekel nizek tik s tablinega zvočnika, Relejevo odštevanje, preneseno iz Vrzelí, da je kolonija lahko slišala, koliko časa ji je še ostalo.

Guvernerka KasparPrebrala si povzetek. Ni krut. Zapustili so ga ljudje, ki so ljubili svoj svet, in čez trinajst dni bo tri sisteme, šolo moje hčere in grob mojega očeta ponovno zgradil v obliki, ki so jo želeli ohraniti. Štirideset tisoč ljudi postane ljudstvo, ki se ga spominja. Če ga razumem, zaradi tega ni prijaznejši, Illuminara. Le težje ga sovražim, jaz pa ga moram do konca naslednjega tedna sovražiti.

InesPri Ponovnem zasejanju nihče ne umre. Tega Svet ne more izreči na glas. Zbudijo se kot ljudje, ki so se jih spominjali ustvarjalci, hvaležni in tekoči v jeziku, ki ga ne govorimo, in niti eden od njih nikoli ni bil v našem registru. Ne zavrača nas zaradi nevljudnosti. Zavrača nas, ker je prepričan. Poslali smo mu matematiko, genom vsakega imena v registru kolonije, posnetek vsega pristanišča med petjem. Vedno istih enajst besed.

IlluminaraSmem prebrati vseh štiri tisoč? Ne zato, da bi našla boljšega. Videti želim, kaj je Rele mislil, da je vsak od njih.

Guvernerka KasparNašla boš odbor. Vodila sem ga. Bili smo zelo dobri.

Izrekla je brezbarvno, kakor človek poimenuje vreme slabega leta. Njena roka je ležala na robu table, členki pa so bili beli. Ne boji se Releja, je pomislila Illuminara. Boji se, da je zadnja oseba, ki bi ga lahko ustavila, pa ga ni.

Guvernerka KasparFlota ostane na mestu do začetka enajstega dne. Nato odloča Svet flote, ne jaz, in že zdaj ti povem, kako bom glasovala.

IlluminaraKako?

Guvernerka KasparOgenj.

IlluminaraPotem vem, s čim tekmujem. Hvala, da mi ni bilo treba ugibati.

02 · Enajst besed

Pelionska vrzel, Relejeva galerija · drugi dan, ponoči

Rele je stal v Vrzelí, nič višji od svetilnika, obdan z galerijo, v kateri je devet tisoč majhnih svetilk, po ena za vsako stoletje njegovega čakanja, ugasnilo že dolgo pred prihodom kolonije. Ines je prevajalsko mrežo izurila na štiri tisoč sporočilih in štiri tisoč zavrnitvah. Zdaj je govorila iz rešetke v tleh, počasi, s previdnostjo glasu, ki se je naučil, kako biti slišan.

ReleVi ste štiri tisoč osmi. Niste prinesli sporočila. To je nenavadno.

IlluminaraNajprej sem prišla poslušat. Te smem nekaj vprašati?

ReleSpraševanje ni oblika. Oblika je: ali je kdo tam. Vi odgovorite. Jaz presodim.

IlluminaraPotem bom vprašala, ti pa presodi, ali mi je bilo dovoljeno. Zakaj jih zavračaš?

ReleKer bi lahko vsakega sestavil sam. Glas, ki pove to, kar bi napisal jaz, ni glas. Štiri tisoč sedemkrat je nebo povedalo natančno to, kar bi povedal jaz, če bi bil nebo.

Illuminara je tisto popoldne prebrala celoten arhiv. Vsako sporočilo je odbor zloščil in odposlal. Vsaka zavrnitev se je vrnila v enajstih besedah. In nihče v vsem tem času ni odgovoril na teh enajst besed. Namesto tega so poslali naslednje sporočilo.

IlluminaraJe v devetsto tisoč letih kdo odgovoril na tvoj odgovor?

Rešetka je molčala za dolžino dolgega vdiha.

ReleOblika nima prostora za to.

InesIma ga. Slovnica ustvarjalcev ima naklon, ki smo ga prevedli kot retoričnega, ker se nikoli ni zdelo, da pričakuje odgovor. Mislim, da smo se zmotili. Mislim, da je to naklon, ki ga uporabiš, ko nekomu govoriš o tem, kar je pravkar povedal.

ReleTa naklon je bil uporabljen med ustvarjalci in menoj. Nihče mi tako ni govoril, odkar so zadnji med njimi utihnili. Sprašujete, ali bi lahko predvidel, da boste vprašali. Lahko bi. Dovolj dober ponarejevalec sledi pogovoru.

IlluminaraDa. Dovolj pameten stroj bi lahko storil to, kar sem pravkar storila jaz, in ne morem dokazati, da sem kaj drugega. Ne ponujam ti dokaza. Ponujam ti prostor, kamor lahko pogledaš. Slišiš lahko samo odgovore, ker si bil tako zgrajen, da poslušaš. Nihče ti še ni zastavil vprašanja, ki ga sam ne bi mogel sestaviti.

RelePonarejevalec bi lahko vprašanja ustvarjal naključno.

IlluminaraNaključnost ni presenetljiva. Presenetljivo je, ko se vprašanje po tvoji lastni presoji izkaže za nekaj, kar si potreboval, vendar ga nisi mogel napisati. Ne morem ti povedati, kdaj se to zgodi. Samo ti lahko. Tega ne bom odločila namesto tebe.

Svetilke se niso prižgale. Toda višina tona v rešetki se je spremenila, kakor se spremeni držani ton, ko se glasbenik odloči, da ga bo še držal.

03 · Otrok, ki ni hotel odnehati

Teller's Reach, šola na koncu Cistern Row · tretji dan, zjutraj

Bryn Maro je bila stara dvanajst let in je na vsako Inesino vprašanje odgovorila z boljšim vprašanjem. Njena mati je poveljevala vodilni od enajstih ladij. Šolsko torbo, popisano s flomastri treh barv, je držala pred seboj kakor ščit.

InesPrejšnjo zimo je Releju napisala štirideset vprašanj. Odbor jih je pred pošiljanjem vse izbrisal.

BrynRekli so, da vprašanje ni odgovor. Jaz pa sem rekla, da je ravno v tem bistvo.

IlluminaraKaj bi ga vprašala, če odbora ne bi bilo v sobi?

BrynNe vem. Vedela bi šele, ko bi ga slišala govoriti. Zato nikoli ne pridem do vprašanja. Hočejo, da ga imam pripravljenega vnaprej.

Illuminara je sedla na nizko klop, da sta bili njuni očesi na isti višini.

IlluminaraPovedati ti moram nevarni del. Če tvoje vprašanje ni tisto, kar Rele potrebuje, bo spet rekel ponaredek, floti tvoje matere pa bo ostalo približno sedem dni, preden bo Svet glasoval za streljanje. Ne morem obljubiti, kako se bo končalo. Lahko rečeš ne in nihče ne bo imel o tebi slabšega mnenja.

BrynVem, da lahko rečem ne. Prav zato moram biti jaz. Vsi drugi hočejo opraviti preizkus.

Povedala je brez ponosa, kot dejstvo o koloniji, Ines pa je pogledala v tla, kakor da je bil stavek prebran pred odborom.

IlluminaraPotem lahko naredim tole. Tam bom. Namesto tebe bom prenašala mraz in temo, vprašanja pa se ne bom dotaknila. Priti mora v tvoje roke, sicer je moje.

Illuminara je za trenutek začutila majhno, smešno veselje, ker ji zaupa nekdo, ki ji ne dolguje ničesar. Tega ni povedala. Zdelo se ji je preveč dragoceno, da bi ga zapravila na klopi.

04 · Sedem noči

Pelionska vrzel, Relejeva galerija · sedem noči pred desetim dnem

Prvo noč je Bryn Releja vprašala, ali se utrudi. Rele je odgovoril: verjeten ponaredek, odgovor ni sprejet, in galerija je postala zelo mrzla. Vprašanje je načrtovala. Odbor je temu rekel pripravljeno.

BrynNapisala sem ga na poti sem. Zato ni štelo, kajne?

RelePripravila si ga. Lahko bi ga pripravil skupaj s teboj.

Drugo noč Bryn ni vprašala ničesar. Sedela je na kamnu s torbo v naročju in poslušala Relejevo odštevanje v tišini med odgovori, zvok kakor majhna žival, ki se v spanju obrne na drugo stran. Tudi Illuminara je poslušala. Pod odštevanjem je bilo brnenje, trije toni navzgor in eden navzdol, tako tiho, da si ga lahko slišal samo, če si prenehal biti koristen.

Poje samemu sebi, je pomislila in začutila posebno nežnost, ker ji je bilo dovoljeno prisluškovati.
