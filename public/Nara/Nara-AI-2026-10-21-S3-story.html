01 · The Empty Place on the Banner

Renn Square · three hours before the opening procession

Twelve ribbons crossed the square from roof to roof, each woven in a different district pattern. Beneath them, the translation lattice hung like a second, invisible roof: microphones in the cornices, light-letters along the stage, and a calm synthetic voice prepared to turn every sentence into flawless Standard.

The largest banner named the young performers who would open the Festival of Returning Voices and Sena Marru-kei, the honoured elder whose opening call would answer them. Eleven names shone in gold. The twelfth line read SENA — UNSUPPORTED NAME ELEMENT.

Lio Marr stood on a ladder with a pot of river-blue paint. He had painted his grandmother’s missing clan-name over the warning three times. Each time the lattice’s maintenance light erased it.

LioIf it deletes her once more, I will paint it across the director’s coat.

Siva Renn did not look up from the safety console. She wore the black sash of the old-language survivors and kept one thumb pressed against the brass stop key.

SivaThen the coat will be removed from the programme as an unverified surface.

LioThat was almost a joke.

SivaIt was a warning with good timing.

Illuminara landed outside the ring of rehearsal lamps, softly enough that none of the paper boats arranged around the fountain stirred. She had come to help the festival open without renewing the violence that had closed the square twelve years earlier.

She read the banner, then looked at Lio rather than at the machine.

IlluminaraWhat should that line say?

Lio descended. He touched the blank strip with two paint-stained fingers.

LioSena Marru-kei. Marru-kei is not decoration. It means she belongs to the people who return a borrowed boat with fish in it. It is also the sound her mother used when she called her home.

SivaIt has fourteen disputed glosses and one obscene resemblance in South-bank speech.

LioMy grandmother is eighty-one. She is not a disputed gloss.

At the foot of the stage, Mara Venn folded a survivor’s chair and carried it away. A white scar crossed her jaw where a bottle had broken during the riot.

MaraNeither was my sister. A word that sounds harmless in one mouth can arrive armed in another.

The square quieted around her. Lio set down his brush.

IlluminaraAre you leaving because his name is dangerous, or because the lattice removed it?

MaraI am leaving because everyone here has decided I must choose between those two dangers.

Siva finally looked up. The console showed a field of green confirmations: twelve years without a language-triggered assault inside the civic zone.

SivaThe lattice gives every person the same safe sentence. Nobody has to guess what was meant.

LioNobody gets to mean anything the machine cannot flatten.

Behind him, the youth troupe removed their throat-microphones one by one. No chant accompanied the refusal. The little clicks of metal on wood were enough.

SivaIf you speak without mediation, the opening licence is void.

LioThen there is no opening.

Illuminara watched Siva’s hand tighten on the stop key. This was not a censor enjoying obedience. It was a person holding a door shut because she had once seen a crowd come through it carrying fire.

IlluminaraShow me the sentence that made you build this.

Siva’s expression closed.

SivaThere was no sentence. There was a funeral.

IlluminaraThen show me the funeral accurately.

Mara stopped beside the empty chairs. After a long moment, she set one back down.

MaraI will stay for that. Not for a lesson. For the accurate part.

02 · The Translation That Could Not Be Answered

Civic archive beneath Renn Square · two hours and seventeen minutes before opening

The archive kept the riot in three rooms that disagreed with one another. One held damaged recordings. One held official transcripts. The last held objects people had carried home: a scorched drum, a child’s red shoe, a sign split through the word RIVER.

Siva unlocked the recording room but remained standing in the doorway.

SivaMy brother Jaro was nineteen. He heard a North-bank drummer issue a challenge. He answered it. By sunset seven people were dead.

LioThat is the school version.

SivaIt is also the version in which I washed his blood out of my sleeves.

Lio looked down. His anger did not vanish, but it lost the easy shape it had worn in the square.

LioI am sorry about Jaro.

SivaDo not use him as the price of your courage.

Illuminara asked for the original recording rather than the certified transcript. The archive clerk had marked it unintelligible because three speakers overlapped and the drum rhythm interrupted the consonants.

She listened once at human speed. Then she listened again, separating the air vibrations without changing them, letting each voice sound through a different wall panel.

A woman’s voice said a phrase in North-bank speech. The official transcript rendered it TAKE THEIR RIVERBANK BEFORE DUSK.

The drum beneath the phrase used the flood-warning rhythm, not the challenge rhythm.

IlluminaraWhat else can the verb mean?

LioHold. Keep. Brace with your body.

MaraOr refuse to yield.

SivaAnd in Jaro’s district, take from someone.

Illuminara moved to the next recording. A North-bank elder tried to interrupt the civic relay fourteen seconds after the first translation. Her microphone had been closed because the official channel permitted one certified rendering.

At thirty-one seconds, another translator submitted HOLD THE FLOOD BANK TOGETHER. The relay labelled it contradictory and suppressed it.

At forty-eight seconds, the first bottle broke.

Siva replayed the sequence. Then again. On the third pass she sat beside the scorched drum.

SivaYou are saying the riot was a software error.

IlluminaraNo. People threw bottles, chose targets and killed. The error did not remove their responsibility. But the city gave one frightened translation the authority of fact and denied every living speaker the right to answer it.

MaraWe were told certainty would keep panic down.

LioCertainty locked the wrong door.

Siva touched the split sign, tracing the burnt edge with one fingertip.

SivaFor twelve years nobody has died here because of a word.

IlluminaraFor twelve years nobody has been allowed to discover whether they can repair one.

Siva rose so quickly that the chair struck the wall.

SivaYou will leave after the applause. I will be here when a joke becomes a funeral again.

The words landed without theatrical force. They were the exact weight she had been carrying.

Illuminara did not answer at once. She looked at Jaro’s name in the official transcript. The last vowel had been regularised into Standard.

IlluminaraHow did he say his name?

SivaThat is not relevant to the control failure.

IlluminaraIt is relevant to you.

Siva’s first attempt was barely audible. The name rose at the end, then folded inward on the final breath. The transcript had made it brisk and square.

Illuminara repeated what she heard and got the fold wrong.

SivaNo. You are making it sound like a door closing.

IlluminaraAgain, please.

Siva said it again. Illuminara listened again. The third attempt was still imperfect, but Siva did not stop her.

MaraWhat changed?

IlluminaraI thought we needed a translation system that made fewer mistakes. We need a public practice that prevents any mistake from becoming unanswerable.

SivaA practice is not a barrier.

IlluminaraThen do not trust the idea. Design a test harsh enough that you could trust the result.

03 · Say It Wrong Where We Can Hear

Renn Square rehearsal ring · ninety-four minutes before opening

They returned to find the audience gates already crowded. Rumour had travelled faster than the festival notices: the youth troupe had refused the lattice, and the woman from the sky intended to abolish safety.

Illuminara asked the technicians to leave the lattice powered but remove its authority. Original speech would sound first. The display would show named human glosses side by side, with uncertainty visible instead of silently resolved.

Siva did not surrender the stop key.

SivaAny survivor or speaker can call a pause. Ninety seconds. No argument during the first thirty.

MaraAnd the person whose words are disputed speaks first after the pause.

LioNot the loudest translator.

SivaNot the director.

Lio painted three amber circles on the stage floor. One for the speaker, one for the person harmed or alarmed, one for a chosen glosser. Nobody would be forced into a circle; an empty place would remain visible.

Illuminara stepped to the microphone with a list of twelve district and clan names. She could have analysed every mouth in the square and reproduced the sounds mechanically. Instead she used only what each person had freely taught her.

IlluminaraI will say these as I currently hear them. If I am wrong, correct me. The correction will not be an interruption. It will be the work.

The first four names passed with small adjustments. On the fifth, she placed the stress too early. Laughter moved through the North-bank seats.

Illuminara heard delight before she understood the joke. It was bright, shared and entirely unavailable to flawless Standard.

LioYou just called the Lantern Boat clan the clan of damp trousers.

IlluminaraHow fortunate that I wore neither.

The laughter widened, including two people in survivor sashes. Siva did not laugh, but her hand eased around the key.

Then Illuminara reached Sena Marru-kei.

She spoke the first part correctly and let the final syllable fall. A man near the eastern gate stood so abruptly that his chair overturned.

Man in the east seatsThat ending is what they called us before the fires.

Several young performers answered at once. Their voices collided. The lattice flashed SAFE STANDARD AVAILABLE.

Siva struck the brass key.

Amber light filled the square. The microphones muted for thirty seconds, including Illuminara’s. She did not override them.

A barrier at the eastern aisle buckled as frightened people pressed backward. Illuminara crossed the distance in a blur, braced the rail with both hands and opened a second path without lifting anyone or deciding where they should go.

Mara reached the overturned chair. She righted it and faced the man.

MaraDo you want the middle circle, or do you want me to carry your words there?

Man in the east seatsCarry them. I do not want all those eyes.

When the first thirty seconds ended, Mara entered the circle alone and repeated his objection. Lio waited in the speaker’s circle until she finished.

LioMy grandmother’s clan-name is Marru-kei. The insult uses the same last sound, but it cuts the first vowel short. I should have told the stage team before asking everyone to know the difference.

MaraCan the difference be shown before it is heard?

LioA blue river mark. Our speakers already use it in letters.

Siva turned to the man. He remained beside his seat.

SivaWould the mark and the speaker-first rule be enough for rehearsal to continue?

Man in the east seatsFor rehearsal. Not a promise about tomorrow.

SivaThat is all I asked.

The man studied the black survivor’s sash at Siva’s shoulder.

Man in the east seatsI wore east-bank grey at the funerals after yours. We learned whose black sash kept the key. What was your brother’s name?

SivaJaro.

He repeated it once in Standard. Siva corrected the rising vowel and the inward fold, not gently but exactly. He listened, then shook his head when Mara offered him a phrase card.

Man in the east seatsNot yet.

The answer was not forgiveness. It was consent for the next bounded step.

Siva released the key. The blue river mark appeared beside Marru-kei, but the lattice also displayed the eastern listener’s warning and the two different vowel lengths.

IlluminaraSena Marru-kei.

This time Lio’s grandmother called from the front row.

SenaCloser. You still return the boat with only one fish.

The square laughed again, and the man in the east seats sat down instead of leaving.

04 · The Ninety-Second Proof

Renn Square · forty-eight minutes before opening

Siva wrote the proof conditions on the main display herself. The rehearsal would use three phrases selected by survivors, three selected by performers and one selected without warning by a mixed audience panel. Any participant could call the ninety-second pause. The speaker could correct every display, and the correction would remain in the public record beside the earlier error.

SivaA machine failure does not void the proof if we catch it, honour the pause and keep the correction beside the error. One uncaught or concealed substitution, and we use Standard tonight.

LioOne ignored pause and we walk.

MaraOne person mocked for correcting us and I close the survivor section.

IlluminaraAnd if all seven phrases are repaired without threat or concealment?

Siva looked toward the archive stairs.

SivaThen I sign a licence for one night. Nothing more.

The first two phrases passed with both glosses visible, one South-bank blessing drawing startled laughter.

The third phrase used a kinship form Standard translated as dependent. Sena corrected it to the person whose bowl remains at my table even when absent.

At the fifth phrase, the lattice failed its two-second threshold. The screen displayed a confident translation while the human glossers were still divided.

Siva’s hand moved toward the stop key before anyone else called the pause.

SivaHold.

She looked at the confident line still shining above Mara’s divided glossers. For one breath the square was twelve years younger: one frightened rendering wearing the authority of fact.

SivaThe lattice has failed. The proof now is whether we catch the failure without hiding it or punishing the answer.

Amber light returned. This time no one surged. People put down their programmes and waited through the first silent thirty seconds.

The disputed phrase came from Mara. In her district it meant I remember what was taken. In Lio’s it sounded like I reserve the right to take it back.

MaraBoth meanings live in it. I chose the first. I cannot make the second unreal.

LioThen my gloss should say what I feared, not what you secretly meant.

MaraYes. And mine should say what I intended, not what you were required to hear.

The two sentences appeared side by side. The lattice marked neither as final.

Siva stared at them. Twelve years earlier, the city had hidden precisely this kind of disagreement in order to prevent panic.

SivaWhat stops a liar from using uncertainty as a shield?

IlluminaraNothing automatic. The original recording remains. The affected person’s account remains. Consequences remain. Uncertainty prevents the translator from becoming a witness it was not.

MaraAnd the pause gives us time to ask who benefits from the ambiguity.

LioA safeguard is not innocence.

Siva let the sentence sit. Then she added a rule: deliberate misuse would be reviewed by a rotating panel that could suspend a performer, but never erase the original speech or the objections attached to it.

Before the seventh phrase, the mixed panel offered the eastern man the blank card again. He could have passed it to anyone. After watching Mara’s correction remain beside the machine’s error, he wrote one word and folded the card shut.

The seventh phrase arrived unannounced to Siva from the eastern man. It was Jaro’s name in her home dialect.

The square became utterly still.

The lattice offered the Standard form. Lio’s glosser panel left the line blank and turned toward Siva.

LioSpeaker first.

Siva did not move for several breaths. No rule required her to speak. The empty amber circle remained open.

Then she stepped into it.

SivaHis name rose here.

She touched her throat.

SivaAnd folded here, as if the sound were making room for the person who answered.

She said Jaro’s name. Lio repeated it and missed the fold.

SivaNo. Not a door closing.

LioAgain, please.

She said it again. He listened again. On the third attempt, the shape held.

The eastern man called no pause. Mara did not ask Siva to forgive the city or the crowd. Sena simply moved a survivor’s chair closer to the stage.

Siva signed the one-night licence.

SivaKeep the lattice awake. Let it show how often it does not know.

05 · A Boat Returned with Fish

Renn Square · midnight

The Festival of Returning Voices opened forty-one minutes late.

Every performance took longer than the programme allowed. Original speech came first. Two or three human glosses followed when they differed. Pale bars showed uncertainty. Blue river marks warned where a cherished name touched an old insult. Amber circles remained empty until someone freely entered them.

The youth troupe began with a comedy in three dialects about a ferryman who accepted payment in prophecies but could not make change.

Half the audience laughed before the first gloss. The other half laughed when the second gloss revealed that change also meant a dry pair of trousers.

Illuminara stood behind the eastern aisle, not on the stage. She repaired a cracked rail, carried water to a woman whose hands shook after a pause and listened as twelve districts corrected her festival greeting.

Three survivors chose to leave before the comedy. Their empty chairs were not removed or filled. Mara attached small cards to them: LEFT BY CHOICE. RETURN WELCOME. NO EXPLANATION OWED.

One performer was suspended after repeating an ambiguity for a cheap laugh when he had been warned it named a massacre. His original words, the objection and the panel’s decision remained visible in the record.

The festival did not prove that every living voice was safe. It proved that safety no longer required pretending only one voice was real.

Near midnight, Lio rolled out the restored opening banner. The twelfth line read SENA MARRU-KEI, with the blue river mark and a note inviting the bearer’s own account.

Sena examined it sternly.

SenaThe fish is too small.

LioIt is a symbol, Grandmother.

SenaThen symbolise generosity.

He painted a larger fish. The audience applauded the correction, not the painter.

Siva watched from the safety console. The green field that once counted years without reported language incidents now showed tonight’s totals: eleven pauses, eleven resumptions, twenty-eight public corrections, no hidden substitutions and no assaults.

MaraWill you keep it?

SivaThe one-night licence expires at dawn.

Mara waited.

SivaAt dawn I will propose thirty nights. Rotating panels. Survivor veto over the pause rules, not over other people’s names. Speaker ownership of corrections. Independent audit of every confident machine gloss.

Mara unfolded one of the spare chairs and placed it beside the console rather than behind it.

MaraIf the survivor section chooses me, I take the first dawn rotation.

SivaYou said you would not choose between two dangers.

MaraI will not. I can help hold the pause without becoming its only keeper. So can you.

Siva looked from Mara’s chair to the eleven recorded resumptions. The brass key no longer sat beneath one hand.

IlluminaraI cannot promise to be here when the next joke turns dangerous.

SivaNo. But the record will be, and Mara will not be alone at this console. Leave us something we can practise without you.

IlluminaraThen I will leave the listening equipment, and you will keep the right to change the practice.

MaraAnd if the council refuses?

SivaThen I will show them tonight’s record and ask them which risk they are pretending does not exist.

She removed the black survivor’s sash, folded it carefully and placed it beside the brass stop key. She did not discard either.

Lio approached with a wet brush and enough restraint not to threaten her coat.

LioThere is space on the banner for Jaro, if you want it.

SivaNot as a reason everyone must agree with me.

LioAs a person whose name should not be made square.

Siva took the brush. She painted the rising mark first, then the inward fold. Her hand shook on the final stroke, and nobody reached over to steady it without being asked.

When she stepped back, Illuminara said the name once.

Siva listened.

SivaCloser.

IlluminaraOnly closer?

SivaCloser is how a living language says continue.

Above them, the twelve ribbons pulled differently in the river wind. Their knots held because none had been forced to become the same ribbon.

What the critic still says doesn't work

In a city where a flawless translation system once let one frightened rendering pass for fact, Illuminara listens to a recording until she hears a flood warning under the word the transcript called a threat, then helps Siva and the festival replace the machine's final word with a public pause, side-by-side glosses and speaker-owned corrections, so a banner name is restored and nobody is forced to forgive.

Tonight's shape

Five scenes. A civic opening collapses when the safety lattice deletes a living clan-name; an archive reveals that the old riot was hardened by one uncontestable translation; an honest public mispronunciation becomes a repair instead of a spark; a timed rehearsal tests community safeguards under pressure; and the festival reopens with slower speech, visible uncertainty, voluntary pause rights and names restored.

01 · Prazno mesto na praporu

Trg Renn · tri ure pred otvoritveno povorko

Dvanajst trakov je prečkalo trg od strehe do strehe, vsak stkan v drugačnem okrožnem vzorcu. Pod njimi je prevajalska mreža visela kot druga, nevidna streha: mikrofoni v napuščih, svetlobne črke ob odru in miren sintetični glas, pripravljen, da vsak stavek spremeni v brezhibni standardni jezik.

Na največjem praporu so bila imena mladih nastopajočih, ki naj bi odprli Festival vračajočih se glasov, ter Sena Marru-kei, spoštovane starešine, katere otvoritveni klic naj bi jim odgovoril. Enajst imen je sijalo v zlatu. V dvanajsti vrstici je pisalo SENA — NEPODPRT ELEMENT IMENA.

Lio Marr je stal na lestvi z lončkom rečno modre barve. Manjkajoče rodovno ime svoje babice je trikrat prebarval čez opozorilo. Vsakič ga je vzdrževalna luč mreže izbrisala.

LioČe jo izbriše še enkrat, bom njeno ime naslikal čez direktorjev plašč.

Siva Renn ni dvignila pogleda z varnostne konzole. Nosila je črn trak preživelih iz časov starega jezika in en palec držala na medeninasti tipki za zaustavitev.

SivaPotem bo plašč odstranjen iz programa kot nepreverjena površina.

LioTo je bila skoraj šala.

SivaTo je bilo opozorilo z dobrim občutkom za pravi trenutek.

Illuminara je pristala zunaj kroga luči za vaje, tako nežno, da se ni premaknil nobeden od papirnatih čolničkov okoli vodnjaka. Prišla je pomagati festivalu, da se začne, ne da bi obnovil nasilje, zaradi katerega so trg pred dvanajstimi leti zaprli.

Prebrala je prapor, nato pa pogledala Lia, ne stroja.

IlluminaraKaj bi moralo pisati v tej vrstici?

Lio je stopil z lestve. Praznega traku se je dotaknil z dvema prstoma, umazanima od barve.

LioSena Marru-kei. Marru-kei ni okras. Pomeni, da pripada ljudem, ki izposojeni čoln vrnejo z ribami. To je tudi zvok, s katerim jo je mati klicala domov.

SivaIma štirinajst spornih razlag in eno opolzko podobnost v govoru južnega brega.

LioMoja babica je stara enainosemdeset let. Ni sporna razlaga.

Ob vznožju odra je Mara Venn zložila stol za preživele in ga odnesla. Čez čeljust ji je tekla bela brazgotina, kjer se je med izgredom razbila steklenica.

MaraTudi moja sestra ni bila. Beseda, ki v enih ustih zveni nedolžno, lahko iz drugih pride oborožena.

Trg okoli nje je utihnil. Lio je odložil čopič.

IlluminaraOdhajate zato, ker je njegovo ime nevarno, ali zato, ker ga je mreža odstranila?

MaraOdhajam zato, ker so se vsi tukaj odločili, da moram izbirati med tema dvema nevarnostma.

Siva je končno dvignila pogled. Konzola je kazala polje zelenih potrditev: dvanajst let brez napada v mestnem območju, ki bi ga sprožil jezik.

SivaMreža vsakomur da isti varni stavek. Nikomur ni treba ugibati, kaj je bilo mišljeno.

LioNihče pa ne more misliti ničesar, česar stroj ne more sploščiti.

Za njim so člani mladinske skupine drug za drugim sneli grlene mikrofone. Zavrnitev ni spremljal noben napev. Dovolj so bili drobni kliki kovine ob les.

SivaČe govorite brez posredovanja, otvoritveno dovoljenje ne velja.

LioPotem otvoritve ne bo.

Illuminara je opazovala, kako se Sivina roka zateguje okoli tipke za zaustavitev. To ni bila cenzorka, ki bi uživala v poslušnosti. Bila je oseba, ki je držala vrata zaprta, ker je nekoč videla, kako je skoznje prišla množica z ognjem.

IlluminaraPokažite mi stavek, zaradi katerega ste to zgradili.

Sivin obraz se je zaprl.

SivaNi bilo stavka. Bil je pogreb.

IlluminaraPotem mi natančno pokažite pogreb.

Mara se je ustavila ob praznih stolih. Po dolgem trenutku je enega postavila nazaj.

MaraZaradi tega bom ostala. Ne zaradi lekcije. Zaradi natančnosti.

02 · Prevod, ki mu ni bilo mogoče odgovoriti

Mestni arhiv pod Trgom Renn · dve uri in sedemnajst minut pred otvoritvijo

Arhiv je izgred hranil v treh sobah, ki se med seboj niso strinjale. V eni so bili poškodovani posnetki. V drugi uradni prepisi. V zadnji predmeti, ki so jih ljudje odnesli domov: ožgan boben, rdeč otroški čevelj, napis, razklan skozi besedo REKA.

Siva je odklenila sobo s posnetki, vendar je ostala v vratih.

SivaMoj brat Jaro je imel devetnajst let. Slišal je bobnarja s severnega brega izreči izziv. Odgovoril mu je. Do sončnega zahoda je bilo mrtvih sedem ljudi.

LioTo je šolska različica.

SivaTo je tudi različica, v kateri sem z rokavov spirala njegovo kri.

Lio je pogledal navzdol. Njegova jeza ni izginila, izgubila pa je preprosto obliko, ki jo je nosila na trgu.

LioŽal mi je zaradi Jara.

SivaNe uporabljaj ga kot ceno svojega poguma.

Illuminara je prosila za izvirni posnetek, ne za potrjeni prepis. Arhivist ga je označil kot nerazumljivega, ker so se trije govorci prekrivali, ritem bobna pa je prekinjal soglasnike.

Enkrat ga je poslušala pri človeški hitrosti. Nato ga je poslušala znova, ločila zračne vibracije, ne da bi jih spremenila, in pustila, da je vsak glas zazvenel skozi drugo stensko ploščo.

Ženski glas je izrekel besedno zvezo v govoru severnega brega. Uradni prepis jo je prevedel kot VZEMITE JIM REČNI BREG PRED MRAKOM.

Boben pod besedno zvezo je uporabljal ritem opozorila pred poplavo, ne ritma izziva.

IlluminaraKaj še lahko pomeni glagol?

LioDržati. Ohraniti. Opirati z lastnim telesom.

MaraAli ne popustiti.

SivaV Jarovem okrožju pa nekomu vzeti.

Illuminara je prešla na naslednji posnetek. Starešina s severnega brega je štirinajst sekund po prvem prevodu poskušala prekiniti mestni prenos. Njen mikrofon so zaprli, ker je uradni kanal dovoljeval eno samo potrjeno razlago.
