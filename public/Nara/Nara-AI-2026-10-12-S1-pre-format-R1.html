THE DOOR IN THE BAKERYVRATA V PEKARNI

A cold-room door opens on a second kitchen, and the town's clocks, radios and geese have begun to slip. Illuminara has until noon, a ministry with a block of concrete, and two bakers who will not be made to give the door up.

01 · Loaf for Loaf

At twenty to six on the seventh morning, Orsa Keller unlatched the cold-room door of her bakery in Kesselbach and found a second kitchen on the other side, as she had every morning that week. The flour in there was the same flour. The oven burned a quiet blue, and the dough on its board had finished rising while she was still saying good morning.

"Loaf for loaf," said Tem, who baked there, and passed one through the frame. It was enormous and weighed almost nothing: crust like a paper lantern, crumb full of holes. Orsa gave him her rye in return, close-grained, the size of a brick, and he grunted as he caught it. That was the trade they had made on the first day. They had kept it fourteen times, and it had felt like the fairest thing in either world.

Outside, the town was coming apart at the edges. The radios had been slipping off their stations since Tuesday, so that the morning news turned up between the dial marks and then nowhere. The church clock gained until Old Josef, who wound it, stopped apologizing for it. And a hundred and forty grey geese, bound south, had circled the square for three days and would not come down.

The Ministry of Standards had pinned a notice to the bakery's front door: the premises would be sealed with poured concrete at noon. Tem had one too, from the Hearth Guild, with a different seal and a different noon. He had stuck it on his wall beside a drawing of a goose.

"They both want the door," Tem said.

"They want the bread," said Orsa. "The door is just where it comes out."

She had written to the one person she thought might know how to look at a thing without owning it. Illuminara arrived at six with Wren Odira, who carried a stopwatch and the expression of someone who had volunteered to keep the clock because nobody else would.

02 · A Number It Can Embarrass

"Don't tell me what's wrong yet," Illuminara said, before anyone could. "Shut it and latch it, and let me measure. I will say what I think once there is a number it can embarrass."

Inspector Dalia Pryce of the Ministry had brought a frequency counter in a leather case and the look of a woman who was used to being blamed for the weather. For an hour the door stayed shut. Wren read the church clock against her stopwatch every ten minutes: four minutes gained, to the second, steady as a pulse. Pryce's counter put the town's station nine point six kilohertz low and sliding another one point four every ten minutes. "A closed door, and it still slides," said Pryce. "Residue. Seal it and it decays." She wrote that down as her theory, and she wrote the numbers first.

Illuminara laid her palm on the frame. Force paths were legible to her the way a tide is to a sailor, and this one showed a slow, patient lean toward the other kitchen, like a held breath. What the lean meant was not handed to her, and she said so. "It leans. I do not know whether the lean is the sickness or only its shadow. Tell me everything that crossed, each way."

"Fourteen loaves each way," said Orsa. She had it chalked on a flour sack. "Loaf for loaf."

"Weigh them."

Orsa owned a balance with brass weights, older than the bakery. She set a rye on one pan and brought the weights down to nine hundred and twelve grams. Tem passed one of his through the frame. She set it on the pan: four hundred and eight.

Nobody spoke. Tem looked at his loaf as if it had lied to him. "But a loaf is a loaf."

"Not to a scale," said Illuminara. "Five hundred and four grams a trade, fourteen trades. Seven thousand and fifty-six grams have left her side and gone into yours."

She did not announce the cause. She took the chalk and wrote two sentences on the sack. The first: If the town is reading a standing balance, then a net trade of about three kilos back toward Orsa should cut the slide from 1.4 to 0.8 kilohertz per ten minutes, and the clock's gain from four minutes an hour to about two and a quarter, after a delay I cannot yet name. The second: If it does not, I am wrong.

"I have a rival," said Pryce. "An open door leaks, however you balance what goes through it. Throughput."

"And I've got another," said Tem. "My loaves come out of a blue oven hot, and yours go in cold. It is the heat."

"Good," said Illuminara. "Then I will need a second trade that can embarrass both of you."

03 · Seven Minutes

The door, Orsa had learned on the third night, opened only for two hands. Alone, with her palm on the latch, she found a cold room with potatoes in it; with Tem's palm on his side, a doorway. Nobody had designed it that way. It meant that nothing had ever crossed that both of them had not chosen to send.

"If I send you four kilos and take one back," Tem said, "my side gets lighter. My dough rises in forty seconds now. The Guild's kettles boil when they please. Light is how I became fast, and I don't know what I am without it."

"Slower, probably," said Illuminara. "I don't know. It is yours to choose."

Tem looked at his blue oven. "My grandmother said a dough you needn't wait for is a dough nobody's learned anything from." He put his hand on the latch. "Go on, then."

At twelve minutes past seven the last loaf crossed: ten of Tem's hollow loaves, four thousand and eighty grams, against Orsa's rye and a roll, a thousand even. Net three thousand and eighty toward Orsa's side. Wren started the watch.

Four minutes: nothing. Pryce stood over her counter with her arms folded, the way people stand over a pot. Five. Six. At seven minutes the counter stuttered like a man changing his mind and settled on zero point eight. Across the square Josef's bell struck the quarter, and Wren, checking, said, "The clock has slowed. Not stopped. Two and a quarter an hour."

"Don't look at me like that," said Pryce. "I'll sign the sheet. But it is one number, and one number is a coincidence with good manners."

"Then take the next," said Illuminara. "Seven minutes is how long the town takes to feel a change in the balance. I could not have told you that this morning. Now it is something to predict with."

Tem had weighed his ten loaves on his own side's spring scale before sending them, and chalked 3.7 kilos. Orsa's brass weights had said 4.08. He stared at the two numbers for a while. "Springs lie across a door," he said slowly. "My grandmother never trusted one. We weighed the First Oven's grain on a beam." He was already reaching for his coat. "The Guild keeps the Charter beam in the hall. I'll be back before the next trade."

04 · The Loud Loaf

He was gone forty minutes. When he came back he had the Charter beam under one arm, two brass pans on a bar of black oak, and behind him in the frame, in a green coat, stood Master Ilse Varro of the Hearth Guild with a writ in her hand.

Tem hung the beam across the doorway so that one pan lay on each side and the pivot sat in the frame itself. "Whatever pulls on a pan, here or there, the pivot is where both kitchens agree. It compares. It does not read."

"It compares pulls," said Illuminara, "and your spring has just told us the two kitchens may not agree about what a kilo pulls. Before it weighs a town's bread, show me it is fair." She asked for the plainest test she knew. Orsa set her brass kilo on her pan. Varro, who had not yet spoken, unwrapped the Guild's sealed standard kilo from its felt and set it on Tem's. The beam hung level in the frame. Then the two of them carried the pans across the threshold, brass to Tem and standard to Orsa, and hung them again. Level.

"Had a kilo pulled harder on one side," Illuminara said, "the swap would have tipped it. It did not. The beam is fair, the town's brass is the Guild's kilo, and it is the spring that lies." Tem said a spring remembered the metal of its own world and he had never once thought to ask the other world's opinion. Varro folded the felt over her standard without a word. It was the first thing she had done that morning before objecting to it.

The second trade was five thousand grams each way, level to the gram: Orsa's five ryes and a cob of four hundred and forty, cold from the cellar at six degrees, against twelve hollow loaves and a heel of a hundred and four, straight from the blue oven, ninety in the crumb. "Ten kilos through the door," said Pryce, "twice the first. Mine says it screams."

"Mine says nothing at all," said Illuminara. "Tem's says the hot ones push the slide the wrong way. Write down who said what."

At twenty-seven minutes past eight the last crust crossed. Wren set the watch. At seven minutes, nothing. At fourteen, nothing. The counter sat at zero point eight as if it had been painted there, and the church clock kept its two and a quarter.

Pryce took out her pencil and struck one line from her notebook, throughput, and initialed the crossing-out. It was the most expensive thing anyone did all day. "Ten kilos," she said, "and the door doesn't care. Fine. Then seal it anyway. Whatever it is, you have proved it is real."

"Seal it now," said Illuminara, "and you freeze nearly four kilos of deficit into this town for good. The clock gains two and a quarter minutes an hour for as long as there is concrete, and the dial goes on sliding with it. Your own counter will tell you so every morning, and you will not be able to open the door to pay it back."

Varro cleared her throat in the frame. "The Guild has a prior claim. The Charter of the First Oven..."

"Says the oven belongs to whoever feeds the town from it," said Tem. "I read it to you myself when I was your apprentice. Master, it opens for two hands. Take my latch and you hold a cold cupboard in a world with no Orsa in it." He did not raise his voice.

"Same to you, Inspector," said Orsa. "My flour, my door. You are welcome to a gauge. You cannot have the latch."

05 · Level to Fifty Grams

The repayment took the rest of the morning, and it was nobody's miracle. Three trades, with both bakers calling every weight aloud and Wren chalking the running balance on the sack. At half past nine Tem sent five hollow loaves against one rye, two thousand and forty against nine hundred and twelve, and the deficit fell from 3,976 grams to 2,848. At a quarter past ten, the same trade again: 1,720. At three minutes past eleven, five loaves against a rye-and-seed cob of three hundred and sixty, and the sack read 40. A cob of four hundred would have cleared it, but Orsa had none left, and her oven would not give her one before morning. Each time the counter answered seven minutes later, as it had before, to zero point six, to zero point three, to a flicker. Illuminara kept out of the weighing, except to read the lean in the frame and say, each time, whether it had eased.

Orsa made the first rule, with her hand on the latch. "Level to fifty grams to send. Whatever the sack says under fifty is owed, and the owing goes first through the door in the next crossing, before anything else. And we both say the number aloud. If you can't say it, you don't send it." Tem made the second: the ledger stays in the doorway, chalk on oak, where either of them can read it. Illuminara added the only piece she was better placed to see: the town was already reading the balance for free. The clock and the station were the gauges. The Ministry could read the counter, the Guild its kettles. Neither would hold a hand.

Pryce read it twice and said it was irregular, which was true, and that it was also the only arrangement under which she could see the thing she was meant to protect, which was also true. Then she asked for one thing. The counter would stand in the square on a post, where any child could read it, and if it ever showed a slide above a quarter of a kilohertz per ten minutes, both bakers would send nothing but repayment until it fell. "A beam can be dropped," she said. "A town cannot be asked to trust a beam." Orsa weighed it and agreed, and wrote beside it that the bakers could cancel it together and the Ministry could not cancel it alone.

Varro had stood a long minute in Tem's kitchen. Then she said that a beam which had been right at eight o'clock would not stay right for ever, that nobody had checked this one in nine years, and that she would not see a town's bread weighed on it unproven. She would bring the standards herself each month, with Orsa at the other pan, and the pans would be swapped as they had been today; either baker could take the beam back from the Guild's keeping the same day. She signed with a pen she asked to borrow. Tem noticed that she had asked for a duty and not a key.

The sack read 40 and stayed there. At ten past eleven the counter was no longer sliding, and Illuminara said what the morning had shown. "There are two clocks here, and we have been timing only one. The rate follows the balance, seven minutes behind it. That is the one we have tested, and it has answered every time. Where the dial sits is a different quantity. It has its own pace home, and Wren can time it now that the rate has stopped." Wren already was. The station stood twenty-seven and a half kilohertz low and came back at four and a half every ten minutes, a flat steady walk. The geese had begun to circle, her Tuesday notes said, when the dial passed eight low. By her arithmetic they would land at seven minutes to noon.

They landed at eight minutes to. One came down in the square, then ten, then all hundred and forty, in a long untidy landing, and went straight for the crusts the bakers' children had brought them.

There were costs. Orsa's rye now travelled in pairs with Tem's airy loaves, and she would bake six hours longer each week. Tem's dough took four minutes to rise, and he complained about it bitterly and happily. The church clock stood fifty-one minutes fast, and Josef, who had wound it for forty years, put it right with his own hands, which took him an hour and a ladder, and said it was the best hour he had spent that month. The forty grams were still owed, and until they were paid the clock would gain half a minute a day. The concrete order was withdrawn rather than torn up, and sat in both offices as an unsigned threat.

At ten past twelve the radios found their marks. Orsa and Tem stood at the beam in the doorway with a pan each. "Level?" said Tem. "Level to forty," said Orsa, "and forty owed." They put their hands on the latches. The door opened for two hands.

At twenty to six the next morning, before the bread, a single roll of exactly forty grams crossed from Tem's side with nothing sent back, and Tem chalked the sack clean. Seven minutes later Wren looked up from her stopwatch and said that the church clock, for the first time in a week, had kept true.

Vrata hladilnice se odprejo v drugo kuhinjo, mestne ure, radijski sprejemniki in gosi pa so začeli uhajati iz reda. Illuminara ima čas do poldneva, ministrstvo ima blok betona, dva peka pa se vrat ne nameravata odreči.

01 · Hlebec za hlebec

Dvajset minut do šestih sedmega jutra je Orsa Keller odpahnila vrata hladilnice v svoji pekarni v Kesselbachu in na drugi strani zagledala drugo kuhinjo, kakor vsako jutro tistega tedna. Moka tam je bila enaka moki pri njej. Peč je tiho gorela z modrim plamenom, testo na deski pa je že vzhajalo, medtem ko je še izrekala dobro jutro.

"Hlebec za hlebec," je rekel Tem, ki je pekel tam, in ji enega podal skozi okvir. Bil je velikanski in skoraj brez teže: skorja kakor papirnata svetilka, sredica vsa luknjičasta. Orsa mu je v zameno dala svoj rženi hlebec, zbite sredice in velik kot opeka, on pa je zagodrnjal, ko ga je ujel. Tako sta trgovala prvi dan. Izmenjavo sta ponovila štirinajstkrat in zdela se jima je najpravičnejša stvar v obeh svetovih.

Zunaj je mesto po robovih razpadalo. Radijski sprejemniki so od torka drseli s svojih postaj, tako da so se jutranje novice pojavile med oznakami na številčnici, nato pa nikjer. Cerkvena ura je prehitevala, dokler se stari Josef, ki jo je navijal, za to ni več opravičeval. Sto štirideset sivih gosi na poti proti jugu pa je tri dni krožilo nad trgom in se ni hotelo spustiti.

Ministrstvo za standarde je na vhodna vrata pekarne pripelo obvestilo: prostore bodo opoldne zapečatili z ulitim betonom. Tudi Tem je imel svoje obvestilo, od Ceha ognjišč, z drugačnim pečatom in drugačnim poldnevom. Na steno ga je prilepil ob risbo gosi.

"Oboji hočejo vrata," je rekel Tem.

"Hočejo kruh," je rekla Orsa. "Vrata so samo kraj, kjer pride ven."

Pisala je edini osebi, za katero je mislila, da zna nekaj pogledati, ne da bi si to hotela lastiti. Illuminara je prišla ob šestih z Wren Odiro, ki je nosila štoparico in izraz nekoga, ki se je prostovoljno javil za merjenje časa, ker tega ni hotel nihče drug.

02 · Številka, ki jo lahko spravi v zadrego

"Ne povejte mi še, kaj je narobe," je rekla Illuminara, še preden je kdo utegnil spregovoriti. "Zaprite in zapahnite vrata ter mi dovolite meriti. Kaj si mislim, bom povedala, ko bomo imeli številko, ki lahko to misel spravi v zadrego."

Inšpektorica Dalia Pryce z ministrstva je prinesla merilnik frekvence v usnjenem kovčku in izraz ženske, ki je bila vajena, da jo krivijo za vreme. Vrata so eno uro ostala zaprta. Wren je vsakih deset minut primerjala cerkveno uro s svojo štoparico: štiri minute pridobljene ure, natančno do sekunde, enakomerno kot utrip. Po Pryceinem merilniku je bila mestna postaja devet celih šest kilohertza prenizko in je vsakih deset minut zdrsnila še za eno celo štiri. "Vrata so zaprta, pa še vedno drsi," je rekla Pryce. "Ostanek. Zapečatite jih in pojemal bo." To je zapisala kot svojo teorijo, številke pa je zapisala prve.

Illuminara je položila dlan na okvir. Poti sil so bile zanjo berljive kakor plima za mornarja in ta je kazala počasen, vztrajen nagib proti drugi kuhinji, kakor zadržan dih. Kaj nagib pomeni, ji ni bilo podarjeno kot odgovor, in to je tudi povedala. "Nagiba se. Ne vem, ali je nagib bolezen ali samo njena senca. Povejte mi vse, kar je šlo skozi, v obe smeri."

"Štirinajst hlebcev v vsako smer," je rekla Orsa. Zabeleženo je imela s kredo na vreči moke. "Hlebec za hlebec."

"Stehtajta jih."

Orsa je imela tehtnico z medeninastimi utežmi, starejšo od pekarne. Na eno skledo je položila rženi hlebec in nanj odmerila devetsto dvanajst gramov. Tem ji je skozi okvir podal enega svojih. Položila ga je na skledo: štiristo osem.

Nihče ni spregovoril. Tem je pogledal svoj hlebec, kakor da se mu je zlagal. "Ampak hlebec je hlebec."

"Za tehtnico ne," je rekla Illuminara. "Petsto štiri grame na izmenjavo, štirinajst izmenjav. Sedem tisoč šestinpetdeset gramov je zapustilo njeno stran in odšlo na tvojo."

Vzroka ni razglasila. Vzela je kredo in na vrečo zapisala dva stavka. Prvi: Če mesto odčitava trajno ravnovesje, bi morala neto izmenjava približno treh kilogramov nazaj proti Orsi zmanjšati zdrs z 1,4 na 0,8 kilohertza na deset minut, pridobivanje ure pa s štirih minut na uro na približno dve minuti in četrt, po zamiku, ki ga še ne znam določiti. Drugi: Če se to ne zgodi, se motim.

"Jaz imam tekmujočo razlago," je rekla Pryce. "Odprta vrata puščajo, ne glede na to, kako uravnotežite, kar gre skoznje. Pretok."

"In jaz imam še eno," je rekel Tem. "Moji hlebci pridejo vroči iz modre peči, vaši pa gredo noter hladni. Kriva je toplota."

"Dobro," je rekla Illuminara. "Potem bom potrebovala drugo izmenjavo, ki lahko v zadrego spravi oba."

03 · Sedem minut

Orsa je tretjo noč ugotovila, da se vrata odprejo le za dve roki. Ko je sama držala dlan na zapahu, je našla hladilnico s krompirjem; ko je imel Tem dlan na svoji strani, se je odprl prehod. Nihče jih ni zasnoval tako. Pomenilo je, da skoznje nikoli ni šlo nič, česar se ne bi oba odločila poslati.

"Če ti pošljem štiri kilograme in dobim enega nazaj," je rekel Tem, "bo moja stran postala lažja. Moje testo zdaj vzhaja v štiridesetih sekundah. Cehovski kotli zavrejo, kadar se jim zljubi. Zaradi lahkosti sem postal hiter in ne vem, kaj sem brez nje."

"Verjetno počasnejši," je rekla Illuminara. "Ne vem. Odločitev je tvoja."

Tem je pogledal svojo modro peč. "Moja babica je pravila, da je testo, na katerega ni treba čakati, testo, iz katerega se ni nihče ničesar naučil." Položil je roko na zapah. "Pa dajmo."

Ob dvanajst minut čez sedmo je skozi šel zadnji hlebec: deset Temovih votlih hlebcev, štiri tisoč osemdeset gramov, v zameno za Orsin rženi hlebec in žemljico, skupaj natanko tisoč. Neto tri tisoč osemdeset gramov proti Orsini strani. Wren je sprožila štoparico.

Štiri minute: nič. Pryce je stala nad merilnikom s prekrižanimi rokami, kakor ljudje stojijo nad loncem. Pet. Šest. Pri sedmih minutah je merilnik zajecljal kakor človek, ki si premisli, in se ustalil na nič celi osem. Na drugi strani trga je Josefov zvon odbil četrt, Wren pa je preverila in rekla: "Ura se je upočasnila. Ni se ustavila. Dve minuti in četrt na uro."

"Ne glejte me tako," je rekla Pryce. "Podpisala bom zapisnik. Toda to je ena številka, ena številka pa je naključje z lepimi manirami."

"Potem vzemite naslednjo," je rekla Illuminara. "Sedem minut mesto potrebuje, da začuti spremembo ravnovesja. Zjutraj vam tega ne bi mogla povedati. Zdaj lahko s tem napovedujemo."

Tem je svojih deset hlebcev pred pošiljanjem stehtal na vzmetni tehtnici na svoji strani in s kredo zapisal 3,7 kilograma. Orsine medeninaste uteži so pokazale 4,08. Nekaj časa je strmel v številki. "Vzmeti čez vrata lažejo," je počasi rekel. "Moja babica ni nikoli zaupala nobeni. Žito za Prvo peč smo tehtali na gredni tehtnici." Že je segal po plašču. "Ceh hrani Listinsko tehtnico v dvorani. Vrnem se pred naslednjo izmenjavo."

04 · Glasni hlebec

Ni ga bilo štirideset minut. Ko se je vrnil, je pod eno roko nosil Listinsko tehtnico, dve medeninasti skledi na prečki iz črnega hrasta, za njim pa je v okvirju v zelenem plašču stala mojstrica Ilse Varro iz Ceha ognjišč z nalogom v roki.

Tem je tehtnico obesil čez vrata, tako da je bila ena skleda na vsaki strani, vrtišče pa je sedelo v samem okvirju. "Kar koli vleče skledo, tukaj ali tam, je vrtišče kraj, kjer se obe kuhinji strinjata. Primerja. Ne odčitava."

"Primerja vleke," je rekla Illuminara, "tvoja vzmet pa nam je pravkar povedala, da se kuhinji morda ne strinjata, kako močno vleče kilogram. Preden z njo stehtamo mestni kruh, mi pokažite, da je pravična." Prosila je za najpreprostejši preizkus, kar jih je poznala. Orsa je na svojo skledo položila medeninasti kilogram. Varro, ki še ni spregovorila, je iz klobučevine odvila cehovski zapečateni standardni kilogram in ga položila na Temovo. Tehtnica je v okvirju obvisela vodoravno. Nato sta obe prenesli skledi čez prag, medeninasto k Temu in standardno k Orsi, ter ju znova obesili. Vodoravno.

"Če bi kilogram na eni strani vlekel močneje," je rekla Illuminara, "bi se tehtnica ob zamenjavi nagnila. Ni se. Tehtnica je pravična, mestni medeninasti kilogram je enak cehovskemu, laže pa vzmet." Tem je rekel, da se vzmet spominja kovine svojega sveta in da mu še nikoli ni prišlo na misel, da bi vprašal za mnenje drugi svet. Varro je brez besed ovila klobučevino okoli standarda. To je bilo prvo, kar je tisto jutro storila, ne da bi temu ugovarjala.

Druga izmenjava je obsegala pet tisoč gramov v vsako smer, uravnoteženih do grama: Orsinih pet rženih hlebcev in štiristo štirideset gramov težak hlebček, hladnih iz kleti s šestimi stopinjami, v zameno za dvanajst votlih hlebcev in sto štiri grame težak krajec, naravnost iz modre peči, z devetdesetimi stopinjami v sredici. "Deset kilogramov skozi vrata," je rekla Pryce, "dvakrat toliko kot prvič. Moja razlaga pravi, da bo zavreščalo."

"Moja pravi, da se ne bo zgodilo nič," je rekla Illuminara. "Temova pravi, da bodo vroči hlebci potisnili zdrs v napačno smer. Zapišite, kdo je rekel kaj."

Ob sedemindvajset minut čez osmo je skozi šla zadnja skorja. Wren je sprožila štoparico. Po sedmih minutah nič. Po štirinajstih nič. Merilnik je vztrajal pri nič celi osem, kakor da je bila vrednost naslikana nanj, cerkvena ura pa je ohranjala dve minuti in četrt.

Pryce je izvlekla svinčnik, iz zvezka prečrtala eno vrstico, pretok, in prečrtano parafirala. To je bilo najdražje dejanje kogar koli tisti dan. "Deset kilogramov," je rekla, "pa vratom ni mar. Prav. Potem jih vseeno zapečatimo. Karkoli že je, dokazali ste, da je resnično."

"Če jih zapečatite zdaj," je rekla Illuminara, "boste v tem mestu za vedno zamrznili skoraj štiri kilograme primanjkljaja. Ura bo pridobivala dve minuti in četrt na uro, dokler bo tam beton, številčnica pa bo drsela z njo. Vaš lastni merilnik vam bo to povedal vsako jutro, vrat pa ne boste mogli odpreti, da bi dolg vrnili."

Varro si je v okvirju odkašljala. "Ceh ima prednostno pravico. Listina Prve peči ..."

"Pravi, da peč pripada tistemu, ki iz nje hrani mesto," je rekel Tem. "Ko sem bil vaš vajenec, sem vam jo sam prebral. Mojstrica, odprejo se za dve roki. Če vzamete moj zapah, boste imeli hladno omaro v svetu brez Orse." Glasu ni povzdignil.

"Enako velja za vas, inšpektorica," je rekla Orsa. "Moja moka, moja vrata. Merilnik je dobrodošel. Zapaha ne boste dobili."

05 · Poravnano na petdeset gramov

Vračilo je trajalo ves preostanek dopoldneva in ni bilo čudež nikogar. Tri izmenjave, oba peka sta vsako težo izgovorila naglas, Wren pa je tekoče stanje pisala s kredo na vrečo. Ob pol desetih je Tem poslal pet votlih hlebcev v zameno za en rženi hlebec, dva tisoč štirideset gramov proti devetsto dvanajstim, primanjkljaj pa je padel s 3.976 gramov na 2.848. Ob četrt čez deseto sta enako ponovila: 1.720. Tri minute čez enajsto je šlo pet hlebcev v zameno za tristo šestdeset gramov težak rženo-semenski hlebček in na vreči je pisalo 40. Štiristo gramov težak hlebček bi ga izničil, vendar Orsi ni ostal nobeden, njena peč pa ga ne bi dala pred jutrom. Merilnik se je vsakič oglasil sedem minut pozneje kakor prej: nič cela šest, nič cela tri, nato migljaj. Illuminara se ni vmešavala v tehtanje, le brala je nagib v okvirju in vsakič povedala, ali je popustil.

Orsa je z roko na zapahu določila prvo pravilo. "Za pošiljanje poravnano na petdeset gramov. Karkoli na vreči piše pod petdeset, ostane dolg in dolg gre skozi vrata prvi pri naslednji izmenjavi, pred vsem drugim. Oba številko izgovoriva naglas. Če je ne moreš izgovoriti, ne pošiljaš." Tem je določil drugo: knjiga ostane v vratih, s kredo na hrastu, kjer jo lahko prebereta oba. Illuminara je dodala edino, kar je lahko videla bolje od drugih: mesto je ravnovesje že odčitavalo brezplačno. Ura in postaja sta bila merilnika. Ministrstvo je lahko bralo števec, Ceh svoje kotle. Nobeden ne bo držal roke na zapahu.

Pryce je pravilo dvakrat prebrala in rekla, da je neredno, kar je bilo res, ter da je to tudi edina ureditev, pri kateri lahko vidi stvar, ki naj bi jo varovala, kar je bilo prav tako res. Nato je prosila za eno stvar. Merilnik naj stoji na stebru na trgu, kjer ga lahko prebere vsak otrok, in če bo kdaj pokazal zdrs večji od četrt kilohertza na deset minut, peka ne bosta pošiljala ničesar razen vračila, dokler ne pade. "Tehtnica lahko pade," je rekla. "Od mesta ne moremo zahtevati, naj zaupa tehtnici." Orsa je predlog pretehtala in privolila ter zraven zapisala, da ga lahko peka skupaj razveljavita, ministrstvo pa ga samo ne more.

Varro je dolgo minuto stala v Temovi kuhinji. Nato je rekla, da tehtnica, ki je bila pravilna ob osmih, ne bo ostala pravilna za vedno, da je devet let ni nihče preveril in da ne bo gledala, kako se mestni kruh tehta na nedokazani tehtnici. Vsak mesec bo sama prinesla standardne uteži, Orsa bo stala pri drugi skledi, skledi pa bodo zamenjali kakor danes; kateri koli pek bo lahko še isti dan vzel tehtnico nazaj iz cehovske hrambe. Podpisala je s peresom, za katero je prosila, da si ga izposodi. Tem je opazil, da je prosila za dolžnost, ne za ključ.

Na vreči je pisalo 40 in tako je ostalo. Deset minut čez enajsto merilnik ni več drsel in Illuminara je povedala, kaj je pokazalo dopoldne. "Tukaj sta dve uri, mi pa smo merili samo eno. Hitrost sledi ravnovesju s sedemminutnim zamikom. To smo preizkusili in vsakokrat se je odzvala. Mesto številčnice je druga količina. Domov se vrača v lastnem tempu in Wren ga lahko meri zdaj, ko se je hitrost ustavila." Wren ga je že merila. Postaja je bila sedemindvajset celih pet kilohertza prenizko in se je vsakih deset minut vračala za štiri cele pet, v enakomernem, nespremenljivem koraku. Po njenih torkovih zapiskih so gosi začele krožiti, ko je številčnica zdrsnila za osem prenizko. Po njenem računu bodo pristale sedem minut do poldneva.

Pristale so osem minut do poldneva. Ena se je spustila na trg, nato deset, nato vseh sto štirideset v dolgem, neurejenem pristanku in šle so naravnost h skorjam, ki so jim jih prinesli otroci pekov.

Bili so tudi stroški. Orsin rženi kruh je zdaj potoval v parih s Temovimi zračnimi hlebci in vsak teden bo pekla šest ur dlje. Temovo testo je za vzhajanje potrebovalo štiri minute in nad tem se je glasno in veselo pritoževal. Cerkvena ura je prehitevala za enainpetdeset minut in Josef, ki jo je navijal štirideset let, jo je z lastnimi rokami naravnal, za kar je potreboval uro in lestev, ter rekel, da je bila to najboljša ura, ki jo je porabil tisti mesec. Štirideset gramov je bilo še vedno dolgovanih in dokler ne bodo povrnjeni, bo ura pridobivala pol minute na dan. Odredbo o betoniranju so umaknili, ne raztrgali, in je v obeh uradih ostala kot nepodpisana grožnja.

Deset minut čez poldne so radijski sprejemniki našli svoje oznake. Orsa in Tem sta stala pri tehtnici v vratih, vsak s svojo skledo. "Poravnano?" je rekel Tem. "Poravnano na štirideset," je rekla Orsa, "in štirideset dolga." Roki sta položila na zapaha. Vrata so se odprla za dve roki.

Naslednje jutro dvajset minut do šestih je pred kruhom s Temove strani prečkala ena sama žemljica, težka natanko štirideset gramov, ne da bi se karkoli vrnilo, Tem pa je z vreče zbrisal dolg. Sedem minut pozneje je Wren pogledala s štoparice in rekla, da je cerkvena ura prvič po tednu dni tekla točno.
