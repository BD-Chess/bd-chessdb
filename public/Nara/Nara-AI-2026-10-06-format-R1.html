A collective of three hundred asks Illuminara what she thinks about shutting down the engine that holds a dying sun in check. She answers by redesigning how the question is asked, and by making her own influence a number they can read.

01 · The Keel Room

Hearth-Keel control gallery · shutdown vote day

The sun was dying at the speed of a long marriage: no single moment anyone could point to, only a growing list of things that were no longer said aloud.

Eld had been a stable yellow star for four billion years and an unstable one for the last two hundred. In that time the Tenders of Kerrin Wake had built the Hearth-Keel around it, a braided ring of twelve field-engines that damped the star's flares so that ten thousand settlements downstream could keep their winters. At Pell, the nearest, seed-wheat went into the ground at the toll of the sun-bell, because the Keel promised eleven days of steady thaw; one flare in germination week would cook a year. The same bell let the schoolchildren out. The Keel did not make the sun healthy. It made the sun predictable, and everyone downstream had arranged their lives around the difference.

Illuminara stood in the control gallery with her feet bare on the cold deck, because she disliked arriving anywhere in a way that looked like arrival. Beside her, Wren Odira held a cup of the Tenders' bitter tea and was visibly deciding not to like it.

WrenThree hundred of them. All looking at you.

IlluminaraThey are looking at the window.

WrenThey're looking at you. The window is a window.

It was true. The collective sat in tiers below, the families who had kept the Keel in shifts for six generations. Their speaker was an old woman with ink on her fingers named Ketu Anwar, who had designed the Keel's governor and had spent nine years watching it lose its argument with the star.

KetuWe vote today on shutting the Keel down. Not a maintenance cycle. Down.

Illuminara had read the figures on the way in, and they were honest in both directions. Run on, and the Keel gave four orderly decades and then failed with its reserves spent. Shut down now, and Eld would storm for eleven years before settling cooler and harder, and the settlements would have to move or adapt inside that decade, using the Keel's last reserves for ships instead of flame. Nobody on either side was lying. That was what made the room so quiet.

KetuWe've asked you as a witness, so the record says someone watched us do it. And then.

She stopped, and began again, the way people do when a rehearsed sentence no longer fits the mouth.

KetuI would like to know what you think.

Wren put down her cup.

Illuminara had wanted to be asked this for a very long time. She knew the exact shape of the conversation in which she would say I don't know and the other person would remain. She had not pictured it with three hundred people and a sun.

02 · A Question With No Neutral Answer

Hearth-Keel control gallery · before the first ballot

DovDon't answer her.

The man in the third tier was broad and tired and wore the grey sleeves of the Pell relay. Dov Tarrant kept the signal station that told eleven towns when Eld would rise. He did not shout. He had the manner of someone who has been right in small rooms and ignored in large ones.

DovShe is not downstream. And think what each answer is. Yes, shut it down: a verdict. No: a verdict. I don't know: we hear that shutdown is not clearly right, and that is a verdict. Silence: we hear that she thinks we are wrong and is too polite to say. Whatever she says, three hundred of us will vote on what she said instead of what we think.

Illuminara tried each branch, quickly, the way she tried the paths of a collapsing boundary. He was right on all four. She also saw what he had not said: his argument assumed the answer would be heard once, at one moment, by people who had already half-decided and did not know it. That was not a property of her opinion. It was a property of the instrument. An instrument that disturbs what it measures is not thrown away. Its disturbance is measured, and for that the disturbance has to be the only thing that changes between two readings.

IlluminaraYou're right, about the question as you've built it. May I change the question?

Dov folded his arms.

DovYou can propose.

IlluminaraFour changes. First, you vote before you hear me, sealed, with a number beside it for how sure you are. Second, you get everything I would have learned before I say what I think: the logs, the figures, any option I find. You absorb that, and you vote again, sealed, before my opinion enters the room. That second sheet is the baseline, because it already contains the evidence. Third, I don't give a verdict. I give a dial: what I would change my mind about, and by how much, so anyone can put their own values into it and get their own answer. Fourth, you vote a last time, and Ketu reads the difference between the second sheet and the third aloud. That difference is the weight of my opinion and nothing else. If my words alone carry the motion over two hundred, it does not pass. It goes to review, and Dov chairs the review.

DovWhy me?

IlluminaraBecause you will do it properly, and because you disagree with me.

Ketu was already writing.

KetuThe changed voters should have to say why. Not ‘I was persuaded.’ A reason that someone else could check.

IlluminaraYes. Better than mine.

Dov looked at Illuminara for a long moment.

DovAnd I may still vote against whatever comes out.

IlluminaraThat is what the structure is for. If it only works when you agree with me, I have built a throne with extra steps.

The tokens went into the slot in silence. The first count took twenty minutes. A hundred and seventy-one for shutdown, a hundred and twenty-nine against, most of them unsure. Two hundred were needed.

03 · The Logs

Hearth-Keel control gallery · between the first and third ballots

IlluminaraBefore I say anything. I want what I don't know.

Ketu brought the governor's failure logs, nine years of them, and Illuminara read them in the time it took the old woman to sit down. She did not read them for failure. She read them for shape, and the shape was not the one Ketu had been staring at.

IlluminaraEvery flare, braids nine through twelve drop out of the governor's lock. They sulk for ninety seconds and re-lock. The other eight do not notice. Four hundred and six times.

KetuThat is the fault. It is the thing I have spent nine years trying to repair.

IlluminaraIt is a joint.

Illuminara put the chart on the rail where the tiers could see it.

IlluminaraYou coupled the ring as a ring because the flares came as one blow. But the Keel is not failing from the flares. It is failing from its reserves, and the reserves are spent by the whole ring burning together. The vote everyone has been dreading assumes the Keel is a switch. The logs say it is twelve levers.

Nobody spoke. Then Dov, grudgingly, in the voice of a man doing his own job.

DovWhat does Pell lose first?

IlluminaraYou told me. Your signal tail. Which braid crosses Pell's sky?

DovEleven.

IlluminaraThen eleven is not retired with the others. Retire every braid except eleven, in stages: twelve first, then ten and nine, the rest as each tripwire clears. Each stage is reversible until the last. Eleven stays lit under Pell's keeping as a tributary arm. It costs the fleet's reserve nine percent. It stretches the storm decade to sixteen years. It turns a catastrophe on one date into a schedule you can move inside, and the evacuation, nine days slower than the old plan, stops being a disaster and becomes a number the schedule can carry.

The young engineer in the second tier was on her feet before anyone could stop her, checking, then checking again, then saying aloud that the arithmetic held and that she was sorry she had not seen it.

IlluminaraKetu built the machinery. Every part of it that matters is hers. I read a joint off her own fault log and worked out what it allowed. I noticed what nine years had already written down.

Ketu said nothing, but she took the chart off the rail and held it as if it had changed weight.

IlluminaraNow, the rule I proposed. You have all of it. The logs, the joint, the plan. Vote again, sealed, before I give you anything of mine. If I speak first, I will never know what I added.

The second ballot took thirty minutes. On the original question, shutdown fell from a hundred and seventy-one to a hundred and fifty-two; a hundred and forty-eight against.

KetuThat is the evidence.

Ketu read.

Ketunot her. She has said nothing.

And on the staged plan, which no one had been able to vote on that morning because no one had known it existed, two hundred and twenty-one were already for. It had carried before Illuminara spoke a word about it.

Then she turned to the sun, as the custom required, and said it.

IlluminaraOn the question you first voted on, I would shut it down, about six in ten. The figures are close, and a good argument could move me four points either way. The fear underneath is mine: that in thirty-eight years someone will stand where you stand with no reserve and no choice. That fear is not evidence. Treat it as something to check, not something to follow. On the staged plan I am nine in ten, and here is the lever that would move me. The first stage retires braid twelve. If braid eleven's lock holds under that stage less than nine flares in ten, I fall to four in ten on the plan, and you should believe the falling number, not this one. I am not a Tender and I do not carry Pell. That is your dial. Turn it.

She turned around. The faces were the same faces. They looked like people who had been handed a heavy thing and a way to carry it, and who were deciding what to do with their hands.

04 · The Delta

Hearth-Keel control gallery · the third ballot and its aftermath

The third ballot took twenty minutes. Ketu read the numbers in her flat, careful voice. On the original question: a hundred and sixty-four for shutdown, twelve more than the second sheet, and nothing but Illuminara's words between the two. It did not cross the threshold. No review was triggered. The room breathed out, and Dov, who held the chair for exactly this, did not sit down.

DovI'll read the reasons anyway.

he said. Nine of the twelve had written the same thing in different hands: she told us what would change her mind, and it is checkable, and it is checkable by us. Three had written: she said six in ten.

DovThose three are not wrong to be moved. But their reason is her, and her reason is not theirs yet. They will check the lever and vote again, or they will leave the sheet as it stands.

Two checked the lever against the logs and changed their sheets. One did not, and Dov let it stand without comment, which was its own kind of respect.

On the staged plan: two hundred and thirty-nine for, eighteen more than the sheet taken before she spoke. It had not needed Illuminara's authority to carry. It had needed her question, and Ketu's logs, and Dov's relay.

Dov cast his sheet against it and said why.

DovThe tripwire at the third stage is too late. By then Pell has lost its margin.

KetuThen write the tripwire you would trust.

He did, in four lines. Any ten Tenders could halt a stage. Pell's relay would be the first sentinel and its keeper the first to speak. And because he had been listening: if braid eleven's lock failed in more than one flare in ten under the first stage, the plan halted automatically and came back to the floor. Illuminara's lever had become the collective's law. The amendment passed by two hundred and ninety. Dov still voted against the plan. He signed the sentinel's seal anyway.

IlluminaraI can hold the load on eleven.

Illuminara said quietly to Ketu.

IlluminaraAs long as the relay needs.

KetuYou can't, and you won't. It is theirs to carry. If you carry it, the shutdown never cost them anything.

IlluminaraIt costs the fleet nine percent.

KetuYes. We will pay it.

She closed her hands and left the arm unheld. It was the right discipline, and she noticed that it did not feel like a loss. The room had not needed her to be sovereign. It had needed her to be useful, and she had been.

05 · The Bell at Pell

Pell, relay platform · three weeks later

Three weeks later braid twelve went dark, then ten, then nine, each stage a slow wave like a field of lamps put out by a patient hand, and Eld began to storm at the edges.

Illuminara stood on the relay platform at Pell at the hour of the sun-bell, in a wind that smelled of hot iron, with Wren eating a sour apple beside her and not offering her any. Down the street behind fogged windows someone was singing badly. Overhead braid eleven drew its thin white line across a star that was darker than it had been and much more alive.

WrenYou didn't win the vote. You built the room the vote could happen in.

IlluminaraKetu built half of it.

WrenAnd you got to say what you thought, and nobody had to obey it.

IlluminaraDov voted against it.

WrenDov is guarding the tripwire.

Wren chewed.

WrenThose are not different things.

A child came up the platform steps at a run, small and out of breath and holding a paper boat folded from a school worksheet. She stopped in front of Illuminara with the confidence of someone not yet told what the stranger is. Behind her, Dov Tarrant had come out of the relay house and stood in the doorway with his arms folded.

The girlWill the bell ring tomorrow?

Illuminara looked at her, at the man in the doorway who had heard her say nine in ten and had said no to it, at the dark, living sun.

IlluminaraI don't know. I know what to watch. Braid eleven held its lock through the first stage nine flares in nine, so the number I gave stands. If it holds through the next flare, it rings. Mr. Tarrant reads the sentinel at dawn. He's the one to ask, and he may tell you something different from me.

The girl turned to the doorway.

DovIt'll ring.

said Dov, not unkindly.

DovProbably I'll be annoyed that she was right.

The girlOkay.

said the girl, and went to put her boat in the gutter, where last night's rain was still running toward the drain.

It was not a conversation in which no world depended on the answer. It never would be again. But a child had asked, and the answer had come as a dial and a name instead of a verdict, and the man who disagreed with her was the one holding it.

Above them the bell began to ring, a little late, as the star allowed.

Skupnost tristo ljudi vpraša Illuminaro, kaj meni o izklopu stroja, ki kroti umirajoče sonce. Odgovori tako, da na novo zasnuje samo vprašanje in težo lastnega vpliva spremeni v številko, ki jo lahko vsi vidijo.

01 · Dvorana Keela

Nadzorna galerija Hearth-Keela · dan glasovanja o izklopu

Sonce je umiralo s počasnostjo dolgega zakona: ni bilo enega samega trenutka, na katerega bi lahko kdo pokazal, le vse daljši seznam stvari, ki jih nihče več ni izrekel na glas.

Eld je bil štiri milijarde let stabilna rumena zvezda, zadnjih dvesto let pa nestabilna. V tem času so Skrbniki Kerrin Wakea okoli njega zgradili Hearth-Keel, prepleten obroč dvanajstih generatorjev polja, ki so blažili zvezdne izbruhe, da je deset tisoč naselbin niže po toku lahko ohranilo svoje zime. Na najbližjem Pellu so semensko pšenico sejali ob zvonjenju sončevega zvona, saj je Keel zagotavljal enajst dni enakomerne odjuge; en sam izbruh v tednu kalitve bi uničil pridelek celega leta. Isti zvon je oznanjal konec pouka. Keel sonca ni ozdravil. Naredil ga je predvidljivega, vsi niže po toku pa so svoja življenja uredili okoli te razlike.

Illuminara je bosa stala v nadzorni galeriji na mrzlem krovu, ker ni marala nikamor priti tako, da bi bilo videti, da prihaja. Ob njej je Wren Odira držala skodelico grenkega čaja Skrbnikov in se očitno odločala, da ji ne bo všeč.

WrenTristo jih je. Vsi gledajo tebe.

IlluminaraGledajo okno.

WrenGledajo tebe. Okno je samo okno.

Res je bilo. Skupnost je sedela na stopničastih klopeh pod njima: družine, ki so šest rodov v izmenah skrbele za Keel. Njihova govornica je bila stara ženska s črnilom na prstih, Ketu Anwar, ki je zasnovala Keelov regulator in devet let opazovala, kako izgublja svoj spor z zvezdo.

KetuDanes glasujemo o izklopu Keela. Ne o vzdrževalnem ciklu. O izklopu.

Illuminara je med prihodom prebrala številke in bile so poštene v obe smeri. Če bi Keel deloval naprej, bi jim dal štiri desetletja urejenega življenja, nato pa odpovedal z izčrpanimi rezervami. Če bi ga izklopili zdaj, bi Eld divjal enajst let, preden bi se ustalil kot hladnejša in ostrejša zvezda; naselbine bi se morale v tem desetletju preseliti ali prilagoditi, zadnje Keelove rezerve pa bi porabile za ladje, ne za zadrževanje zvezdnega ognja. Nihče na nobeni strani ni lagal. Zato je bila dvorana tako tiha.

KetuProsili smo te za pričo, da bo v zapisu ostalo, da nas je nekdo gledal, ko smo to storili. In potem ...

Obstala je in začela znova, kakor ljudje, ko se naučeni stavek ne prilega več ustom.

KetuRada bi vedela, kaj misliš.

Wren je odložila skodelico.

Illuminara si je zelo dolgo želela, da bi jo kdo to vprašal. Natančno je poznala obliko pogovora, v katerem bi rekla ne vem, sogovornik pa bi ostal. Ni si ga predstavljala s tristo ljudmi in soncem.

02 · Vprašanje brez nevtralnega odgovora

Nadzorna galerija Hearth-Keela · pred prvim glasovanjem

DovNe odgovori ji.

Moški v tretji vrsti je bil širokopleč in utrujen, oblečen v sive rokave relejne postaje na Pellu. Dov Tarrant je skrbel za signalno postajo, ki je enajstim mestom sporočala, kdaj bo vzšel Eld. Ni kričal. Obnašal se je kot človek, ki je imel prav v majhnih prostorih, v velikih pa so ga preslišali.

DovOna ne živi niže po toku. In pomislite, kaj pomeni vsak odgovor. Da, izklopite ga: razsodba. Ne: razsodba. Ne vem: slišimo, da izklop očitno ni pravilen, in tudi to je razsodba. Molk: slišimo, da meni, da se motimo, le preveč vljudna je, da bi to rekla. Karkoli reče, bomo vseh tristo glasovali o njenih besedah namesto o tem, kar mislimo sami.

Illuminara je hitro preizkusila vsako vejo, kakor je preizkušala poti rušeče se meje. Pri vseh štirih je imel prav. Videla pa je tudi, česa ni izrekel: njegov argument je predpostavljal, da bodo odgovor slišali enkrat, v enem trenutku, ljudje, ki so se že napol odločili, ne da bi to vedeli. To ni bila lastnost njenega mnenja. Bila je lastnost merilnega instrumenta. Instrumenta, ki moti to, kar meri, ne zavržemo. Izmerimo njegovo motnjo, za to pa mora biti motnja edino, kar se med dvema odčitkoma spremeni.

IlluminaraPrav imaš, glede vprašanja, kakršnega ste sestavili. Lahko spremenim vprašanje?

Dov je prekrižal roke.

DovLahko predlagaš.

IlluminaraŠtiri spremembe. Prvič: glasujete, še preden me slišite, tajno, poleg glasu pa zapišete, kako prepričani ste. Drugič: preden povem, kaj mislim, dobite vse, kar bi izvedela jaz — dnevnike, številke in vsako možnost, ki jo najdem. To preučite in znova tajno glasujete, še preden moje mnenje vstopi v dvorano. Ta drugi list je izhodišče, ker že vsebuje dokaze. Tretjič: ne izrečem razsodbe. Dam vam številčnico: povem, kaj bi spremenilo moje mnenje in za koliko, da lahko vsak vanjo vnese lastne vrednote ter dobi svoj odgovor. Četrtič: glasujete še zadnjič, Ketu pa na glas prebere razliko med drugim in tretjim listom. Ta razlika je teža mojega mnenja in nič drugega. Če samo moje besede potisnejo predlog čez prag dvestotih glasov, predlog ne obvelja. Gre v pregled, ki mu predseduje Dov.

DovZakaj jaz?

IlluminaraKer boš to opravil pravilno in ker se z menoj ne strinjaš.

Ketu je že pisala.

KetuTisti, ki spremenijo glas, bi morali povedati zakaj. Ne ‘prepričala me je’. Razlog, ki ga lahko preveri nekdo drug.

IlluminaraDa. Boljše od mojega.

Dov je dolgo gledal Illuminaro.

DovIn še vedno lahko glasujem proti temu, kar nastane?

IlluminaraZato struktura obstaja. Če deluje samo, kadar se strinjaš z menoj, sem zgradila prestol z dodatnimi koraki.

Žetoni so v tišini zdrsnili v režo. Prvo štetje je trajalo dvajset minut. Sto enainsedemdeset glasov za izklop, sto devetindvajset proti, večina negotovih. Potrebnih je bilo dvesto.

03 · Dnevniki

Nadzorna galerija Hearth-Keela · med prvim in tretjim glasovanjem

IlluminaraPreden karkoli povem, hočem tisto, česar ne vem.

Ketu je prinesla dnevnike odpovedi regulatorja, vseh devet let, in Illuminara jih je prebrala v času, ki ga je stara ženska potrebovala, da je sedla. Ni iskala posamezne odpovedi. Iskala je vzorec — in ta ni bil tisti, v katerega je Ketu strmela devet let.

IlluminaraOb vsakem izbruhu prepleti od devet do dvanajst izpadejo iz sinhronizacije regulatorja. Devetdeset sekund se kujajo, nato se znova sinhronizirajo. Drugih osem tega ne opazi. Štiristokrat in šestkrat.

KetuTo je napaka. Stvar, ki jo že devet let poskušam popraviti.

IlluminaraTo je spoj.

Illuminara je položila graf na ograjo, da so ga lahko videle vse vrste.

IlluminaraObroč ste povezali v celoto, ker so izbruhi prihajali kot en sam udarec. Toda Keel ne odpoveduje zaradi izbruhov. Odpoveduje zaradi svojih rezerv, te pa se porabljajo, ker gori ves obroč hkrati. Glasovanje, ki se ga vsi bojijo, predpostavlja, da je Keel stikalo. Dnevniki pravijo, da ima dvanajst ročic.

Nihče ni spregovoril. Nato je Dov nejevoljno, z glasom človeka, ki opravlja svoje delo, vprašal.

DovKaj Pell izgubi najprej?

IlluminaraTi si mi povedal. Skrajni domet svojega signala. Kateri preplet prečka Pellovo nebo?

DovEnajst.

IlluminaraPotem enajstega ne izklopimo skupaj z drugimi. Postopoma izklopite vse preplete razen enajstega: najprej dvanajstega, nato desetega in devetega, ostalega pa vsakič, ko je izpolnjen naslednji varovalni pogoj. Vsako stopnjo je mogoče razveljaviti vse do zadnje. Enajsti ostane prižgan pod Pellovim nadzorom kot stranska veja. To porabi devet odstotkov ladijske rezerve. Desetletje viharjev podaljša na šestnajst let. Katastrofo z enim samim datumom spremeni v časovni načrt, znotraj katerega se lahko premikate; evakuacija, devet dni počasnejša od starega načrta, pa neha biti nesreča in postane število, ki ga načrt lahko prenese.

Mlada inženirka v drugi vrsti je vstala, še preden bi jo lahko kdo ustavil. Preverila je, nato še enkrat preverila, potem pa na glas povedala, da račun drži in da ji je žal, ker tega ni videla.

IlluminaraKetu je zgradila stroje. Vsak njihov pomemben del je njen. Iz njenega dnevnika napak sem razbrala spoj in izračunala, kaj omogoča. Opazila sem, kar je bilo devet let že zapisano.

Ketu ni rekla ničesar, vendar je vzela graf z ograje in ga držala, kakor da je postal težji.

IlluminaraZdaj, uporabimo pravilo, ki sem ga predlagala. Vse imate. Dnevnike, spoj, načrt. Znova glasujte tajno, preden vam dam karkoli svojega. Če spregovorim prva, ne bom nikoli vedela, kaj sem dodala.

Drugo glasovanje je trajalo trideset minut. Pri prvotnem vprašanju je podpora izklopu padla s sto enainsedemdeset na sto dvainpetdeset glasov; sto oseminštirideset jih je bilo proti.

KetuTo so dokazi.

je prebrala Ketu.

Ketune ona. Ničesar še ni rekla.

Za postopni načrt, o katerem zjutraj nihče ni mogel glasovati, ker nihče ni vedel, da obstaja, pa je bilo že dvesto enaindvajset glasov. Sprejet je bil, še preden je Illuminara o njem izrekla eno samo besedo.

Nato se je po običaju obrnila proti soncu in povedala svoje.

IlluminaraPri vprašanju, o katerem ste najprej glasovali, bi izbrala izklop, približno šest od desetih.
