A dimensional accident delivers a million incompatible emergencies to one hospital. Space is not the shortage: each open door brings a different local physics, and treating one patient can kill another three corridors away. Illuminara must discover the order-sensitive grammar shared by the doors, turn mutual exclusion into a repairable clinical schedule, and leave ordinary medical teams able to run it without her.

01 · The Corridor with No End

Saint Orra Hospital · from 09:12

At 09:12, Saint Orra Hospital had forty-two emergency doors. At 09:13, it had one million and six.

They overlaid the building: brass arches, membranes of rain, black rectangles edged in frost, circles no larger than plates and gates tall enough for ships. Behind each waited a patient, a medical team or a crowd carrying someone who had reached the only stable point left after a chain of universes struck one another.

Chief physician Sira Venn marked a red line on the floor and ordered every patient to remain inside the light cast by their own door. The local field around each threshold preserved enough of its home physics to keep its bearer alive. Saint Orra's laws were not neutral.

Three patients proved it. Omi, a boy with a torn lung, breathed through a copper web because oxygen left his blood only while current ran clockwise around his chest. Two doors away, Nhelo had transparent mineral plates instead of red cells; clockwise current made them align, sharpen and cut. Above them floated Sirr, a surveyor whose injuries unfolded four seconds behind their causes. An ordinary monitor shocked him for a heartbeat that had not arrived.

A nurse tore away the monitor. Sira reversed Omi's coil by hand. Nhelo's daughter Vale wrapped her mother in a field-cloth brought through their door. For nine seconds, all three treatments held.

Then Omi's current entered his brass frame, emerged from eleven others and turned Nhelo's diagnostic arch into a blade. Sirr's delayed field returned the surge after the machine was dark. Across the million thresholds, alarms answered one another like birds startled in a forest.

Illuminara arrived inside the widening note. Gold geometry spread from her hands between the returning paths of force. She did not suppress Omi's current; she gave it an empty path on which to complete itself. The arch around Nhelo fell dark. Sirr's surge ended in Illuminara's field.

Every ward was still mostly empty. Sira looked at her hospital map.

SiraBeds are not the problem.

IlluminaraNo. Sequence is.

03 · Seventeen Verbs

Saint Orra Hospital and the doors · during parallel testing

The million door teams tested in parallel while Illuminara built candidate grammars and let evidence destroy them. Preserve, invert, delay and split were not enough: boundaries could bind force to a surface, erase residue or move an effect backward. Each name promised what would leave and what debt remained.

Renn tested a steel trolley through three doors. A, B, C melted its wheels; C, B, A left them frosted but whole. Salt exposed stored direction, a magnetic knot showed inversion, and a consented cell revealed residue that acted only when the loop closed.

Seventeen behaviors explained the hazards observed so far and predicted forty unused tests. Thirty-nine passed. The fortieth was Door 701.

The model said preserve after invert would be safe. The child's air bladder collapsed inward. The clock lost twenty-one minutes in one breath: 00:27:06.

The home medic held the failing bladder against the lens so nobody could turn the loss into a number alone. The child was awake, counting each shallow breath with two fingers. Sira canceled three scheduled crossings; their teams accepted the delay only after seeing the test trace and the shrinking red margin together.

Illuminara froze every live passage and laid the successful traces over the failure. When only the inner skin had crossed an altered boundary, pressure returned. When only the outer skin had changed, it returned. The collapse appeared only after both surfaces carried different passage histories. She asked the home team what the bladder meant by pressure.

Illuminara changed one edge. The grammar had treated pressure as an object. Here it was a relation that vanished when either surface changed its history. No eighteenth behavior was needed; one of the seventeen acted on relations, not things. The next adversarial test held, then a second in reversed order held.

The grammar became a directed conflict graph. Every clinical phase—assessment, stabilization, intervention, recovery—left a residue. Some phases could share a room. Some needed time between them. Some could begin only after an empty cleansing transition.

06 · One Hospital, Many Homes

Saint Orra Hospital · the next shift

By the next shift, the hospital no longer tried to pull every patient through. A safe diagnostic could cross one way; a treatment pattern could return the other. Local teams altered the grammar when their evidence survived challenge.

At a junction Illuminara had never touched, Renn called amber for Erel, whose blood treated pressure as memory. A door engineer objected to the proposed cleansing order. Renn stopped, reversed two shutters and asked Erel's home team to test. Their lamp stayed clear. Erel crossed, received the membrane repair and walked back under her own gravity. Eighteen patients had arrived beyond recovery; their doors stayed open for chosen witnesses. No inhabited door was sacrificed to improve the count. Many routes no longer led home, and Ilex formed a resettlement assembly before Saint Orra could call anyone a permanent guest without consent.

Omi woke asking whether the doors were still there. Nhelo held one transparent hand to the light so he could see that her blood plates lay flat. Sirr laughed four seconds after Vale's joke, exactly when his team expected him to.

Renn called blue again. The load flowed into sinks the local teams had built, not into Illuminara. The next patient entered, and no alarm answered elsewhere.

Dimenzijska nesreča pripelje milijon nezdružljivih nujnih primerov v eno bolnišnico. 01 · Hodnik brez konca
