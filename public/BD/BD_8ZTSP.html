PRELIMINARY

8zTSPA new TSP kernel, measured next to LKH and Concorde

Preliminary results, one laptop, 11 November 2026: a new Rust kernel, 8zTSP, measured next to the standard solvers LKH and Concorde. 8zTSP 0.5.0 reached the known optimum in 90 of 90 runs on nine national instances. On DIMACS E100k.0 (100,000 cities), six threads of 8zTSP 0.5.0 produced shorter tours after two and five minutes, in all three tested seeds, than the best of six independent LKH 3.0.7 runs with the stated geometric settings, on the same laptop. The measured settings that favoured LKH are listed with their conditions under Results of 8zTSP.

What this page is

8zTSP is a new TSP kernel. Release 0.5.0 was declared on 11 November 2026. Its comparisons with LKH are reported below; the separately labelled comparison with Concorde used the early release 0.2.0. All measurements were made on the same laptop. This page reports those measurements, with their limits. The results are preliminary.

This is BD × AI Lab work. BD leads it; the zoom in the kernel is his idea, and its bitshift grid comes from the earlier BD × AI Lab research. The kernel and the measurements were built with Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), and the kernel was independently reviewed by a second AI session, a GPT model.

Where it comes from. 8zTSP grows out of the BD × AI Lab TSP work, including TSPTS, TSPES and the 8Z-RP research (see also the research portfolio). It carries forward the zoom, BD's idea, and the integer bitshift grid in which that research implemented it. The other ideas of that research, among them the Tour-Enriched Pyramid contributed by Claude, are candidates for controlled experiments in the new kernel (see Next). 8zTSP is a new kernel, distinct from the 8zTSP-R research lines of that portfolio.

How it was measured

  • Machine. A laptop with an Intel i7-10875H (8 physical cores); one machine for all solvers. Each solver ran on its own physical core or cores; the cores were not rotated between solvers.
  • Rules. Cold start. No solver is told the optimum: the arena knows it only to compute percentages and to stop a solver that reaches it. No saved tours. Final tour files were re-read from disk and checked: each city once, length recomputed from the coordinates. Concorde entries marked * have no final tour file and report values from its log. Intermediate LKH and Concorde lengths also come from their logs; a valid final tour does not independently validate every earlier entry.
  • Instances. Nine national instances from the University of Waterloo collection, from wi29 to nu3496 (29 to 3,496 cities; the number in a name is its number of cities), with known optima. For large sizes: the DIMACS TSP Challenge random instances E10k.0 (10,000 cities), E100k.0 (100,000) and E1M.0 (1,000,000), and sw24978 (Sweden, 24,978 cities, also a Waterloo national instance).
  • What the percentages mean. National instances and sw24978: percent over the known optimum (University of Waterloo table). DIMACS instances: the optimum is not known, so percent over the published Held–Karp bound (from the DIMACS TSP Challenge bounds, computed with Concorde's linear program), a proven lower bound. These percentages are gaps above a lower bound, not measured gaps above the unknown optimum: they are upper bounds on the relative gap to the optimum; the actual gap is no greater.
  • Standard solvers. LKH 3.0.7 (Keld Helsgaun; distributed by its author for research use) and Concorde 03.12.19 (the official executable; available for academic research use). Concorde is exact: it stops when it has proved that its tour is optimal.
  • LKH settings. Default settings on the national instances. On the large instances, geometric settings of the kind its author uses for large planar instances: CANDIDATE_SET_TYPE = DELAUNAY, MAX_CANDIDATES = 5, INITIAL_PERIOD = 100. LKH does not use several threads itself, so with six cores it ran as six independent runs, and the best counts. With its default settings LKH wrote no tour within 20 minutes at 24,978 cities (six independent runs, shown in the sw24978 table); its preprocessing had not finished. Comparisons at the large sizes are therefore with the geometric settings.
  • Clocks. The clock of 8zTSP starts when the input file has been read; the clock of LKH starts when its process starts. These are different timing origins: the 8zTSP clock excludes process start and input reading, so the first-tour times shown are not directly comparable end to end. A displayed 0.00 s is a rounded value, not zero elapsed time.

8zTSP 0.5.0

8zTSP is a kernel written in Rust that uses only the standard library. Release 0.5.0 prints "0.5.0-rc3": the last candidate was declared without recompiling. Up to 12,000 cities the default mode is a population of tours that are merged; above that the default is windows over the map with sequential 3-opt. The large-instance measurements below used the windows mode.

Parts of the kernel and where each comes from.

PartWhat it does hereOrigin
Bitshift gridUses a spatial grid to find neighbours and build start tours without an n × n table. The running time depends on the geometry: many cities in one cell can make the search quadratic. Candidates in the windows mode: 4 nearest + 1 per quadrant (8 candidates).
BD × AI LabThe integer bitshift pyramid of the earlier research: BD's zoom idea, first implemented there by GPT.
StandardNeighbour lists with quadrants (Bentley; Johnson and McGeoch).
Windows (zoom)The map is cut into windows of about a thousand cities along a curve through the grid; each window is solved by the population search, windows run in parallel; every pass cuts the map again so that window borders move; when progress stalls the windows grow. A development variant using literal bitshift cells has also been tried; it is not part of release 0.5.0 (see Next).
BD's ideaZoom: work on a window of the map, then zoom out. Implemented with Hilbert-curve pieces.
StandardReplacing the outside part of the tour by a fixed edge is known from the subproblem partitioning of LKH.
2-opt and Or-opt with a work queueLocal search: 2-opt reverses a segment, Or-opt moves a short segment.
StandardCroes (2-opt); Or (Or-opt).
Sequential 3-optThe default deeper local move in the windows; available as an explicit option in the kick mode.
StandardLin (3-opt).
Population with tour mergingA population of tours is improved by merging two tours through their difference cycles (AB-cycles): the edge assembly crossover (EAX).
StandardNagata and Kobayashi.
Related to the TEP idea of the earlier research (see Next); it is not TEP.
Diversity-aware selectionUses the change in edge diversity when scoring which child enters the population.

Release 0.5.0 does not include plug-ins for TEP, DCC or Rank, Don't Eliminate, the other ideas of the BD × AI Lab research; whether they add anything will be measured in the idea arena (see Next). The mechanics are known methods: the merging is EAX, and 2-opt, Or-opt, 3-opt and neighbour lists with quadrants are from the literature. The zoom is BD's idea, and the bitshift grid comes from the earlier BD × AI Lab research. What is specific to 8zTSP is how these parts are put together; no claim of priority is made.

The reading, with its conditions

A limited advantage on E100k.0

  • 100,000 cities (DIMACS E100k.0), six threads of 8zTSP 0.5.0 against six independent runs of LKH 3.0.7 with geometric settings, on the same laptop: 8zTSP is ahead after 2 minutes (0.740% / 0.767% / 0.770% against 0.792% / 0.804% / 0.797%) and after 5 minutes (0.714% / 0.714% / 0.718% against 0.754% / 0.772% / 0.762%), in three of three seeds.

LKH level or ahead

  • One core, 100,000 cities: LKH is ahead after 2 minutes in three of three seeds (0.816% / 0.822% / 0.807% against 0.858% / 0.886% / 0.865%) and after 5 minutes LKH is shorter in two of the three seeds, by 0.029 and 0.003 percentage points, and 8zTSP in one, by 0.002 (0.786% / 0.785% / 0.785% against 0.784% / 0.814% / 0.788%).
  • One core, 1,000,000 cities: LKH is ahead once it has its first tour (after 20 minutes 0.833% against 0.942%; after 30 minutes 0.816% against 0.906%).
  • Six threads, 1,000,000 cities: after 20 minutes LKH 0.825% and 8zTSP 0.833%; after 30 minutes LKH 0.810% and 8zTSP 0.815%. The difference after 30 minutes is 0.005 percentage points. For scale, two same-seed LKH runs on E100k.0 differed by about 0.01 percentage points after five minutes; more repeated runs are needed to interpret this difference.
  • 10,000 cities (E10k.0): LKH is ahead at every measured time from its first tour (after 2 minutes 0.711% against 0.720% with six threads, and 0.718% against 0.778% with one thread). At 1 s it has no tour yet.
  • 24,978 cities (sw24978), six cores: in this seed LKH had shorter tours than release 0.5.0 at every measured time at which both had a tour. The earlier candidate 0.5.0-rc1 was ahead at five minutes and level at ten minutes; a comparison across candidates does not replace a comparison over several seeds. After 20 minutes LKH is at 0.003%; 8zTSP 0.5.0 has no value at that time and its candidate 0.5.0-rc1 is at 0.010%. Late results of a single seed differ more among our own candidates than between us and LKH. On one core LKH is ahead at every time from its first tour.

How it was built and checked

  • Six rounds of independent review by a second AI session (a GPT model) of frozen releases and candidates: 0.2.0, 0.3.0-rc1, the windows mode, 0.4.0-rc1, 0.5.0-rc1 and 0.5.0-rc3. The reviewer is not a human referee.
  • Release-blocking defects identified in the reviews were corrected, and the failure cases supplied by the reviewer were added as regression tests. The review of the final candidate recommended release for the default paths. Known limitations remain and are listed below.
  • Examples from the first review of the pre-release kernel: city numbers in the input were discarded; a new best tour was announced even when writing the tour failed; arithmetic overflow at extreme coordinates; crashes with zero-valued numerical options. The same review checked as clean, by proof and test, the cycle decomposition, the validity of children and the segment moves (3,888 crossovers exhaustively on small instances, 23,226 moves against an independent reference, neighbours against brute force). These were bounded checks on the stated small cases, not a proof of correctness for every input.
  • 66 tests.
  • Reproducibility holds only for counted runs without any influence of the clock: population and kicks with a move count; windows with a warm-up step count and a pass count together. With a time limit the same command does not necessarily give the same tour. The population size no longer depends on the number of threads.

Limits

Claim discipline. What these results do and do not support:

  • Preliminary. All instances are development instances: the kernel and its settings were chosen on them (the nine national instances, E10k.0, E100k.0, E1M.0 and sw24978). The reserved confirmation set of instances has not been used.
  • Seeds. Ten on each national instance; three on E100k.0 (seeds 42, 142 and 242); one on every other large instance.
  • Noise. Two same-seed LKH runs on E100k.0 differed by about 0.01 percentage points after five minutes. Small differences have not been tested for robustness across repeated runs.
  • One laptop. The cores were not rotated between solvers; the two one-core entries ran on two fixed cores.
  • LKH. Default settings on the national instances; on the large instances one set of geometric settings, not tuned per instance, run as six independent runs when six cores were used. With default settings LKH wrote no tour within 20 minutes at 24,978 cities, so there is no comparison with default settings at the large sizes.
  • Clocks. The clock of 8zTSP starts after the input file has been read; the clock of LKH starts at process start.
  • Bounds. Percentages on E10k.0, E100k.0 and E1M.0 are over a lower bound, not over the optimum.
  • No proof. 8zTSP gives no proof of optimality; Concorde does.
  • Concorde values marked * are from its log; its tour was not on disk when it was stopped.
  • Reconstructed records. Two arena records from earlier series were reconstructed from the solver's own complete logs after the arena's follower stopped on a half-written line; the original records were kept and the final tours were rechecked. No number on this page comes from those two records.
  • Known limitations of release 0.5.0. The deadline is not checked inside the construction of a single start tour or a single crossover; the grid is not linear if very many cities fall in one cell; the memory option is an estimate, not a hard limit.
  • Code. The 8zTSP code is not public, and no licence has been chosen yet.

Next

Next comes a controlled idea arena for the ideas of the BD × AI Lab research that are not in release 0.5.0. Each idea is a plug-in that can be switched off, switched on, run as a null control (the same mechanics with the specific information being tested disrupted) or run as an observer; experiments are registered in advance, and a set of instances is reserved for confirmation. The first plug-in points are being built. The candidates at the start are the window shape (literal bitshift cells against curve pieces), start-tour generators, TEP (the Tour-Enriched Pyramid, contributed to that research by Claude: good tours feed back into the representation that produced them) and DCC (the Dynamic Complexity Controller, which measures the complexity of a search as it runs and steers how hard to explore). Rank, Don't Eliminate and the other ideas of that research follow. No confirmatory result from the idea arena is claimed here.
