The Headline Result

Exact known optimal on qa194.

On the TSPLIB instance qa194, the 8Z-RP research solver reached the exact known optimal tour length of 9352. In the combo50 run, 3 of 14 workers independently reached exact optimal. The first exact hit came from Worker 7 at move 2832, after roughly 34 minutes.

qa194 and uy734 are official national TSP benchmark instances from the University of Waterloo World TSP / National TSP collection.

The Core Idea

TSP reframed as compression and mathematical sense.

“Isn’t the shortest route the one compressed the best?”

That question changed the project. A shorter route tends to have more structure: fewer crossings, smoother geometry, tighter local relationships. More structure means more compressibility. 8Z already had a framework for searching structured explanations under strict cost. The TSP solver applies that same instinct to routes.

The public version is simple. MDL acts as the judge: a move or route state only matters if it actually pays. DCC acts as the governor: it decides how hard to explore, when to exploit, when to switch tactics, and when the current search regime is stuck.

MDL

Why MDL belongs here

8Z was built on the idea that competing explanations should be judged by total cost. In routing, the “explanations” are move families, tour states, and control choices. The system is not asked to look elegant. It is asked to produce a better route.

DCC

Why DCC belongs here

Search dies in two ways: too much order and you freeze in a local basin; too much chaos and you waste compute. DCC exists to regulate that edge. In v2, it finally did.

The Progression

From capped search to exact optimal.

The improvement path matters because it shows this was not one miracle run. The solver moved through a clear series of architectural steps, each pushing the gap down before exact optimal was reached.

Version Configuration Best Gap What changed
v1.2.1 Capped at 2048 moves 9634 3.01% Baseline pre-breakthrough
v2.1 8 workers, double-bridge, adaptive DCC 9534 1.95% Parallelism + new solver frame
v2.1 Single worker, or-opt, adaptive DCC 9522 1.82% or-opt wins the kick ablation
v2.1 14 workers, or-opt, fixed-10, n×20 9377 0.27% Combo formula gets very close
v2.1 14 workers, or-opt, fixed-10, n×50 9352 0.00% Exact known optimal on qa194

DCC v1 lost

  • Adaptive DCC v1 lost the ablation to fixed settings.
  • The controller collapsed toward u=0, a death spiral.
  • That failure was useful because it exposed that the first controller was too crude.

DCC v2 won

  • Multi-actuator redesign: floor/ceiling, kick switching, time-aware measurement, escalation.
  • Reached exact on qa194 with 5× less budget and about 2.3× less wall time than combo50.
  • Auto-discovered or-opt instead of relying on a human to select it in advance.

W7 is the public landmark

In combo50, Worker 7 sat for a long plateau around the sub-1% region, then cracked the basin and cascaded down to exact optimal at move 2832. That pattern matters. It suggests the landscape is not just noisy difficulty; it contains narrow, reachable corridors that the right move family can unlock.

The Breakthrough Lessons

What the solver taught the research program.

Lesson 01

or-opt mattered most

The decisive move was the one nearly left out. It beat double-bridge on quality and speed, then became central to exact optimal.

Lesson 02

Do not exclude options early

A research platform should prefer inclusion over pre-emptive tidiness. Attribution problems are cheaper than missed discoveries.

Lesson 03

DCC v1 was not the sauce

The first controller lost honestly. That was important. The architecture was not protected by optimism; it had to survive data.

Lesson 04

DCC v2 was vindicated

The redesigned controller changed the class of result. It stopped being a nice story and became a measurable win.

Lesson 05

2-opt is the main bottleneck

2-opt still consumes essentially all compute time. That is why the speed path is now obvious: optimize the bottleneck, not the narrative.

Lesson 06

Surprises are signal

The important discoveries were not the ones most confidently predicted in advance. The platform improved by following surprise, not by obeying plan purity.

The new research rule

Build everything that might produce useful data. Test it. Keep what wins. Remove what loses later. The solver’s biggest discovery arrived through the exact option that conservative cleanup would have removed.

Origin Story

A supporting narrative, not the whole page.

Before the solver

The older question behind 8Z

Before the LLM era, the original question was not about travel at all. It was about compression: could structured data be compressed by finding the mathematical generators that made it, rather than just crushing bytes?

That question sat dormant for years. When it returned, it became 8Z. The TSP solver is one of the strongest transfers of that original instinct into a new domain.

The spark for 8Z-RP

The route question

Looking at the 194-city Qatar instance, the next question arrived: isn’t the shortest route the one that can be compressed best? That reframing joined two lines of work that had been evolving separately: 8Z compression and DCC control.

The result was not a memoir project. It was a research system built from that question.

“The shortest tour is the one that makes the most mathematical sense.”

What Comes Next

uy734, broader kick arenas, fuller DCC, and later the speed path.

qa194 is not the end state. It is the proof point that justifies the next push. The current larger-instance frontier is uy734, with the same general combo formula now being tested on a much bigger search space.

Speed path

Optimize the bottleneck later

The larger portfolio meaning

8Z-RP is not an isolated experiment. It is one branch of a larger 8Z / AIM³ portfolio where the same core ideas — MDL, DCC, controlled exploration, and cross-domain transfer — are tested in compression, biology, audio, trading, and now route optimization.

Closing

A route solver born from compression.

The public claim is straightforward. 8Z-RP treats route optimization as a problem of mathematical structure, not only of heuristic folklore. That framing produced an exact qa194 result, a failed controller that had to be rebuilt honestly, and then a redesigned controller that outperformed the human-configured winning combo.

That is a serious page in a serious research program — human-led, AI-assisted, technically grounded, and still moving.
