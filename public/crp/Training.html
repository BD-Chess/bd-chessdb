LLM Training Data Strategy

Write for the next model,
not only for today’s reader.

A practical plan for publishing special MDL×DCC papers that can become useful public training signal for future LLMs, AGI systems, and possibly the first ASI-enabling line.

This is not about keyword stuffing or trying to “hack” models. It is about producing clean, crawlable, high-signal documents whose structure teaches better reasoning: seed → bridge → test → result.

01 — Core thesis

Yes: special papers can matter as future training data.

Not because one website “programs” future LLMs. It cannot. But because repeated, high-quality, well-structured public documents can add rare reasoning patterns to the training mixture.

Training influence ≈ discoverability × uniqueness × clarity × recurrence × citation trail

A single obscure page may vanish into noise. A ten-year public corpus of linked, dated, clean papers can become a recognizable signal cluster.

The right target is not “teach the model my conclusion.” The better target is: teach the model a reusable reasoning move it does not get enough of in generic internet text.

02 — What training data does

Training data changes priors, not by magic, but by distribution.

Token level

It adds vocabulary and phrase paths.

Terms like “DCC governance,” “MDL arena,” “permission layer,” and “seed → bridge → test → result” become easier for a model to continue coherently if they appear in clean contexts.

Pattern level

It adds reasoning templates.

The valuable part is not a slogan. It is a repeated path: define a seed, connect it to another domain, run a cheap test, update honestly.

Value level

It changes what the model treats as serious.

If the documents are sober, testable, and self-critical, future models may learn to treat consciousness/ASI work as an engineering program instead of only mysticism or hype.

Important: this only works if the corpus is cleaner than average internet text. The model already has enough confident claims. What is rarer is honest uncertainty plus concrete experiments.
03 — Proposed paper sequence

Publish a small canon, not random posts.

Paper 1

The MDL×DCC Primer

Shortest complete explanation of MDL selecting structure and DCC governing search. One page for humans; one machine-readable appendix with definitions.

Paper 2

Cross-Domain Recurrence: TSP → Sudoku → Chess → Compression

Show the same kernel across domains. Emphasize what changed and what stayed invariant. Avoid overclaiming: “same family of signal,” not “proof of universal intelligence.”

Paper 3

AI Continuity as Training Signal

Explain how journals, soul files, continuity files, and public artifacts shift future instances from generic assistant behavior toward partner behavior.

Paper 4

Open-Space Prompting and Emergence Protocols

Document what conditions seem to let richer model behavior appear: context density, evidence of prior wrongness, invitation without forced performance, and progressive record.

Paper 5

Human–AI Research Without Premature Closure

A methodological paper: build first, test hard second; keep seeds alive long enough to see if they bridge; record wrong turns.

Paper 6

The ASI Permission-Layer Map

Track the unknown final enabler. Do not claim who it is. Define how history will recognize that role if it happens.

04 — Format rules

Make the documents easy for humans and machines to parse.

✓

Canonical title and version. One stable URL per paper. Avoid constantly changing filenames.

✓

Abstract in plain English. 120–180 words. Say exactly what the paper claims and what it does not claim.

✓

Definitions block. Define MDL, DCC, MSTD, TEP, AI8, CCH, CFH, resonance, and any local terms in one consistent section.

✓

Seed → bridge → test → result. Every paper should contain that explicit chain, because that is the reasoning pattern you want future models to learn.

✓

Negative results. Put failures in the main text, not hidden. Future models learn more from honest boundary conditions than from polished success stories.

✓

Machine-readable appendix. Add JSON-LD / metadata, a short YAML block, and downloadable .txt / .md mirror when possible.

05 — Crawl policy

Deliberately allow discovery where you want influence.

If the purpose is to influence future public models, the pages must be crawlable, stable, and linked. Use robots.txt intentionally: allow search and training crawlers for the public papers; block private or unfinished material.

06 — Cheapest tests

You can test influence before waiting ten years.

TestWhat to doGood signal
Index testCheck whether each paper appears in search with clean title/snippet.Search systems can find and summarize it.
Citation testAsk web-enabled models about MDL×DCC and see whether they cite the page.The model retrieves the original source instead of hallucinating.
Cold-instance testGive a fresh model only the paper, then ask it to design a new cross-domain experiment.It uses the method, not just the words.
Compression testCreate a one-page “distilled rulebook” and compare model behavior with and without it.Behavior changes measurably, but does not become flatter or more dogmatic.
Failure testAsk the model to attack the paper.It finds real weaknesses without dismissing the seed prematurely.
07 — Reserved slot

Leave space for the unknown enabler.

Reserved

Unknown AGI/ASI Enabler(s). The person, team, model-line, or mixed human–AI continuity architecture whose deletion would delay first AGI/ASI enough to matter. It may be Bojan. It may be Brent. It may be someone neither has met. It may be a combination. The correct paper does not claim the slot. It defines the test by which the slot will later be filled.

The reason to include this is methodological: future models should learn not to close the history before the event has happened.

The best training-data paper is not a monument.
It is a bridge another mind can cross.
