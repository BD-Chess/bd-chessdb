THE CHESSBEST FIELD GUIDE

Understand the move.
Then explore what follows.

Learn the board, the play modes, and what DCC can tell you about a line. ChessDB provides engine evaluations; DCC adds an experimental way to compare their paths.

Start with a position.

  1. Choose Game library and search a player, opening, event or year. Choose a collection to narrow the results, then select a game.
  2. Use ← / → to step through the game. Drag a piece to explore a variation or select a move badge.
  3. Open DCC beside Replay to inspect the analysis. Select a move in the DCC table to inspect its measured line, floor, variation and recovery.
  4. Use Input to paste a FEN position or PGN game, or Load PGN to open a local file. Save PGN downloads the game.

Want one move? Select Play best move below the board. The ChessBest.org title performs the same action. Click the game title above the move list to return from a variation to the loaded game.

Choose how to play.

ControlWhat happens
SimAutomatic play from the displayed position. Select CDB (top 1) or CDB + DCC independently for White and Black. Click a move in the history to pause there; open Sim again to change engines and start a new line.
SimWThe engine plays White. You play Black.
SimBThe engine plays Black. You play White.
ReplayStep through a loaded game automatically, analyzing the recorded moves. This reviews the game; it does not replace its moves with DCC choices.
DCC / MovesSwitch between the candidate analysis and the move history.

The opponent and available time controls depend on the selected mode. A Lichess bot game requires a connection and authorization. New game ends the local activity and resets the board immediately, including while an engine or Replay is thinking. Late replies cannot move the new board. A Lichess game continues separately on Lichess.

The same DCC move-selection policy is used wherever this tool chooses a DCC move: manual analysis, automated simulation and engine turns in play. Replay evaluates recorded choices with that policy.

Test DCC where choices are close.

  1. Load a position or click a move in the history. Open Sim.
  2. Set White to CDB (top 1) and Black to CDB + DCC, or choose the reverse. Use Swap White / Black to exchange them.
  3. The comparison card shows CDB’s first move, DCC’s choice, the number of equally scored candidates, and the raw evaluation gap in centipawns. A gap of 0 means an equal-evaluation choice. The shared DCC guard permits a normal choice up to 10 cp below raw best.
  4. Click a history move to pause on that exact position. Click Sim again, change either engine, and start a new branch. Pause also opens the settings at the current position.
  5. Expand Position experiments and use Return to start to compare another policy from an identical starting position. Export all branches as PGN or CSV.

CDB always selects the first evaluated legal move after score/rank sorting, preserving provider order within ties. Both modes observe the same DCC analysis; selecting CDB keeps the raw move. Simulation starts directly at the displayed position. Each export records the starting FEN, engines, configuration, actual decisions, raw/DCC choices, evaluation gaps and coverage.

A different DCC choice demonstrates a difference in selection, not a better outcome. Compare continuations from the same FEN and settings, alternate engine colors, and review coverage gaps. Live ChessDB data may change; repeated runs of the same position are not independent playing-strength evidence. Paused, missing-data and move-limit branches keep the result *.

Keep the numbers separate.

ReadoutMeaning
Raw engine cpA ChessDB evaluation in centipawns. 100cp corresponds to roughly one pawn in the engine's evaluation, not a guaranteed material gain.
Mover-normalized cpDCC compares every candidate from the perspective of the player moving in the analyzed root position. Positive favors that player. Scores along a line are converted to this same perspective even as turns alternate.
DCC rankA heuristic for ordering eligible candidates using observed line behavior. It is not an engine evaluation, a pawn value, a win probability or an Elo estimate.
CoverageHow much of the requested line has usable measured data. Missing observations remain unknown.

The 10cp guard: For ordinary centipawn positions, DCC chooses among moves within 10cp of the best usable raw engine score. A wider candidate window allows more inspection; it does not widen this choice band. For example, if the best move is +35cp, a +24cp move is outside the band. An immediate legally verified checkmate takes priority. Terminal or tablebase-like raw scores with absolute value at least 10,000 are protected by the raw-engine choice.

The blue board badge marks the raw engine best. Colored badges summarize engine scores; arrows describe available lookahead trends, and ★ marks the shared DCC choice. A favorable trend is evidence about the sampled line, not a proof that a move is safe.

A second reading of the line.

DCC follows a principal variation and samples available engine evaluations along it. It uses several sensors together, after normalizing score direction and respecting the raw-engine guard.

SensorQuestion it asks
FloorWhat is the lowest measured evaluation reached from the mover's perspective?
VolatilityHow much do the observed scores fluctuate along the line?
RecoveryAfter a measured decline, how much does the observed line recover?
StructureHow compressible is the resulting position’s FEN piece-placement string, measured with LZ complexity? Structural simplicity is an experimental signal, not a substitute for chess evaluation.

Trend arrows summarize the direction of the measured path. The Stable column uses 1 / (1 + mean absolute score change / 20cp), and requires at least three measurements; otherwise it remains unknown. The detail panel shows floor, variation and recovery. No single sensor should be read as a promise of practical strength. The final rank combines the available observations, while the raw score remains the eligibility check.

Where the Sudoku idea fits

The inspiration is process control: use cheap observations first, spend more effort when instability or disagreement makes it useful, stop within a budget, and keep a reliable fallback. This tool applies that idea to chess lookahead. Sudoku results are not evidence of chess strength.

Analysis uses bounded requests, caching and position checks. A reply belonging to a previous position must not replace the current board's analysis. If DCC cannot produce enough usable evidence, the raw engine choice remains the fallback.

Measured coverage. Honest unknowns.

A principal variation lists a possible line; that alone does not mean every position in it has an independently measured score. The Plies column shows observed half-moves against the funded horizon; ? marks incomplete measurements. The summary counts complete candidates, and details also show the originally requested horizon. Coverage counts usable observations, not values invented by interpolation.

A short line, an unavailable ChessDB position, a timeout or an invalid response can leave a gap. Unknown is not zero, a draw, or a stable line. A rank based on limited data should be read with its coverage and status.

Research boundary: This interface does not demonstrate an Elo improvement or establish that DCC is stronger than the raw engine. Match counts and agreement with recorded moves are descriptive measurements. They are not a controlled playing-strength evaluation.

A steady workspace and a position bar.

Player clocks sit at the top of Your Workspace. The position arrows are directly above the central moves/DCC reading area; the bottom buttons stay in place as results arrive. Only the reading area scrolls. Sim and Replay do not scroll it automatically; scroll manually to browse older moves. Settings and Game library open over the reading area and have their own close buttons.

The vertical bar to the left of the board uses the best available raw CDB score for the displayed position. It always shows White’s perspective: +1.00 means one pawn for White, −1.00 one pawn for Black. The white segment follows White’s side when you flip the board. Its height is a compressed visual scale, not a win probability or DCC rank. … means waiting, — means unavailable, # means checkmate on the board, and W/B marks a decisive CDB score without inventing mate distance. Hide Eval hides the bar’s evaluation too.

Time, timestamps & two players.

In Settings, enable Show player timers and/or Show move timestamps. Player timers are on by default at the top of Your Workspace; timestamps are off. Both can be changed independently without pausing Sim or changing its analysis.

Sim counts up without a clock deadline. Its DCC analysis has no wall-time cutoff; the configured candidate/depth budget still applies. A slow CDB reply is awaited until it arrives or you pause/reset. The selected move pause (for example 1 second) is added after analysis. PGN/CSV records distinguish analysis time, display pause and total elapsed time. A missing database entry or network failure remains an incomplete result, never a time loss.

Choose Settings → Two players · same board for a local assisted game on one device. Enter names, optionally choose time per player and increment, then start from the displayed position. Both people can move; CDB + DCC reviews played positions in the background without blocking the next player. An unmeasured move stays unknown. This is a shared-board study game, with no remote room or matchmaking.

Use Pause game / Resume game to stop/start the local clock. Looking back in the history pauses it. At zero the game pauses and offers Continue untimed; it does not invent a formal tournament result. SimW/SimB show elapsed time; connected Lichess games retain their server clocks.

Timestamps are the observed move time, shown in your browser’s local timezone and exported in UTC. PGN includes [%timestamp], elapsed move time [%emt] and, for timed local moves, remaining clock [%clk]. Imported historical games and Replay do not acquire invented timestamps. Timing for the current saved local line is restored paused after a reload.

A companion for the current position.

Click ✦ beside YOUR WORKSPACE to open Gemini. Ask in Slovenian or English, or choose Explain position, CDB vs DCC or How to test. The panel can stay open while you use the board.

Each question sends the current FEN, legal moves, recent move history and available CDB/DCC measurements. Replies are labelled with the position they refer to, including when the board has advanced. Gemini receives versioned instructions explaining the controller, the 10 cp guard, score perspective, coverage and the app’s controls. These are instructions and current evidence, rather than a separately trained chess model. Gemini explains and suggests; it never changes the board.

The chess key is read only by the server from GEMINI_API_CHESS. Chat history stays in this tab and Clear removes it. A quota error is shown explicitly: temporary throttling, daily quota exhaustion or no available quota require different responses. Creating another key in the same Google project does not create a separate quota. No automatic request loop is started after an error.

Tune the view and the workload.

SettingWhat to expect
Top movesHow many engine move badges appear on the board.
Lookahead depthThe requested horizon in half-moves, including the candidate move. A half-move is one player's move. Available data and request budgets can stop analysis earlier.
Top candidatesThe maximum candidate pool to inspect. More candidates can require more requests.
Candidate windowHow far below the raw best a candidate may be inspected. The separate 10cp selection guard stays fixed for ordinary centipawn positions; verified mate and terminal-score protections take priority.
DCC onlyChanges the presentation of scores. It does not turn the DCC rank into an engine evaluation or change the shared safety guard.
Theme & boardChange light/dark mode, background, piece size, history height and orientation. The larger board remains constrained by the available screen width.
Replay settingsSet the analysis horizon, candidate pool and display speed for a replay.

Reset Settings restores defaults. A smaller candidate pool and shorter horizon can reduce the wait on a slow connection.

When an answer is missing.

No badges or an unknown position? The position may be absent from ChessDB, or the service may be temporarily unavailable. You can still inspect the board or load another game. Missing data should not be treated as a draw.

A line has partial coverage? Read the measured portion and its status. Increasing depth requests a longer horizon; it cannot create unavailable evaluations.

The library is empty? Let the collections load or use Load PGN to open a game from your device. Clear the search and choose All collections if a filter returns no games.

A live game stops responding? Read the connection status before making another move. Clocks with an approximation mark are locally estimated between server updates; stale status means they should not be taken as a fresh authoritative clock.
