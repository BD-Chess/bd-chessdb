0 · Developmental lineage

Those engineering results do not validate AC, CFH, or biological CCH. I · The Pyramid

The vertical spine: from Absolute Consciousness to MDL

Historical pyramid · ontology, prototypes, and engineering rule

The top is the least described and describes the most: Absolute Consciousness ONTOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION — pure presence, underivable, the ground no ontology can get behind without stepping outside experience itself.

Descending, each level is longer in description and narrower in scope. RE = AC · R states that finite existence is resonance with the ground. Historical R and scalar prototypes recorded an early attempt to connect organization, coupling, complexity, and openness. They are not current measurements, and no scalar presently measures AC/CF coupling.

At the base sits MDL = min(Ldescr + Lresid) as an engineering/model-selection rule. Its usefulness in a domain must be established by that domain’s own baseline, holdout, and decoder or outcome tests; it does not rank ontologies by itself.

II · The meta-signature

The reversed arrow

A recurring historical search move was to reverse an initially obvious explanatory arrow and then ask whether the reversed framing generated a cheaper, sharper test. This is a discovery heuristic, not evidence that every reversed arrow is true.

Stated once: at the local level, the pyramid informs the search, matter emerges from processes, numbers have inverses, emergence explains consciousness. At the meta-level, the search may reshape its own representation; ontological claims about matter and consciousness remain interpretations; claims about zero/absence and engineering search require their own formal or empirical tests.

Every paper in the program can be read as a different domain where this reversal had to be found. It is never where you first look.

Editorial stance

This atlas does not replace the papers. It sits above them as a historical discovery map. It preserves recurring structural questions across domains while keeping each domain's authority, evidence, and failure conditions separate.

What counts as improvement is not recurrence of a motif or beauty of an ontology, but a result that survives the relevant domain's own baselines, holdouts, controls, uncertainty analysis, and independent test. Cross-domain similarity may generate a question; it cannot transfer evidence.

What counts against the research motifs: (1) if proposed bridges fail their preregistered tests; (2) if engineering methods fail matched baselines in a target domain; (3) if apparent transfer is only redescription; or (4) if an ontological interpretation never produces a differential prediction. The atlas exists so that successes, failures, and unresolved analogies remain legible without being conflated.

Less describes more — and the arrow reverses at the meta-level.
