BD Hedge Separation PiX Strategy

The current explanation of the tested engine: begin with an equal long+short hedge, re-anchor a deeply losing side, use a bounded mega-rescue to move its weighted entry, harvest the rescued excess, and retain a residual hedge. This page contains only the completed v1.3c evidence and the short v1.4 outlook.

Real BTC/ETH/SOL 1m replay, 1 January–14 June 2026, virtual fills only. No live-order path.

What the v1.3c strategy actually was

Initial state
Equal hedge

Open LONG and SHORT at the same notional.

Loss response
Re-anchor

Close and reopen the deeply losing side when its trigger and trend gate pass.

Recovery engine
Mega rescue

Increase the re-anchored losing side to a bounded multiple of the original base.

Profit realization
Excess close

When the rescued side recovers beyond its weighted entry, close the excess and keep a residual.

Not tested in v1.3c: adding to the profitable side, staged winner exits, winner trailing, or force-closing the winner when mega rescue fires. Older manual text implying those behaviors has been removed.

Open both sides. The base size is expressed as fixed notional derived from base_sol × $150, then converted to asset quantity at the first close.
Identify the losing side. Re-anchor is eligible when its ROE crosses the configured negative trigger, the opposite side is positive, and the trend gate passes.
Close and reopen the loser. Reopen after the configured number of completed 1m bars and at the configured base multiple.
Allow mega rescue after re-anchor. If the same side again crosses the trigger, add until the configured total multiple is reached, capped by max_total_side_multiplier.
Harvest the rescue. When that rescued side moves the configured percentage beyond its weighted average in the profitable direction, close everything above the residual keep multiple.
Respect event caps. Each policy limits re-anchor and mega-rescue counts; the ending residual hedge remains open unless the replay stop ends the path.
ROE approximation
price move % ≈ |ROE %| ÷ leverage

How profit taking worked

What happened

  • The enlarged formerly losing side recovered past its weighted average entry.
  • The engine closed the excess quantity in one event.
  • A configured 1×, 2×, 3× or 5× base residual remained.
  • Realized rescue profit and ending unrealized hedge PnL were both counted in final MTM.

What did not happen

  • No adding to the already profitable side.
  • No +200%/+400% winner ladder.
  • No multi-stage winner scale-out.
  • No trailing winner stop.
  • No execution of the stored close_winner_on_mega flag.

Wallet size is an output, not a strategy gate

v1.3c used a fixed virtual $500 wallet. That baseline is useful for comparison, but it must not decide whether a strategy exists. A trader may intentionally allocate $100, $1,000, $10,000 or much more to different risk lanes.

v1.4 must report the minimum tested wallet for full-path survival, probability of total loss, PnL by horizon and capital efficiency—while retaining even strategies that fail a chosen wallet.

Known limits of the current evidence

Engine semantics

  • reanchor_target_separation_roe was named and stored but never checked in the replay.
  • mega_rescue_close_winner_on_trigger was stored but never executed.
  • Winner-side pyramiding and trailing were absent.
  • The fixed-$500 drawdown stop truncated some high-PnL paths.

Validation scope

  • One historical Jan–Jun 2026 interval.
  • Virtual fills, not exchange execution.
  • Cross-margin and liquidation mechanics remain simplified.
  • 6/6 is a profile-score win against controls, not guaranteed raw-PnL dominance.
  • Zero-cost is a diagnostic scenario, not a universal live-cost assumption.

Short v1.4 outlook

Capital truth

Full-path replay, wallet tiers, minimum tested wallet, no strategy deletion by wallet size.

Temporal truth

Rolling starts and ends, forced-close accounting, walk-forward validation and short-horizon blast statistics.

Strategy completeness

Active separation, winner-side adds, staged exits, trailing, phenotype deduplication and stronger cost/liquidation stress.
