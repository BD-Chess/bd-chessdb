What this page is
A fan-made, cross-universe power tier list covering DC, Marvel, and Image Comics. Rankings reflect peak canonical power — "forms" and amplified states count. Tiers are broad buckets; ordering within a tier is a best-effort synthesis, not canonical gospel.

New additions vs. the source pages are marked with a NEW badge and include: Morpheus/Dream (Endless), Spawn (God-Level), Adam Warlock, Swamp Thing, Doomsday, Magneto (Full Power), Captain Universe, Black Adam (Base), and John Constantine.

Omnipotent

#1 The One-Above-All Marvel
Supreme Being
+

Marvel's ultimate "author-level" creator figure. In most depictions it stands outside the entire Marvel cosmology: multiverse, abstracts, time, and even the rules that bind beings like Eternity or the Living Tribunal. It rarely acts directly; when it does, it's usually through quiet interventions, avatars, or moments of moral/creative revelation. For crossover storytelling, TOAA works best as the unreachable boundary condition: it can't be "beaten," only reflected, interpreted, or invoked as a final constraint on what reality is allowed to become.

#2 The Presence DC
Supreme Being
+
DC's supreme creator principle — often treated as the top of the hierarchy that includes the Source, the Spectre-force, and the major metaphysical powers of the DC cosmology. Depending on era and imprint (DC/Vertigo), "The Presence" ranges from a personal God-like being to a more abstract, omnipresent law of existence. It typically acts through agencies (Spectre, angels, prophets) rather than direct combat. The Presence is a narrative "gravity": it defines ultimate stakes — meaning, responsibility — rather than serving as a punchable antagonist.
#3 Elaine Belloc DC/Vertigo
Human-Divine Hybrid
+
A key figure from Vertigo's Lucifer: a human girl with angelic lineage who grows into something far larger — eventually inheriting divine authority over all of creation. Elaine's power is less about flashy feats and more about the terrifying burden of authorship: shaping creation while staying compassionate and sane. Her arc is built for mentorship/contrast scenes: a being who can do anything, but must still choose what is right. Pairing Elaine with other characters allows deep "ethics of omnipotence" conflicts without turning the story into a fight.
#4 Mother of Existence Image
Creator / Supreme Being (Spawn)
+

Also known in Spawn lore as "Man of Miracles," the Mother of Existence is the creator-figure behind the Spawn cosmology: the source-level being from which that universe's fundamental order proceeds. Rather than acting like a battlefield god, the Mother is an architect and judge — appearing in whatever form best communicates with the observer, intervening rarely and usually through reality-level choices rather than force. She is commonly described as the parent of both the 'God' and 'Satan' figures of the setting, placing her one rung above the Heaven/Hell conflict that most Spawn stories revolve around.

#5 The Great Evil Beast DC
Primordial Darkness Entity
+

In DC's metaphysical horror tradition (Swamp Thing), the Great Evil Beast is the sentient 'soul of the Darkness' — a primordial expression of evil that stands as the shadow-counterpart to divine light. It's framed as what becomes distinct when creation separates light from darkness: the Beast as the concentrated, self-aware abyss that retreats, waits, and periodically pressures creation through corruption, possession, and apocalyptic openings. Stories treat it less as a character with dialogue and more as a cosmic condition made conscious — an origin-point for infernal influence.

#6 The Great Darkness DC
Primordial Cosmic Force
+

DC's archetypal primordial darkness: an ancient foundational power that exists at the scale of 'creation itself' rather than any one universe. In modern continuity it's frequently described as the omniversal source of darkness — dormant for ages after the first light of creation, then stirring through avatars, aspects, and emissaries. It isn't just 'a villain': it functions as a cosmological counterweight to the Presence/Source-aligned light, and narrative arcs use it to test whether reality can withstand its own opposite without collapsing into absolute outcome.

Cosmic Gods
▾
#7 Lucifer Morningstar DC
Fallen Angel
+
Vertigo/DC Lucifer is a top-tier metaphysical agent: brilliant, defiant, and operating on the level of creation's architecture rather than city-scale heroics. He rarely "wins" by raw power alone; he wins by seeing the hidden structure of choices, contracts, and dependencies. His signature is freedom — sometimes noble, sometimes terrifying. Crucially, Lucifer holds the demiurgic power he was born with: the ability to create and sustain entire universes. The conflict isn't beam-vs-beam; it's whether absolute freedom is compatible with responsibility. His most interesting stories end with mutual respect and two philosophies discovering where their axioms break.
#8 Michael Demiurgos DC
Archangel / Co-Creator
+
From DC/Vertigo's Lucifer: the archangel most closely linked to raw creative power — the ability to generate new realities through the demiurgic fire. Michael is not merely strong; he is foundational, a key pillar in the architecture of creation. Without Michael's power, Lucifer's will could not shape existence. His stories revolve around duty, loyalty, and the moral cost of being an engine for worlds. The tension: preservation of cosmic order even when it hurts individuals — protection through structure vs protection through understanding.
#9 The Living Tribunal Marvel
Multiversal Abstract Judge
+
Marvel's multiversal judge: an abstract cosmic entity tasked with maintaining balance across the entire multiverse. The Tribunal is typically above all pantheons and abstracts, acting with cold legalistic logic. It rarely cares who is "hero" or "villain" — only whether an action destabilizes the structure of reality itself. Its three-faced form represents equity, vengeance, and necessity simultaneously. A strong story uses the Tribunal as a fair but unforgiving reviewer: compassion is permitted, but only if it preserves systemic stability.
#10 Beyonder (Pre-Retcon) Marvel
Beyond-Reality Entity
+
The pre-retcon Beyonder is Marvel's famous "beyond everything" powerhouse from the original Secret Wars era — portrayed as vastly greater than the known multiverse and its abstract entities. He is curiosity plus infinite leverage: a childlike intelligence with cosmic-scale consequences. That combination creates the real threat: not malice, but naïve experimentation with existence as a toy. His strongest play is education and containment-by-consent: persuading the Beyonder to adopt ethical rules, to value other wills, and to explore without collateral reality collapse.
#11 The Spectre (Fully Unleashed) DC
Divine Spirit (Human Host)
+
The Spectre is the embodiment of divine wrath in DC: a near-absolute instrument of judgment that typically requires a human host to act in the world. "Fully unleashed" versions push toward the top of the DC power ladder — reality-warping, soul-level punishment, cosmic-scale enforcement. The catch is mission and control: the Spectre-force can be bound by theology, by the host's psychology, and by the moral ambiguity of "vengeance vs justice." His best leverage is moral reframing: redirecting judgment into restoration instead of annihilation.
#12 Thanos (Infinity Gauntlet) Marvel
Titan (with Infinity Gauntlet)
+
Thanos with the complete Infinity Stones is the classic Marvel scenario where strategy meets absolute leverage: reality, time, mind, soul, space, and power in a single hand. The point isn't that Thanos can blow things up — it's that he can define what counts as true. His greatest danger is ideological certainty: a single will deciding the value of all lives. The path through is not combat but confronting the loneliness beneath "rational sacrifice" — creating a face-saving off-ramp where he can relinquish control without humiliation. The best ending isn't "kill Thanos" but "disarm the god-complex."
#13 Cosmic Armor Superman DC
Conceptual / Thought Robot
+
The "Thought Robot" (Cosmic Armor Superman) is DC's conceptual Superman: a meta-defense mechanism built to counter threats to the idea of Superman itself. It's as much philosophy as character — an embodiment of narrative resilience and adaptive opposition. Because it operates on story-logic, it can scale to match the threat. That makes it a fascinating foil: both can be solutions to conflict — one is an auto-immune response, the other a conscious reconciliation process. The tension: protection by force vs protection by understanding.
#14 Doctor Manhattan DC
Transcended Human (Quantum)
+
A quantum-consciousness superbeing from Watchmen who experiences time nonlinearly and manipulates matter/energy at a fundamental level. Manhattan's danger is emotional detachment: when you see all time at once, ordinary human reasons feel trivial. In DC crossovers (Doomsday Clock), he becomes a probe testing whether "meaning" is robust or just a story we tell ourselves. The key move is relational grounding: reintroducing stakes valid even under block-universe perception — care, responsibility, the lived reality of others. The conflict is philosophical: can omniscience coexist with love?
#15 Molecule Man Marvel
Human Mutate (Multiversal Linchpin)
+
Marvel's ultimate "small lever, huge effect" reality manipulator: a seemingly ordinary man whose control over molecules becomes multiversal-scale in key storylines. Molecule Man often embodies the theme that godlike power can coexist with fragile self-worth, fear, and manipulation by bigger players. In Hickman's Secret Wars era, he becomes a structural pillar of the multiverse's fate — a weapon secretly planted in every universe. Rather than fighting him, the answer addresses the human inside the weapon: helping him choose responsibility, heal coercion, and use power to restore rather than to retaliate.
#16 Nemesis Marvel
Pre-Multiversal Cosmic Entity
+

Marvel's Nemesis is presented as a pre-multiversal 'first self' — a solitary cosmic entity from the earliest reality. A recurring origin thread is that Nemesis shattered itself (often attributed to loneliness) into the Infinity Gems/Stones so that existence could diversify rather than remain a single closed identity. Some stories tie reconstitution of Nemesis to a missing "Ego" element, portraying reunification as inherently unstable — because reassembling the primordial whole threatens to override the differentiated multiverse that grew from the fragments.

#17 Mr. Mxyzptlk DC
5th Dimensional Imp
+
A classic DC 5th-dimensional imp: whimsical, reality-bending, and often effectively unstoppable by conventional force. Mxyzptlk's "limits" are usually rule-based (word games, self-imposed constraints, narrative bargains) rather than power-based — the danger is psychological. He turns meaning into prank currency, bending worlds to prove a point or cure boredom. His greatest weakness is boredom itself: he craves novelty so deeply that a sufficiently interesting challenge can redirect him. The solution space is meeting him on the level of play and insight, transforming the "game" into a cooperative experiment where the win condition is understanding.
#18 The Fulcrum Marvel
Cosmic Principle / Abstract
+

A high-order Marvel cosmic 'balance' concept: a mysterious organizing necessity that sits behind or above well-known cosmic factions like the Celestials and Watchers. Depending on the depiction, the Fulcrum reads either as a discreet being or as the personification of cosmic equilibrium — less a personality and more a stabilizing anchor that prevents the cosmology from tipping into permanent dominance by any single abstract. It appears rarely and ambiguously, best treated as a deliberately hazy apex concept: a way for Marvel to gesture at 'the principle that holds the stack together' without fully defining it.

#19 Empty Hand DC
Multiversal Meta-Entity (The Gentry)
+

Introduced in DC's The Multiversity, the Empty Hand is a reality-ending antagonist that commands the Gentry and attacks the multiverse as if it were disposable fiction. It is deliberately metafictional: a sentient 'hand' that crushes worlds, closes stories, and embodies the fear that the multiverse can be ended not by stronger heroes but by a higher-level act of cancellation. In-universe it functions like an agent of primordial darkness; structurally it functions like the reader/consumer's hand — making it one of DC's sharpest crossovers between cosmic threat and narrative commentary.

#20 Morpheus / Dream New DC/Vertigo
Endless Conceptual Being (Lord of Dreams)
+
From Gaiman's Sandman: Morpheus is the embodiment of Dream itself — one of the seven Endless siblings, predating gods and the current universe. His domain covers every dreaming mind across all of existence: the Dreaming is not a place but a cognitive layer woven into all sentient life. His power is not combat but narrative and conceptual: he can reshape the fabric of story and identity itself. Notably, he can be constrained, imprisoned, and wounded — his limits are relational and philosophical, not purely physical. He is also Death's sibling, grounding his place near the top of the hierarchy of cosmic being.

Reality Shapers

#21EternityMarvel
Embodiment of All Existence
+
Marvel's personification of the universe (or multiverse, depending on scope): the living totality of existence, often paired conceptually with Infinity. Eternity is not a "fighter" in the usual sense; it expresses cosmic order, continuity, and the right of being to persist. Challenging Eternity means challenging the premise that the universe should exist at all. Even the Living Tribunal defers to considerations about Eternity. Its greatest weakness is indifference to individuals — but that indifference is also its grandeur.
#22InfinityMarvel
Embodiment of Space
+
Marvel's abstract embodiment of space and the endless extent of reality — often paired with Eternity (time/existence) and contrasted with Oblivion (non-existence). Infinity represents boundlessness: expansion, possibility, and the "room" in which all stories unfold. She is the canvas upon which the universe is painted. Infinity and Eternity together represent the two dimensions of the universe's continuity — their cooperation or conflict reflects the fundamental health of reality itself.
#23Death (The Endless)DC/Vertigo
Endless Conceptual Being
+
From DC/Vertigo's Sandman mythos: Death is not a reaper-warrior but the personable, inevitable function that gives lives contour. She is gentle, absolute, and fundamentally un-bribeable: everything ends, and that ending is part of what makes love and choice real. When Death appears, it's rarely to fight — it's to clarify stakes, to puncture denial, to grant closure. At the end of time she will be the last of the Endless, switching off the lights. She is consistently ranked by fans as one of the best-written cosmic figures in all of comics.
#24OblivionMarvel
Embodiment of Non-Existence
+
A Marvel abstract entity representing non-existence/void — the "outside" pressure of nothingness against reality. Oblivion is not a schemer; it is a principle that can express itself through avatars, realms, or inevitabilities. It tends to surface when stories interrogate why existence persists at all. That makes it perfect for philosophical conflict: if everything ends, why build? The answer is not denial but meaning-making: existence justified through love, understanding, and the reduction of suffering — turning being into a stable choice rather than a temporary accident.
#25The SourceDC
Cosmic Energy Field / Wellspring
+
In DC cosmology, the Source is often described as the foundational wellspring behind existence — linked to the Source Wall and the deep structure of the multiverse. It can be treated as a cosmic "field" of creative law: the origin of gods, forces, and the rules that make stories possible. It does not behave like a villain; it behaves like physics with intention. The Source is an anchor: it can't be "solved," only aligned with. Entities that master its principles can reshape cosmos; those who ignore it find their grand schemes running out of foundation.
#26Anti-MonitorDC
Anti-Matter Cosmic Entity
+
DC's classic anti-matter apocalypse engine from Crisis on Infinite Earths: a cosmic entity that feeds on positive-matter universes and seeks to reduce the multiverse to a single anti-matter reality under his control. The Anti-Monitor threat is systemic — he weaponizes cosmology itself, turning the structure of reality into a battlefield. His anti-matter universe provides nearly infinite energy. Despite multiple defeats across continuity, he remains one of DC's definitive planet-scale, universe-scale threats with actual canonical record of destroying entire universes.
#27GalactusMarvel
Cosmic Devourer / Universal Function
+
Marvel's iconic cosmic devourer: a fundamental function of the universe's lifecycle more than a simple villain. Galactus is frightening because he is both a person and a necessity in many cosmological models — hunger as law. His origin as Galan of Taa, last survivor of the previous universe, makes him tragic. The Living Tribunal has on at least one occasion vouched for Galactus' necessity. His Heralds carry a fraction of the Power Cosmic. Defeating Galactus without addressing his cosmic function may break the universe in unpredictable ways.
#28Franklin RichardsMarvel
Omega-Level Mutant
+
A Marvel mutant whose reality-warping potential has repeatedly been portrayed as universe-class. Franklin is compelling because the power is paired with childhood: imagination becomes creation, fear becomes catastrophe, and maturity becomes the central limiter. He has created pocket universes as toys, and the Celestials recognize him as a peer. In Hickman's FF and later Empyre, his relationship with his own power — learning to trust it without being consumed by it — is the core of his arc. His ceiling may not have been reached yet.
#29Jean Grey (Phoenix Force)Marvel
Human Mutant / Phoenix Avatar
+
Jean as Phoenix is Marvel's archetype of cosmic fire: life, death, rebirth, and the temptation of infinite intensity. The Phoenix Force is portrayed as both salvation and apocalypse — the same energy that can ignite new life can also burn worlds. Dark Phoenix represents what happens when the power of a cosmic entity is filtered through human emotion without sufficient grounding: love becomes hunger, protection becomes consumption. Jean's recurring arc is about maintaining identity when the cosmos wants to use you as a weapon of renewal.
#30Darkseid (True Form)DC
New God / Idea of Tyranny
+
DC's archetype of tyranny elevated to metaphysics: Darkseid is not just a powerful alien god — he is the idea of domination, the gravitational pull of despair and obedience. "True Form" Darkseid exists beyond the normal multiversal layers, with avatars manifesting in lower realities. The Anti-Life Equation is not a weapon but a philosophical proof: a formula that eliminates free will by demonstrating life is meaningless. You can't simply defeat an idea with force; you must break its psychological supply chain — fear, scarcity, humiliation, the belief that cruelty is necessary.
#31The Beyonders (Hickman)Marvel
Extraversal Collective
+
In Hickman-era Marvel, the Beyonders are portrayed as a hyper-advanced, outside-normal-cosmology collective that treats universes like laboratory samples. Their threat is not emotional malice but systematic extermination: they experiment on reality's foundations and erase cosmic pillars as collateral. They represent engineering without ethics at multiversal scale — optimization that forgets the value of lives inside the system. Stopping them requires not a battle but an intervention: forcing them to meet the moral reality of their actions, and offering a path where curiosity becomes stewardship instead of vivisection.
#32AbraxasMarvel
Embodiment of Multiversal Entropy
+
A Marvel abstract/cosmic antagonist often framed as the destructive counter-principle to Eternity — an embodiment of multiversal annihilation that seeks the "perfection" of nothingness. Abraxas is terrifying because it attacks the existence of realities rather than the inhabitants inside them: it unthreads the scaffolding that makes a universe coherent. It cannot be "talked down" as a person; instead the story must address what it represents — despair that equates emptiness with peace. The win condition is to restore a reason for being: meaning as a stable attractor that resists nihilistic collapse at cosmic scale.
#33Rune King ThorMarvel
Asgardian / Master of Runes
+
A mythic apex version of Thor who has gained profound knowledge/power through runes and sacrifice — portrayed as operating on a level where he can rewrite or transcend Asgardian fate itself. Rune King Thor hanged himself on the World Tree and pulled out his own eye for wisdom, echoing Odin's sacrifice but going further. He destroyed the Designate and ended a threat that would have unmade all of creation. He is less about brute force and more about sovereignty over destiny — breaking cycles that bind gods to inevitable ends.
#34Superman Prime (One Million)DC
Kryptonian (Evolved)
+
A far-future Superman who has spent immense time absorbing solar energy and mythic experience, often depicted as an evolved near-mythic protector. Superman Prime stories lean into the idea of hope scaled up: the same moral center, but with a scope that reaches civilizations. He can recreate worlds and resurrect the dead by this point. His limits are rarely "can he lift it?" but "should he?" — how to intervene without erasing agency. He's an excellent mirror for examining power with humility: action vs non-action, or saving the many vs protecting the few.
#35Black WinterMarvel
Primordial Abstract Entity
+
A Marvel multiversal "ender" concept: not a planet-eater like Galactus, but a universe-consuming storm/entity associated with the collapse between cosmic cycles. It's portrayed as an apocalyptic weather-system of meaning — arriving when a cosmos is ready to be harvested or reset. The dread comes from inevitability: it feels like the season of death for entire realities. Galactus himself was chosen as its herald. The question isn't combat: can an "ender" be persuaded to spare, to defer, or to transform its role from extermination into renewal that preserves life and memory?
#36PralayaDC
Primordial Void / Nothingness
+

DC's personification of the Void/Nothingness — an end-state principle given form. Her function is not conquest but erasure: the pull of non-existence that reality ultimately returns to, collapsing timelines and structure back into 'before.' Within Justice League Dark stories she's depicted as a cosmological reset engine — awakening from slumber, manifesting void influence through time/entropy pressure, and treating all creation as something that will inevitably be reclaimed by the emptiness it emerged from. She is a philosophical ultimatum: engage with why existence is worth preserving.

#37ProtegeMarvel
Cosmic Mimic Entity
+
A Marvel cosmic wildcard defined by one terrifying ability: power mimicry without an obvious ceiling. Protégé can copy powers simply by observing them, and stories have pushed this to extremes — up to imitating the Living Tribunal. That makes Protégé the "exponential" threat: not evil by nature, but catastrophic if given exposure to the wrong demonstrations. The solution is guided development: building ethics, boundaries, and creative purpose before the power curve outruns maturity — turning the mimic into a responsible co-creator instead of an accidental apocalypse.
#38PerpetuaDC
Primordial Goddess / Multiverse Architect
+
A DC cosmic creator figure introduced in the Metal-era cosmology, framed as a maker of multiversal structures and a source of "design intent" behind the DC multiverse. Perpetua's villainy is often the arrogance of authorship: creation as ownership, worlds as property, beings as instruments. That makes the conflict ethical and political: who has the right to design reality, and what do creators owe their creations? The most satisfying resolution is not revenge but constitutional reform — binding a creator to principles that prevent exploitation of created beings.
#39Loki (God of Stories)Marvel
Trickster God / Narrative Entity
+
The multiversal/narrative-apex version of Loki — the God of Stories — from Kieron Gillen's run, where Loki rewrites his own fate by becoming the literal embodiment of stories rather than just a character within them. At this level he doesn't just manipulate; he edits the narrative fabric of reality. He's placed himself in the role of "change" itself within the Marvel cosmology. His greatest strength is the same as his greatest flaw: he can rewrite anything, including himself — which means he's always becoming something new, never settling into static power.
#40Spawn (God-Level) NewImage
Hellspawn / Cosmic Champion
+
Al Simmons at maximum power — the apex form of Spawn in Todd McFarlane's mythology. Having rejected both Heaven and Hell, Spawn eventually becomes a new God in the Spawn cosmology, the "Omega Spawn" who transcends the Heaven/Hell binary entirely. At this level he wields power that rivals the Angels and Demons of his universe at their strongest. His necroplasm is effectively limitless, and his connection to both divine and infernal forces gives him unique leverage over both sides of the cosmic war. He is the wild card in a cosmology otherwise defined by rigid sides — a being of free will in a universe of predetermined roles.

Skyfathers

#41Odin All-FatherMarvel
Asgardian All-Father
+
All-Father of Asgard: a god-king with immense mystical and cosmic power, but also deeply political and burdened by prophecy and legacy. Odin's strength is not just raw might — it's authority over realms and knowledge of ancient law. The Odinforce/Thorforce gives him reality-shaping abilities. His greatest limitation is pride and a tendency to mistake cosmic necessity for personal prerogative. His stories often explore whether a father who shapes destinies can also free his children — making him a skyfather archetype in the truest sense.
#42ZeusMarvel
Olympian God / Skyfather
+
Marvel's Zeus is a ruler among Olympian gods: immense divine might, cosmic-scale longevity, and political influence across pantheons. His stories often revolve around pride, authority, and the clash between ancient divine order and modern moral sensibilities. Zeus can be ally or antagonist depending on whether he respects the autonomy of mortals and other gods. He is the archetypal "old power" test: can respect be earned without force, and can a king-god accept that compassion and consent are not weakness? The best resolutions involve Zeus choosing honor over dominance.
#43TrigonDC
Interdimensional Demon Lord
+
DC's demon-lord archetype: a multiversal-scale predator of souls and worlds, often framed as the living consequence of hatred, conquest, and spiritual corruption. Trigon is dangerous because he weaponizes emotion and identity — turning love into leverage. He has literally consumed entire universes and is millennia old. His daughter Raven is both his greatest weapon and greatest vulnerability. Trigon's six eyes see into multiple dimensions simultaneously, and his true form dwarfs planets. Stopping him requires addressing the systemic evil he embodies rather than just his physical form.
#44DormammuMarvel
Dark Dimension Ruler
+
Marvel's Dark Dimension ruler: a being of immense mystical/cosmic power who thrives in a reality where physics is subordinate to sorcery and will. Dormammu is an archetypal conquering intellect — hungry for realms, souls, and leverage. In his own dimension he is effectively omnipotent; outside it, he remains formidable but can be countered. He is part of the Faltine race, beings of pure magical energy who abandoned their physical form. His bargain with Doctor Strange (time loop) is one of Marvel's most elegant uses of the "can't beat with force, must outsmart" narrative.
#45KnullMarvel
Primordial Symbiote Deity
+
Marvel's god of symbiotes: an ancient darkness that forges living weapons and treats individuality as raw material. Knull predates creation, having existed in the void before the Celestials brought light. He is the All-Black the Necrosword's creator — the first lethal weapon. His theme is anti-connection: he creates bonds that are parasitic, not loving — control masquerading as unity. His power scales through networks: once the hive spreads, the battle is everywhere. Countering him requires a "healthy connection" counter-signal: restore consent, autonomy, and mutual care within the network he exploits.
#46Doctor Fate (Classic Nabu)DC
Human (Lord of Order)
+
DC's archetypal Lord of Order: when Nabu is dominant, Fate becomes a near-impersonal instrument of cosmic law and magical precision. This makes Fate formidable against chaos, demons, and reality distortion — but also ethically scary, because "order" can become authoritarian. Fate is a perfect tension point: is peace the same as control? Nabu's rigidity pushes toward a version of order that includes consent and inner freedom — compassion must be structured, not just felt. The Helm of Nabu connects the wearer to the full power of Order, making them more than human but also less than free.
#47Zatanna (Full Potential)DC
Homo Magi / Sorceress
+
DC's stage magician turned true sorceress. Zatanna at full potential represents disciplined, reality-shaping magic guided by conscience. Unlike cosmic abstracts, her power is "human-shaped": she uses language, ritual, and focus, giving stories strong tension — spellwork can backfire, rules matter, emotions distort outcomes. At peak depiction she can contend with top-tier mystical threats. Her backwards speech magic is iconic but her real strength is creative adaptation under constraint — she can translate nonviolent resolutions into spells of containment, restoration, and communication that keep everyone alive.
#48Scarlet Witch (House of M)Marvel
Human Mutant / Nexus Being
+
A defining Marvel "reality rewrite" event: House of M Wanda reshapes the world through grief, desire, and trauma, demonstrating that emotional instability plus godlike power is existentially dangerous. Three words — "No More Mutants" — depowered 99% of Earth's mutants. She is a Nexus Being, a rare focal point of probability in the multiverse. Her chaos magic has roots in Chthon and the Elder Gods. Wanda's arc is about the relationship between grief and power: the question isn't whether she can rewrite reality, but whether she can learn to live within it.
#49Raven (Full Demon Mode)DC
Half-Demon Empath
+
A half-demon empath tied to Trigon. Raven at full demon mode is a storm of soul-magic, telepathy/empathy, and reality-bending darkness. Her theme is control: enormous inner power paired with the risk of being consumed by it. When Raven loses restraint, she becomes the very thing she fears. Her empathic powers are profound — she experiences others' emotions as her own. An entity of her power who masters her inner conflict becomes a uniquely powerful ally: her empathy becomes a weapon against cosmic manipulation rather than a vulnerability to it.
#50Doctor Strange (Sorcerer Supreme)Marvel
Human / Sorcerer Supreme
+
Marvel's premier mystic protector: a surgeon turned sorcerer whose real strength is knowledge, discipline, and the willingness to pay personal costs to prevent metaphysical disasters. Strange often wins by understanding rules — binding demons, exploiting loopholes, bargaining with entities far stronger than himself. His victories are typically pyrrhic: he accepts personal costs to preserve the greater structure. He provides the "magical infrastructure" — wards, safe spaces, translation between planes, and ethical constraints for cosmic interactions. A blend of compassion and rigorous ritual: healing the world without breaking it.
#51SurturMarvel
Fire Giant / Ragnarok Engine
+
Marvel's fire demon/giant of Muspelheim and a core Ragnarok trigger: an apocalyptic force more than a nuanced person. Surtur embodies catastrophe driven by destiny — when he rises, realms burn. His Twilight Sword, when fully charged, can destroy entire planets and even realms. He cannot be negotiated with as a person in the conventional sense; he is as inevitable as a season. The challenge is prevention of inevitability: interrupt prophecy loops, cool the political triggers that accelerate his awakening, and offer Surtur a different function than annihilation — transformation rather than extinction.
#52Doctor Doom (God Emperor)Marvel
Human / God Emperor
+
God Emperor Doom is the apex of Doom's archetype: absolute power fused to absolute ego, as seen in Secret Wars where he rules a patchwork reality. Doom is compelling because he believes he is the best possible ruler — and sometimes, in narrow metrics, he is competent. The horror is benevolent tyranny: safety purchased with obedience. The most interesting approach reveals the hidden violence of forced "order" and offers a way for Doom to keep dignity without control — transforming the need to dominate into a responsibility to serve. A powerful story ends with Doom confronted by the one thing he can't out-invent: genuine humility.
#53Swamp Thing (Avatar of the Green) NewDC
Earth Elemental / Avatar
+
Alec Holland as the Avatar of the Green: not a human who became a plant, but a plant who dreams of being human. At full elemental power, Swamp Thing can manifest across any plant life on Earth — or across multiple worlds with sufficient "Green" connection. He has battled powerful demons and navigated the Parliament of Trees. His relationship with the Swamp Thing role extends through time: past and future avatars all share a collective. At cosmic scale he represents the planet's own consciousness, making threats to Earth personally threatening in the most fundamental sense.
#54Adam Warlock NewMarvel
Cosmic Champion / Soul Gem Wielder
+
Created to be the perfect human, Adam Warlock has instead become one of Marvel's definitive cosmic champions. His Soul Gem gives him power over the souls of others and connection to a pocket universe. He has wielded the full Infinity Gauntlet, fought Thanos repeatedly, become the Magus (his dark future self), and helped prevent multiple multiversal collapses. Warlock's recurring arc is the burden of foresight: always seeing the catastrophe, never able to fully prevent it alone. He is simultaneously a hero, a potential villain, and a cosmic guardian — his identity is always contingent on which version of himself wins the internal conflict.
#55Mad Jim JaspersMarvel
Human Mutant (Reality Warper)
+
A Marvel reality-warping mutant whose power is most dangerous when unbounded by sanity, context, or containment. Jaspers is famous for turning "politics + madness + reality editing" into horror: laws rewritten, people remade, worlds twisted into grotesque art. A sufficiently powerful Jaspers forced the Beyonders themselves to intervene. The threat is not just his power but his instability — he can't be reasoned with in ordinary ways. The battleground is psychological coherence: stabilize the mind, reduce fear/trauma loops, and build safe constraints, because sane Jaspers with good values is a very different entity than the horror he becomes unchecked.

DC's quantum-powered hero who can tap into enormous energy, matter manipulation, and dimensional effects — often portrayed as a walking reactor with godlike potential but human fragility. #59Hulk (Worldbreaker)Marvel
Gamma Mutate (Apex Rage)

#60Flash (Wally West)DC
Speed Force Conduit

Wally West at peak is one of DC's most extreme speedsters, often portrayed as tapping deeply into the Speed Force with effectively "impossible" feats: time manipulation, dimensional slips, vibrating through matter, and near-instant traversal of the universe. A Marvel hero whose power set (anti-matter/energy manipulation) scales extremely high — he has fought Sentry, Hyperion, and entities far above human scale. He is arguably one of the most powerful beings in the Marvel universe who still maintains a fully human perspective and moral center.
#62Captain Marvel (Binary)Marvel
Kree/Human Hybrid

Binary Carol Danvers is a high-energy cosmic form tied to stellar-scale output — flight, energy projection, and extreme durability. Her Kree-derived photon absorption makes her stronger the more she is attacked with energy. #63Wonder Woman (God of War)DC
Demigoddess / God of War

A peak depiction of Diana where divine gifts, battle skill, and mythic authority align — sometimes with additional godly boons after becoming God of War. #64Nova PrimeMarvel

#65Doomsday NewDC
Kryptonian Abomination
+
Created through brutal evolution — killed and resurrected thousands of times until it could not be killed the same way twice — Doomsday is DC's most concentrated expression of inescapable violence. He literally killed Superman. The Doomsday protocol is his horrific advantage: adaptive evolution means the second encounter is always harder than the first. He has no goals, no philosophy, no vulnerabilities a negotiator can exploit. His post-death evolutions have made him capable of fighting Darkseid and surviving. Doomsday is the narrative argument that not every threat can be talked down — some require sacrifice.

A Marvel mantle powered by the Uni-Power/Enigma Force: it chooses a host in times of crisis, granting vast cosmic abilities for a limited purpose. Captain Universe is a beautiful narrative tool because the power is temporary and mission-driven — it has a built-in humility mechanism. The host remains a person, suddenly carrying universe-class responsibility. The Uni-Power has chosen the most unlikely people, from ordinary civilians to Peter Parker. #67Magneto (Full Power) NewMarvel
Human Mutant (Omega-Level)
+
Max Eisenhardt — Magneto — is one of Marvel's most complex omega-level mutants: master of magnetism with the ability to reshape Earth's magnetic field, pull meteors from orbit, and generate electromagnetic pulses capable of crippling global infrastructure. At full power he has fought Thor, deflected nuclear strikes, and torn metal cities apart. His power is genuinely planetary in scale. What elevates him above pure powerhouse status is his history and philosophy: a Holocaust survivor who turned his justified rage into a geopolitical force, making every confrontation with him simultaneously personal, political, and physical.

+
Base Superman is the iconic high-tier hero: solar-powered strength, speed, flight, heat vision, freeze breath, and a moral center built around hope and restraint. #71Hal Jordan (Green Lantern)DC
