1. Executive Summary

Round 2 tested 9 encoder variants built by different LLMs, all starting from the same gemZ v9.3 baseline architecture. The encoders were benchmarked on a corpus of 50 FASTA files spanning tiny transcripts (1.5 KB) through full human chromosomes (140 MB).

Key finding: Seven of nine encoders converged to near-identical compression totals (~305.4 MB), differing by less than 1 KB on most files. This indicates the gemZ v9.3 architecture is already near its ceiling for this codec family — the LLMs made marginal tweaks rather than architectural breakthroughs. The two outliers (GLMz at 311.5 MB, GPTz at 364.1 MB) regressed from the baseline.

The GeCo3 reference compressor achieves ~266.4 MB (16.88%), placing it roughly 39 MB ahead of all 8Z variants. This is the real competitive gap to close.

5. The Two Outliers

GLMz (GLM 5 DeepThink) — 6.1 MB worse

311.5 MB total, 2% regression from baseline. The log shows GLMz sometimes selects BYTE mode using brotli where the baseline picks NIB — this byte-mode variant is suboptimal on mid-to-large genomes. GLM's changes hurt more than they helped.

GPTz (ChatGPT 5.2 Thinking) — 58.7 MB worse

364.1 MB total, a catastrophic 19.2% regression from baseline (worse than plain 7z!). The log reveals GPTz uses only RAW mode with BZ2/LZMA codecs — no NIB transform, no periodic detection. ChatGPT's rewrite discarded the core genomic-aware features. Ironically, GPTz wins on 7 tiny files (<10KB) due to lower container overhead, but loses massively on everything else.

10. Key Findings & Recommendations

Finding 1: The gemZ v9.3 architecture has plateaued

Seven LLMs independently optimized the same codebase and converged within 1 KB of each other. The architecture is at its local optimum. Incremental LLM-driven tuning yields diminishing returns approaching zero.

Finding 2: Round 2 regressed ~3.3 MB vs Round 1

Round 1's CLA/GPT encoders achieved 302.1 MB on the same corpus. Round 2's gemZ-based encoders hit 305.4 MB. The HM4 hybrid architecture from Round 1 (with CTX3, per-stream-best codecs, solid-vs-split battle, and multiple block sizes) was slightly superior. A merge of Round 1 and Round 2 techniques is warranted.

Finding 3: GPTz (ChatGPT 5.2 Thinking) catastrophically failed

ChatGPT's encoder lost the NIB transform and PERIODIC detection — the two features that account for the entire compression advantage over generic compressors. At 364.1 MB it's worse than 7z (342.4 MB). This is a cautionary tale: LLM rewrites can destroy domain-specific features without understanding their purpose.

Finding 4: The real competition is GeCo3 (266.4 MB)

GeCo3 achieves ~16.9% ratio vs 8Z's ~19.4% — a 39 MB / 12.8% gap. GeCo3 uses finite-context models with arithmetic coding, fundamentally different from 8Z's transform-then-compress architecture. To close this gap, 8Z needs to move beyond LZ-family outer codecs toward statistical/context-mixing approaches (ANS/rANS, PPM, or CM).

Finding 5: Small-file container overhead matters

GPTz wins 7/11 small files (<100KB) despite terrible compression — its container format has lower overhead. The gemZ format adds ~300-400 bytes of metadata that dominates the compressed output for tiny files. An adaptive container format (minimal header for small inputs) would reclaim these wins.

Finding 6: Per-organism adaptive mode selection has untapped potential

GROz's byte-mode wins on chr19 (gene-dense) while nib-mode wins on most other chromosomes. An MDL-driven transform selector that tests byte/nib/raw per-block (as the Round 1 HYB encoder does) can capture the best of both.
